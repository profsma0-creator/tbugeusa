## Sources and supported additions

### Godoy et al. (2018)

- **Source:** A comprehensive overview on stress neurobiology: Basic concepts and clinical implications. *Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience, 12*, Article 127. DOI: 10.3389/fnbeh.2018.00127.
- **Access:** Publisher full-text sections “History of Stress Research” and opening discussion of allostasis; author/citation block checked. Other sections were not read in full.
- **Use:** Sections 1.2.3, 1.3, and 1.6: homeostasis, adaptation, allostasis, Selye's historical contribution, and the general stress-system framework. The historical source is cited as a review rather than misrepresented as direct inspection of Cannon's or Selye's original works.
- **Limit:** The review discusses debate about allostasis; the reconstruction retains this qualification. No quantitative result or clinical protocol is inferred.
- **Record:** [1](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2018.00127/full).

### Goldstein and Kopin (2007)

- **Source:** Evolution of concepts of stress. *Stress, 10*(2), 109–120. DOI: 10.1080/10253890701288935.
- **Access:** Publisher PDF, historical overview and modern concepts sections, including discussion of homeostasis, nonspecificity, and allostasis. Metadata checked against the publisher PDF and the indexed record. The journal publication year is 2007; a later online-hosting date is not substituted for it.
- **Use:** Sections 1.2.3 and 1.3: active regulation, historical development, patterned responses rather than a universal unitary response, and distinctions among allostasis and cumulative burden.
- **Limit:** This is a systems account with a particular conceptual position, not a universal consensus statement about every use of the terms.
- **Records:** [2](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10253890701288935); [7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17514579/?dopt=Abstract).

### Scoville and Milner (1957)

- **Source:** Loss of recent memory after bilateral hippocampal lesions. *Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery, and Psychiatry, 20*(1), 11–21. DOI: 10.1136/jnnp.20.1.11.
- **Access:** Primary paper hosted by McGill University, opening methods/results and anatomical limitations; original publication metadata corroborated by the PubMed record. The 2000 reprint is not substituted for the 1957 original.
- **Use:** Section 1.4: memory impairment after bilateral medial temporal surgery, the distinction from uniform intellectual loss, and limits of attributing effects to one isolated structure.
- **Limit:** No inference that these patients had PTSD or that the study directly established mechanisms of traumatic memory. No quotations or detailed individual outcomes are copied into the textbook.
- **Records:** [1](https://www.mcgill.ca/neuro/files/neuro/milner1957.pdf); [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13406589/).

### Pitman et al. (2012)

- **Source:** Biological studies of post-traumatic stress disorder. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 13*(11), 769–787. DOI: 10.1038/nrn3339.
- **Access:** Publisher abstract/key points and PubMed abstract/figure legends; author list corroborated against the bibliography already supplied in the original book. The subscription full text was not read in full.
- **Use:** Sections 1.5–1.7: psychophysiological measures, limits of applying a simple chronic HPA-overactivity model to PTSD, and historical structural/functional imaging questions.
- **Limit:** Findings are situated as a 2012 synthesis, not represented as an exhaustive 2026 assessment. Figure legends were consulted for interpretation, but no publisher figure was reproduced.
- **Record:** [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn3339).

### Karolinska Institutet (1977)

- **Source:** The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 1977 [Press release]. NobelPrize.org.
- **Access:** Official historical press release, including the account of releasing hormones, the 1969 structural work, radioimmunoassay, and the respective contributions of Guillemin, Schally, Yalow, and Berson.
- **Use:** Section 1.6: complementary advances in identifying regulatory substances and measuring hormones.
- **Limit:** This historical account is not used as a contemporary anatomy reference or clinical standard. Its historical terminology is not reproduced uncritically.
- **Record:** [1](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1977/press-release/).

### Hinojosa, George, and Ben-Zion (2024)

- **Source:** Neuroimaging of posttraumatic stress disorder in adults and youth: Progress over the last decade on three leading questions of the field. *Molecular Psychiatry, 29*(10), 3223–3244. DOI: 10.1038/s41380-024-02558-w.
- **Access:** Publisher abstract and introductory material; author list, pages, issue, date, and DOI corroborated by publisher/indexed search records.
- **Use:** Section 1.7: development of PTSD imaging since the 1990s; pre-existing versus acquired differences; longitudinal design, treatment change, replication, and limits on routine clinical implementation.
- **Limit:** No numerical effect estimate, treatment comparison, diagnostic criterion, or proposed biotype is endorsed from this review. It is not described as the latest available review in 2026.
- **Record:** [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-024-02558-w).

### U.S. Food and Drug Administration (undated citation)

- **Source:** About biomarkers and qualification. Official FDA Biomarker Qualification Program page.
- **Access:** Full page portions defining biomarkers, categories, context of use, and qualification.
- **Use:** Section 1.2.3: the distinction between biological indicators and measures of how a person feels or functions; the need to specify intended use.
- **Limit:** No unverified publication/update date is assigned. No claim that a trauma biomarker has been qualified is made.
- **Record:** [1](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/biomarker-qualification-program/about-biomarkers-and-qualification).
