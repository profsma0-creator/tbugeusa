## The Table Where Generations Meet

**And this chapter is about a piece of furniture, and it is the last of the structures in this Part, and it is the one that most houses of God do not own.**

**The reason is not cost, and it is not space. It is that a table requires both generations to be present at the same time and to be able to speak, and that is precisely what the ordinary arrangement of a religious house prevents.**

**Because a pulpit runs in one direction.** A man stands, and the congregation sits, and the words travel downward and do not return. **Which is the correct arrangement for the reading and the preaching of the word of God, and it is an ordinance and not an accident, and this chapter is not an argument against it.**

**And a table has no such direction, and the change is not decorative.**

**At a table a man can be interrupted, and a question can be asked without permission, and the man with the least standing can begin a sentence that the most senior person present has to finish listening to. Which means that a house of two generations and no table will have a monologue, and the monologue will be excellent, and the young men will leave in their twenties.**

**And the sentence that settles the case is not an instruction but a promise about the end of the age:**

*And I appoint unto you a kingdom, as my Father hath appointed unto me; that ye may eat and drink at my table in my kingdom, and sit on thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel* (Luke 22:29–30).

**At my table in my kingdom.**

**Which means the ultimate arrangement of the people of God is not a congregation listening to a speaker. It is a company at a meal, and the ones speaking at it are the ones who were told to sit on thrones, and the meal is served by the One who is Lord of both.**

### The meal that preceded the best advice in the Old Testament

**And the first case is a consultation that reorganised the government of Israel, and it happened the day after a dinner.**

**Jethro, the father-in-law of Moses, had come out to the wilderness when he heard what God had done for Israel, and the record of the meeting begins with a sacrifice and a meal:**

*And Jethro, Moses' father in law, took a burnt offering and sacrifices for God: and Aaron came, and all the elders of Israel, to eat bread with Moses' father in law before God* (Exodus 18:12).

**And all the elders of Israel came to eat bread.**

**Which means the meal was attended by the two generations of the leadership of the nation — Moses the mediator and the elders of Israel — and it was eaten *before God*, which is the phrase Scripture uses for a covenant meal.**

**And the consultation happened the next day, in public, and it began with a question from the old man:**

*And it came to pass on the morrow, that Moses sat to judge the people: and the people stood by Moses from the morning unto the evening. And when Moses' father in law saw all that he did to the people, he said, What is this thing that thou doest to the people? why sittest thou thyself alone, and all the people stand by thee from morning unto even?* (Exodus 18:13–14).

**What is this thing that thou doest to the people?**

**Which is the question every older man should be able to ask a young leader, and it is an observation followed by a question and not an accusation.** He had watched a full day of the operation before he said anything, and the two clauses of his question were accurate: *thou sittest thyself alone*, and *all the people stand by thee.*

**And Moses answered, and his answer was a defence of his practice and it was sincere:**

*Because the people come unto me to enquire of God: when they have a matter, they come unto me; and I judge between one and another, and I do make them know the statutes of God, and his laws* (Exodus 18:15–16).

**Which is a good answer.** He was doing the work of a mediator and a teacher, and he was doing it himself, and the reason was that he was uniquely qualified to do it.

**And the old man's reply is the most direct sentence in the passage, and it is worth noting that it is delivered to the man who had stood in the presence of God on a mountain:**

*And Moses' father in law said unto him, The thing that thou doest is not good. Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people that is with thee: for this thing is too heavy for thee; thou art not able to perform it thyself alone* (Exodus 18:17–18).

**The thing that thou doest is not good.**

**Which means the older man contradicted the younger man's practice, in front of the elders of Israel, on the day after the meal, and the grounds he gave were practical and not moral.**

**And then he gave the counsel, and it is in three movements, and the first is the one that young leaders forget because it is the most religious and the least popular:**

*Hearken now unto my voice, I will give thee counsel, and God shall be with thee: Be thou for the people to God-ward, that thou mayest bring the causes unto God: and thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws, and shalt shew them the way wherein they must walk, and the work that they must do* (Exodus 18:19–20).

**Be thou for the people to God-ward.**

**Which means the older man's first instruction was to tell the younger man to do less administration and more intercession and teaching.** The delegation was the third movement, not the first; **the first was to keep the young man in the presence of God, and the second was to keep him in the book.**

**And then the structure, with its four tiers and its one condition:** *able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness* (18:21).

**And the criterion for the appointment is the same one that appears in the pastoral letters a thousand years later, and the whole passage turns on it.** The men to be appointed were to be chosen for character and not for capacity, **and the phrase *hating covetousness* is the same concern that produced *not greedy of filthy lucre* in Timothy's list.**

**And then the sentence that makes the counsel safe to receive:**

*If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so, then thou shalt be able to endure, and all this people shall also go to their place in peace* (Exodus 18:23).

**And God command thee so.**

**Which means the older man submitted his own counsel to God's approval, and said so in the sentence.** He did not say *I am older and therefore this is the will of God.* **He said: do this if God commands you — which is the difference between a father and a man who wants to be obeyed.**

**And the end of the scene is the model of how a young leader should receive advice from an older man who has no jurisdiction over him:**

*So Moses hearkened to the voice of his father in law, and did all that he had said* (Exodus 18:24).

**And did all that he had said.**

**Which means the man who had spoken with God face to face, and whose face shone, and who had been in the cloud, listened to his father-in-law, a priest of Midian, and changed the government of the nation.** And the passage does not present the counsel as a revelation from God. **It presents it as the word of an old man delivered after dinner, and the young man did all that he had said.**

### The council in Jerusalem, and the silence given to the younger men

**And the second case is the most formal decision recorded in the New Testament, and its procedure is the most instructive thing in it.**

**The question was whether Gentile converts must be circumcised and keep the law, and the dispute could not be settled locally.**

**And the assembly was constituted of both generations:**

*And the apostles and elders came together for to consider of this matter* (Acts 15:6).

**Apostles and elders. Which means the men who had been with Christ from the beginning and the men who had been appointed in the churches they founded, in one room, considering one matter.**

**And the order of speaking is recorded, and it is worth noting that the first long speech was from Peter, an apostle and an elder; the second was from Paul and Barnabas, reporting evidence; and the third was from James, the Lord's brother, who was not one of the twelve.**

**And before the testimony was given, the assembly was silent:**

*Then all the multitude kept silence, and gave audience to Barnabas and Paul, declaring what miracles and wonders God had wrought among the Gentiles by them* (Acts 15:12).

**The whole multitude kept silence.**

**Which is the rarest event in any religious assembly, and it was occasioned by the decision to hear evidence from men who had been on the mission field.**

**And the outcome was a letter, and its superscription names the parties in the order of their authority and adds a third category that most institutional documents omit:**

*Then pleased it the apostles and elders, with the whole church, to send chosen men of their own company to Antioch ... And they wrote letters by them after this manner: The apostles and elders and brethren send greeting unto the brethren which are of the Gentiles in Antioch and Syria and Cilicia* (Acts 15:22–23).

**The apostles and elders and brethren.**

**Which means the decision was made with the consent of the whole assembly, published over the name of the whole assembly, and carried by named men from among them.**

**And the additional detail is that the delegation was composed of men who had been chosen by the whole company and not by the leadership alone.** *Judas surnamed Barsabas, and Silas, chief men among the brethren.* **Which means the younger men carried the decision, and their names are in the record.**

**And the sentence that governs the whole procedure is in the letter itself, and it is the standard for every meeting of two generations that intends to produce anything:**

*For it seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us, to lay upon you no greater burden than these necessary things* (Acts 15:28).

**It seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us.**

**Which means the assembly did not vote and then claim God's approval, and it did not claim a revelation and then dispense with the discussion.** The two clauses are in the same sentence, in that order, **and the burden of proof in the whole matter was assigned to the party proposing to add requirements.**

**And the practical lesson for a house of two generations is the shape of the proceeding.** A matter is raised. Both generations are present. The evidence is heard, including the evidence of what God has done among the outsiders. Silence is kept for testimony. The matter is decided. A written instrument is produced, naming the parties. **And the men who carry it are named, and some of them are new.**

### The meal at Bethany, and the three postures

**And the third case is the one that shows what a house with a table looks like when everything is in order, and it is described in three verses.**

*Then Jesus six days before the passover came to Bethany, where Lazarus was which had been dead, whom he raised from the dead. There they made him a supper; and Martha served: but Lazarus was one of them that sat at the table with him. Then took Mary a pound of ointment of spikenard, very costly, and anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped his feet with her hair: and the house was filled with the odour of the ointment* (John 12:1–3).

**Take the four persons and their positions.**

**Jesus, at the table, as the guest and the reason for the meal.**

**Lazarus, at the table, sitting — and the text says so explicitly, *one of them that sat at the table with him*, which is a deliberate detail about a man who had been four days dead and had walked out of a tomb at the Lord's command.** He is the evidence, present at the meal, saying nothing, **and his presence was the testimony.**

**Martha, serving — and the same woman who had earlier complained that her sister had left her to serve alone (Luke 10:40), now serving and saying nothing. Which means she had grown.**

**And Mary, at the feet, doing something that made no sense to the other diners, and whose act filled the whole house.**

**Which means the table holds four different roles at once, and none of them is a pulpit.** And the record of the following verses is that the objection to Mary's act came from the treasurer of the twelve (12:4–6), **which means that a table of two generations is also the place where the house's own corruption can become visible.**

**And the same family had already supplied the pattern of the two postures in one house, in the sentence that has governed Christian devotion ever since:**

*And she had a sister called Mary, which also sat at Jesus' feet, and heard his word* (Luke 10:39).

**Which means the sitting at the feet and the serving in the kitchen are not rival vocations, and that the Lord's verdict on the day Mary chose the feet was that she had chosen *that good part, which shall not be taken away from her* (10:42) — and He did not, in saying so, tell Martha to stop serving.**

**Which means a house with a table needs both and must not make either one the measure of the other.**

### Emmaus, and the two men who received the Scriptures on a road and the Lord at a table

**And the fourth case is the greatest of them, and it is the fullest account in Scripture of a teaching session conducted by the Lord Himself, and it happened on a walk and concluded at a meal.**

**Two disciples were travelling from Jerusalem to Emmaus on the afternoon of the resurrection, and they were talking about everything that had happened, and they were sad (Luke 24:13–17).**

**And the Lord joined them, and their eyes were held, and He began with a question: *what manner of communications are these that ye have one to another, as ye walk, and are sad?***

**Which is the first element of the method. The conversation began with the younger men speaking, and with the older man asking them what they thought.** And the answer was a full statement of their position, delivered by Cleopas, and it was largely correct and entirely discouraged (24:18–24).

**And the second element is the rebuke, and it was not gentle:**

*Then he said unto them, O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken: ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory? And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself* (Luke 24:25–27).

**Which means the Lord corrected them before He taught them, and that the correction was about the slowness of their hearts rather than the feebleness of their minds.** And then He spent the rest of the journey expounding the whole canon.

**And the third element is that they did not know who He was.** Which means the greatest Bible conference in the history of the world was conducted by an unrecognised teacher to two men who thought they were receiving an interesting stranger's opinion, **and their assessment of the session is recorded afterwards, and it is the sentence that ought to be the measure of every sermon:**

*And they said one to another, Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?* (Luke 24:32).

**Did not our heart burn within us?**

**Which is the only reliable testimony to the presence of God in a teaching session, and it is not given by the speaker.**

**And the fourth element is the table, and the whole point of the passage turns on the order of events:**

*And they drew nigh unto the village, whither they went: and he made as though he would have gone further. But they constrained him, saying, Abide with us: for it is toward evening, and the day is far spent. And he went in to tarry with them. And it came to pass, as he sat at meat with them, he took bread, and blessed it, and brake, and gave to them. And their eyes were opened, and they knew him; and he vanished out of their sight* (Luke 24:28–31).

**He made as though he would have gone further.**

**Which means the Lord tested them at the door.** He did not force His company on them; He behaved as a traveller who would continue, **and the invitation was theirs, and it was based on the lateness of the day and not on their expectation.**

**And then He sat at meat, took bread, blessed it, and broke it, and their eyes were opened.**

**Which is to say that three hours of exposition on the road had not opened their eyes, and the breaking of bread did.**

**Which means the table is not a supplement to the teaching. It is the place where the teaching becomes recognition.** Which is the same arrangement the Lord instituted with His own hands on the night before He died, in the words Paul received and delivered:

*For I have received of the Lord that which also I delivered unto you, That the Lord Jesus the same night in which he was betrayed took bread: and when he had given thanks, he brake it, and said, Take, eat: this is my body, which is broken for you: this do in remembrance of me. After the same manner also he took the cup, when he had supped, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood: this do ye, as oft as ye drink it, in remembrance of me. For as often as ye eat this bread, and drink this cup, ye do shew the Lord's death till he come* (1 Corinthians 11:23–26).

**Till he come.**

**Which means the table carries the whole time span of the church in two directions at once — back to a death and forward to a return — and that a house which teaches its young people what the bread means has given them the mechanism for crossing both.**

**And the last detail of the Emmaus account is that the two of them got up and walked seven miles back to Jerusalem that same hour to tell the others, and the account of what they said is one line:**

*And they told what things were done in the way, and how he was known of them in breaking of bread* (Luke 24:35).

**Which means a house that has a table will have its members doing at midnight what the congregation did on the day of Pentecost: *did not our heart burn within us* is a sentence that a man says to somebody else, and then goes and tells.**

### The Lord at supper, and the two things He did at it

**And the fifth case is the institution of the table itself, and it contains two acts that fall on the two generations, and they were performed at the same meal on the same night.**

**The first is that He told the truth about the future, and He told it while the bread was on the table:**

*And when the hour was come, he sat down, and the twelve apostles with him. And he said unto them, With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer: for I say unto you, I will not any more eat thereof, until it be fulfilled in the kingdom of God* (Luke 22:14–16).

**With desire I have desired.**

**Which means that the last supper was not a formality and not a farewell. It was the thing He had been looking forward to.**

**And the second is that when a dispute arose at the table, He did not end the meal or rebuke the men.**

*And there was also a strife among them, which of them should be accounted the greatest* (Luke 22:24).

**Which means that in the same room, on the same evening, within hours of the institution of the sacrament of His body and blood, the twelve argued about which of them was the greatest.**

**And His answer was not a rebuke of ambition. It was a redefinition of greatness, and it was addressed to both generations at once:**

*And he said unto them, The kings of the Gentiles exercise lordship over them; and they that exercise authority upon them are called benefactors. But ye shall not be so: but he that is greatest among you, let him be as the younger; and he that is chief, as he that doth serve. For whether is greater, he that sitteth at meat, or he that serveth? is not he that sitteth at meat? but I am among you as he that serveth* (Luke 22:25–27).

**Let him be as the younger.**

**Which is the sentence that governs every table in this book.** The one who is greatest is to take the position of the younger, and the one who is chief is to take the position of the servant, **and the ground He gives is His own conduct: *I am among you as he that serveth.***

**Which means that the arrangement at a table of two generations is not seniority with humility added. It is an inversion.**

**And the same act was performed a few hours earlier in the same room, and the record of it is the one that settles the question of who is to serve whom:**

*Ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am. If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you. Verily, verily, I say unto you, The servant is not greater than his lord; neither he that is sent greater than he that sent him* (John 13:13–16).

**Which means the Lord of the house took the towel on the night when His authority was at its most absolute, and that the instruction to the two generations is not that the older should condescend to serve the younger, but that both should wash one another's feet.**

**And the promise attached to the act is the sentence that should be framed above every table in every house of God:**

*If ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them* (John 13:17).

### The Rechabites, and what a house can hold for three hundred years

**And the last case is not a table at all but the thing a table produces, and it is the most remarkable family record in the Old Testament.**

**Jeremiah was commanded to set wine before the whole house of the Rechabites, in a chamber of the temple, in front of the men of Judah, and to invite them to drink (Jeremiah 35:2–5).**

**And they refused, and their refusal is a statement of their constitution:**

*But they said, We will drink no wine: for Jonadab the son of Rechab our father commanded us, saying, Ye shall drink no wine, neither ye, nor your sons for ever: neither shall ye build house, nor sow seed, nor plant vineyard, nor have any: but all your days ye shall dwell in tents; that ye may live many days in the land where ye be strangers ... But we have dwelt in tents, and have obeyed, and done according to all that Jonadab our father commanded us* (Jeremiah 35:6–7, 10).

**Which means a command given by one man to his household three hundred years earlier was still being kept, exactly, by his descendants, in the temple, under the eyes of the king's court, with the wine poured out in front of them.**

**And note what the house kept and what it did not.** It kept the prohibition, the tents, the absence of vineyards and the absence of houses — **and its members were in Jerusalem at that moment only because of the Chaldean army, which means the rule had survived a national catastrophe and a forced migration.**

**And the principle of the command, when it is stated by the father, is generational: it was given *that ye may live many days in the land where ye be strangers.***

**And the verdict of God on the household is one of the highest in the Old Testament, and it was delivered by contrast with the nation:**

*Thus saith the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Because ye have obeyed the commandment of Jonadab your father, and kept all his precepts, and done according unto all that he hath commanded you: therefore thus saith the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Jonadab the son of Rechab shall not want a man to stand before me for ever* (Jeremiah 35:18–19).

**Shall not want a man to stand before me for ever.**

**Which is the promise of an unbroken succession, given to a family that had kept a single household command for three centuries.**

**And the rebuke that accompanies it is addressed to the nation, and it is devastating: the Rechabites kept their father's word, and Judah did not keep God's (35:14–16).**

**Which means that the mechanism of a house that transmits is not complicated, and the Rechabites demonstrate it.** A command was given once, in a household, with reasons. **It was written into the ordinary life of the family — tents, no vineyards, no wine — so that it was practised every day rather than remembered once a year. And it was practised by every member: *we, our wives, our sons, nor our daughters* (35:8).**

**And the reason the table belongs in this chapter is that a household command of that kind is transmitted at meals.** A family that lives in tents and does not drink wine explains why, at a table, to children who ask, for three hundred years. **And a family that has no table has no venue at which the question can be asked.**

### What must be at the table, and what must not

**And the practical construction of this chapter consists of the items that must be present and the items that must be excluded.**

**What must be present is six things.**

**The text.** At every table in Scripture that matters, the Scriptures are opened. *Beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself* (Luke 24:27). **And the household ordinance of Deuteronomy 6 assumes that the words are the subject of the conversation: *and shalt talk of them.***

**The question.** The Lord began with one and Jethro began with one. *What is this thing that thou doest to the people?* **And a table at which no question can be asked is a lecture delivered in a dining room.**

**And the questions must run both ways.** Jethro questioned Moses; and Elihu, the youngest man in the book of Job, was present at a conversation between four older men, and he spoke, and Scripture records his reasoning for waiting:

*Now Elihu had waited till Job had spoken, because they were elder than he. When Elihu saw that there was no answer in the mouth of these three men, then his wrath was kindled. And Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite answered and said, I am young, and ye are very old; wherefore I was afraid, and durst not shew you mine opinion. I said, Days should speak, and multitude of years should teach wisdom. But there is a spirit in man: and the inspiration of the Almighty giveth them understanding. Great men are not always wise: neither do the aged understand judgment* (Job 32:4–9).

**Which is the doctrine of the speaking order at any table of two generations, and it contains both halves.** The young man waited, out of respect; he spoke when the older men had finished and had no answer; **and he stated the reason a young man's contribution is required — because *there is a spirit in man*, and wisdom is not guaranteed by the number of a man's years.**

**And note the manner: *let me not, I pray you, accept any man's person, neither let me give flattering titles unto man* (Job 32:21).** Which is what a table produces when it is working, and what it prevents when it is not.

**The bread.** The meal is not a formality to be rushed through before the meeting. In every one of the cases in this chapter the food is on the table and the conversation takes place over it.

**The correction.** The Lord rebuked the two disciples and then taught them, and the same evening He corrected the twelve for their ambition. **Which means a table that never produces a correction in either direction is a table at which somebody is being polite rather than being a father.**

**And the record.** The council in Jerusalem produced a letter and named its carriers. **And the household that keeps no record will have children who ask *what mean ye by this service* and parents who cannot answer.**

**And what must be excluded is three things.**

**The lecture.** Which is the failure mode of the older generation at a table: turning the meal into a sermon with no room for a question. **And the test of whether it has happened is whether a young man has interrupted you in the last year.**

**The flattery.** Which is the failure mode of the younger: the habit of agreeing with everything, and of saying the pleasant thing, and of treating the older man's opinions as beyond examination. **And Absalom's method was applied at a gate and not at a table, and it was flattery that grew in the absence of any place where the older man could be contradicted.**

**And the meeting that never decides.** The council in Jerusalem came together *to consider of this matter* and it decided it, in writing, and sent the decision out. **And a house whose senior men have been meeting for years and producing nothing has replaced a table with a gesture.**

### And the end of the matter

**And the reason this chapter belongs in the architecture of a generational covenant is stated in the vision of the church that the Lord Himself left in the last book of the Bible:**

*Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me* (Revelation 3:20).

**I will sup with him, and he with me.**

**Which means the Lord's own description of the closest relation He offers a believer is a meal at which there is conversation in both directions.** And the promise was given to a church that had grown rich and lukewarm and had not noticed that He was outside the door (3:17, 20).

**Which means that the recovery of a house begins with a knock at the door and a man opening it and setting a place.**

**And the last word belongs to the wisdom literature, and it is the general principle which the whole chapter has been demonstrating:**

*Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established* (Proverbs 15:22).

*Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety* (Proverbs 11:14).

*For by wise counsel thou shalt make thy war: and in multitude of counsellors there is safety* (Proverbs 24:6).

**Which is stated three times in three different forms, and the reason for the repetition is that a man who is planning something will always believe that he does not need advice.**

**And the counsel that establishes a purpose is not the counsel of men who agree with it.** The multitude of counsellors in the three verses is a body of men who are consulted and who can say no. **Which means a house that has no table has no counsellors, whatever the size of its board.**

**And the practical instruction of this chapter is therefore one item long.**

**Put a table where the two generations can both sit, and put it in the diary, and put the text on it, and let the questions run both ways, and keep the record.**

**And expect the following, because the Lord promised it: *if ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them.***

### Principles

**First: the ultimate arrangement of the people of God is a table and not a pulpit.** *That ye may eat and drink at my table in my kingdom, and sit on thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel.*

**Second: the pulpit runs in one direction by ordinance, and a table does not, and a house with only the first will have a monologue and will lose its young men.**

**And third: the best structural advice in the Old Testament was given the day after a meal, to a man who had spoken with God.** *And Aaron came, and all the elders of Israel, to eat bread with Moses' father in law before God.*

**And fourth: an older man who has watched the whole operation may ask a plain question and finish it with an equally plain judgement.** *What is this thing that thou doest to the people? ... The thing that thou doest is not good.*

**And fifth: the first counsel a young leader needs is not delegation but presence and teaching.** *Be thou for the people to God-ward, that thou mayest bring the causes unto God: and thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws.*

**Sixth: counsel from an older man is safe to receive when it is submitted to God's approval in the sentence itself.** *If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so, then thou shalt be able to endure.*

**And seventh: the man who spoke with God face to face hearkened to his father-in-law and changed the government of the nation.** *So Moses hearkened to the voice of his father in law, and did all that he had said.*

**Eighth: a decision of two generations requires the silence of the whole assembly for testimony, and it produces a written instrument naming the parties.** *Then all the multitude kept silence, and gave audience to Barnabas and Paul*; and *it seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us.*

**And ninth: the decision is published over the name of the whole church, and the younger men carry it.** *It pleased the apostles and elders, with the whole church, to send chosen men of their own company ... Judas surnamed Barsabas, and Silas.*

**Tenth: a house with a table holds four postures at once — the Lord present, the evidence sitting, the service done, and the devotion poured out at the feet.** *Martha served: but Lazarus was one of them that sat at the table with him.*

**And eleventh: three hours of exposition did not open the eyes of the men on the Emmaus road, and the breaking of bread did.** *And it came to pass, as he sat at meat with them, he took bread, and blessed it, and brake, and gave to them. And their eyes were opened.*

**Twelfth: the Lord told His men the truth about the future with the bread on the table.** *With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer: for I say unto you, I will not any more eat thereof, until it be fulfilled in the kingdom of God.*

**And thirteenth: greatness at a table is inverted, and the ground given is the Lord's own conduct.** *But he that is greatest among you, let him be as the younger; and he that is chief, as he that doth serve ... but I am among you as he that serveth.*

**And fourteenth: a household command kept for three hundred years produced the promise of an unbroken succession.** *Because ye have obeyed the commandment of Jonadab your father ... Jonadab the son of Rechab shall not want a man to stand before me for ever.*

**And fifteenth: the young man must wait, and then must speak.** *I am young, and ye are very old; wherefore I was afraid, and durst not shew you mine opinion ... But there is a spirit in man: and the inspiration of the Almighty giveth them understanding. Great men are not always wise.*

**And sixteenth: the Lord's own description of intimacy with a believer is a meal with conversation.** *If any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me.*

### For the fathers

- **Put a table in the diary, with food on it, and let the subject be the Scriptures.** *And Aaron came, and all the elders of Israel, to eat bread with Moses' father in law before God.*
- **Watch the operation before you comment on it, and then ask a plain question.** *What is this thing that thou doest to the people?*
- **Give your first counsel about prayer and teaching, and your second about delegation.** *Be thou for the people to God-ward ... moreover thou shalt provide out of all the people able men.*
- **Submit your advice to God's approval in the sentence itself.** *If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so.* An older man who cannot say that has replaced counsel with command.
- **Keep silence for the younger men's evidence, and let the decision be published over the whole church's name.** *It seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us.*
- **Read the young man who has waited all evening, and when he speaks, let him finish.** *Great men are not always wise: neither do the aged understand judgment.*
- **And take the towel, and expect the young to wash your feet too.** *Ye also ought to wash one another's feet.*
- **And do not let the meeting end without a decision and a record.** *And they wrote letters by them after this manner.*

### For the young

- **Ask at the table, and ask about the service and not about your prospects.** *What mean ye by this service?* is the question the ordinance anticipates.
- **Honour the speaking order: wait, and then speak when the older men have no answer.** *Now Elihu had waited till Job had spoken, because they were elder than he.*
- **And when you speak, refuse to flatter.** *Let me not, I pray you, accept any man's person, neither let me give flattering titles unto man.* Agreement is not honour, and the house that receives only agreement is being dismantled.
- **Take the walk and the table together.** The two disciples received the exposition on the road and recognised Him at the meal, and they said afterwards that their hearts burned during the walk.
- **Learn to serve at the table without resenting the kitchen.** Martha served, Mary sat, and the Lord told Martha that Mary had chosen the good part — and He did not tell Martha to stop serving.
- **Say what you actually think about the future of the house, out loud, in the room where it can be answered.** Jeremiah's two disciples were sad and said so, and the answer they received was the whole of Scripture.
- **And if the table in your house is only a pulpit with plates on it, ask for the change and offer to set the table.** The Rechabites kept a household command for three hundred years because it was practised at every meal by every member of the family.

### Questions for reflection

1. Where in your house do the two generations sit at the same table, with the Scriptures open and questions allowed in both directions?
2. What has an older man said to you in the last year that began *the thing that thou doest is not good*, and did you hearken?
3. What has a younger man said to you that you did not want to hear, and what did you do with it?
4. When did your senior men last produce a written decision and name the men who would carry it?
5. If a child at your table asked what the bread means, what would the answer be, and who would give it?
