## The Young Must Not Become Fatherless

**A generation can be lost without a single one of its young people dying, and it can be lost without a single one of them sinning first.**

That is the claim of this chapter, and it is the reason the previous chapters of this Part have insisted that the transfer of revelation must reach a young man with a father still attached to it.

**The opposite condition has a name in Scripture, and the name is used of a nation and of an individual and of a spiritual state.** The name is *fatherless*, and the writers of Scripture treat it not as a misfortune but as a category of judgement.

**And the last sentence of the Old Testament is about precisely this.**

### The last sentence before four hundred silent years

*Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord: and he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse* (Malachi 4:5–6).

**Consider where this sentence sits.** It is the closing words of the Hebrew canon's final prophetic book, the last thing God is recorded as saying before the four hundred years of silence that end at the birth of John the Baptist.

**And the last thing said is not about worship, or sacrifice, or the temple, or the Law. It is about the direction of two hearts.**

**Note the two directions, and note that they are not the same movement.**

**The fathers' hearts must turn to the children.** That is the older generation, addressed first, and its failure is the failure of attention. **The heart of a father can be occupied with the work of God while being turned away from his own descendants, and Malachi treats that as the terminal illness of a nation.**

**And the children's hearts must turn to the fathers.** That is the younger generation, and its failure is the failure of honour. **The turn of heart is mutual, which means it is not a programme for young people and it is not a programme for old people. It is a reconciliation with two parties who each owe the other something.**

**And the stake named is extraordinary: *lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.***

**The Hebrew is *cherem* — the ban, the devoted thing, the total destruction that was pronounced on the Canaanite cities.** The consequence attached to a failure of generational transmission at the end of the Old Testament is nothing less than the curse of the land.

**This is not hyperbole in a minor prophetic postscript.** It is the diagnosis of why a whole civilization becomes eligible for judgement, and the diagnosis is that the older generation stopped facing its young and the younger stopped facing its old.

**And the mechanism appointed to fix it is named: an Elijah ministry.**

*And he shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just; to make ready a people prepared for the Lord* (Luke 1:17).

**The angel adds a line to Malachi's text: *and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just.*** The turning of hearts and the turning of the disobedient are the same operation described twice. **A young man who is out of order is, in the vocabulary of Luke 1, a young man whose heart has not been turned to his fathers.**

**And John's work is summarised as *to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.*** Which tells us that a prepared people, in God's vocabulary, is a people whose generations are facing one another.

**If that is the precondition of the Lord's coming, then the state of a generation's fathers and young men is not a matter of church culture or preference. It is a matter of readiness.**

### Ten thousand escorts and very few fathers

**And Paul, writing to a church he had founded and which had accumulated a considerable number of teachers, makes a distinction that the whole subject turns on.**

*I write not these things to shame you, but as my beloved sons I warn you. For though ye have ten thousand instructors in Christ, yet have ye not many fathers: for in Christ Jesus I have begotten you through the gospel. Wherefore I beseech you, be ye followers of me* (1 Corinthians 4:14–16).

**The Greek for *instructors* is *paidagogous*, and the word is not the word for a teacher.**

In the Greek household the *paidagogos* was a slave whose duty was to escort the boy to school, to keep him out of mischief on the way, and to deliver him to the master. **He carried authority, he administered correction, and he had no paternal relationship with the child at all. He was a custodian, not a father.**

**And Paul says that a congregation can accumulate ten thousand such custodians and still be fatherless.**

**Which is the most practically useful sentence in the New Testament on this subject, because it names the specific spiritual condition of a well-taught, well-attended, well-structured body of believers: it can be surrounded by men who carry the boy to school and not one man who has begotten him.**

**And Paul states his own ground of relationship with four words: *I have begotten you.***

**The Greek is *egennesa*, from *gennao*.** It is the verb of procreation, and Paul uses it of the church at Corinth because he was the one through whose preaching they came to life. **Their existence in Christ has a human instrument, and the instrument regards himself as their father, and it is not a metaphor for affection. It is a statement of origin.**

**And then note what a father supplies which the escort cannot.**

**Verse 16: *be ye followers of me.*** The Greek is *mimetai mou ginesthe* — become imitators of me. **The escort cannot say that. The escort can say *do the assignment*, but only a father can say *watch me*.**

**Verse 17: *For this cause have I sent unto you Timotheus, who is my beloved son, and faithful in the Lord, who shall bring you into remembrance of my ways which be in Christ.***

**Two things are in that sentence.** The first is that Paul's *ways* are transferable enough to be carried by a third party — Timothy can remind the Corinthians of Paul's ways, which means a father's manner of life is a communicable curriculum and not a private temperament.

**And the second is that the son is the transmission mechanism.** Paul does not send a manual. **He sends the man he begat, to convey the ways of the man who begat him, to a church he begat.** Three generations in one sentence, operating simultaneously.

**That is what a father is for, and the escort cannot do it, and no institution can hire enough escorts to close the gap.**

### What the nursing father supplies

And the most detailed account of paternal function in the New Testament is Paul's description of his own ministry in Thessalonica, and every clause of it is a duty.

*But we were gentle among you, even as a nurse cherisheth her children: so being affectionately desirous of you, we were willing to have imparted unto you, not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls, because ye were dear unto us* (1 Thessalonians 2:7–8).

**The word for *nurse* is *trophos*, and it is the word for the woman who suckles an infant.** Paul, who had planted churches, debated philosophers, and endured beating and imprisonment, describes his posture toward a congregation as the posture of a nursing mother.

**And the phrase that matters for our subject is *our own souls.***

**He gave them the gospel, and the gospel was not the whole of what he gave.** The Greek is *tas heauton psychas* — his own life. **This is the difference between the escort and the father stated in a single word: the escort gives information, and the father gives himself.**

**And the next verses make the same point by way of his labour.**

*For ye remember, brethren, our labour and travail: for labouring night and day, because we would not be chargeable unto any of you, we preached unto you the gospel of God* (1 Thessalonians 2:9).

**He worked so that he would not cost them anything.**

**Which is the financial rule of fatherhood, and it is the opposite of what most religious systems produce.** A father supports his children. **A man who takes his living from the ones he is supposed to be forming has reversed the polarity, and Paul's phrase for it — *chargeable* — is the language of a burden placed on the wrong party.**

**And he states the principle outright elsewhere:**

*Behold, the third time I am ready to come to you; and I will not be burdensome to you: for I seek not yours, but you: for the children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children. And I will very gladly spend and be spent for you; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved* (2 Corinthians 12:14–15).

**Three things in that passage describe the paternal disposition.**

**The first is the object of the seeking: *not yours, but you.*** A man who is building a following seeks the followers. **A father seeks the person, and would rather have the person without their resources than their resources without the person.**

**The second is the direction of the provision: *the children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children.*** In the ancient world the obligation ran both ways, and adults were expected to support their ageing parents. **Paul states the obligation in the parent-to-child direction and says that is the picture of a spiritual father.**

**And the third is the cost, and it is stated without a hedge: *I will very gladly spend and be spent for you.***

**The Greek for *spend* is *dapaneso*, to expend resources, and for *be spent* it is *ekdapanetheisomai*, the intensified form — to be utterly consumed.** So the sentence reads: I will gladly exhaust my resources and then be exhausted myself on your account. **And the clause after it is the honest pastoral footnote of every true father — *though the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved.***

**And this is what Moses was protesting about when the burden of a whole people was laid on him.**

*Have I conceived all this people? have I begotten them, that thou shouldest say unto me, Carry them in thy bosom, as a nursing father beareth the sucking child, unto the land which thou swarest unto their fathers?* (Numbers 11:12).

**The phrase is *as a nursing father*, *ha'omen*.** And Moses' argument is that carrying a people in that manner is the work of a begetter, and he did not beget them. **The point of the protest is the same as Paul's point: the work described is paternal, and it cannot be performed by a man who is not willing to hold the relationship.**

**And Job offers the plainest statement of the same duty.**

*I was a father to the poor: and the cause which I knew not I searched out* (Job 29:16).

**Note the two halves.** The first is the relationship. The second is the work that came out of it — *the cause which I knew not I searched out*. **A father investigates. A father does not dismiss a case because it is unfamiliar to him, and he does not send the young man away because the complaint is inconvenient.**

### The young man whose fatherhood had an expiry date

And there is a case in the histories of the kings which is so exactly fitted to this chapter that it must be examined in detail, because it shows that the danger is not merely having no father. **It is having one whose paternal work was never completed.**

**Joash came to the throne of Judah as a boy of seven years, saved from a massacre by his aunt and hidden in the temple for six years while Athaliah ruled.**

**And for as long as his guardian lived, he was a good king.**

*And Joash did that which was right in the sight of the Lord all the days of Jehoiada the priest* (2 Chronicles 24:2).

*And Jehoash did that which was right in the sight of the Lord all his days wherein Jehoiada the priest instructed him* (2 Kings 12:2).

**Read the second verse slowly, because the second clause is a condition on the first.** He did what was right *all his days wherein Jehoiada instructed him*. **The approved period has an end date, and the end date is the death of the instructor.**

**And the Chronicler records the death with an extraordinary honour.**

*But Jehoiada waxed old, and was full of days when he died; an hundred and thirty years old was he when he died. And they buried him in the city of David among the kings, because he had done good in Israel, both toward God, and toward his house* (2 Chronicles 24:15–16).

**He was buried with the kings, and the reason given is that he had done good *toward God, and toward his house*.** The man who fathered a king is counted good in both directions at once.

**And then the next verse, which is one of the saddest transitions in all of Scripture.**

*Now after the death of Jehoiada came the princes of Judah, and made obeisance to the king. Then the king hearkened unto them. And they left the house of the Lord God of their fathers, and served groves and idols: and wrath came upon Judah and Jerusalem for this their trespass* (2 Chronicles 24:17–18).

**The princes come, they flatter, and the king listens, and within one verse the nation is in apostasy.** And the man who leads them there is a king who, a chapter earlier, had repaired the temple of God.

**And the final act is the one that reveals the state of his heart.**

*And the Spirit of God came upon Zechariah the son of Jehoiada the priest, which stood above the people, and said unto them, Thus saith God, Why transgress ye the commandments of the Lord, that ye cannot prosper? because ye have forsaken the Lord, he hath also forsaken you. And they conspired against him, and stoned him with stones at the commandment of the king in the court of the house of the Lord* (2 Chronicles 24:20–21).

**He murdered the son of the man who had saved his throne and raised him.** And the Chronicler's verdict is a single sentence, and it is the sentence this chapter exists to examine:

*Thus Joash the king remembered not the kindness which Jehoiada his father had done to him, but slew his son. And when he died, he said, The Lord look upon it, and require it* (2 Chronicles 24:22).

**Notice that the Chronicler calls Jehoiada *his father*.**

**Joash had a father, and the father was faithful, and the father lived to a hundred and thirty, and the father's work did not hold.** And the reason must be stated plainly, because it is the most important negative lesson in this Part.

**Jehoiada fathered Joash into compliance and not into conviction.**

**A boy of seven hidden in a temple, crowned by a priest, and guided for decades by a man of God will be obedient as long as the guidance continues.** And obedience that is produced by the presence of a guardian ends when the guardian is buried. **The evidence that it was never the young man's own faith is that the first flatterers to arrive were believed, and the first prophet to arrive was killed.**

**And here is where the two errors of fatherhood sit side by side.**

**The first error is to leave a young man fatherless.** He receives no formation and drifts by default.

**And the second error is to father him so completely that his spiritual decisions are all made in the presence of a man, and none of them are ever made in the presence of God.**

**The second error is more subtle and produces more impressive results during the father's lifetime, which is why it is so common among good and sincere men.** A tightly governed young man looks magnificent. **He does what he is told, he quotes what he has heard, he exhibits the discipline of the house. And the test is entirely deferred until the day your coffin enters the ground, when the princes arrive and make their obeisance and he hearkens to them.**

**Which means that the goal of fathering cannot be obedience. It must be the transfer of the father's God.**

**That is what Elisha asked for at the Jordan and what Joash never received.** *Where is the Lord God of Elijah?* is the right question for a son to ask, and a father's duty is to have made sure that the son knows the answer and knows the God, and not merely the method.

### Fatherlessness in the vocabulary of Scripture

**And now we must be precise about what the word means, because it is used with more weight in Scripture than in ordinary speech.**

*Sing unto God, sing praises to his name: extol ye him that rideth upon the heavens by his name JAH, and rejoice before him. A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation. God setteth the solitary in families: he bringeth out those which are bound with chains: but the rebellious dwell in a dry land* (Psalm 68:5–6).

**Note the two things God does in that passage, and note that the second is the remedy for the first.** He is *a father of the fatherless*, and He *setteth the solitary in families*. **The divine method for dealing with fatherlessness is the creation of families, which means that God's usual instrument for fathering a fatherless young man is another human being.**

**And the confessions of Lamentations give the condition its national form.**

*Remember, O Lord, what is come upon us: consider, and behold our reproach. Our inheritance is turned to strangers, our houses to aliens. We are orphans and fatherless, our mothers are as widows* (Lamentations 5:1–3).

**Look at the sequence.** The inheritance goes to strangers, the houses to aliens — and then the confession that the survivors are orphans. **Fatherlessness, in that passage, is not the state of a bereaved family. It is what a dispossessed nation calls itself, because the transfer of inheritance stopped and the generation that remained had no one above it to hand anything on.**

**Which is the link to everything in this Part.** A generation that has been given no deposit is experienced by that generation as fatherlessness. **It is not merely that they have no mentor; it is that they have been disinherited, and they can feel the absence even when they cannot name it.**

**And God offers Himself in exactly that capacity:**

*When my father and my mother forsake me, then the Lord will take me up* (Psalm 27:10).

*Wilt thou not from this time cry unto me, My father, thou art the guide of my youth?* (Jeremiah 3:4).

**The second verse is in the middle of God's own complaint against a faithless nation, and the phrase He puts in their mouth is *the guide of my youth*.** It is the phrase a young person uses of a man who walks ahead of him, and God offers it of Himself. **So the young man without a guide has a standing invitation, and it is issued by God, and the word He chooses to describe the role is paternal.**

**And the Lord Jesus classified the post-ascension state with the same vocabulary.**

*I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you* (John 14:18).

**The Greek word translated *comfortless* is *orphanous*.** It is the word for orphans. **What the Lord said to His disciples that night was: I will not leave you orphans.**

**And He was about to leave.** Within hours He would be arrested, and within two days buried, and after forty days He would ascend. **The permanent condition of the church between the ascension and the return is a condition the Lord Himself describes as a household in which the father has gone, and the promise He makes regarding it is that He will not leave it fatherless.**

**So the New Testament's entire apparatus of apostles, elders, and spiritual fathers must be understood as the means by which the Father of the fatherless keeps His promise through human instruments.** And that is the highest possible statement of what an ordinary faithful man does when he takes a young man under his roof.

### The diagnosis in Isaiah

And a whole nation's condition is described by Isaiah in a passage that is read as a promise far more often than it is read as a plague.

*And I will give children to be their princes, and babes shall rule over them. And the people shall be oppressed, every one by another, and every one by his neighbour: the child shall behave himself proudly against the ancient, and the base against the honourable* (Isaiah 3:4–5).

**This is in a list of judgements.** Isaiah 3 opens with the removal of the staff and the stay, of the mighty man and the man of war, of the judge and the prophet and the prudent and the ancient (Isaiah 3:1–3), and then the sentence above. **The removal of the ancient is the judgement, and children in authority is the consequence.**

**And verse 12 states it again, and adds a detail that ought to be read in every generation that is proud of its young leadership.**

*As for my people, children are their oppressors, and women rule over them. O my people, they which lead thee cause thee to err, and destroy the way of thy paths* (Isaiah 3:12).

**Note that the young rulers are described as *oppressors* and as those who *cause thee to err* and *destroy the way*.** The passage is not a celebration of youth. **It is the portrait of a society in which the old have been removed and the young have inherited authority without having inherited wisdom, with the result that the path itself is destroyed and everyone under them is led wrong.**

**And the specific symptom named in verse 5 is *the child shall behave himself proudly against the ancient.***

**So there is a test available to any generation that wishes to know which condition it is in.**

**If the young are being raised up under the hand of fathers, corrected by them, sent by them, and finally commissioned by them, the fruit will be honour, and the ancient will be treated with reverence.**

**And if the young have been given prominence without formation, and if the older men in the room have become decorative, then the young will eventually behave proudly against the ancient, and it will be read by everyone as boldness.**

**The two conditions produce superficially similar scenes and completely different texts.** One is Joel 2, where the old men dream dreams while the young men see visions and both are together. **The other is Isaiah 3, where the child behaves proudly against the ancient and the nation is under judgement and does not know it.**

### The heir under tutors until the time appointed of the father

And Galatians supplies the framework in which the whole process is meant to be understood.

*Now I say, That the heir, as long as he is a child, differeth nothing from a servant, though he be lord of all; but is under tutors and governors until the time appointed of the father* (Galatians 4:1–2).

**The heir owns everything and controls nothing, and the period of that condition is not indefinite and is not determined by his own readiness. It is *the time appointed of the father*.**

**That single clause defines both failures.**

**The father who never appoints the time leaves an heir who is legally lord of all and functionally a servant for the rest of his life.** And the observation to be made about that arrangement is that it is stable, pleasant for the father, and produces a man who has never carried anything.

**And the son who appoints his own time has taken a prerogative that belongs to the father**, and the taking of it is precisely what Galatians describes as the posture of a child who has decided he is grown.

**And note that the father's responsibility in the interval is not merely custody.** *And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord* (Ephesians 6:4).

**The two words are *paideia* and *nouthesia* — training and admonition — and the pair has already appeared in this book in the discussion of the sin of silent fathers.** *Paideia* is the whole formation of a person by discipline, correction, instruction and example. **And *nouthesia* is the placing of truth into the mind by word.**

**So the father owes the young man two things at once: a formation that shapes his character, and a body of truth placed into his understanding.** And the warning that precedes the command is that the process, badly handled, produces wrath. **Which is the difference between a father and a tyrant: both apply pressure, and only one of them is trying to produce a man.**

### Three ways a young man becomes fatherless

**And the condition has three forms, and they call for three different responses.**

**The first is the young man with no father at all.** The man is absent, or dead, or was never known, or is present in the house and absent from the life. **And in a generation with high rates of family breakdown, this is the majority form of the problem, and it is the form that the church is commanded to address by the example of Job and by the promise of Psalm 68:5.**

**The second is the young man whose father is present and unavailable.** This is the most common form among religious people, and it is the form Malachi describes when he speaks of the fathers' hearts needing to turn. **A man can be at every meeting, preaching every week, known to thousands, and never once have taken one young man aside and asked him about the state of his soul.**

**And it should be said plainly that a father's availability is not measured by his calendar but by his attention.** The professional religious man is frequently a man whose whole working life is conducted in front of crowds and almost none of it in front of one person. **And the tell is whether any young man can say of him what Paul's churches could say of Paul: *ye know how we exhorted and comforted and charged every one of you, as a father doth his children* (1 Thessalonians 2:11).**

**Note the phrase *every one of you*.** Not the group, not the congregation, not the class. **Fathering is done one at a time, and no amount of public teaching substitutes for it.**

**And the third form is the young man whose father formed him thoroughly and gave him everything except the father's God.** This is the Joash condition, and it is the hardest to detect because it looks like the best result. **The test of it is a simple question, and it is addressed to the young man himself: what do you do when nobody is watching and nobody is available to be asked?** If the honest answer is *I wait until I can ask*, then the formation has produced dependence rather than sonship.

### Timothy: the young man who had no believing father

And the remedy for all three forms is set out in the life of the young man who became Paul's closest companion.

*Then came he to Derbe and Lystra: and, behold, a certain disciple was there, named Timotheus, the son of a certain woman, which was a Jewess, and believed; but his father was a Greek* (Acts 16:1).

**His father was a Greek, and the implication of the passage, given that Timothy had not been circumcised, is that his father was not a believer and had prevented the sign of the covenant.**

**So Timothy is precisely the young man who should have been lost, and the account of how he was not is given in the last letter Paul wrote to him.**

*When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in thee, which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice; and I am persuaded that in thee also* (2 Timothy 1:5).

**Two women, one grandmother and one mother, and a faith that dwelt in them first.** And no believing father anywhere in the account.

**And what he received from his mother and grandmother is stated: *from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures* (2 Timothy 3:15).** The women gave him the text.

**And what he received from Paul is everything else.**

**Paul calls him *my own son in the faith* (1 Timothy 1:2) and *my beloved son* (2 Timothy 1:2), he laid hands on him with the presbytery (1 Timothy 4:14), he took him on his journeys, and he wrote him two letters of paternal instruction in which he says *thou therefore, my son, be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus* (2 Timothy 2:1).**

**The pattern to be learned is this: God supplied the father.**

**A believing mother and grandmother carried the deposit as far as a woman can carry it, and then God put a man in the road.** *For though ye have ten thousand instructors in Christ, yet have ye not many fathers* — and Timothy got one, because a fatherless young man with the Scriptures in him is the precise object of God's paternal attention.

**And the response required of the young man is stated in the same letter, and it is entirely within his own power.**

*But thou hast fully known my doctrine, manner of life, purpose, faith, longsuffering, charity, patience* (2 Timothy 3:10).

**He followed. He stayed. He watched.** And the watchfulness of the disciple is the second half of the transaction, and no father, however willing, can supply it for him.

### The measure of fathering

And so the whole matter can be reduced to one criterion, and it is the criterion the previous chapter established.

**The measure of a father's work is not what his son does while he is present. It is what his son does when he is gone.**

**Jehoiada, by that measure, failed, and the failure was not a failure of effort.** He hid the boy, crowned him, taught him, and served him for decades. **He simply never handed over the God, and so the sonship terminated at the graveside.**

**Elisha, by that measure, succeeded at the riverbank, because a father had sent him out of sight and he came back with the waters parted.**

**And Paul, by that measure, spent his last written words on a young man who had no father by blood and every reason to have none by faith — and turned him into the carrier of everything he had.**

**Which means that the duty owed to the young is not the avoidance of fatherlessness in the abstract.** It is the specific and daily work of turning the heart toward a particular young man, in the presence of a particular God, for long enough that the young man knows Him, and no longer merely knows you.

### Principles

**First: the last sentence of the Old Testament is about the direction of two hearts.** *He shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers.* A generation that is not doing this is not prepared for the Lord's coming, whatever else it is doing.

**Second: the failure is named as a curse on the land.** *Lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.* The stakes of generational reconciliation in Scripture are national, not personal.

**Third: ten thousand instructors do not constitute a father.** *Paidagogos* — the escort who delivers the child to the school and has no paternal relation to him. A structured, well-taught church can be entirely fatherless.

**Fourth: what a father supplies is himself.** *We were willing to have imparted unto you, not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls.* The escort gives information; the father gives his life.

**Fifth: the financial direction of fatherhood runs downward.** *The children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children.* And the posture is to *spend and be spent*, gladly, and to be loved the less for it.

**Sixth: a young man can be fathered into compliance rather than into conviction, and the failing will not be visible until the father's funeral.** Joash did right all the days wherein Jehoiada instructed him, and within a chapter of Jehoiada's burial he was serving idols and stoning the priest's son.

**Seventh: the divine remedy for fatherlessness is family.** *God setteth the solitary in families.* And the usual instrument is not a programme but a person who gives attention to one young man at a time.

**And eighth: God supplies the father.** Timothy had a grandmother, a mother, and no believing father, and *he obtained mercy* in a man. **Which means no young man in this house has an excuse, and no father in it has a defence.**

### For the fathers

- **Turn your heart toward a particular young man, and let him know it.** Malachi does not describe a programme; he describes the direction of a heart, and a heart has an address.
- **Give yourself, not only the message.** *Not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls.* Ask honestly what part of your life a young man has access to.
- **Pay for your own ministry where you can.** *The children ought not to lay up for the parents.* A father who is expensive to his sons has inverted the order.
- **Do not complete his decisions for him.** Your goal is not a young man who obeys you; it is a young man who knows your God. Test the difference — send him where he cannot consult you, and see what he does.
- **Check whether his obedience is conviction or custody.** Joash did right while Jehoiada lived and hearkened to the first flatterers afterwards. Ask what your son does when he is not with you.
- **Do not provoke him to wrath while you form him.** *Paideia* and *nouthesia*, not pressure and control. Tyranny and fatherhood both apply force, and only one of them is trying to produce a man.
- **Father every one of you, one at a time.** *As a father doth his children* is followed by *every one of you*. A crowd cannot be fathered; a person can.

### For the young

- **If you have a father in the faith, stay close.** *Thou hast fully known my doctrine, manner of life, purpose* — Timothy's advantage was proximity, and it is available to you.
- **If you have no father, God has promised to supply one.** *A father of the fatherless ... God setteth the solitary in families.* Timothy had two women and no believing father, and God put Paul in the road. Look for the man, and do not despise the women who carried you to him.
- **Do not appoint your own time.** *Until the time appointed of the father.* A son who announces his own maturity has taken a prerogative that is not his, and the taking of it is itself the proof of childhood.
- **Do not become proud against the ancient.** Isaiah 3 calls it a judgement, not a boldness. The irreverence you feel toward older men is data about you and not about them.
- **Do not merely obey the man; learn his God.** *Where is the Lord God of Elijah?* Ask the question at your own Jordan. If your faith cannot survive your father's absence, it was never yours.
- **And if you are fatherless, do not become a defaulter in the same condition.** The man who was never fathered is the one most able to father, because he knows exactly what was missing.

### Questions for reflection

1. Who is the young man whose heart is turned toward you, and when did you last give him your own soul rather than your information?
2. If the men who have fathered you were all removed tomorrow, what of your faith would still stand and what of it would be found to have been borrowed?
3. Which of the three fatherless conditions best describes your own formation — no father, an unavailable father, or a father who gave you everything except his God?
4. What is your church doing for the young man who has nobody, and is it a programme or a relationship with a name attached?
5. What will your son do in the week after your funeral?
