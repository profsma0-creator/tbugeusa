## Charisma Without Character

**And there is one sentence in the Gospels that should be read by every man who has ever been used by God, because it is spoken to men who were used by God, and the verdict is not what anyone would expect.**

*Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven. Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity* (Matthew 7:21–23).

**Take the three claims in order, because every one of them is a record of genuine activity.**

**They prophesied.** Not that they claimed to; the Lord does not dispute it.

**They cast out devils in His name.** Which is an act of power over the kingdom of darkness, performed by His name.

**And they did many wonderful works.** The Greek word is *dynameis*, powers, the same word used for the miracles of Christ and the apostles.

**Which means the men in that sentence are not frauds who were exposed. They are men whose ministry was real, whose gifts operated, whose results were visible, and who were told at the end that the Lord had never known them.**

**I never knew you.**

**And the tense matters more than the verdict. He does not say that He knew them once and they fell away. He says He never knew them — which means the relationship was absent from the beginning, while the gifts were functioning from the beginning.**

**Which establishes the doctrine of this chapter in one sentence. The operation of a gift is not the evidence of a relationship.**

### God's list of requirements is a character list

**And the proof that this is the mind of God about leadership is not a single verse but two entire lists, and they are the only formal qualification documents in the New Testament.**

**Paul's list for overseers, written to Timothy:**

*A bishop then must be blameless, the husband of one wife, vigilant, sober, of good behaviour, given to hospitality, apt to teach; not given to wine, no striker, not greedy of filthy lucre; but patient, not a brawler, not covetous; one that ruleth well his own house, having his children in subjection with all gravity; (for if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?) not a novice, lest being lifted up with pride he fall into the condemnation of the devil. Moreover he must have a good report of them which are without* (1 Timothy 3:2–7).

**And his list for elders, written to Titus:**

*If any be blameless, the husband of one wife, having faithful children not accused of riot or unruly. For a bishop must be blameless, as the steward of God; not selfwilled, not soon angry, not given to wine, no striker, not given to filthy lucre; but a lover of hospitality, a lover of good men, sober, just, holy, temperate; holding fast the faithful word as he hath been taught, that he may be able by sound doctrine both to exhort and to convince the gainsayers* (Titus 1:6–9).

**Count the items. In the first list there are fifteen requirements, and exactly one of them concerns ability: *apt to teach*. In the second there are thirteen, and one concerns doctrinal competence: *holding fast the faithful word* and being able to exhort and convince.**

**Which means that when God wrote down what He requires of the men who will lead His people, the overwhelming majority of the requirements were about the man and not about the man's gift.**

**Blameless. Sober. Of good behaviour. Hospitable. Not violent. Not greedy. Patient. Not quarrelsome. Ruling his own household. Not a novice. Well spoken of by outsiders.**

**And the list contains no requirement for the gift of prophecy, no requirement for the working of miracles, no requirement for tongues, and no requirement for the ability to move a congregation.**

**Which is not because those things are unimportant. It is because they are not the qualification, and the church has never been permitted to treat them as one.**

**And note the single most frightening clause in the whole document, which occurs in both lists: *having his children in subjection* — *for if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?***

**The household is God's laboratory.** A man may be eloquent in public and absent at home, and the public record will not tell you; **the children will.**

**And then Peter's instruction to the elders, written to men who already held office, and it forbids exactly the three abuses that follow from a gift without a character:**

*The elders which are among you I exhort, who am also an elder ... Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind; neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being ensamples to the flock. And when the chief Shepherd shall appear, ye shall receive a crown of glory that fadeth not away* (1 Peter 5:1–4).

**Not by constraint. Not for money. Not as lords. And the alternative in each case is the same: willingly, of a ready mind, and as an example.**

**Which means that the office is to be carried by the man and not by the man by the office, and that the only method of leadership permitted is the example.**

### Saul: the man who prophesied and threw a javelin

**And the case that proves the doctrine beyond argument is the first king of Israel, because his career contains both a genuine experience of the Spirit and a demonstration that the experience can continue after the obedience has gone.**

**At the beginning he was given a heart, and the gift, and a sign:**

*And the Spirit of the Lord will come upon thee, and thou shalt prophesy with them, and shalt be turned into another man ... And it was so, that when he had turned his back to go from Samuel, God gave him another heart: and all those signs came to pass that day* (1 Samuel 10:6, 9).

**Another heart. The Spirit of the Lord upon him. Prophesying among the prophets. And the proverb of the nation was *Is Saul also among the prophets?* (10:11).**

**Which means Saul's beginning was not fraudulent. He was a man God had genuinely touched and genuinely used.**

**And the turn in his life is recorded in a sentence that is about obedience and not about gifting:**

*For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. Because thou hast rejected the word of the Lord, he hath also rejected thee from being king* (1 Samuel 15:23).

**And then, two chapters later, the awful verse:**

*But the Spirit of the Lord departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the Lord troubled him* (1 Samuel 16:14).

**And here is the detail that makes Saul the central case of this chapter. What happened after the Spirit departed:**

*And it came to pass on the morrow, that the evil spirit from God came upon Saul, and he prophesied in the midst of the house: and David played with his hand, as at other times: and there was a javelin in Saul's hand. And Saul cast the javelin; for he said, I will smite David even to the wall with it* (1 Samuel 18:10–11).

**He prophesied, and then he threw the spear.**

**Which means that the phenomenon was still occurring in the house of a king who had already been rejected, while he was attempting to murder the man God had anointed to replace him.**

**And the same sequence is repeated in 19:9–10, and the record states the reason plainly: *and Saul was afraid of David, because the Lord was with him, and was departed from Saul* (18:12).**

**And the end is stated in a verse that describes the state of a man whose gifts have outlived his obedience:**

*And when Saul enquired of the Lord, the Lord answered him not, neither by dreams, nor by Urim, nor by prophets. Then said Saul unto his servants, Seek me a woman that hath a familiar spirit, that I may go to her, and enquire of her* (1 Samuel 28:6–7).

**The man who had once prophesied among the prophets ended by consulting a medium at Endor, and he died the next day on Gilboa.**

**Which means that the gifts of the Spirit are not a permanent possession and not a certificate of standing.** They were given for the service of the people of God, and they can be withdrawn, **and a man who has operated in them and continues to operate in them may be several stages into his own destruction.**

**And the application for every generation is one question that a man should ask himself with the care he would give to a medical diagnosis: is the activity of God in my ministry the same thing as the activity of God in my life?**

### Samson: "he wist not that the LORD was departed from him"

**And the most chilling sentence in the Old Testament on this subject is spoken about a man whose strength was proverbial and whose discipline was nil.**

**Samson was a Nazarite from the womb, given a commission before his birth, promised as the beginning of Israel's deliverance. And his weakness was disclosed, and Delilah arranged the shave, and the record describes the moment he rose to break the cords as he had done before:**

*And he awoke out of his sleep, and said, I will go out as at other times before, and shake myself. And he wist not that the Lord was departed from him* (Judges 16:20).

**He wist not that the Lord was departed from him.**

**Which means he did not know.** There was no announcement, no earthquake, no audible voice. **He went out expecting the same outcome as the previous three occasions, and the departure of God's power had already occurred, and he was the last person in the story to find out.**

**And note the sequence of the enemy's method, because it is precise.**

**They did not attack the gift. They attacked the vow** — the one thing Samson had control over, and the one thing the gift depended on. **They took his secret, and with it his hair; and then they put out his eyes, and bound him with fetters of brass, and he did grind in the prison house (16:21).**

**Which means that the gift went, the eyes went, the liberty went and the usefulness went, in that order, and the first of them he only discovered in the hour of the crisis.**

**And the account does not end there, which is why it is in the book rather than in a warning list:**

*Howbeit the hair of his head began to grow again after he was shaven* (Judges 16:22).

**And in the last scene of his life, blind, bound and standing between the pillars, he prays:**

*O Lord God, remember me, I pray thee, and strengthen me, I pray thee, only this once, O God, that I may be at once avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes ... Let me die with the Philistines. And he bowed himself with all his might; and the house fell upon the lords, and upon all the people that were therein* (Judges 16:28, 30).

**And he is in the list of the heroes of faith in Hebrews 11.**

**Which means two things at once, and both must be preached.**

**The first is that God's mercy reached a man who had spent his gift on his appetites, and his last act was a genuine act of deliverance, and his name is in the eleventh chapter of Hebrews.**

**And the second is that the restoration did not reach the eyes.** He was buried in his father's sepulchre (16:31), in the territory of his own tribe, having judged Israel twenty years — **and he died blind, and in a building that came down on his own head, and the two-fold price of the departure of the Spirit is there for every subsequent generation to read.**

**And the lesson for a young man with an evident gift is that God's commitment to you is not the same as God's commitment to your eyes.** He will still use you; **and the consequences of the years you spent before the last one do not always come back.**

### Judas, who cast out devils

**And the hardest case in the whole of this subject is the case of the man who belonged to the apostolic company, because it establishes that the gifts were not withheld from him.**

**The twelve were sent out with authority over demons, and the text does not qualify the commissioning by listing the men:**

*Then he called his twelve disciples together, and gave them power and authority over all devils, and to cure diseases. And he sent them to preach the kingdom of God, and to heal the sick ... And they departed, and went through the towns, preaching the gospel, and healing every where* (Luke 9:1–2, 6).

**All twelve. Which included Judas Iscariot.**

**And the record of his inner life, given by the apostle John, is a single sentence about a habit:**

*Then saith one of his disciples, Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, which should betray him, Why was not this ointment sold for three hundred pence, and given to the poor? This he said, not that he cared for the poor; but because he was a thief, and had the bag, and bare what was put therein* (John 12:4–6).

**He was a thief, and he carried the bag.**

**Which means he was stealing from the common fund of a company that was healing the sick and casting out devils.** And he did the works, and he was there when the power went out of the Lord and healed the multitudes, **and he remained a thief the whole time, and nobody in the company knew.**

**And the end is recorded in two verses that describe how far this can go:**

*And supper being ended, the devil having now put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him* (John 13:2).

*And after the sop Satan entered into him. Then said Jesus unto him, That thou doest, do quickly* (John 13:27).

**And Peter's statement to the church after the ascension is the summary:**

*Men and brethren, this scripture must needs have been fulfilled, which the Holy Ghost by the mouth of David spake before concerning Judas, which was guide to them that took Jesus. For he was numbered with us, and had obtained part of this ministry* (Acts 1:16–17).

**Had obtained part of this ministry.**

**Which means that the ministry was genuinely shared and he had a part of it, and it was taken from him, and *his bishoprick let another take* (1:20).**

**And the instruction for a congregation in this case is twofold and both parts are necessary.**

**The first is that the presence of a man with a gift and no character is not a reason to distrust the company.** The Lord continued the work of the twelve while one of them was stealing from the bag, **and He did not expose him, and He did not stop sending him.**

**And the second is that Jesus knew, and said nothing until the hour.** He gave him bread at the table and dismissed him with a sentence, **and He washed his feet.** Which means that patience with such a man is not naivety; **and that the discernment of an older leader is frequently a knowledge that is held without being announced.**

### The man who had no relationship to the name he was using

**And the fifth case is the shortest and one of the funniest and most terrible in the New Testament.**

*Then certain of the vagabond Jews, exorcists, took upon them to call over them which had evil spirits the name of the Lord Jesus, saying, We adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preacheth. And there were seven sons of one Sceva, a Jew, and chief of the priests, which did so. And the evil spirit answered and said, Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye? And the man in whom the evil spirit was leaped on them, and overcame them, and prevailed against them, so that they fled out of that house naked and wounded* (Acts 19:13–16).

**Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye?**

**Which is the answer of the powers of darkness to a man who is using a name he has no relationship with.** And note that the demon had no difficulty with the technique — the name worked when Paul said it. **It recognised the name and did not recognise the men.**

**And the lesson is not about technique at all. It is that a ministry conducted in the name of a Person may have no connection to that Person, and that the powers on the other side can tell the difference even when the congregation cannot.**

### And the men who traded the gift for money

**And three cases in Scripture are given specifically for the man whose gift becomes a business, and they are worth placing side by side.**

**Gehazi, the servant of Elisha, ran after Naaman to collect what his master had refused:**

*But Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, said, Behold, my master hath spared Naaman this Syrian, in not receiving at his hands that which he brought: but, as the Lord liveth, I will run after him, and take somewhat of him ... So he went out from his presence a leper as white as snow* (2 Kings 5:20, 27).

**And Elisha's question to him is the doctrine of the whole chapter in one sentence:**

*Is it a time to receive money, and to receive garments, and oliveyards, and vineyards, and sheep, and oxen, and menservants, and maidservants?* (2 Kings 5:26).

**And the leprosy that was lifted from Naaman was transferred to the servant who thought the deliverance was a commercial opportunity.**

**Simon Magus, seeing the gift of the Spirit conferred by the laying on of hands, offered money for the ability, and Peter's answer is the most severe in the book of Acts:**

*Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money. Thou hast neither part nor lot in this matter: for thy heart is not right in the sight of God* (Acts 8:20–21).

**Thy heart is not right in the sight of God.**

**And Balaam, as established in the previous chapter, saw the vision of the Almighty with his eyes open, and loved the wages of unrighteousness, and taught the enemy how to make the people curse themselves.**

**Three men, one method: the gift converted into income.** And the New Testament's verdict on the third of them is that a church can hold *the doctrine of Balaam*, and that the Lord has something against a congregation that permits it (Revelation 2:14).

### The anatomy of the danger

**And the mechanism by which a man can be gifted and lost can be stated as a list, and every item is drawn from a case already given.**

**The gift is not the evidence of the relationship.** *Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name?* And the answer is *I never knew you.*

**The gift can continue after the obedience has gone.** Saul prophesied in the house with a javelin in his hand, two chapters after the Spirit of the Lord departed from him.

**And a man may not know that the departure has happened.** *And he wist not that the Lord was departed from him.*

**The gift can be exercised at the same time as a settled sin is being practised.** Judas was a thief with the bag while he healed the sick and cast out devils.

**The gift can be hired.** Gehazi ran for the money; Simon offered it; Balaam waited for it.

**The ministry can be pursued for a name rather than for God.** *Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for the house of the kingdom by the might of my power, and for the honour of my majesty?* (Daniel 4:30) — and the answer to that sentence was a period of madness in which the king ate grass (4:33).

**And the man can be measured by his results rather than by his fruit.** *Many wonderful works* is a summary of results; *the fruit of the Spirit* is a description of a life (Galatians 5:22–23). **And only one of the two appears in the lists of requirements for office.**

**And the last item, and the one most applicable to a young man with a gift that works: the gifts given for the good of the congregation can be independent of the state of the man who operates them.** Which is why the church cannot use effectiveness as its test of a man and is commanded to use blamelessness.

### The remedy, and it is character

**And the remedy is not a technique and it is not a single prayer. It is the cultivation of the man, and Scripture gives it in five parts.**

**First: the body must be disciplined, by the man himself.**

*But I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection: lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway* (1 Corinthians 9:27).

**Which is written by an apostle, about himself, in the middle of an argument about his own rights.** And the word translated *keep under* is an athletic term meaning to strike a blow under the eye, **and the fear expressed is not of losing salvation but of being *adokimos*, disapproved, disqualified from the prize.**

**Second: the spirit must be ruled, because the man who does not rule his own spirit is a defensible position that has lost its walls.**

*He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls* (Proverbs 25:28).

**Which means that a man with an unguarded temper, an unguarded appetite, or an unguarded tongue has no defences at all, whatever his gift, and the enemy does not have to besiege him; he can walk in.**

**Third: the fruit must be produced, and the flesh must be crucified, and both are the work of the Spirit in a man who walks in Him.**

*But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance: against such there is no law ... And they that are Christ's have crucified the flesh with the affections and lusts. If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit* (Galatians 5:22–25).

**And the list is a list of dispositions, and every one of them is demonstrated by the way a man treats people whom he does not need.**

**Fourth: the man must purge himself, because the same house contains vessels of honour and vessels of dishonour, and the difference is not the material but the use.**

*But in a great house there are not only vessels of gold and of silver, but also of wood and of earth; and some to honour, and some to dishonour. If a man therefore purge himself from these, he shall be a vessel unto honour, sanctified, and meet for the master's use, and prepared unto every good work* (2 Timothy 2:21).

**Which is a conditional promise addressed to a young man: the vessel decides.**

**And fifth: the man must be known, because the verdict on those who were sent away was *I never knew you.***

**Which means the whole of this chapter turns on a relationship and not on a performance.** To be known by God is the end for which a man was given a gift in the first place, **and the seal of it is stated in the same letter that contains the warning:**

*Nevertheless the foundation of God standeth sure, having this seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his. And, Let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity* (2 Timothy 2:19).

**The Lord knoweth them that are his. And let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity.**

**Two clauses, and the seal covers them both. And the second clause is the ordinary evidence of the first.**

### And the test that the fire will apply

**And the closing word belongs to Paul, because he describes the judgement of the works of a believer and the description settles the question of what will be examined.**

*Now if any man build upon this foundation gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay, stubble; every man's work shall be made manifest: for the day shall declare it, because it shall be revealed by fire; and the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is. If any man's work abide which he hath built thereupon, he shall receive a reward. If any man's work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire* (1 Corinthians 3:12–15).

**Every man's work shall be made manifest.**

**And the test is *of what sort it is* — not how much of it there was, and not how visible it was, and not how many people were present when it happened.**

**Which means that the fire will not test the gift. It will test the work, and the work is what the man built with the gift, out of what he was.**

**Gold and silver and precious stones are things that survive the fire. Wood, hay and stubble are things that produce a great deal of light and heat and are gone in the morning.**

**And a man may have a ministry that was wood and hay his whole life, and be saved so as by fire, and lose the thing he gave his years to.**

**Which is the most sobering possibility in this book, and it is the reason that the next chapter is about the difference between wisdom and obedience — because a man can be gifted, wise, fruitful, famous and finished, and find on the last day that the fire consumed all of it and that what survived was the little he did out of love.**

### Principles

**First: men whose gifts operated genuinely will hear the sentence *I never knew you*.** *Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works?* — and the verdict was that He never knew them.

**Second: the only formal qualification documents in the New Testament are character documents, and only one item in each concerns ability.** *A bishop then must be blameless ... apt to teach*; and *if any be blameless ... holding fast the faithful word.*

**And third: the test of a man's household is God's own laboratory.** *For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?*

**Fourth: the gifts can outlive the obedience.** *But the Spirit of the Lord departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the Lord troubled him* — and in the next chapters Saul prophesied in his own house with a javelin in his hand.

**And fifth: a man may be operating in the gifts and be entirely unaware that the power has gone.** *And he wist not that the Lord was departed from him.*

**Sixth: a settled sin can coexist with a public ministry for years.** *He was a thief, and had the bag* — and he was one of the twelve who were given power and authority over all devils.

**Seventh: an accurate prophecy can be delivered by a man whose heart is not right with God.** *Thou hast neither part nor lot in this matter: for thy heart is not right in the sight of God.*

**And eighth: a gift used for gain produces a leprosy that cleaves to a man and to his seed.** *The leprosy therefore of Naaman shall cleave unto thee, and unto thy seed for ever.*

**Ninth: the powers of darkness recognise relationship and not technique.** *Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye?*

**And tenth: the remedies are the discipline of the body, the rule of the spirit, the fruit of the Spirit, the purging of the vessel, and being known by God.** *I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection, lest ... I myself should be a castaway*; *he that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down*; *the fruit of the Spirit is love*; *if a man therefore purge himself from these, he shall be a vessel unto honour*; *the Lord knoweth them that are his.*

**And eleventh: the fire will try the work and not the gift, and it will ask what sort it was.** *Every man's work shall be made manifest ... and the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is.*

> I wrote unto the church: but Diotrephes, who loveth to have the preeminence among them, receiveth us not ... neither doth he himself receive the brethren, and forbiddeth them that would, and casteth them out of the church.
> — 3 John 9–10

### For the fathers

- **Test the man and not the gift, because God's own lists are character lists.** *A bishop then must be blameless ... not a novice ... moreover he must have a good report of them which are without.*
- **Look at the household before you look at the platform.** *For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?*
- **Do not ordain a man because his gift is useful to you.** Gehazi had a master, a ministry and an anointing, and he ran after the money.
- **And be prepared to know something about a young man and say nothing until the hour.** The Lord kept Judas in the company, washed his feet, and gave him bread at the table, and the work went on.
- **And remember that a man's gift can continue after his obedience has failed, and that a congregation cannot tell.** Saul prophesied in his house with the javelin in his hand, and the men around him did not run out of the room.

### For the young

- **Ask what your gift is worth without your character.** *And though I have the gift of prophecy ... and have not charity, I am nothing.* Nothing. Not diminished; nothing.
- **Rule your own spirit, and let no man see you lose your temper twice.** *He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls.*
- **Keep your body under subjection, and do it before you have anything to lose.** *Lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway.*
- **Never take money for the exercise of a gift, and never let a gift become your income without the plain warrant of Scripture.** *Thy money perish with thee.*
- **And do not assume that the activity of God in your ministry is the activity of God in your life.** Saul enquired of the Lord and got no answer, and he had been the anointed king for years.
- **And know that the fire will not grade your results.** *Every man's work shall be made manifest ... and the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is.*
- **And let your ambition be the thing that the men in Matthew 7 lacked:** to be known by Christ. *The Lord knoweth them that are his. And, Let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity.*

### Questions for reflection

1. If your gift were taken away tomorrow, what would remain of your ministry — and what would remain of you?
2. Which of the fifteen character requirements of 1 Timothy 3 is the weakest in your own life, and who knows it?
3. What is in your hand while you prophesy — a javelin, a bag of money, or a towel?
4. Whose household must you examine before you examine a man's theology?
5. When the fire tries your work, of what sort will it be found — gold and silver and precious stones, or wood and hay and stubble?
