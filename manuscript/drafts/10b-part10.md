## Creating Houses of Transmission

**And if fatherhood is a relation, it needs somewhere to happen, because the thing that the previous chapter described cannot be conducted in a corridor.**

**And Scripture never leaves the transfer of the faith to goodwill. It appoints four structures, and it gives each of them an ordinance, a rhythm and a method. And the four are the household, the school, the assembly, and the institution.**

**And the reason the structures must be built deliberately is that each of them decays, and it decays in the same way, and the decay is not announced.**

**And the sentence that describes the speed of it is one of the most disturbing verses in the Old Testament, and it is about a nation that had just received everything:**

*And also all that generation were gathered unto their fathers: and there arose another generation after them, which knew not the Lord, nor yet the works which he had done for Israel* (Judges 2:10).

**The generation that had seen the Jordan part and the walls of Jericho fall was gathered to its fathers, and the generation after it knew nothing.**

**And there is no account of a war that destroyed the records, and no account of a persecution of the faithful, and no verse that names the man who failed.** The verse is simply a statement about what happens to a house that is not built to transmit, **and it happened inside the lifetime of the men who had crossed the river on dry ground.**

### The household, and the ordinance of the ordinary day

**And the first house of transmission in Scripture is the household, and its constitution is in the fourth chapter of Deuteronomy, and it is given before the nation reaches the land and therefore before there is a temple, a priesthood in residence, or a school.**

*Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God is one Lord: and thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: and thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up* (Deuteronomy 6:4–7).

**Take the order of the instructions, because the order is the method.**

**The words are to be *in thine heart* first.** Which means the transmission begins with the state of the transmitter and not with the material. **A man cannot teach diligently what he has not received, and the verb for teaching — *shinantam*, to sharpen, to repeat incisively — assumes that the man has something sharp.**

**And then the four occasions: sitting in the house, walking by the way, lying down, rising up.**

**Which is a description of a whole day and not of a meeting.** Not a class, not a service, not a curriculum with a timetable. **The four occasions are the four postures of an ordinary life: at rest indoors, in motion outdoors, at the end of the day, and at its beginning.**

**Which means the household is not a venue for teaching. It is the schedule in which teaching happens, because it constitutes the whole of a person's time.**

**And the verses that follow give the visible form of it:**

*And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes. And thou shalt write them upon the posts of thy house, and on thy gates* (Deuteronomy 6:8–9).

**On the hand, between the eyes, on the doorposts, on the gates.**

**Which means the word of God is to be attached to the four things a man uses every day — his hands, his eyes, his house, his comings and goings — and the whole design is that he should be unable to get through a day without meeting it.**

**And the second giving of the same instruction, in the eleventh chapter, explains why the arrangement is so domestic:**

*Therefore shall ye lay up these my words in your heart and in your soul ... that your days may be multiplied, and the days of your children, in the land which the Lord sware unto your fathers to give them, as the days of heaven upon the earth* (Deuteronomy 11:18, 21).

**The days of your children.**

**Which means the object of the household ordinance is the multiplication of days across generations, and the mechanism is the fluency of the parents in the word of God.**

**And the household has its own rite, and it is the oldest one in Scripture, and its form is a shared meal with a question attached:**

*Speak ye unto all the congregation of Israel, saying, In the tenth day of this month they shall take to them every man a lamb, according to the house of their fathers, a lamb for an house: and if the household be too little for the lamb, let him and his neighbour next unto his house take it according to the number of the souls* (Exodus 12:3–4).

**According to the house of their fathers. A lamb for a house.**

**Which means the original unit of the covenant community was not the congregation and not the individual. It was the household, and the household was large enough or joined with a neighbour to be exactly the size of a lamb.**

**And the ordinance was to be kept for ever, and it was to be explained every year:**

*And ye shall observe this thing for an ordinance to thee and to thy sons for ever ... And it shall come to pass, when your children shall say unto you, What mean ye by this service? That ye shall say, It is the sacrifice of the Lord's passover, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt, when he smote the Egyptians, and delivered our houses* (Exodus 12:24, 26–27).

**And the people bowed the head and worshipped.**

**Which means the explanation and the worship are recorded as the same act. The father answers the child, and the household bows.**

**And the wisdom literature of Israel is largely a household document, and its form is a father speaking.** *Hear, ye children, the instruction of a father, and attend to know understanding. For I give you good doctrine, forsake ye not my law. For I was my father's son, tender and only beloved in the sight of my mother. He taught me also, and said unto me, Let thine heart retain my words: keep my commandments, and live* (Proverbs 4:1–4).

**Five generations are visible in that paragraph.** The speaker is a father with children, he was a son, his father taught him, and he is now repeating the transmission. **And the sentence *I was my father's son* is the whole doctrine of the household house in four words: the man knows himself to have been somebody's son, and therefore he teaches as somebody's father.**

**And the psalm that carries the entire programme is the seventy-eighth, and it gives the reason the household exists in the form of a statute:**

*Give ear, O my people, to my law ... I will open my mouth in a parable: I will utter dark sayings of old: which we have heard and known, and our fathers have told us. We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the Lord, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done. For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: that the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children* (Psalm 78:1–6).

**Read the chain, because it is four generations long and it is stated in one sentence.**

**What *we have heard and known* and what *our fathers have told us*.** Then the current generation: *we will not hide them from their children.* Then the generation to come: *even the children which should be born.* Then the fourth: *who should arise and declare them to their children.*

**Which means that the obligation of a man is not to the generation immediately below him. It is to the generation that his children will address.**

**And the purpose clause is the last three verses and it contains a warning about what the house is for:**

*That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments: and might not be as their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation; a generation that set not their heart aright, and whose spirit was not stedfast with God* (Psalm 78:7–8).

**Set their hope. Not forget. Keep.**

**Three objectives, and the third verse is the one that makes the whole psalm a house document rather than a hymn.** The generation being taught is being taught specifically in order **not** to resemble the generation that failed before it — *and might not be as their fathers.*

**Which means a household of transmission is a household that has done its own accounting, and knows which of the family's patterns are to be repeated and which are to be broken.**

### The school, and the sons of the prophets

**And the second house is the school, and Scripture's account of it is far more detailed than most readers realise, because the phrase *the sons of the prophets* occurs more than forty times and describes an organised community with buildings, meals, debts, and a chain of command.**

**The institution existed in Samuel's day, and the description of it is precise:**

*And Saul sent messengers to take David: and when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing as appointed over them, the Spirit of God was upon the messengers of Saul, and they also prophesied* (1 Samuel 19:20).

**Samuel standing as appointed over them.**

**Which means there was a recognised head, a gathered body, and a place — the company at Naioth in Ramah — and a man with the authority of appointment over it.**

**And the practice of the community included instruments and prophecy as a corporate discipline, which is a combination that many centuries of later church life have separated:**

*After that thou shalt come to the hill of God, where is the garrison of the Philistines: and it shall come to pass, when thou art come thither to the city, that thou shalt meet a company of prophets coming down from the high place with a psaltery, and a tabret, and a pipe, and a harp, before them; and they shall prophesy* (1 Samuel 10:5).

**And in the reigns of Elijah and Elisha the schools were established at Bethel, at Jericho, and at Gilgal, with the younger men described by the same family title:**

*And the sons of the prophets that were at Bethel came forth to Elisha, and said unto him, Knowest thou that the Lord will take away thy master from thy head to day?* (2 Kings 2:3).

**And the community had premises that had become inadequate, and it conducted its own building programme:**

*And the sons of the prophets said unto Elisha, Behold now, the place where we dwell with thee is too strait for us. Let us go, we pray thee, unto Jordan, and take thence every man a beam, and let us make us a place there, where we may dwell* (2 Kings 6:1–2).

**And the community ate together, and its food was a matter of concern, and the older prophet was involved in the crisis of a bad pot:**

*And Elisha came again to Gilgal: and there was a dearth in the land; and the sons of the prophets were sitting before him: and he said unto his servant, Set on the great pot, and seethe pottage for the sons of the prophets ... So they poured out for the men to eat. And it came to pass, as they were eating of the pottage, that they cried out, and said, O thou man of God, there is death in the pot.* (2 Kings 4:38, 40).

**Sitting before him.** Which is the posture of a school, and the phrase *sitting before him* is the same phrase used in the New Testament of Mary of Bethany at the feet of Jesus (Luke 10:39).

**And when a hundred men had to be fed, the miracle was administered in the form of a canteen and the objection came from the server:**

*And his servitor said, What, should I set this before an hundred men? He said again, Give the people, that they may eat: for thus saith the Lord, They shall eat, and shall leave thereof* (2 Kings 4:43).

**Which means the school's size was a hundred.**

**And the output of the school was men sent on assignments of national consequence, and the record of one of them is a complete set of mission instructions:**

*And Elisha the prophet called one of the children of the prophets, and said unto him, Gird up thy loins, and take this box of oil in thine hand, and go to Ramoth-gilead ... Then take the box of oil, and pour it on his head, and say, Thus saith the Lord, I have anointed thee king over Israel. Then open the door, and flee, and tarry not* (2 Kings 9:1–3).

**Which means a young man from the school anointed a king of Israel and was told to run.**

**And the most important thing about the schools of the prophets is that the doctrine they carried did not die with the teachers.** The community at Bethel was standing when Elijah went up, and the sons of the prophets were watching from Jericho (2 Kings 2:15), **and four generations later the tradition was still in place in the shape of the men who spoke to Elisha and the sons who buried him.**

**And the second form of the school is the musical and prophetic establishments founded by David, and the account of them gives the model of a teaching institution with a curriculum and graded students:**

*Moreover David and the captains of the host separated to the service of the sons of Asaph, and of Heman, and of Jeduthun, who should prophesy with harps, with psalteries, and with cymbals ... So the number of them, with their brethren that were instructed in the songs of the Lord, even all that were cunning, was two hundred fourscore and eight. And they cast lots, ward against ward, as well the small as the great, the teacher as the scholar* (1 Chronicles 25:1, 7–8).

**Two hundred and eighty-eight men, instructed in the songs of the Lord, all of them skilled.**

**And then the sentence that should be read by every director of a training programme: *as well the small as the great, the teacher as the scholar.***

**The teacher and the scholar cast lots together, which means they took their turns by the same process and shared the same roster.**

**Which means the school was not a two-tier system in which the trained taught the untrained. It was a community in which the teachers were still on the same list as the learners, and both were subject to the same order of service.**

**And the record of the function is that these men *prophesied, according to the order of the king* (25:2) — which means the institution had a governing authority, a system of rotas, a body of material, a standard of skill, and an outward accountability.**

**Which is a description of everything that makes an institution durable and everything that a generation of enthusiasts finds tedious.**

### The assembly, and what the gathered house is for

**And the third house is the assembly, and it is the one that the New Testament describes in the most organic terms and the most structural terms at the same time.**

**Its practices are four, and the earliest description of the church after Pentecost lists them:**

*And they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers* (Acts 2:42).

**Doctrine, fellowship, the breaking of bread, and prayers.**

**Which means the assembly's work is constituted by teaching, relationship, a shared meal, and corporate prayer, and a church that has lost any one of the four is running an institution with a gap in it.**

**And the house has a definition given by Paul to Timothy, and it is the most concentrated sentence in the New Testament about what a church is:**

*But if I tarry long, that thou mayest know how thou oughtest to behave thyself in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth* (1 Timothy 3:15).

**The house of God. The church of the living God. The pillar and ground of the truth.**

**Which means the assembly exists to hold up something.** A pillar holds a building and a *hedraiōma*, a ground or stay, secures a foundation. **And the truth is what the assembly is holding, and the assembly that stops holding it has no other reason to exist.**

**And the assembly's identity is described in the Ephesians passage that joins the household to the building in a single image:**

*Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God; and are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone; in whom all the building fitly framed together groweth unto an holy temple in the Lord* (Ephesians 2:19–21).

**Household and building, family and structure, fitly framed together and growing.**

**Which means the assembly is both a family and an edifice, and it must be built as both, and a house that is only a building has no children and a house that is only a family has no walls.**

**And the letter to the Hebrews supplies the doctrine of the house and the distinction that makes it a house of transmission rather than a monument:**

*Who was faithful to him that appointed him, as also Moses was faithful in all his house. For this man was counted worthy of more glory than Moses, inasmuch as he who hath builded the house hath more honour than the house ... But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end* (Hebrews 3:2–3, 6).

**Whose house are we, if we hold fast.**

**Which means the house is not a place. It is a people, identified as the house by their holding fast, and the holding fast is a present and continuous act and not a past decision.**

**And the offices of the house are given as an appointment and not as a preference:**

*And God hath set some in the church, first apostles, secondarily prophets, thirdly teachers, after that miracles, then gifts of healings, helps, governments, diversities of tongues* (1 Corinthians 12:28).

**God hath set.** And the list begins with four teaching and governing functions before it reaches the spectacular ones, **which means the assembly's machinery is apostolic, prophetic, didactic, and administrative before it is miraculous.**

**And the ministry of the assembly is described by Paul in two phrases that answer the question of where the work happens:**

*And how I kept back nothing that was profitable unto you, but have shewed you, and have taught you publickly, and from house to house* (Acts 20:20).

*For I have not shunned to declare unto you all the counsel of God* (Acts 20:27).

**Publicly, and from house to house.**

**Which means the assembly and the household are not competitors, and that the ministry Paul is describing to the Ephesian elders was carried on in both, and that the reason he could say he had declared *all the counsel of God* is that he had used both.**

**And the purpose of the offices is stated in the Ephesians list, and it is not the filling of a pulpit:**

*And he gave some, apostles; and some, prophets; and some, evangelists; and some, pastors and teachers; for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ: till we all come in the unity of the faith ... that we henceforth be no more children, tossed to and fro, and carried about with every wind of doctrine* (Ephesians 4:11–14).

**For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry.**

**Which means the offices exist to make the congregation into workers, and the measure of an assembly is not the quality of its preaching but the number of its members who are doing the work.**

**And the last element of the assembly's constitution is the ordinance of the table, and the Lord's own institution of it is addressed to a memory that must cross a gap:**

*And he took bread, and gave thanks, and brake it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me* (Luke 22:19).

*For as often as ye eat this bread, and drink this cup, ye do shew the Lord's death till he come* (1 Corinthians 11:26).

**Till he come.**

**Which means the table is the assembly's instrument for crossing time in both directions, backward to an event and forward to a return, and that a congregation which observes it without teaching the young what it means has turned a transmission into a ritual.**

### The institution, and the ordinance of the elders

**And the fourth house is the institution, and it is the least romantic and the most necessary, and it is the one that the New Testament establishes in the first missionary journey.**

**The pattern is stated in a single verse on the return leg of that journey, and the four elements of it are worth extracting:**

*And when they had preached the gospel to that city, and had taught many, they returned again to Lystra, and to Iconium, and to Antioch, confirming the souls of the disciples, and exhorting them to continue in the faith, and that we must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God. And when they had ordained them elders in every church, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed* (Acts 14:21–23).

**Ordained them elders in every church.**

**Which means the apostolic practice was to constitute a congregation by appointing men to govern it, and that the appointment was made quickly, deliberately, and in every church and not in the important ones.**

**And the work of appointing was left to delegates with an explicit instruction and a stated objective:**

*For this cause left I thee in Crete, that thou shouldest set in order the things that are wanting, and ordain elders in every city, as I had appointed thee* (Titus 1:5).

**Set in order the things that are wanting.**

**Which means the assumption is that every young church will be incomplete, and that the identification and naming of deficiencies is itself the apostolic task, and that the apostolic delegate is judged by whether he left the church with an ordered structure.**

**And the institution's function is to hold the deposit, and its officers are the men whose mouths are the place where the knowledge of God is sought:**

*For the priest's lips should keep knowledge, and they should seek the law at his mouth: for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts* (Malachi 2:7).

**The priest's lips should keep knowledge.**

**Which is a custodial responsibility.** The lips keep the knowledge the way a treasury keeps coin, and a house whose officers cannot answer questions about the law of God has lost the custody.

**And the institution exists so that the deposit survives a gap, and the mechanism for that is stated in the last chapter of the four-generation chain:**

*And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also* (2 Timothy 2:2).

**Which is a written instruction to a young leader, from an older one who expected to die within months, telling him to deposit the material in men who would deposit it in others.** And the instruction is given to a man who was himself a son, and who was told to produce men *able to teach others also*, **which is the definition of an institution: a structure that continues to transmit when the men who founded it are dead.**

**And the instance of the school of Tyrannus gives the model of an institution founded by an apostle in a rented room and run for two years, whose output reached a whole province:**

*But when divers were hardened, and believed not, but spake evil of that way before the multitude, he departed from them, and separated the disciples, disputing daily in the school of one Tyrannus. And this continued by the space of two years; so that all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks* (Acts 19:9–10).

**Separated the disciples. Disputing daily. Two years. And all they which dwelt in Asia heard.**

**Which means the largest expansion of the gospel recorded in the book of Acts after Pentecost was the output of a daily teaching institution that lasted two years**, and that the programmatic element and the spiritual element were the same operation.

### The four things that decay, and when

**And the reason the houses must be maintained is that each of them loses four things, and the loss is in a fixed order and the first of them is invisible.**

**The first to go is the memory of the works of God.** *And there arose another generation after them, which knew not the Lord, nor yet the works which he had done for Israel.* Which means the events had been experienced by the older generation and had not been transferred, **and the transfer is a deliberate act because experience does not transmit itself.**

**And the second is the reason for the practices.** The stones at Gilgal stood for centuries; the answer to *what mean these stones?* required a man who knew. **And when the answer is lost the object remains, and the object is then available to be served.**

**And the third is the relation between the generations.** *And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children.* Which is a statement that the hearts require turning, **meaning that the natural drift is away, and that a house in which the generations are on good terms without effort is a house that has not yet been tested.**

**And the fourth is the standard.** *They feared the Lord, and served their own gods* was the condition of a settled compromise, and the third generation did not know there had ever been a question (2 Kings 17:41).

**And the timing is worth naming, because a house of transmission is lost in the second generation and discovered in the third.**

**The first generation has the events, the emotions, the costs and the reasons, and it can answer any question because it was there.**

**The second generation has the practices and the answers it was taught, and it maintains them faithfully, and it notices nothing, because the maintenance of a practice is indistinguishable from the possession of a reason.**

**And the third generation has the practices and no reason at all, and it either abandons them or divinises them, depending on its temperament.**

**Which means that the healthiest-looking period of a house's life is frequently the second generation, and that it is the period in which the loss is happening.**

### And the four things that must be kept

**And the construction of a house of transmission therefore consists of four holdings, and Scripture gives the maintenance for each of them.**

**The first is a kept text.** A written, read, memorised, memorised-out-loud, publicly-read body of material that the whole house can hear and repeat. **Which is what Deuteronomy 31 ordains in a seven-year cycle, and what Nehemiah 8 describes in a half-day reading with interpretation, and what 1 Timothy 4:13 requires of a young leader — *give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine.***

**And the practical form of it is that the house must own the text and hear it as a text, and not only as a subject of sermons.** A congregation that has heard a thousand sermons and never heard an epistle read through in one sitting has a kept subject and an unkept text.

**And the second holding is a kept table.** *And thou shalt teach them diligently ... when thou sittest in thine house.* And the assembly's *breaking of bread*, and the exchange of the household at Passover. **Which means the transmission takes place where people eat, and that a house with no shared meals has no venue.**

**And the third holding is a kept task.** The sons of the prophets cut beams and ate pottage and were sent to anoint kings. **Timothy was sent into Macedonia and left behind at Ephesus and told to *do the work of an evangelist* (2 Timothy 4:5).**

**Which means that a man is not formed by instruction but by an assignment, and that the assignments must be real and must be capable of being failed.**

**And the fourth holding is a kept record.** A written account of what God has done, a register of what has been taught, a list of the names, and the specification handed on. **Which is what Deuteronomy is; what the *book of the chronicles* of the kings is; what the genealogies that the returned exiles were required to prove are (Ezra 2:62); and what Paul meant by *the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses.***

**And a house that keeps no record is a house that has made its third generation dependent on somebody's memory.**

### And the practical construction

**And the instruction of this chapter can be reduced to a list of acts that a house of God can begin in a single week, and none of them requires money.**

**Write down what God has done in your house, and read it aloud to the children once a year.** The four occasions of Deuteronomy 6 will supply the opportunities; the record supplies the content.

**Teach the answers before the questions arrive.** *And it shall come to pass, when your children shall say unto you, What mean ye by this service?* requires that the father be prepared. **Which is the reason for the catechism, the memorised psalm, and the family reading — not to eliminate questions but to make the answers available.**

**Give every young man an assignment with a date and a standard.** The school of the prophets did not produce men by lectures; it sent them. **And a young man who has never been given something he could fail at has not been trained, however many courses he has attended.**

**Name the officers and write down what they are responsible for.** *Ordained them elders in every church* implies a list. **A house without a written distribution of responsibility will centralise every decision in one man, and will be destroyed the day he is unavailable.**

**Keep the assemblies and the meals, and keep them both.** *Publickly, and from house to house.* And teach the children at the table what the bread means.

**And appoint the thing that outlives you.** A successor, a written specification, a body of men *able to teach others also*, and a building if you need one.

**And the last word of the chapter is the Hebrews sentence, because it states the condition on which the whole arrangement stands:**

*But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end* (Hebrews 3:6).

**Whose house are we, if we hold fast.**

**Which means that the houses in this chapter are the ordinary means and not the security, and that the founder of the house is a Son, and that the holding fast is the act of a body of people and not the property of a structure.**

**And the sentence that settles the matter is the one that was quoted at the beginning of the Part.** The house is not built by its architecture and it is not maintained by its habits. **It is built of people who know God, and the reason a house fails is always and only that the people in it stopped knowing Him.**

### Principles

**First: Scripture appoints four houses for the transmission of the faith — the household, the school, the assembly and the institution — and each of them has an ordinance, a rhythm and a method.** *And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children*; *Samuel standing as appointed over them*; *they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship*; *ordained them elders in every church.*

**Second: the household's method is the whole of an ordinary day and not a meeting.** *When thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.*

**And third: the household's rite is a meal with an explanation attached, and the explanation is given to a child's question.** *And it shall come to pass, when your children shall say unto you, What mean ye by this service? That ye shall say, It is the sacrifice of the Lord's passover.*

**Fourth: the obligation of a household is not to the next generation but to the one after it.** *That the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children.*

**And fifth: a household of transmission knows which of the family's patterns must not be repeated.** *And might not be as their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation.*

**Sixth: the schools of the prophets were organised communities with premises, meals, debts, a chain of command and a mission output.** *The place where we dwell with thee is too strait for us*; *the sons of the prophets were sitting before him*; *set on the great pot*; *take this box of oil in thine hand, and go.*

**And seventh: the teacher and the scholar were on the same roster.** *They cast lots, ward against ward, as well the small as the great, the teacher as the scholar.*

**Eighth: the assembly is defined as the pillar and ground of the truth, and it exists to hold something up.** *That thou mayest know how thou oughtest to behave thyself in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth.*

**And ninth: the offices of the assembly exist to make the congregation into workers, and the measure of a church is the number of its members doing the work.** *For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ.*

**Tenth: the assembly's work is carried on publicly and from house to house, and the two are not in competition.** *I kept back nothing that was profitable unto you, but have shewed you, and have taught you publickly, and from house to house.*

**And eleventh: the institution exists so that the deposit survives the death of the men who founded it.** *The things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.*

**And twelfth: the four things a house loses are its memory, its reasons, its relations and its standard, and the loss happens in the second generation and is discovered in the third.** *And there arose another generation after them, which knew not the Lord, nor yet the works which he had done for Israel.*

**And thirteenth: the house is held by holding fast, and the holding fast is a continuous act.** *Whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end.*

### For the fathers

- **Write down what God has done in your house, and read it to the children once a year.** Experience does not transmit itself, and the record is the instrument that carries it across.
- **Keep the four occasions of Deuteronomy 6, and use them.** Sitting, walking, lying down, rising up. A house that only teaches at a meeting has four fifths of its time unaccounted for.
- **Teach the answer before the question arrives.** *What mean ye by this service?* is asked once, by a child, and the father who is surprised by it will give a vague answer and lose the moment.
- **Teach your household which of the family's patterns must not be repeated.** *And might not be as their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation.* That sentence requires a decision and a story.
- **Give every young man an assignment he can fail at, with a date.** The schools of the prophets sent men out; the school of Tyrannus argued daily for two years. You need both.
- **Name your officers and write down what they own.** *Ordained them elders in every church* implies a list, and a house with no written division of responsibility is a house with one point of failure.
- **Keep the table.** The household Passover, the breaking of bread, and the meals at which *the sons of the prophets were sitting before him* are the same ordinance in three forms.
- **And keep the assembly and the house-to-house ministry together, because the counsel of God cannot be declared by one of them alone.** *Publickly, and from house to house.*

### For the young

- **Learn which house you are in, and let it be a real one.** A group chat is not a school and a podcast is not a father; the sons of the prophets had a pot, a place to dwell, and a man sitting in front of them.
- **Do not resent being on the same roster as your teachers.** *They cast lots, ward against ward, as well the small as the great, the teacher as the scholar.* The list you share with them is your credential.
- **Take the assignment and go.** *Take this box of oil in thine hand, and go to Ramoth-gilead.* A young man who has never been given a mission with consequences has not begun to be trained.
- **Learn the text as a text, and not only through sermons.** *Give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine.* Read the epistle whole before you preach from one verse of it.
- **Ask your questions at the table, and expect an answer.** *And it shall come to pass, when your children shall say unto you, What mean ye by this service?* Your question is written into the ordinance, and it is not an interruption.
- **And be one of the faithful men who will teach others.** *The same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.* The chain does not stop at you, and your usefulness is measured by whether it continues.

### Questions for reflection

1. Which of the four houses — household, school, assembly, institution — is weakest in your life, and what would it cost to repair it this year?
2. What have you written down about what God has done in your house, and who has read it?
3. When did a child last ask you what a practice of your house means, and what did you say?
4. Which man have you sent out on an assignment with a date, a standard, and the possibility of failure?
5. If your generation were gathered to its fathers tomorrow, what reason would be left for the practices of your house?
