## The Builders After the Exile

**And now the case in Scripture in which a generation inherits a ruin rather than a promise, and has to build on ground its fathers had seen in glory.**

**The people had come home.** The decree of Cyrus had released them, the altar had been rebuilt, the foundation of the temple had been laid, **and then the work had stopped, and the years had passed, and a new generation had grown up in a city whose wall was broken down and whose sanctuary was a building site.**

**And the books that record what they did — Ezra, Nehemiah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi — are the most detailed account in Scripture of a generational building project, and the details are unsparing.**

**They were told the time was not right. They built their own houses first. They wept when they compared their work with their fathers' memory. They were stopped by their neighbours, defeated by their own rubble, and discouraged by their enemies. And a century later, with the house standing and the wall finished, the children of the builders could not speak the language of the covenant.**

**And God's promise to them in the middle of it is the sentence this chapter is built on: *The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former* (Haggai 2:9).**

### "The time is not come"

**And the book that opens the account begins with a diagnosis of the excuse, and the excuse is a liturgical one.**

*Thus speaketh the Lord of hosts, saying, This people say, The time is not come, the time that the Lord's house should be built. Then came the word of the Lord by Haggai the prophet, saying, Is it time for you, O ye, to dwell in your cieled houses, and this house lie waste? Now therefore thus saith the Lord of hosts; Consider your ways. Ye have sown much, and bring in little; ye eat, but ye have not enough; ye drink, but ye are not filled with drink; ye clothe you, but there is none warm; and he that earneth wages earneth wages to put it into a bag with holes* (Haggai 1:2–6).

**Three things in that passage.**

**The first is the form of the excuse: *the time is not come.***

**Note that the people were not refusing to build. They were postponing it, and the postponement had a theological shape.** It is always possible to argue that the moment is not right — that the political situation is unsettled, that the resources are insufficient, that the season for such work is in the future. **And the argument is unfalsifiable, which is why it is so useful to a generation that would rather not.**

**The second is the comparison God draws: *is it time for you, O ye, to dwell in your cieled houses, and this house lie waste?***

**The Hebrew for *cieled* is *sephunim*, panelled, roofed in with cedar.** The returning exiles had built and finished their own houses — not tents, not shelters, but panelled houses with ceilings. **And the house of God was a foundation and a pile of timber.**

**Which means the postponement was not caused by poverty. It was caused by a budget, and the budget had been set by what the people considered urgent.**

**And the third element is the economics, and this is the part that every generation should read carefully: *ye have sown much, and bring in little ... and he that earneth wages earneth wages to put it into a bag with holes.***

**The judgement is not a famine and not a plague. It is an inexplicable inefficiency.** They sowed and the harvest was poor; they ate and were not satisfied; they drank and were not filled; they clothed themselves and were not warm; they earned and the money disappeared. **And the reason given is that the house of God lay in ruins while their own houses were completed.**

**Which is the ordinary condition of a church that has postponed its own work for two decades.** The congregation is not destitute. Its members have houses, cars, savings and businesses. **And the work of the house is a foundation with a pile of wood on it, and everyone has an explanation for why the moment is not yet.**

**And God's remedy is stated in four imperatives, and the third is the one that matters:** *Go up to the mountain, and bring wood, and build the house; and I will take pleasure in it, and I will be glorified.*

**He did not say *pray for the house*, or *plan for the house*, or *raise funds for the house*.** He said go to the mountain, get the timber, and build. **The remedy for a postponed work is the beginning of physical labour on it.**

### The twenty-three days that reversed it

**And the response to the prophecy is one of the swiftest and most complete changes of direction in the Old Testament, and the timescale is given with a precision that must be intended.**

**Haggai's first message was delivered on the first day of the sixth month, in the second year of Darius.** And the record of the obedience is dated:

*Then Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, with all the remnant of the people, obeyed the voice of the Lord their God, and the words of Haggai the prophet, as the Lord their God had sent him, and the people did fear before the Lord. Then spake Haggai the Lord's messenger in the Lord's message unto the people, saying, I am with you, saith the Lord. And the Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and the spirit of Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, and the spirit of all the remnant of the people; and they came and did work in the house of the Lord of hosts, their God, in the four and twentieth day of the sixth month, in the second year of Darius the king* (Haggai 1:12–15).

**Twenty-three days between the prophecy and the resumption of work.**

**And note who obeyed: the governor, the high priest, and *all the remnant of the people*.**

**The three estates of the nation, in a single verse, and the obedience was simultaneous.** Zerubbabel was the civil authority of the Persian province; Joshua was the religious authority; **and the people — the same people who had said that the time was not come — obeyed.**

**And note what God said on the day they started: *I am with you.***

**Not before. Not to the men who were considering the matter, and not to the men who had the excuse ready.** The declaration of His presence was given on the day the work began.

**And the stirring of the spirit is attributed to God and described in the same sentence as the obedience.** *And the Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel ... and the spirit of Joshua ... and the spirit of all the remnant of the people; and they came and did work.*

**Which means that in the same event God stirred the spirit and the men obeyed.** The two statements are not in tension; they describe the same act from two sides. **And the practical implication for a generation that has been waiting for a stirring before it starts building is that the stirring in Haggai's account is recorded in the same verse as the arrival on the site.**

### "Who hath despised the day of small things?"

**And the next problem was not external. It was the effect of the older generation's memory on the younger one's work, and God addressed it directly.**

*Who is left among you that saw this house in her first glory? and how do ye see it now? is it not in your eyes in comparison of it as nothing? Yet now be strong, O Zerubbabel, saith the Lord; and be strong, O Joshua, son of Josedech, the high priest; and be strong, all ye people of the land, saith the Lord, and work: for I am with you, saith the Lord of hosts: according to the word that I covenanted with you when ye came out of Egypt, so my spirit remaineth among you: fear ye not* (Haggai 2:3–5).

**The question is addressed to the old men, and it is not a rebuke.**

**Some of them had seen Solomon's temple before the destruction. And the second temple, as a building, was nothing in comparison** — we know from Ezra and from the later history that it lacked the ark, the shekinah, the urim and thummim, and the fire from heaven. **And the old men's assessment was factually correct.**

**And God does not tell them they are wrong. He tells them to be strong, and He tells them to work.**

**The pattern of the verse is: acknowledge the comparison, command strength three times to three parties, command work, and add the promise *I am with you*, and the closing *fear ye not*.**

**Which is the model for how an older generation should speak to a younger one about a building that is smaller than the one they remember.**

**They are not required to pretend that the first house was not greater.** The comparison is not forbidden, and the grief is not unbelief. **And the old men were commanded to be strong and to work on the smaller house, which is the act that makes the comparison harmless.**

**And then the promise, which is the sentence that every discouraged builder in a generation of small things needs:**

*The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former, saith the Lord of hosts: and in this place will I give peace* (Haggai 2:9).

**Greater glory in a lesser building.**

**And the mechanism is given by Zechariah, in the words that were spoken to the governor about the mountain in front of him:**

*This is the word of the Lord unto Zerubbabel, saying, Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the Lord of hosts. Who art thou, O great mountain? before Zerubbabel thou shalt become a plain: and he shall bring forth the headstone thereof with shoutings, crying, Grace, grace unto it ... The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house; his hands shall also finish it; and thou shalt know that the Lord of hosts hath sent me unto you. For who hath despised the day of small things?* (Zechariah 4:6–10).

**Four elements in that passage, and all four are for a builder.**

**The mountain becomes a plain, and it becomes a plain by the Spirit and not by might or power.** The obstacle in front of the work is not addressed by the size of the workforce.

**And the headstone is brought out with shoutings of *grace, grace unto it*.** Which means the completion is attributed to grace by the men who are completing it, **and the shout is the sound of a work finishing.**

**And the specific promise: *the hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house; his hands shall also finish it.***

**Which means the man who began would be the man who ended — a promise given to a governor who had laid the foundation sixteen years earlier and had watched the work stop.** The promise is that the interruption would not be permanent, and that the same hands would complete what they had started.

**And the last question is the one that stands over every generation that inherits a diminished work.** *For who hath despised the day of small things?*

**The Hebrew is *yom qetannoth*, the day of smallnesses — the day of little beginnings.** And the question is asked by God, and it is a warning and not a compliment. **The contempt for small beginnings is the attitude that stops the work, and it is usually found in the people who remember something larger.**

**And the remedy in the verse is a detail: *they shall rejoice, and shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel.***

**The plummet is the plumb line, the instrument of a man who is checking a course of stone.** Which means the rejoicing is attached to the ordinary technical work of a builder. **The sign that the day of small things is not despised is that somebody is holding a plumb line and somebody is watching him with joy.**

### The two sounds in the same courtyard

**And the most affecting passage in the Old Testament about a generational transition is the account of the day the foundation was laid, and it records two noises that nobody could tell apart.**

*And when the builders laid the foundation of the temple of the Lord, they set the priests in their apparel with trumpets, and the Levites the sons of Asaph with cymbals, to praise the Lord, after the ordinance of David king of Israel. And they sang together by course in praising and giving thanks unto the Lord; because he is good, for his mercy endureth for ever toward Israel. And all the people shouted with a great shout, when they praised the Lord, because the foundation of the house of the Lord was laid. But many of the priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who were ancient men, that had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy: so that the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people: for the people shouted with a loud shout, and the noise was heard afar off* (Ezra 3:10–13).

**Take the details in order.**

**The worship was organised according to *the ordinance of David king of Israel*** — the older generation's liturgical inheritance was being used for the new building. The instruments, the courses of singers, the cymbals: all of it inherited, all of it in use.

**And the whole congregation shouted when the foundation was laid.**

**And the ancient men — the priests, the Levites, and the chief fathers who had seen the first house — *wept with a loud voice.***

**And the two sounds were indistinguishable.** *The people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people.*

**Which is one of the most honest sentences in Scripture, and one of the most useful.**

**A congregation in transition makes two noises at once, and God hears both, and neither is sin.** The old men's weeping was the sound of a man comparing a foundation with a memory of glory, **and the young men's shouting was the sound of a man seeing a foundation where there had been rubble for sixteen years.**

**And note that the worship did not stop because of the weeping.** The trumpets were blown, the singers sang *his mercy endureth for ever*, and the foundation was laid **with both sounds in the air at the same time.**

**Which means a house of God does not have to resolve the generational grief before it can build.** It has to build, and let the weeping and the shouting be heard together, **and refuse to require either party to stop.**

**And the practical instruction for both parties is in the sentence itself.**

**The older men were not told to be silent, and they were not told that their grief was unbelief.** They wept aloud, in public, at the ceremony. **And the leadership did not rebuke them.**

**And the younger men were not told to moderate their shout out of respect for the weeping.** Both sounds were heard *afar off*, which means the report of the day carried beyond the congregation, **and what the neighbours heard was the sound of a building being raised.**

### The interruption, and the prophets who restarted it

**And between the foundation and the completion there were sixteen years in which nothing was done, and the book explains both the external and the internal cause.**

*Then the people of the land weakened the hands of the people of Judah, and troubled them in building, and hired counsellors against them, to frustrate their purpose, all the days of Cyrus king of Persia, even until the reign of Darius king of Persia* (Ezra 4:4–5).

**And note the earlier refusal, which was correct:**

*Let us build with you: for we seek your God, as ye do; and we do sacrifice unto him since the days of Esar-haddon king of Assur, which brought us up hither. But Zerubbabel, and Jeshua, and the rest of the chief of the fathers of Israel, said unto them, Ye have nothing to do with us to build an house unto our God; but we ourselves together will build unto the Lord God of Israel* (Ezra 4:2–3).

**The offer of partnership was refused, and the refusal was right, and the consequence was a campaign of obstruction that lasted through two reigns.**

**Which is the ordinary price of a right refusal, and it must be stated for the sake of every leader who has had to decline an offer of help that would have compromised the work.** The refusal was correct and it cost the work sixteen years, **and both statements are true, and a young leader must not be surprised by the second.**

**And the work restarted not by a change of policy and not by a new funding source, but by prophecy:**

*Then the prophets, Haggai the prophet, and Zechariah the son of Iddo, prophesied unto the Jews that were in Judah and Jerusalem in the name of the God of Israel, even unto them. Then rose up Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and Jeshua the son of Jozadak, and began to build the house of God which is at Jerusalem: and with them were the prophets of God helping them* (Ezra 5:1–2).

**Two prophets, and the two leaders, and the work resumed.**

**And the summary of the whole project names the mechanism:**

*And the elders of the Jews builded, and they prospered through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo. And they builded, and finished it* (Ezra 6:14).

**They prospered *through the prophesying*.**

**Which means the house was built by a combination of the word of God and the labour of the elders, and the two were inseparable.** The prophecy alone would have produced conviction and no building; the labour alone would have produced a building with no reason to exist. **And the people who built were the elders of the Jews — the older men — and the men who spoke were prophets, one of whom was a young man with a vision for the night (Zechariah), and one of whom was a man with a message about panelled houses.**

### The wall, and the whole city in the list

**And the second phase of the work is the wall, and the account of who built it is the longest list of names in the Old Testament outside the genealogies, and it was written to be examined.**

**Nehemiah's method is in a single sentence, and it is a method any young leader can copy:**

*Then I told them of the hand of my God which was good upon me; as also the king's words that he had spoken unto me. And they said, Let us rise up and build. So they strengthened their hands for this good work* (Nehemiah 2:18).

**He reported the evidence of God's favour, and the people responded by strengthening their own hands.**

**And the record of the building is a chapter of trades and families and districts:**

*Then Eliashib the high priest rose up with his brethren the priests, and they builded the sheep gate* (Nehemiah 3:1).

*And next unto them the Tekoites repaired; but their nobles put not their necks to the work of their Lord* (Nehemiah 3:5).

*And next unto him repaired Shallum the son of Halohesh, the ruler of the half part of Jerusalem, he and his daughters* (Nehemiah 3:12).

**Three details from one chapter, and each is a lesson.**

**The high priest built with his own hands, at the sheep gate.** The man at the head of the religious order of the nation was laying stone in the wall, **and the gate he chose was the one the flocks came through.**

**The Tekoites repaired their section, and their nobles would not.** Which means the chapter records a noble family that refused to work — **and the record is public and it has been read aloud for two and a half thousand years.** Whoever wrote that list intended the refusal to be remembered.

**And Shallum the ruler of half of Jerusalem repaired the wall *he and his daughters*.**

**A ruler and his daughters, in a list otherwise composed of sons.**

**Which means that a generation-building project in Scripture includes women at every level — from the prophetesss and the singing women of the tabernacle, to the daughters of Shallum laying stone in a wall, to the women who laboured alongside Paul in the gospel, to Priscilla expounding the way of God to Apollos.**

**And the list is a rebuke to every culture that keeps the daughters out of the work.** Shallum's daughters are in the canonical record of a wall, and they are identified by their father's household and not by their marital status, **which means the men of that generation took their daughters into the labour of the house of God.**

### The rubble problem

**And the halfway point of the wall brought a crisis that most building projects reach and few speak about.**

*So built we the wall; and all the wall was joined together unto the half thereof: for the people had a mind to work* (Nehemiah 4:6).

*And Judah said, The strength of the bearers of burdens is decayed, and there is much rubbish; so that we are not able to build the wall* (Nehemiah 4:10).

**The people had a mind to work, and the work reached half its height, and then the bearers collapsed saying there was too much rubbish.**

**The Hebrew for rubbish is *aphar*, dust, or *sh'at*, refuse — the piled debris of a city that had been destroyed a hundred and forty years earlier.** To build a wall on an old site, the ruined stone and burnt timber must be cleared before any new courses can be laid. **And it was the clearing that exhausted the workers, not the building.**

**Which is the most common reason a work stalls in the second generation.** The founders' work was building; the successors' first work is often demolition and clearance. **And clearing rubble produces nothing visible, encourages nobody, and takes three times as long as anyone expects.**

**And Nehemiah's response is instructive in three parts.**

**He organised the defence by family: *I even set the people after their families with their swords, their spears, and their bows* (4:13).** The unit of the work was the household, and the household was also the unit of the defence. **Which is a structural point: a work organised by families survives a crisis better than one organised by individuals.**

**He addressed the fear directly and religiously: *Be not ye afraid of them: remember the Lord, which is great and terrible, and fight for your brethren, your sons, and your daughters, your wives, and your houses* (4:14).**

**Note the last clause: *your houses*.** The men were told to fight for their households, **and the wall they were building was the protection of those households, which means the work and the reason for the work were the same object.**

**And he made the work continuous under arms: half the men worked and half held the weapons, and the builders carried their tools in one hand and a sword at their side, and *none of us put off our clothes, saving that every one put them off for washing* (4:23).**

**Which is the practical shape of a house of God that is building and contending at the same time.** There is no season in which the contention stops. **And a generation that waits for peace before it builds will never build.**

### And the teaching that made the building last

**And the wall was completed in fifty-two days (Nehemiah 6:15), and when it was finished the people did the thing that would determine whether the building survived, and it is the most important passage in these books for our subject.**

*And all the people gathered themselves together as one man into the street that was before the water gate; and they spake unto Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses, which the Lord had commanded to Israel. And Ezra the priest brought the law before the congregation both of men and women, and all that could hear with understanding ... And he read therein ... from the morning until midday, before the men and the women, and those that could understand; and the ears of all the people were attentive unto the book of the law ... And Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people; (for he was above all the people;) and when he opened it, all the people stood up ... So they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading* (Nehemiah 8:1–5, 8).

**Four elements of the mechanism, and every one of them is a lesson in transmission.**

**The reading was public and stood: a wooden pulpit was built for the purpose, Ezra stood above the people, and when he opened the book they all stood up.** The posture of the congregation was the posture of men receiving an authoritative word.

**The reading lasted from morning until midday.** Which means the whole assembly gave half a day to the reading, and the reading was of the law of Moses — not a sermon and not a summary.

**The congregation included *both of men and women, and all that could hear with understanding*.** The phrase defines the inclusion, and it is a criterion of capacity rather than of status: whoever could understand was present. **Which means the children who could follow were in the crowd.**

**And the reading was accompanied by interpretation: *they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading.***

**The Hebrew *mephorash* — distinctly, clearly — and *som sekhel*, giving understanding.** The Levites were stationed among the crowd, and they explained what was read (8:7–8). **Which means the pattern for the transmission of the deposit is a public reading with an explanation, addressed to men, women and children, over hours.**

**And the effect was joy, and the joy was commanded:** *for this day is holy unto our Lord: neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the Lord is your strength* (8:10).

**Which is the answer to the question of what makes a building project survive.** A wall built by men who have heard the law read for half a day, with its sense explained to them, is a wall that will be defended. **A wall built by men who have been given a contract will be abandoned at the first threat.**

### And the relapse, and the test of the children's language

**And the books do not end with the wall, because the record is honest, and what happened within a generation of Nehemiah's departure is the most precise diagnosis of a lost generation in Scripture.**

*In those days also saw I Jews that had married wives of Ashdod, of Ammon, and of Moab: and their children spake half in the speech of Ashdod, and could not speak in the Jews' language, but according to the language of each people* (Nehemiah 13:23–24).

**Their children could not speak the language.**

**Not: their children were idolaters; and not: their children had denied the God of their fathers.** The sentence is a linguistic observation, **and it is the diagnostic that every generational failure produces.**

**The children of the builders could not read the law, because the law was in a language they no longer possessed.**

**And the chapter records the cause bluntly: *but in all this time was not I at Jerusalem* (13:6).**

**Nehemiah had been away, and in his absence the compromises had been made.** Which means the work required continuing oversight and could not be secured by a completed building. **The wall and the temple were finished; the transmission was not, and the transmission is what the next generation needed.**

**And the last book of the Old Testament, written about the same generation, is full of the same complaint:**

*A son honoureth his father, and a servant his master: if then I be a father, where is mine honour? and if I be a master, where is my fear? saith the Lord of hosts unto you, O priests, that despise my name* (Malachi 1:6).

**Which brings the story the whole way back to the sentence with which this book opened.** The last word of the Old Testament is about fathers and children, **and it is spoken to a generation that had rebuilt the house and the wall and lost the language.**

### The whole century, and who finished it

**And the timeline of the return is worth setting out, because it makes plain what a generational work actually looks like.**

**The decree of Cyrus, and the first return under Zerubbabel — around 538 BC. The altar rebuilt, and the foundation of the temple laid within a couple of years, with the old men weeping and the young men shouting.**

**The work halted within a few years, and stayed halted for about sixteen years.**

**The prophecy of Haggai and Zechariah, and the resumption in the second year of Darius, around 520 BC. The temple completed in the sixth year of Darius, around 516 BC — twenty-two years after the foundation had been laid.**

**Then a gap of almost sixty years.**

**Ezra's return with the law, around 458 BC.**

**And Nehemiah's wall in fifty-two days, around 445 BC.**

**A century from the decree to the wall, and four generations of builders.**

**And the completion was not accomplished by the men who began it. Zerubbabel laid the foundation; other men finished the temple. Nehemiah built the wall; Ezra read the law; and the men who finally inhabited the city were the children and grandchildren of the builders.**

**Which is the doctrine of this chapter in a single observation: a work that God intends to last is frequently longer than the working life of the men who start it, and it is completed by a generation that inherits a foundation, a pile of rubble, a prophecy, and a book.**

### Principles

**First: the postponement of the house of God is usually an economic decision disguised as a calendar one.** *The time is not come ... Is it time for you, O ye, to dwell in your cieled houses, and this house lie waste?*

**Second: the consequence of postponement is an inexplicable poverty.** *Ye have sown much, and bring in little ... and he that earneth wages earneth wages to put it into a bag with holes.*

**Third: the remedy is physical labour and it began in twenty-three days.** *Go up to the mountain, and bring wood, and build the house.* And God's *I am with you* was spoken on the day the work started.

**Fourth: the stirring of the spirit and the obedience are recorded in the same verse.** *And the Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel ... and they came and did work.* A generation that waits for a feeling before it starts building has not read Haggai.

**Fifth: the day of small things is not to be despised, and the grief of the older generation must be allowed.** *Who is left among you that saw this house in her first glory?* The comparison is not forbidden; the leaving off of work because of it is.

**Sixth: a lesser building can have greater glory.** *The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former ... and in this place will I give peace.*

**Seventh: the mountain becomes a plain by the Spirit, and the plumb line is the sign of a live work.** *Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit ... they shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel.*

**Eighth: a congregation in transition makes two sounds and both are legitimate.** *The people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people.* Neither party was rebuked, and the foundation was laid.

**And ninth: the work requires both prophecy and labour, and it includes everybody.** *They prospered through the prophesying of Haggai ... And the elders of the Jews builded*; the high priest built with his own hands; the nobles of Tekoa refused and are remembered for it; and Shallum repaired the wall *he and his daughters.*

**And tenth: the wall was finished and the language was lost.** *Their children spake half in the speech of Ashdod, and could not speak in the Jews' language.* A completed building is not a transferred deposit.

### For the fathers

- **Do not let the calendar conceal the budget.** *The time is not come* was said by men in panelled houses. If the work is postponed while the members are comfortable, the problem is not the season.
- **Go and start on the site.** *Go up to the mountain, and bring wood, and build the house.* The presence of God was declared on the day the work began, not on the day it was planned.
- **Be strong and work on the smaller house, and do not require the young to pretend the comparison does not exist.** The old men wept and the young men shouted, and both sounds were heard afar off, and neither was rebuked.
- **Let the daughters work.** *Shallum ... he and his daughters.* And record who refused: *the nobles put not their necks to the work of their Lord.*
- **Clear the rubbish before you complain about the pace.** The bearers collapsed because of the rubble, not the wall. Make the clearing visible, budget it, and tell the people it is the work.
- **And read the book aloud, distinctly, with the sense given, until they understand.** Half a day of reading with interpretation is what makes a wall defensible.

### For the young

- **You are probably inheriting a foundation and a pile of rubble, and both are the work.** Do not despise the day of small things, and do not confuse the clearing with the building.
- **Expect the older men to weep.** Their grief over the first house is not unbelief and not a judgement on you. Work anyway, and let them be in the room.
- **Do not despise prophecy and do not neglect labour.** The house prospered *through the prophesying* and it was built by elders with tools in their hands.
- **Organise by families, and fight for your houses.** *Fight for your brethren, your sons, and your daughters, your wives, and your houses.* A work organised by households survives a crisis that destroys a work organised by individuals.
- **And know that the test of your generation is not the building.** Nehemiah's wall was finished and the children could not read the law. **Teach them the language of the covenant, or the wall will stand and the faith will not.**
- **And be prepared for the work to be longer than you are.** A century from the decree to the wall, and four generations of builders. The promise was that the latter house would be greater; it did not promise that you would see it finished.

### Questions for reflection

1. What is your house's equivalent of the panelled ceiling — the personal comfort that is being funded by a postponed work?
2. Which pile of rubble has to be cleared before anything can be built, and has anybody been told that clearing it is the work?
3. Whose weeping have you interpreted as opposition, and what would it cost to let him weep in public?
4. Which part of your inheritance is being ignored because the day of small things has been despised?
5. Can the next generation read the deposit, or only hear about it?
