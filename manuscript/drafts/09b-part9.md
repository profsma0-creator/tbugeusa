## The Idolatry of Elders

**And now the danger on the other side, and it is the more subtle of the two, because it has the appearance of fidelity and it is defended with the vocabulary of the covenant.**

**The idolatry of the young is the worship of the self. It is visible, and it is ugly, and it destroys the man who practises it. The idolatry of elders is the worship of the vessel, and it is the one form of idolatry in Scripture that can be preached from a pulpit without being noticed.**

**And the sentence that describes it is spoken by God through Jeremiah at the gate of the house that bore His name:**

*Trust ye not in lying words, saying, The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord, are these* (Jeremiah 7:4).

**Three times the phrase is repeated.** Which means it was a liturgical refrain — the chant of a congregation that had made the existence of the building into an argument for its own safety, **and God told the prophet to stand at the gate of that building and tell the people entering it that the words were lies.**

### The ark in the camp

**And the first case is the one that ought to end the discussion, because the men who committed the error were the elders of Israel, and the relic they used was the ark of the covenant itself.**

**Israel had been beaten by the Philistines, and the question they asked was correct:**

*And when the people were come into the camp, the elders of Israel said, Wherefore hath the Lord smitten us to day before the Philistines? Let us fetch the ark of the covenant of the Lord out of Shiloh unto us, that, when it cometh among us, it may save us out of the hand of our enemies* (1 Samuel 4:3).

**The elders of Israel said.**

**And their reasoning is the whole doctrine of this chapter in one clause: *that, when it cometh among us, it may save us.***

**The ark was not a tool.** It was the footstool of the presence of God, made to specification, overshadowed by the cherubim, kept in the holiest place, entered once a year with blood. **And the elders proposed to carry it into a battle as a device for securing a victory, and the record says they fetched it, and the sons of Eli carried it (4:4).**

**And the shout was enormous:**

*And when the ark of the covenant of the Lord came into the camp, all Israel shouted with a great shout, so that the earth rang again* (1 Samuel 4:5).

**And the Philistines heard it and were afraid, and said *God is come into the camp* (4:7) — which means the reputation of the ark was a real military asset.**

**And the outcome is recorded in two verses:**

*And the Philistines fought, and Israel was smitten, and they fled every man into his tent: and there was a very great slaughter; for there fell of Israel thirty thousand footmen. And the ark of God was taken; and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain* (1 Samuel 4:10–11).

**Thirty thousand men, the ark captured, the priesthood of Eli's line destroyed, and the high priest dead on the same day when he heard the news (4:18).**

**And the name given to the child born at that hour was the verdict on the whole generation:**

*And she named the child Ichabod, saying, The glory is departed from Israel: because the ark of God was taken, and because of her father in law and her husband* (1 Samuel 4:21).

**The glory is departed from Israel.**

**And here is the detail that makes this passage the foundation of the chapter.** The ark was in Philistine hands for months, and God dealt with the Philistines Himself — the image of Dagon fell before it, and the men of Ashdod and Gath were smitten with emerods (5:1–12) — **and the people who had carried it into battle as a talisman were left defeated, and the ark did not return to them until they had put away the strange gods and were gathered at Mizpeh to fast and confess (7:2–6).**

**Which means the presence of God was never attached to the object and never available as a technique.** The Philistines could not keep it and Israel could not use it, **and the nation's recovery began not with the return of the ark but with the repentance of the people and the ministry of a prophet.**

### The ephod at Ophrah

**And the second case is gentler in its origins and equally fatal in its effect, and it concerns a man whom Scripture treats as a hero.**

**After the defeat of Midian, the men of Israel offered Gideon a dynasty:**

*Rule thou over us, both thou, and thy son, and thy son's son also: for thou hast delivered us from the hand of Midian* (Judges 8:22).

**And Gideon refused in the best sentence in his life: *I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you: the Lord shall rule over you* (8:23).**

**And then he asked for the gold of the spoil, and received about a thousand seven hundred shekels of it, and made an ephod, and put it in his own city, at Ophrah.**

*And all Israel went thither a whoring after it: which thing became a snare unto Gideon, and to his house* (Judges 8:27).

**Which means the memorial of the greatest deliverance in the book of Judges became the idol of the generation that followed it.**

**And note the two parts of the sentence. Israel went a whoring after it, and it became a snare to Gideon and to his house.** The man who made it was entangled by it. **Which is the ordinary fate of the leader who leaves behind an object of veneration: he is bound by the thing he built, and he cannot take it back.**

**And the ephod was in all probability intended as a good thing.** Gideon was not setting up Baal. He was making a priestly garment of gold — a visible memorial of the victory, in his own town, at which the people could inquire of God. **And the generation that had seen the angel and the fleece needed no such object. The generation that had only heard the stories went to it as pilgrims.**

**Which is the mechanism of this whole chapter.** A work of God produces an artefact, the artefact outlives the generation that understood it, **and the following generation attaches to the artefact the faith that belonged to the God who did the work.**

### Nehushtan

**And the clearest case in Scripture, and the one with the sharpest ending, is a piece of brass that Moses made in the wilderness and that the people of God burned incense to for seven hundred years.**

**The object had been commanded.** Israel was dying of serpent bites in the desert, and God said to Moses: *Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live* (Numbers 21:8). And Moses made a serpent of brass, and the people looked and lived.

**A commanded remedy, given in mercy, attached to no power of its own, delivered to a generation of dying men.**

**And seven hundred years later, in the reign of Hezekiah, the record says:**

*He removed the high places, and brake the images, and cut down the groves, and brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made: for unto those days the children of Israel did burn incense to it: and he called it Nehushtan* (2 Kings 18:4).

**Unto those days the children of Israel did burn incense to it.**

**The serpent of Moses had become the object of a cult, and it was being served with incense in the temple of the Lord, and nobody had stopped it.**

**And note what Hezekiah did, because there are two acts in the verse and the second is a doctrinal act.**

**He broke it in pieces.**

**And he called it Nehushtan — which is a nickname composed of two words meaning *brass* and *a thing*, that is, *a piece of brass*, or *just brass.***

**He stripped it of its sacred name.**

**Which is the necessary work of every generation that inherits a relic from a deliverance it did not see.** The object must be identified for what it now is, in plain words, and named accordingly, **and Hezekiah did this to the single most historically authenticated artefact of the wilderness generation.**

**And the man who broke the serpent was the king who *trusted in the Lord God of Israel; so that after him was none like him among all the kings of Judah, nor any that were before him* (2 Kings 18:5).**

**Which means the destruction of the relic was not a loss of faith. It was the evidence of it.**

### "The temple of the Lord, the temple of the Lord"

**And the third case broadens the doctrine from objects to institutions, and it is Jeremiah's first sermon, delivered at the gate of the house of God.**

*Stand in the gate of the Lord's house, and proclaim there this word, and say, Hear the word of the Lord, all ye of Judah, that enter in at these gates to worship the Lord. Thus saith the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, Amend your ways and your doings, and I will cause you to dwell in this place. Trust ye not in lying words, saying, The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord, are these* (Jeremiah 7:2–4).

**And then the test, and it is a list of moral facts:**

*For if ye throughly amend your ways and your doings; if ye throughly execute judgment between a man and his neighbour; if ye oppress not the stranger, the fatherless, and the widow, and shed not innocent blood in this place, neither walk after other gods to your hurt: then will I cause you to dwell in this place, in the land that I gave to your fathers, for ever and ever* (Jeremiah 7:5–7).

**And then the indictment, which is the most devastating description of religious hypocrisy in the Old Testament:**

*Will ye steal, murder, and commit adultery, and swear falsely, and burn incense unto Baal, and walk after other gods whom ye know not; and come and stand before me in this house, which is called by my name, and say, We are delivered to do all these abominations? Is this house, which is called by my name, become a den of robbers in your eyes? Behold, even I have seen it, saith the Lord* (Jeremiah 7:9–11).

**We are delivered to do all these abominations.**

**The sentence is the confession of a people who had concluded that the presence of the house among them was a licence rather than a call.** The temple guaranteed them, so the commandments were negotiable.

**And God's answer cites a precedent that had already occurred and that every man in the crowd knew:**

*But go ye now unto my place which was in Shiloh, where I set my name at the first, and see what I did to it for the wickedness of my people Israel ... Therefore will I do unto this house, which is called by my name, wherein ye trust, and unto the place which I gave to you and to your fathers, as I have done to Shiloh* (Jeremiah 7:12, 14).

**Go and look at Shiloh.**

**Which is a sentence that every generation which trusts a building should be sent to obey.** Shiloh was where God had set His name at the first; it was the place of the tabernacle, of Hannah's prayer, of Samuel's calling. **And it was destroyed, and in Jeremiah's day it was a ruin that a man could walk to and inspect.**

**And the crowd's reaction to that sermon, recorded in the next chapter, is the institutional reflex:**

*Now it came to pass, when Jeremiah had made an end of speaking all that the Lord had commanded him to speak unto all the people, that the priests and the prophets and all the people took him, saying, Thou shalt surely die. Why hast thou prophesied in the name of the Lord, saying, This house shall be like Shiloh, and this city shall be desolate without an inhabitant? And all the people were gathered against Jeremiah in the house of the Lord* (Jeremiah 26:8–9).

**Thou shalt surely die.**

**Which is what a house does to the man who says that the house can fall.** The threat was not to the doctrine of God but to the standing of the building, **and the priests and the prophets of that building were the men who made it.**

**And they were right about one thing: the house did fall, twenty years later, in 586 BC, and everything Jeremiah said about it came to pass.**

### The tombs of the prophets

**And the fourth case is the one that most exactly describes the elder generation's idolatry, and the Lord diagnosed it in the last week of His life.**

*Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! because ye build the tombs of the prophets, and garnish the sepulchres of the righteous, and say, If we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them in the blood of the prophets. Wherefore ye be witnesses unto yourselves, that ye are the children of them which killed the prophets* (Matthew 23:29–31).

**Take the mechanism apart.**

**They built the tombs and garnished the sepulchres.** Which is to say they honoured the prophets, expensively, publicly, and posthumously. **The dead prophet cannot contradict the living institution, and honouring him costs nothing and generates reverence.**

**And they said, *if we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them.***

**Which is an act of distancing, and it is the exact opposite of repentance.** Repentance identifies with the fathers' sin and turns from it; **this identifies the fathers' sin and disclaims it, and leaves the disposition which produced it entirely intact.**

**And the Lord's verdict is that the disclaimer is the proof of the lineage: *ye be witnesses unto yourselves, that ye are the children of them which killed the prophets.***

**And within days they did to Him what their fathers had done to the prophets.**

**And Stephen, in the same city and the same council chamber, made the accusation explicit:**

*Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost: as your fathers did, so do ye. Which of the prophets have not your fathers persecuted? and they have slain them which shewed before of the coming of the Just One; of whom ye have been now the betrayers and murderers* (Acts 7:51–52).

**And the summary of the whole history is given by the chronicler in two verses that describe the patience of God and the end of it:**

*And the Lord God of their fathers sent to them by his messengers, rising up betimes, and sending; because he had compassion on his people, and on his dwelling place: but they mocked the messengers of God, and despised his words, and misused his prophets, until the wrath of the Lord arose against his people, till there was no remedy* (2 Chronicles 36:15–16).

**He had compassion on his people, and on his dwelling place.**

**Which is the astonishing phrase of the passage.** God's concern was not only for the people but for the building they had corrupted; **and He sent messengers early and repeatedly because of His compassion for both, and the men who were supposed to be the guardians of the dwelling place mocked, despised and misused the messengers.**

**Which means that the elders' idolatry does not consist in loving the house of God too much; it consists in loving it in a way that cannot hear Him speak about it.**

### Making the word of God of none effect

**And the fifth case is the most formal, because it is a legal case, and it concerns a practice with a doctrinal justification that cancelled a commandment.**

**The scribes and Pharisees came to Jesus with a complaint about the tradition of the elders, and He answered by reversing the charge:**

*Why do thy disciples transgress the tradition of the elders? for they wash not their hands when they eat bread. But he answered and said unto them, Why do ye also transgress the commandment of God by your tradition?* (Matthew 15:2–3).

**And then He gave the example:**

*For God commanded, saying, Honour thy father and mother: and, He that curseth father or mother, let him die the death. But ye say, Whosoever shall say to his father or to his mother, It is a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; and honour not his father or his mother, he shall be free. Thus have ye made the commandment of God of none effect by your tradition* (Matthew 15:4–6).

**And Mark's account adds the verdict on the whole system:**

*Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition ... Making the word of God of none effect through your tradition, which ye have delivered: and many such like things do ye* (Mark 7:9, 13).

**And many such like things do ye.**

**Which means the Corban rule was not an isolated abuse. It was one instance of a method, and the method was the multiplication of practices which had the appearance of devotion and the effect of releasing men from plain obedience.**

**And the quotation with which the Lord finished the passage is from Isaiah, and it is a description of the elders' worship in every generation:**

*This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me. But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men* (Matthew 15:8–9; Isaiah 29:13).

**In vain they do worship me.**

**Which is the most alarming statement in this chapter, because the worship described is not absent. It is being offered — with the mouth, with the lips, in the right place, by the right people — and it is called vain.**

**And the reason is that the doctrine being taught is the commandment of men rather than of God.** Which is how the idolatry of elders completes itself: not by substituting another god, **but by substituting a human practice for the divine command and by continuing to call the result worship.**

### Fearing the Lord and serving their own gods

**And the final case is the most sobering, because it describes an arrangement that nobody in it could see.**

**When Assyria resettled Samaria with peoples from five cities, they were taught the manner of the God of the land by a priest, and the result is described in one verse:**

*They feared the Lord, and served their own gods, after the manner of the nations whom they carried away from thence* (2 Kings 17:33).

**And the chapter's conclusion states how long it lasted:**

*So these nations feared the Lord, and served their graven images, both their children, and their children's children: as did their fathers, so do they unto this day* (2 Kings 17:41).

**Both their children and their children's children.**

**Which is the destination of every generation that settles a compromise rather than repenting of it.** The first generation knows it is a compromise and calls it wisdom. The second generation inherits it as the tradition. **The third generation has no idea that there was ever a question, and would be offended to be told.**

**And this is the most personal form of the elders' idolatry for the reader of this book, because it is the one that is hardest to detect in one's own house.** A congregation can hold a form of worship it has inherited, and a set of practices it has never examined, and a vocabulary it cannot trace to Scripture, **and fear the Lord sincerely the entire time, and be serving its own gods without knowing it.**

**Which is the reason the next chapter in this Part is about tradition, because tradition is the mechanism by which this happens, and the New Testament is not against it. It is against the tradition that makes the word of God of none effect.**

### The anatomy of the danger

**And the mechanism of the elders' idolatry can be diagnosed as precisely as the young man's, and the list is as follows.**

**It confuses the vessel with the presence.** The ark carried into battle, the temple trusted as a guarantee, the brass serpent burnt with incense. **And every generation of God's people has a set of vessels that God once used and that He is not obliged to use again.**

**It preserves the record while refusing the obedience.** *Will ye steal, murder, and commit adultery ... and come and stand before me in this house, which is called by my name, and say, We are delivered to do all these abominations?*

**It measures the living generation by the dead one.** The latter house is compared to the former and despised (Haggai 2:3), **and the man who compares it has forgotten that the promise attached to the latter house was a greater glory (Haggai 2:9).**

**It uses the language of fidelity to disguise the fear of loss.** Which is why the objection to the young is so often phrased as a concern for doctrine, order, and the honour of the house.

**It loves the praise of men.** *Nevertheless among the chief rulers also many believed on him; but because of the Pharisees they did not confess him, lest they should be put out of the synagogue: for they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God* (John 12:42–43). **Note that they believed. The failure was not in the mind but in the will.**

**It defends the institution instead of the truth.** *But that it spread no further among the people, let us straitly threaten them* (Acts 4:17). **The council had the healed man standing in front of them and admitted the miracle, and their concern was the spread.**

**It makes rules in advance so that nobody has to decide.** *For the Jews had agreed already, that if any man did confess that he was Christ, he should be put out of the synagogue* (John 9:22). **And the parents of the blind man used that rule to avoid answering a question they could not answer falsely.**

**And it settles, over time, into a compromise that the grandchildren will defend as the faith of the fathers.** *As did their fathers, so do they unto this day.*

### And the remedy

**And the remedies are in the same Scriptures, and they are as specific as the dangers.**

**First: identify the relic, and if it is being served, break it.** Hezekiah *brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made ... and he called it Nehushtan.* **The naming is part of the work, and the work is done by the man who trusts God rather than by the man who trusts the object.**

**Second: submit the practice to the command, and let the command win.** *Amend your ways and your doings, and I will cause you to dwell in this place.* And the test that Jeremiah gives is not liturgical but moral: judgement between a man and his neighbour, the stranger, the fatherless, the widow, and innocent blood (7:5–6).

**Third: walk to Shiloh and look at it.** *Go ye now unto my place which was in Shiloh, where I set my name at the first, and see what I did to it.* **A generation that has seen one house of God destroyed for the same faults is a generation that can be cured of its confidence in buildings.**

**And fourth: do not silence the living word for the sake of the institution's peace, and remember Gamaliel.**

*And now I say unto you, Refrain from these men, and let them alone: for if this counsel or this work be of men, it will come to nought: but if it be of God, ye cannot overthrow it; lest haply ye be found even to fight against God* (Acts 5:38–39).

**A Pharisee said that in the council of the elders, and it is the wisest sentence recorded from that chamber. It is also the minimum standard for an older generation dealing with a younger one it does not understand.**

**And the maximum is the apostles' answer to the same council: *whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye* (Acts 4:19); and *we ought to obey God rather than men* (Acts 5:29).**

**And the fifth remedy is the confession that the Pharisees refused to make, and it is the sentence the young are owed by the old.**

**Not *if we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them*, which is a disclaimer. But the confession of the pattern, in the present tense, by the men who are in the room: that the house of God has a history of killing prophets and garnishing their tombs, and that we are all capable of it, and that the man standing in front of us may be the one we are about to do it to.**

**And the test of whether a house has enthroned something other than God is a single question, and it should be asked of every generation that finds itself resisting a young man.**

**Is there any way in which this young man could be right, and this house still stand?**

**If the answer is no — if his being right would be the end of us — then the house has already put something on the throne beside God, and it is not his youth that is the danger.**

### Principles

**First: the idolatry of elders is the worship of the vessel, and it can be preached from a pulpit without being noticed.** *Trust ye not in lying words, saying, The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord, are these.*

**Second: the relics of a former deliverance become the idols of the following generation.** The ark carried into battle, the ephod at Ophrah, the brass serpent of Moses — *all Israel went thither a whoring after it: which thing became a snare unto Gideon, and to his house.*

**Third: the presence of God is never available as a technique.** The elders of Israel said *let us fetch the ark ... that it may save us*, and thirty thousand men fell and the ark was taken.

**And fourth: the recovery did not begin with the return of the ark but with the repentance of the people.** *Then the children of Israel did put away Baalim and Ashtaroth, and served the Lord only* (1 Samuel 7:4).

**Fifth: a relic commanded by God in one generation must be broken in another if it is being served.** Hezekiah *brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made ... and he called it Nehushtan.*

**Sixth: an institution's existence is no guarantee of its future.** *Go ye now unto my place which was in Shiloh, where I set my name at the first, and see what I did to it*; and *I shall do unto this house ... as I have done to Shiloh.*

**Seventh: the reflex of a threatened house is to threaten the messenger.** *The priests and the prophets and all the people took him, saying, Thou shalt surely die.*

**And eighth: honouring dead prophets while despising living ones is the marks of the same lineage.** *Ye build the tombs of the prophets ... ye be witnesses unto yourselves, that ye are the children of them which killed the prophets.*

**Ninth: the method of the elders' idolatry is the multiplication of practices which release men from plain obedience.** *Making the word of God of none effect through your tradition ... and many such like things do ye.*

**And tenth: the end of a settled compromise is a third generation that does not know there was ever a question.** *They feared the Lord, and served their own gods ... both their children, and their children's children: as did their fathers, so do they unto this day.*

**And eleventh: the minimum standard for an older generation is Gamaliel's caution, and the maximum is the apostles' refusal to obey men rather than God.** *If this counsel or this work be of men, it will come to nought ... lest haply ye be found even to fight against God.*

### For the fathers

- **Name the relic and, if the house is serving it, break it.** *He called it Nehushtan.* A plain name for the thing that used to be holy is the beginning of a reform.
- **Let the command judge the practice.** *Amend your ways and your doings*; and the test of a house that trusts God is judgement, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow, and innocent blood.
- **Go and look at Shiloh.** God destroyed a house where His name had been set at the first, and the ruins were still there for Jeremiah's congregation to inspect.
- **Do not threaten the messenger.** *Thou shalt surely die* was said by the priests and the prophets of the temple — and the temple fell twenty years later.
- **Take Gamaliel's caution as your minimum and the apostles' resolve as your standard.** If the work is of men it will come to nought; if it is of God you cannot overthrow it; and the danger of resisting it is that a man is found *even to fight against God.*
- **And make the confession the Pharisees would not make — in the present tense.** Not *if we had been in the days of our fathers*, but: this house has killed prophets before, and we are able to do it now.

### For the young

- **Expect to be resisted by men who are sincere.** The elders of Israel carried the ark into battle in good faith, and thirty thousand men died.
- **Do not build your ministry on a relic.** The ephod at Ophrah was a genuine memorial, and it ensnared the man who made it and the house after him.
- **Test every inherited practice by the command.** If a tradition releases a man from a plain commandment, it has already made the word of God of none effect, and it will be defended with Scripture.
- **Watch for the rule made in advance.** *The Jews had agreed already, that if any man did confess that he was Christ, he should be put out of the synagogue.* When a house has pre-agreed its verdict, the question is no longer open.
- **And know that the honour given to dead prophets is often the price paid to avoid the living one.** The men who garnished the sepulchres were the men who killed the prophet in front of them.
- **And be patient with a house that is slow.** Gamaliel's counsel gives you room; God is able to make your work stand, and if it is of men, better that it fall now.

### Questions for reflection

1. What in your house is being served because God used it once?
2. If the young man in front of you were right, would the house survive the correction?
3. Which practice in your congregation releases somebody from a plain commandment, and who is defending it?
4. Which prophet is your house garnishing, and which one is it avoiding?
5. When did your generation last say, in the present tense, that its own house is capable of killing the word of God?
