## How Fathers Should Prepare Their Successors

**And now the chapter of method, because the previous two have argued that a relation must be created and that houses must be built, and neither of those tells a man what to do on Monday.**

**And Scripture has a method, and it is not a theory, and it is demonstrated in at least four successions in detail — Moses to Joshua, David to Solomon, Elijah to Elisha, and Paul to Timothy — and the same eight acts occur in each of them in a similar order.**

**And the whole method begins with a prayer that only one man in Scripture is recorded as having prayed, and it is the prayer of a man who was told he was going to die.**

*And Moses spake unto the Lord, saying, Let the Lord, the God of the spirits of all flesh, set a man over the congregation, which may go out before them, and which may go in before them, and which may lead them out, and which may bring them in; that the congregation of the Lord be not as sheep which have no shepherd* (Numbers 27:15–17).

**Set a man over the congregation.**

**Which means the first act of a proper succession is an older man asking God for his own replacement, and asking for it before he is incapable, and using the occasion to specify what the man will need to do.**

**And the specification in that prayer is worth extracting, because it is a job description in four clauses: he must *go out before them*, and *go in before them*, and *lead them out*, and *bring them in.***

**Which means the man asked for is a man in front of the people and not behind them, at the beginning of the movement and at the end of it, in the advance and in the return. And the reason given is pastoral and unglamorous: *that the congregation of the Lord be not as sheep which have no shepherd.***

**And the answer to that prayer contains the first three acts of the method.**

### Act one: identify the man God has already been forming

**And the answer to Moses' prayer is a sentence that identifies a man by his history and not by a vote:**

*And the Lord said unto Moses, Take thee Joshua the son of Nun, a man in whom is the spirit, and lay thine hand upon him* (Numbers 27:18).

**A man in whom is the spirit.**

**Which means the man was already what he needed to be before he was appointed.** He had been Moses' minister for decades, had fought Amalek, had gone up the mountain, had been jealous and been corrected — **and the appointment formalised a reality that already existed rather than creating one.**

**And the practical instruction that follows from that verse is that a man is discovered rather than manufactured.** An older leader's task is not to make a successor out of available material. **It is to recognise the man God has been building in his own congregation, often in an unglamorous role, and to name him.**

**And the recognition is frequently contested by the man himself.** *And Moses said unto the Lord, O my Lord, I am not eloquent, neither heretofore, nor since thou hast spoken unto thy servant: but I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue* (Exodus 4:10). **Which means a man's own estimate of his readiness is not the criterion, and that the call is frequently given to a man who is convinced of his own incapacity.**

### Act two: put him in front of the congregation and give him a charge in their sight

**And the appointment was public, and its three parties were named in advance:**

*And set him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation; and give him a charge in their sight* (Numbers 27:19).

**Before the priest, before all the congregation, and with a charge given in their sight.**

**Which means the succession was not a private arrangement between two men.** It was transacted in the assembly, with the religious authority present as a witness, and with the words spoken aloud so that the people heard what the young man had been told to do.

**And the account records the execution of the instruction in the same terms, and adds a detail:**

*And Moses did as the Lord commanded him: and he took Joshua, and set him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation: and he laid his hands upon him, and gave him a charge, as the Lord commanded by the hand of Moses* (Numbers 27:22–23).

**Which means the whole act was performed exactly as instructed, and that the charge Moses gave was not his own invention but the word of God relayed.**

**And the reason for the publicity is stated in the verse that every retiring leader should read twice:**

*And thou shalt put some of thine honour upon him, that all the congregation of the children of Israel may be obedient* (Numbers 27:20).

**Some of thine honour.**

**Which means the transfer is real and partial and deliberate.** The older man gives of his own standing, publicly, before he dies, **and the purpose is that the congregation may be able to obey the successor while the predecessor is still present to be compared with him.**

**Which is the single most practical instruction in this chapter.** A new leader who has been given authority and no honour will be resisted for years, and the resistance will be led by people who are loyal to the previous man. **And the previous man is the only person who can prevent it, by handing over his own weight in public rather than leaving it in the grave with him.**

**And the pattern is repeated at the highest level in Israel's history.** Moses, standing before the whole nation, transferred the commission with his own voice:

*And Moses called unto Joshua, and said unto him in the sight of all Israel, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou must go with this people unto the land which the Lord hath sworn unto their fathers to give them; and thou shalt cause them to inherit it* (Deuteronomy 31:7).

**In the sight of all Israel.** And the charge was followed by the promise of the presence of God (31:8), **and then, in a separate act, God Himself gave the man the same charge in the tabernacle:**

*And the Lord said unto Moses, Behold, thy days approach that thou must die: call Joshua, and present yourselves in the tabernacle of the congregation, that I may give him a charge. And Moses and Joshua went, and presented themselves in the tabernacle of the congregation ... And he gave Joshua the son of Nun a charge, and said, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou shalt bring the children of Israel into the land which I sware unto them: and I will be with thee* (Deuteronomy 31:14, 23).

**Which means the successor received the same charge three times, from three directions: from God through Moses, from Moses before the nation, and from God directly in the tabernacle.** And the repetition was not redundancy; **it was the establishing of a man's commission in the presence of every authority that would ever be cited against him.**

### Act three: hand over the pattern, and not merely the post

**And the second great succession in Scripture is the one in which the predecessor was refused permission to do the work himself, and what he handed over is described in more detail than anything else in the Old Testament.**

**David's first act after hearing the refusal was a charge:**

*Then he called for Solomon his son, and charged him to build an house for the Lord God of Israel. And David said to Solomon, My son, as for me, it was in my mind to build an house unto the name of the Lord my God: but the word of the Lord came to me, saying, Thou hast shed blood abundantly, and hast made great wars: thou shalt not build an house unto my name* (1 Chronicles 22:6–8).

**Which means he explained his own disappointment to his son, in plain terms, as part of the handover.** He did not pretend that the refusal had been his preference. **He told the young man what he had wanted to do and why he had been prevented, which is the only way a son can inherit a work without also inheriting a grievance.**

**And then the charge, and its content is worth reading in full because it is addressed to a man who was *young and tender* and it contains no promises about the work:**

*Now, my son, the Lord be with thee; and prosper thou, and build the house of the Lord thy God, as he hath said of thee. Only the Lord give thee wisdom and understanding, and give thee charge concerning Israel, that thou mayest keep the law of the Lord thy God. Then shalt thou prosper, if thou takest heed to fulfil the statutes and judgments which the Lord charged Moses with concerning Israel: be strong, and of good courage; dread not, nor be dismayed* (1 Chronicles 22:11–13).

**Which means the priorities are in order.** Wisdom and understanding, the charge concerning the people, and the keeping of the law — **and the building of the house is mentioned once, first, and then subordinated to the law of God.**

**And then the pattern itself, given in writing, and the account of how it was obtained is the sentence this chapter is built on:**

*Then David gave to Solomon his son the pattern of the porch, and of the houses thereof, and of the treasuries thereof, and of the upper chambers thereof, and of the inner parlours thereof, and of the place of the mercy seat, and the pattern of all that he had by the spirit ... All this, said David, the Lord made me understand in writing by his hand upon me, even all the works of this pattern* (1 Chronicles 28:11–12, 19).

**The Lord made me understand in writing by his hand upon me.**

**Which means the pattern was revealed and it was documented, and that the documentation was the deliverable.** A vision held in a man's head dies with him; **a vision reduced to a written specification can be executed by a man who never met him.**

**Which is the most neglected act of succession in the modern church, and the most damaging omission.** Men leave successors a name, a building, a title and a congregation, **and they leave no specification: no statement of what the house is for, what it believes, how it decides, what it will not do, and who is responsible for what.**

**And a successor who inherits a title without a specification will spend his first five years discovering by experiment what his predecessor knew by revelation — and the congregation will spend those years being taught by a man who is learning in public what the dying man could have written down.**

**And the inventory of what David handed over is given across three chapters, and a leader who wants to prepare a successor should be able to reproduce the same list:** the pattern and the specification (28:11–19); the gold and silver by weight for every vessel (28:14–18); the courses of the priests and Levites (28:13, 21); the materials gathered (22:14); the workmen (22:15); the princes and the people (28:21); the charge (22:11–13; 28:9–10, 20); and the public announcement to the assembly (29:1–5).

**Which means the successor was given eight things and not one of them was authority alone.**

**And the fourth succession in the same family is the warning that belongs here.** Jehoshaphat, a good king, divided his estate among his sons and gave the kingdom to the firstborn:

*And their father gave them great gifts of silver, and of gold, and of precious things, with fenced cities in Judah: but the kingdom gave he to Jehoram; because he was the firstborn* (2 Chronicles 21:3).

**Great gifts and fenced cities, and no charge, and no pattern, and no character.**

**And the record of Jehoram's reign is that he killed all his brothers with the sword, and did evil in the sight of the Lord, and walked in the ways of the kings of Israel, and made high places (2 Chronicles 21:4–11), and the nation was lost with him.**

**Which means that the inheritance of material without a specification is not a succession. It is a train of combustible material with the source of ignition included.**

### Act four: give the man assignments that can be failed

**And the succession from Paul to Timothy is the one in which the method of assignment is documented, and it is conducted entirely through placement and correspondence.**

**Timothy is left in a city with a job.**

*As I besought thee to abide still at Ephesus, when I went into Macedonia, that thou mightest charge some that they teach no other doctrine* (1 Timothy 1:3).

**Which is a deployment with an objective, and it was against the young man's preference, because Paul wrote *besought*.** And the objective was not pastoral warmth but doctrinal correction, in a city whose church had elders who had wept on Paul's neck at Miletus (Acts 20:37).

**And he is sent with authority to act on the apostle's behalf.** *So he sent into Macedonia two of them that ministered unto him, Timotheus and Erastus; but he himself stayed in Asia for a season* (Acts 19:22).

**And he is given a standard for appointing other men, which means he was responsible for the second generation before he had finished being the first:**

*And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also* (2 Timothy 2:2).

**And he is told to do the work of an evangelist, which is to say the work the apostle had been doing in those towns:**

*But watch thou in all things, endure afflictions, do the work of an evangelist, make full proof of thy ministry* (2 Timothy 4:5).

**Make full proof of thy ministry.**

**Which means the assignments were real, difficult, capable of being failed, and accompanied by correction from a distance in letters that are now in the canon.**

**And the same element appears in the preparations of Christ, and it appears at the beginning rather than the end.** He sent the twelve while He was still with them; He sent the seventy; He sent them to preach and to heal; **and when they returned He corrected them where they were wrong and instructed them further.**

**And He sent them again, finally, with the whole world as the field, and the only equipment named was His authority and His presence:**

*And Jesus came and spake unto them, saying, All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world* (Matthew 28:18–20).

**Which means the final commissioning was given to men who had already been sent three times, had failed in various ways, and had been corrected. And the equipment was the authority of the sender and the promise of his presence, which are the same two things every successor is given.**

### Act five: hand over the relationships and not only the office

**And this is the act that prepares a successor least often, and it is the easiest of the eight to perform and the most catastrophic to omit.**

**A man's office is a title. A man's ministry is a network of relationships, obligations, confidences and favours — and it is the network that does the work.**

**And David handed over the relationships, and did it by name, in front of the men themselves:**

*And, behold, the courses of the priests and the Levites, even they shall be with thee for all the service of the house of God: and there shall be with thee for all manner of workmanship every willing skilful man, for any manner of service: also the princes and all the people will be wholly at thy commandment* (1 Chronicles 28:21).

**Which the old man said to the young man in the hearing of the persons named.**

**And the record two chapters later describes how the transfer took effect:**

*And they made Solomon the son of David king the second time, and anointed him unto the Lord to be the chief governor, and Zadok to be priest. Then Solomon sat on the throne of the Lord as king instead of David his father, and prospered; and all Israel obeyed him. And all the princes, and the mighty men, and all the sons likewise of king David, submitted themselves unto Solomon the king* (1 Chronicles 29:22–24).

**All Israel obeyed him. The princes, the mighty men, and all the sons of David submitted.**

**Which means the submission of the powerful men and even of Solomon's own half-brothers was secured by the predecessor's arrangements and not by the successor's merit.**

**And Paul's letters are largely an instrument for the same act.** He commends Timothy to churches that do not know him (1 Corinthians 16:10–11); he tells the Philippians that he has *no man likeminded* (Philippians 2:20); he sends greetings from a list of co-workers so that the names become transferable (Romans 16); **and he names men for particular congregations so that when he is gone the relationships continue (Colossians 4:17; 2 Timothy 4:11).**

**And the omission of this act is the commonest reason a succession fails in a congregation that wanted it to succeed.** The successor is given the office and the pulpit, and the older man retains the personal loyalties, the private confidences and the ear of the influential. **And a house cannot be led by a man who does not know what the previous man knows.**

### Act six: tell the truth about the ending, and do it in advance

**And the plainest act of the method is the declaration, and the model is Moses standing before the whole assembly with the fact of his own death on his lips:**

*And he said unto them, I am an hundred and twenty years old this day; I can no more go out and come in: also the Lord hath said unto me, Thou shalt not go over this Jordan. The Lord thy God, he will go over before thee, and he will destroy these nations from before thee, and thou shalt possess them: and Joshua, he shall go over before thee, as the Lord hath said* (Deuteronomy 31:2–3).

**I can no more go out and come in.**

**Which means the man's incapacity was announced by the man himself, in public, in the ordinary language of the day, and it was announced in the same breath as the naming of his successor.**

**And Paul did the same thing with the elders of Ephesus, and the passage contains the whole doctrine of an announced departure:**

*And now, brethren, I commend you to God, and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up, and to give you an inheritance among all them which are sanctified ... For I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock. Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them* (Acts 20:32, 29–30).

**Which means he told them three things: that the deposit was now theirs, that the danger was coming, and that the danger would come from among them.**

**And then he prayed with them, and they wept, and the record of it is unusually detailed:**

*And when he had thus spoken, he kneeled down, and prayed with them all. And they all wept sore, and fell on Paul's neck, and kissed him, sorrowing most of all for the words which he spake, that they should see his face no more. And they accompanied him unto the ship* (Acts 20:36–38).

**Which means the ending was known in advance and was permitted to be grieved.** The successor's task is not to pretend that the old man is still present, and the old man's task is not to slip away without a farewell. **And a congregation that was told in advance can be prepared; a congregation that is surprised cannot.**

### Act seven: bless him, and then leave

**And the last act is the shortest, and it is the one that most older leaders find impossible, because it consists of doing nothing.**

*So Joshua blessed them, and sent them away: and they went unto their tents* (Joshua 22:6).

*And when they had ordained them elders in every church, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed* (Acts 14:23).

**He blessed and sent. They prayed and commended.**

**Which means the final act is a handover to God and not a retention of influence.** And the temptation at this point is enormous, because a man who has built a work for forty years can watch it fail in eighteen months, and he knows it, and he knows exactly what the successor is getting wrong.

**And the model is Joshua, who at the end of his life called the nation together and did the opposite of interfering: he set out the covenant again, and told them plainly what the choice was, and said:**

*And if it seem evil unto you to serve the Lord, choose you this day whom ye will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the flood, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord* (Joshua 24:15).

**As for me and my house.**

**Which means that the last contribution of an older man is his own household and his own example, named publicly, and nothing else.** He does not hold the reins; he states his own position and leaves the nation to decide.

**And the one thing that must be released in that act is the need to approve.** *And when they had ordained them elders in every church, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord.* **The commending is to the Lord, and not to a supervisory arrangement, and it means that the older man has surrendered his judgement of the outcome to God.**

**And the same surrender is stated by Christ in the sentence that every predecessor should have framed:**

*Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you* (John 16:7).

**It is expedient for you that I go away.**

**Which is the only sentence in the Gospels in which a leader states that his departure is better for his followers than his presence.** And the reason is that the successor He was giving them — the Comforter, the Spirit of truth who would *guide you into all truth* (16:13) and *bring all things to your remembrance* (14:26) — **could not be given while the leader was in the way.**

**Which means the greatest work your successor will ever do among the people you raised may be a work that your presence prevents.**

### What must not be handed over

**And the chapter must include the negative list, because it is longer than the positive one and it is drawn from the same cases.**

**Do not hand over an unresolved quarrel.** David, on his deathbed, gave Solomon a charge about Joab and about Shimei (1 Kings 2:5–9), and the record of it is one of the least attractive scenes in the life of a great king. **A man who leaves his enemies to his son has made his son's first act a killing.**

**Do not hand over a debt you incurred for your own house.** The sons of the prophets had a debt in the fourth chapter of 2 Kings — but it was the debt of a household that had lived in the community, and the community's prophet answered for it (4:1–7). **A man who leaves his son a building with a mortgage he cannot service has left him a burden and called it an inheritance.**

**Do not hand over the untested opinions with the tested doctrine.** The distinction between what a man has received from God and what he has concluded in his own study is a distinction he is responsible for making, **and a successor who cannot tell which parts of the inheritance are load-bearing will either defend everything or abandon everything.**

**Do not hand over the monopoly.** Which is the arrangement in which the older man gives the title to his successor and keeps the relationships with the donors, the decisions about property, and the last word on doctrine. **The disciples complained about a man who cast out devils and followed not with them, and the Lord's correction — *forbid him not: for he that is not against us is for us* (Luke 9:50) — is the standing instruction against every ministry that decides that the work belongs to its own succession.**

**And do not hand over the fear.** *Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them: for the Lord thy God, he it is that doth go with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee* (Deuteronomy 31:6). Which is what Moses said to the people, and then again to Joshua, **and it is the last thing a father says, and the reason it must be said is that the young man is going to be afraid.**

### The order of the acts, and the timing

**And the eight acts of the method can be set out in the sequence in which Scripture performs them, and the sequence is not arbitrary.**

**The prayer for a successor comes first, and it comes years before the end.** Moses prayed it when he was told his death was approaching and he was still functioning; the appointment that answered it was made with the whole congregation watching, **and Joshua had been Moses' minister for the whole of the intervening period.**

**The identification comes second, and it identifies a man who already exists.** *A man in whom is the spirit.* The search is not for potential; it is for evidence.

**The exposure comes third, and it lasts for years.** Elisha poured water; Timothy was sent and left and sent again. **And the older man must construct these years deliberately, because an apprenticeship that consists of attendance produces a listener.**

**The transfer of the pattern comes fourth, and it is done in writing.** *The Lord made me understand in writing by his hand upon me.* **Which means the older man should have a document, and its absence is a sign that the work has never been thought through.**

**The public charge comes fifth, and it comes before the final illness and not during it.** *Set him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation; and give him a charge in their sight.*

**The handing over of honour comes sixth, and it is the hardest and the most necessary.** *Thou shalt put some of thine honour upon him, that all the congregation of the children of Israel may be obedient.*

**The transfer of relationships comes seventh, and it is done by name and in front of the people named.** *Behold, the courses of the priests and the Levites, even they shall be with thee.*

**And the blessing and the departure come last.** *So Joshua blessed them, and sent them away: and they went unto their tents.*

**And the timing question has one answer in Scripture and it is not the answer most men want.** The recognised warning in the pastoral letters is that a house must not be handed to a novice, and the criterion is character and not years (1 Timothy 3:6). **And the operational criterion is stated by Christ, and it is about the work rather than the man: *he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee, until thou hast finished all the work* (1 Chronicles 28:20).**

**Which means the succession should be completed while the work is still being finished, and the man who waits until he can no longer work has waited too long to prepare anyone.**

### The test of the preparation

**And the whole chapter can be reduced to three questions, and they are questions an older man can answer in an afternoon.**

**The first is: who knows what I know?**

**Not who has heard my sermons, and not who has a copy of my notes. Who can state the purpose of this house, the boundaries of its doctrine, the plan of its work, the names of its obligations and the reasons for its practices, without asking me?**

**If the answer is nobody, the work has been conducted as a personal possession and not as a house.**

**And the second question is: who has my standing with the people I have led?**

**Which is not the same as who has my title. It is whether the elders, the donors, the young men and the families would say that the successor speaks with the weight the predecessor had — and whether the predecessor has said so himself, in their hearing, in plain words.**

**And the third question is: what will he be free to do differently?**

**And the answering of it is the last act of a father.** David forbade nothing that Solomon did, and the son built a house twice the specification, and the glory filled it (2 Chronicles 5:13–14). **But David also told him, in writing, what the pattern was, so that the freedom was exercised inside a specification and not against one.**

**Which is the balance that the whole book has been arguing for, and it is the balance that this chapter delivers as a method.** The predecessor gives the deposit entire and the freedom entire, **and he does both in writing, in public, in front of the people, while he is still alive, so that no one has to guess afterwards what he would have wanted.**

**And the last word belongs to the man who succeeded by this method and who said so at the end of it.**

**Elisha, at the beginning, rent his own clothes when his father was taken up. And Elisha, in the middle, went back to Bethel and stood where the old prophet had lied to a young man of God, and the record of what he did there is not in Scripture.**

**What is recorded is the sentence the sons of the prophets said at Jericho, and it is the verdict every father is wishing for and cannot buy:**

*The spirit of Elijah doth rest on Elisha* (2 Kings 2:15).

### Principles

**First: the first act of succession is a prayer for a replacement, prayed early and specifying the work.** *Let the Lord, the God of the spirits of all flesh, set a man over the congregation ... that the congregation of the Lord be not as sheep which have no shepherd.*

**Second: the man identified is a man already in whom the Spirit is at work, and not a promising vacancy.** *Take thee Joshua the son of Nun, a man in whom is the spirit, and lay thine hand upon him.*

**And third: the appointment is public, and it is made before the religious authority and before all the congregation.** *And set him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation; and give him a charge in their sight.*

**And fourth: the transfer of honour is deliberate and it is made for the sake of the people's ability to obey.** *And thou shalt put some of thine honour upon him, that all the congregation of the children of Israel may be obedient.*

**And fifth: a man who has been given authority and no honour, while the previous leader is still alive and available for comparison, will be resisted for years.**

**Sixth: the successor must be given the pattern, and the pattern must be in writing.** *All this, said David, the Lord made me understand in writing by his hand upon me, even all the works of this pattern.*

**And seventh: material without a specification is not an inheritance.** Jehoshaphat gave his sons silver, gold, precious things and fenced cities, and left them no charge, and Jehoram killed his brothers and lost the nation.

**Eighth: the successor must be given assignments that can be failed, and the assignment is the formation.** *As I besought thee to abide still at Ephesus*; *so he sent into Macedonia ... Timotheus and Erastus*; *do the work of an evangelist, make full proof of thy ministry.*

**Ninth: the relationships must be handed over by name and in front of the people named.** *Behold, the courses of the priests and the Levites, even they shall be with thee ... also the princes and all the people will be wholly at thy commandment.* And *all Israel obeyed him; and all the princes, and the mighty men, and all the sons likewise of king David, submitted themselves unto Solomon.*

**And tenth: the ending must be announced by the man himself, in public, in advance, and it must be permitted to be grieved.** *I can no more go out and come in*; and *sorrowing most of all for the words which he spake, that they should see his face no more.*

**And eleventh: the final act is a blessing and a departure, and the committal is to God.** *So Joshua blessed them, and sent them away*; *and they commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed.*

**And twelfth: the successor may need the predecessor gone for the greatest part of his own ministry to begin.** *Nevertheless I tell you the truth: It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you.*

**And thirteenth: an older man must not hand on his quarrel, his debt, his untested opinions, his monopoly or his fear.** *Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them: for the Lord thy God, he it is that doth go with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.*

### For the fathers

- **Pray for a successor early, and specify the work in the prayer.** *Set a man over the congregation ... that the congregation of the Lord be not as sheep which have no shepherd.*
- **Write the specification and give it away before you die.** *The Lord made me understand in writing by his hand upon me.* A work described only in your head ends at your funeral.
- **Give him honour in public, and give it while you are still strong enough to be compared with him.** *Thou shalt put some of thine honour upon him, that all the congregation be obedient.*
- **Hand over the relationships by name, in front of the people named.** *Behold, the courses of the priests and the Levites, even they shall be with thee.*
- **Tell the people the truth about your own ending.** *I can no more go out and come in.* And let them weep; the weeping is part of the transfer.
- **And bless him and go, and leave the outcome to God.** *And they commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed.*
- **And do not hand on your quarrel, your debt, your untested opinions, your monopoly or your fear.** *Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them.*

### For the young

- **Expect to be identified by evidence and not by ambition.** *A man in whom is the spirit.* What you have been doing in obscurity is the qualification.
- **Accept the honour when it is publicly given, and do not pretend you do not need it.** *Thou shalt put some of thine honour upon him* — the congregation's obedience depends on it, and a false humility that refuses the transfer damages the people.
- **Read the specification before you change anything.** *All the works of this pattern* was given in writing, and a man who begins by inventing his own house has inherited nothing.
- **Welcome the assignments that can fail, and expect to be corrected from a distance.** *Do the work of an evangelist, make full proof of thy ministry.*
- **Let the old man's departure be grieved and do not rush him out of the room.** The elders wept on Paul's neck; the sons of the prophets tried to go and look for Elijah for three days.
- **And remember that some of your ministry may not begin until he has gone.** *It is expedient for you that I go away.* That sentence is not permission to hasten his going. It is the reason you can be at peace when God takes him.

### Questions for reflection

1. Who currently knows what you know about the work you are leading, and could they write it down without you?
2. What is the written specification of your house, and when did you last read it?
3. Whose honour have you transferred publicly in the last twelve months, and in whose hearing?
4. Which relationships are you still holding that should have been handed over by name?
5. What have you permitted to remain unsaid about your own ending, and what would it cost to say it now?
