## Wisdom Without Obedience

**And this is the last and the most respectable of the three dangers, and it is the one most likely to be found among the readers of a book like this one.**

**The arrogance of youth destroys a man publicly. The idolatry of elders destroys a house institutionally. Untested revelation destroys a congregation by flooding it with words. And wisdom without obedience destroys nothing visible at all — it produces a man who knows, and teaches, and is admired, and does not do.**

**And the Scripture's description of him is an image, and it is deliberately undramatic:**

*But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves. For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: for he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was. But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed* (James 1:22–25).

**A man looks into a mirror, sees his own face, and walks away and forgets what he looks like.**

**Which is the least dramatic failure imaginable, and the most universal, and it is described by James as a man *deceiving his own self.***

**The Greek is *paralogizomenoi heautous*, which means reasoning yourselves into a false conclusion.** The deception is not imposed on the man from outside; **it is performed by the man on himself, using his own powers of reasoning, and the material of the deception is his knowledge.**

**Which is why the study of the word of God is the most dangerous activity in which a believer can engage without obedience. Nothing else can be mistaken for obedience so plausibly.**

### The mirror, and the man in the glass

**And the details of James's image repay slow reading, because every one of them is a mechanism.**

**The mirror is the word of God, and its first function, in James's analogy, is not instruction but self-disclosure.** A man who looks into a mirror sees his own face. **Which means the first thing the word of God does to a reader is to show him what he is — and this happens before it tells him anything about anything else.**

**And the seeing is accurate.** James does not suggest that the man's view of himself was wrong. He saw his natural face. **Which means the danger is not a mistaken reading of the Bible. It is a correct reading of the Bible, followed by a departure.**

**And the departure is the moment of destruction: *he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way.***

**The Greek is a single participle, *apelēlythen*, he has gone away.** He went to his business, his house, his week. **And the knowledge was accurate and the departure was ordinary, and there is nothing in the account that a congregation would notice.**

**And the result: *and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was.***

**Straightway.** Which means the interval between the looking and the forgetting is very short, and this is the part of the passage that every teacher of the word should have weighed. **The retention of what a man has seen about himself in the mirror is measured in hours and not in weeks.**

**And the contrast is not with a man who looks more often. It is with a man who *continueth therein* and is *not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work.***

**The Greek for *continueth* is *parameinas*, from *paramenō*, to remain beside or to stay by.** Which means that the difference between the forgetful man and the blessed man is not intelligence, or memory, or training. **It is duration and action. One stayed and did; the other looked and left.**

**And the promise is attached to the doing and not to the knowing: *this man shall be blessed in his deed.***

**Which is where the blessing is located in the entire New Testament. In his deed. Not in his insight, and not in his understanding, and not in the accuracy of his doctrine.**

### "Search the scriptures ... and ye will not come to me"

**And the supreme case of wisdom without obedience in Scripture is not a hypocrite. It is a body of men whose professional occupation was the study of the text, and it is a sentence from the Lord that should be read by every man who has ever prepared a sermon.**

*Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me. And ye will not come to me, that ye might have life* (John 5:39–40).

**Take the two statements in order.**

**The first is a command, and the men He was speaking to were already doing it.** They searched the Scriptures; that was their trade. **And He confirmed their central conviction about the text — that the Scriptures testify of Him. The Lord does not tell the scholars that they have misread their Bibles.**

**And the second statement is the indictment: *and ye will not come to me, that ye might have life.***

**The will, and not the mind.**

**Which means that the most dangerous position in the world is to be correct about the text and unwilling to come to the person of whom the text speaks.** And the danger is not that the study was wrong. **It is that the study was a substitute for the coming, and the men in front of Him did not know it, because the study felt exactly like obedience.**

**And the same indictment is given in the next chapter, in the temple, about the law itself:**

*Is it not written in your law, I said, Ye are gods? ... but these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name* (John 20:31).

**Which is what the written word was for. Life, through believing, in His name.**

**And Paul's summary of the condition is one phrase, written about the last days:**

*Ever learning, and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth* (2 Timothy 3:7).

**Which is the mirror of James in another form, and it is a description of a house and not of an age.** Teachers, books, conferences, podcasts, translations, courses — **and a congregation that has been learning for thirty years and has not been brought to the knowledge of the truth, because the truth that has been learned has not been done.**

**And the severest sentence on the scholars of the word is in the eleventh chapter of Luke, and it is about their effect on other people:**

*Woe unto you, lawyers! for ye have taken away the key of knowledge: ye entered not in yourselves, and them that were entering in ye hindered* (Luke 11:52).

**Ye entered not in yourselves, and them that were entering in ye hindered.**

**Which means that a man who knows and does not do is not merely failing. He is standing in the doorway.** And the crowds who might have gone in are the ones held back by him.

### "Thou that teachest another, teachest thou not thyself?"

**And the second case is about the effect of the gap on the reputation of God among people who do not believe, and it is the sharpest passage in Romans on the subject.**

**Paul sets out the advantages of the man who has the law — he is called a Jew, he rests in the law, he boasts in God, he knows the will, he approves the excellent things, he is confident that he is *a guide of the blind, a light of them which are in darkness, an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of babes* (2:17–20).**

**Which is an accurate self-description of every trained religious man, including every trained Christian teacher.**

**And then the questions:**

*Thou therefore which teachest another, teachest thou not thyself? thou that preachest a man should not steal, dost thou steal? Thou that sayest a man should not commit adultery, dost thou commit adultery? thou that abhorrest idols, dost thou commit sacrilege? Thou that makest thy boast of the law, through breaking the law dishonourest thou God?* (Romans 2:21–23).

**And then the consequence, which is not about the man at all:**

*For the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you, as it is written* (Romans 2:24).

**The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you.**

**Which means that the knowledge–obedience gap is not a private matter and is not merely a matter of personal loss.** It is the mechanism by which the unbelieving world comes to despise the name of God. **And Paul is quoting the prophets to establish that this had happened before, to a nation with the oracles of God in its possession.**

**And the same sanction is attached to the qualification of a leader, in the letter to Timothy: *moreover he must have a good report of them which are without; lest he fall into reproach and the snare of the devil* (1 Timothy 3:7).**

**Which means that a man's standing with those outside the church is a qualification for office**, and that a house of God that appoints men without it will find that the reproach of those men becomes the reproach of Christ, **in the mouths of people who never read the Book.**

### "A very lovely song"

**And the third case is the most painful of the three, because it describes a congregation that loves the preaching.**

**Ezekiel was told what his hearers were saying about him:**

*Also, thou son of man, the children of thy people still are talking against thee by the walls and in the doors of the houses, and speak one to another, every one to his brother, saying, Come, I pray you, and hear what is the word that cometh forth from the Lord* (Ezekiel 33:30).

**Come, and hear what is the word that cometh forth from the Lord.**

**Which is the invitation of a people who were interested.** They spoke about the prophet in the streets and at the doorways and invited one another to the services. **And then they came:**

*And they come unto thee as the people cometh, and they sit before thee as my people, and they hear thy words, but they will not do them: for with their mouth they shew much love, but their heart goeth after their covetousness* (Ezekiel 33:31).

**They sat as God's people, heard the words of God, and did not do them, and their mouths showed much love while their hearts went after gain.**

**And then the sentence that has convicted preachers in every generation, because it describes the experience of listening to the word of God:**

*And, lo, thou art unto them as a very lovely song of one that hath a pleasant voice, and can play well on an instrument: for they hear thy words, but they do them not* (Ezekiel 33:32).

**A very lovely song.**

**Which means that the word of God can be received as an aesthetic experience.** The prophet had a pleasant voice and played his instrument well, **and the congregation came out to hear him as people go to a concert, and were moved, and went home unaltered.**

**And note that the Lord's judgement of that congregation is not that they were bored. It is that they were entertained.**

**Which is the most subtle danger in a well-taught church. A congregation with excellent preaching, careful exposition, fine singing and attentive listeners can be a congregation in which nothing is ever done, because the excellence of the hearing has become the substitute for obedience.**

**And the remedy in that same chapter is the event. *And when this cometh to pass, (lo, it will come,) then shall they know that a prophet hath been among them* (33:33).**

**Which means that the truth of the preaching will be proved by the fulfilment of what was preached, and that the audience will discover it in the middle of the disaster the sermons had been warning them about.**

**And Hosea's account of the same phenomenon is contained in one line: *I have written to him the great things of my law, but they were counted as a strange thing* (Hosea 8:12).**

**A strange thing.** Not a false thing, and not a despised thing. **Simply something foreign, admired from a distance, and not the law of the man's own life.**

### The two sons, and the two builders

**And the Lord told two parables that make the point with an exactness that leaves no room for the middle ground.**

**The first is about two sons and one instruction:**

*A certain man had two sons; and he came to the first, and said, Son, go work to day in my vineyard. He answered and said, I will not: but afterward he repented, and went. And he came to the second, and said likewise. And he answered and said, I go, sir: and went not* (Matthew 21:28–30).

**And note the two answers.**

**The first is offensive: I will not. And he went.**

**The second is respectful: I go, sir. And he did not go.**

**And the Lord's verdict, spoken to the chief priests and the elders of the people, is that the publicans and the harlots go into the kingdom of God before them (21:31).**

**Which means that the polite refusal and the polite acceptance are not equivalent, and that the second is worse.** The son who said *I go, sir* had the language of obedience and none of the substance, **and the men who heard the parable were the men who had spent their lives saying it.**

**And the second parable is about two houses and one storm, and it ends the Sermon on the Mount:**

*Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock: and the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock. And every one that heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them not, shall be likened unto a foolish man, which built his house upon the sand: and the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell: and great was the fall of it* (Matthew 7:24–27).

**Take the details, because each of them is deliberate.**

**The two men both heard the sayings.** The difference between them is not exposure to the word; it is the doing of it.

**The two houses looked alike while the weather was fine.** There is nothing in the account to suggest a difference in the building, the design, or the materials. **The foundation was invisible, and the congregation could see only the house.**

**And the storm came to both.** *The rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house.* Which means that hearing without doing offers no protection from the events of life; **the storm arrives at the address of the wise and the foolish alike, and it does not check how much a man has heard.**

**And the fall of the second was great.**

**And Luke's version of the same sermon contains the question that ties the whole chapter together: *And why call ye me, Lord, Lord, and do not the things which I say?* (Luke 6:46).**

**Which is the one question that a knowledgeable man cannot answer.** He can explain the doctrine, defend the position, and teach the passage. **And the question is not about his knowledge. It is about the address he uses and the life he lives.**

### And the knowledge that increases the liability

**And the last passage in the chapter is the one that should be read with the greatest care by the residents of well-taught houses, because it establishes that knowing more increases what a man owes.**

*And that servant, which knew his lord's will, and prepared not himself, neither did according to his will, shall be beaten with many stripes. But he that knew not, and did commit things worthy of stripes, shall be beaten with few stripes. For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required* (Luke 12:47–48).

**The servant who knew and did not prepare himself is beaten with many stripes. The ignorant one is beaten with few.**

**Which means that ignorance mitigates and knowledge aggravates, and that the liability of a man is proportionate to what was taught him.**

**And the implication for the houses of God in which careful teaching is a point of pride is severe, and it must be stated plainly. The most accountable congregations on earth are the best-taught ones.**

**An hour of exposition is a ledger entry. Every passage explained to a man who then goes out and does not do it adds to what he owes, and the increase is not in his insight but in his liability.**

**And the same principle is stated by the Lord in another form: *if ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them* (John 13:17).**

**If ye know these things, and if ye do them. The condition is stated twice, and the second condition is the one that decides whether the first produces anything.**

### The remedy

**And the remedy for this danger is not less knowledge. It is the conversion of knowledge into action, and Scripture gives five practical elements.**

**First: hear with the intention to do, which is what the posture of Samuel was for.** *Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth* (1 Samuel 3:9). Which means the response to the word of God is a servant's response, **and a man who comes to the text as a student and not as a servant has already arranged his terms.**

**And James's instruction about the manner of hearing follows the same line: *let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath* (James 1:19).** Which is a description of a listener who is not preparing his reply, **and much of the disobedience of well-taught men begins in the middle of the sermon, when they start composing their response.**

**Second: the reading and the doing must be held together in a single act, as Deuteronomy holds them for the king.** The copy of the law was to be with him and he was to read in it all the days of his life, ***that he may learn to fear the Lord his God, to keep all the words of this law and these statutes, to do them* (Deuteronomy 17:19).**

**Learn, keep, do.** Three verbs, in that order, and the sequence is not optional.

**Third: a man should be examined by somebody who can see his life, and not only by those who hear his teaching.** Which is the reason the household is in the qualification list, and it is the reason the elder must have *a good report of them which are without* (1 Timothy 3:7). **A man's doctrine is examined by his peers; his practice is examined by his wife, his children, and the people who deal with him during the week.**

**Fourth: the pleasure of hearing must never be allowed to stand in place of the deed.** *And, lo, thou art unto them as a very lovely song of one that hath a pleasant voice.* **And the practical form of this discipline is to leave the sermon with a single thing to be done, and to do it before the next one arrives.**

**Fifth: where a house has drifted into hearing without doing, the remedy is the one the Lord gave to a church that had left its first love, and the instruction is not to study more.**

*Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works; or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will remove thy candlestick out of his place, except thou repent* (Revelation 2:5).

**Remember. Repent. Do the first works.**

**Which means that the restoration of a knowledgeable and disobedient house is not accomplished by a deeper understanding of its condition. It is accomplished by doing again the things it did at the beginning.**

**And the promise attached to the reading of the last book of the Bible is not a promise to the reader but to the keeper:** *Blessed is he that readeth, and they that hear the words of this prophecy, and keep those things which are written therein* (Revelation 1:3).

**Readeth, heareth, and keepeth.** And the blessing is not attached to the first two.

### And the conclusion of the whole matter

**And the book of Ecclesiastes ends with the sentence that this whole chapter has been moving towards, and it is the shortest statement of what a life is for:**

*Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man. For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good, or whether it be evil* (Ecclesiastes 12:13–14).

**Fear God, and keep his commandments.**

**And the reason given is that everything will be brought into judgement, including *every secret thing.***

**Which means that the question is never how much a man knew. It is what he did with what he knew, and the whole of Scripture on the subject can be reduced to three statements.**

**The first is that hearing the word of God and doing it are the same event in the mind of God, and a man who separates them has not done anything yet.** *Be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only.*

**The second is that the separation is invisible from the outside and fatal from the inside.** *Deceiving your own selves* — and the man in the mirror walks away and forgets his own face.

**And the third is that the sentence which a knowledgeable and obedient man will hear is not a compliment on his understanding:**

*Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things: enter thou into the joy of thy lord* (Matthew 25:21).

**Well done. Which is said about the doing, and never about the knowing.**

**And the last word of this Part therefore belongs to the oldest of the three dangers and the simplest of the remedies.** The young man must be told that his gift is not his character. The elders must be told that their house is not their God. The prophet must be told that his revelation must be tested. **And the wise man, of whatever age, must be told that the mirror is not the face, and that the only way a man is changed by what he sees in it is by doing what it shows him.**

### Principles

**First: the study of the word without obedience is the most plausible counterfeit of obedience there is, and the deception is self-administered.** *Be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.*

**Second: the first work of the word of God in a man is to show him himself, and the danger is a correct reading followed by an ordinary departure.** *He beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was.*

**And third: the blessing is attached to the doing and not to the knowing.** *But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein ... this man shall be blessed in his deed.*

**Fourth: men can be correct about the text and unwilling to come to the Person, and the study itself can be the substitute.** *Search the scriptures ... and ye will not come to me, that ye might have life.*

**Fifth: the man who knows and does not do is not merely failing; he is standing in the doorway.** *Ye entered not in yourselves, and them that were entering in ye hindered.*

**And sixth: the gap between a teacher's doctrine and his life defames the name of God in front of people who never read the Book.** *For the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you.*

**Seventh: the word of God can be received as an aesthetic experience.** *Thou art unto them as a very lovely song of one that hath a pleasant voice ... for they hear thy words, but they do them not.* And the judgement on that congregation is not that it was bored but that it was entertained.

**And eighth: the son who says *I go, sir* and does not go is worse off than the son who refuses and repents.** *The publicans and the harlots go into the kingdom of God before you.*

**Ninth: hearing without doing offers no protection when the storm comes, and the storm arrives at both addresses.** *And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell: and great was the fall of it.*

**And tenth: knowledge increases liability, so the best-taught houses are the most accountable.** *That servant, which knew his lord's will, and prepared not himself ... shall be beaten with many stripes ... For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required.*

**And eleventh: the remedy for a knowledgeable and disobedient house is not more teaching but the doing of the first works.** *Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works.*

**And twelfth: every secret thing will be brought into judgement, and the commendation is for faithfulness in doing.** *Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man*; and *well done, thou good and faithful servant.*

### For the fathers

- **Do not measure your house by the quality of its hearing.** *Thou art unto them as a very lovely song of one that hath a pleasant voice.* A congregation can be well taught, attentive and unmoved.
- **Ask the question of Romans 2 of yourself before you ask it of the congregation.** *Thou therefore which teachest another, teachest thou not thyself?*
- **Appoint men whose practice can be examined by people who are not in the church.** *Moreover he must have a good report of them which are without.*
- **And remember that the liability of your congregation rises with every sermon.** *That servant, which knew his lord's will, and prepared not himself ... shall be beaten with many stripes.*
- **And where the house has drifted, do not prescribe more study.** *Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works.*

### For the young

- **Do not let your reading of the word become your obedience to it.** *Be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.*
- **Leave every sermon with one thing to do, and do it before the next one arrives.** The mirror is accurate and the memory is short; the interval is measured in hours.
- **Come to the text as a servant and not as a student.** *Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth.* And be slow to speak, because most of the disobedience of the well-taught begins in the middle of the sermon.
- **Let one person who lives with you have the right to ask you what you did with what you learned.** Your teacher's opinion of your insight is worth very little; your household's account of your practice is worth a great deal.
- **And know that the storm does not check how much you have heard.** *The rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house.* Only the foundation that was dug deep will hold.
- **And do not add to what you owe by learning more than you intend to do.** *Unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required.* Knowledge is a ledger, and it is not a light one.

### Questions for reflection

1. What did the last sermon you heard tell you to do, and did you do it?
2. Where in your life is the gap widest between what you know and what you do, and who else knows it is there?
3. Which passage have you studied most carefully, and what has it cost you?
4. Is the preaching in your house a lovely song, or a summons?
5. What are the first works you did when you began, and are you still doing them?
