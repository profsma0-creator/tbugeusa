## The Architecture of a Generational Covenant

**And this is the last chapter of the Part that has been about construction, and its subject is the thing that holds the whole structure together, and the name for it in Scripture is covenant.**

**And the reason the word is unavoidable is that nothing else in the language of institutions survives a change of generation.** A policy can be revised. A constitution can be amended. A tradition can be quietly dropped. A mission statement can be rewritten by whoever is holding the pen, **and all four of them are the same thing: arrangements that the next generation is free to disregard and cannot be blamed for disregarding.**

**And a covenant is not that, and the difference is not the seriousness of the language. It is that a covenant binds parties who were not present when it was made.**

**And the verse that states that principle is the hinge on which this whole book turns, and it is spoken to a nation standing in the plains of Moab, at the end of forty years, having buried a whole generation in the wilderness:**

*Ye stand this day all of you before the Lord your God; your captains of your tribes, your elders, and your officers, with all the men of Israel, your little ones, your wives, and thy stranger that is in thy camp, from the hewer of thy wood unto the drawer of thy water: that thou shouldest enter into covenant with the Lord thy God, and into his oath, which the Lord thy God maketh with thee this day: that he may establish thee to day for a people unto himself, and that he may be unto thee a God, as he hath said unto thee, and as he hath sworn unto thy fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob. Neither with you only do I make this covenant and this oath; but with him that standeth here with us this day before the Lord our God, and also with him that is not here with us this day* (Deuteronomy 29:10–15).

**And also with him that is not here with us this day.**

**Which means the covenant was executed by God with people who had not been born, and stated so in the instrument, and the phrase is in the middle of a list that has already named the captains, the elders, the officers, the little ones, the wives and the woodcutters.**

**And the assembled body in that list is the whole of the nation in its order, and the last clause extends the party to everyone absent — *him that is not here with us this day* — which is to say, every generation that would ever stand in that land.**

**Which means that a generation has a right to be included in what its fathers swore, and that this is the only mechanism by which an inheritance crosses a death.**

**And the rest of this chapter is the architecture of that instrument, and it has eight components, and every one of them appears in the covenants of Scripture and is absent from most mission statements.**

### One: the parties, assembled, in one place, in their orders

**And the first element of the architecture is that the covenant is made in a gathering, and the gathering is described.**

*And Joshua gathered all the tribes of Israel to Shechem, and called for the elders of Israel, and for their heads, and for their judges, and for their officers; and they presented themselves before God* (Joshua 24:1).

**The tribes, the elders, the heads, the judges, the officers, and all of them presented before God.**

**Which means the act was corporate, public, and constituted by the ordinary leadership of the people in its full range.** And the same constitution is repeated at the other great covenants: Josiah *went up into the house of the Lord, and all the men of Judah and all the inhabitants of Jerusalem with him, and the priests, and the prophets, and all the people, both small and great* (2 Kings 23:2); and Nehemiah's instrument was sealed by *the rest of the people, the priests, the Levites, the porters, the singers, the Nethinims, and all they that had separated themselves from the people of the lands unto the law of God, their wives, their sons, and their daughters, every one having knowledge, and having understanding* (Nehemiah 10:28).

**Their wives, their sons, and their daughters, every one having knowledge and understanding.**

**Which means the young were not represented at that covenant. They were present, and they signed.**

### Two: the recital, and it comes before the terms

**And the second element is that a covenant begins with an account of what God has already done, and this is stated at length in every case in Scripture.**

**The book of Deuteronomy is a covenant document, and it opens its stipulations only after thirty chapters of recital.** And the psalm that the congregation sang was pure recital:

*We have heard with our ears, O God, our fathers have told us, what work thou didst in their days, in the times of old* (Psalm 44:1).

**And Joshua's address at Shechem recites the history from Terah to the conquest before it asks for a decision:** *and I took your father Abraham from the other side of the flood, and led him throughout all the land of Canaan* (Joshua 24:3), and so on to *and I have given you a land for which ye did not labour, and cities which ye built not* (24:13).

**And Nehemiah's covenant is preceded by the longest prayer in the Old Testament, which is a recital of the whole national history from Abraham to the captivity (Nehemiah 9:6–37), and the instrument is the direct consequence of it: *and because of all this we make a sure covenant, and write it* (9:38).**

**Which means the terms of a covenant are unintelligible without the history that produced them, and that a generation which signs a document it cannot explain has not entered into the covenant. It has initialled a policy.**

**And the practical consequence is that any covenant between the generations in a house of God must begin with a written account of what God has done in that house** — the date, the people, the events, the provision, and the failures. **And that account is what makes the terms obligatory rather than arbitrary, because the terms are the response to what God did.**

### Three: the terms, in writing, and specific

**And the third element is a written text, and the writing is not incidental.**

*And he took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that the Lord hath said will we do, and be obedient* (Exodus 24:7).

**And Joshua wrote it:**

*So Joshua made a covenant with the people that day, and set them a statute and an ordinance in Shechem. And Joshua wrote these words in the book of the law of God* (Joshua 24:25–26).

**And Nehemiah's generation wrote and sealed it:**

*And because of all this we make a sure covenant, and write it; and our princes, Levites, and priests, seal unto it* (Nehemiah 9:38).

**And the terms of the post-exilic covenant are specific to the point of tedium, and that is their virtue.** The signatories undertook not to intermarry with the peoples of the land (Nehemiah 10:30); to keep the sabbath and the sabbatical year (10:31); to charge themselves *yearly with the third part of a shekel for the service of the house of our God* (10:32); to supply the wood offering (10:34); to bring the firstfruits of their ground and of all fruit of all trees *year by year, unto the house of the Lord* (10:35); and to bring the firstborn of their sons and cattle and the first of their dough to the priests (10:36–37), **and not to forsake the house of their God (10:39).**

**Which means that a covenant is a list and not a sentiment.** It names behaviours, quantities, frequencies and obligations, **and every one of the items can be audited by a man with a ledger.**

**And the lesson for a house of God is that the architecture of a generational covenant requires a written schedule of the practices that carry the faith from one generation to the next:** what is taught, by whom, to whom, when, out of what text, at whose expense, and with what frequency. **And a document that says *we will disciple the young* has said nothing that can be broken.**

### Four: the oath, and the curse, and the sanctions

**And the fourth element is the making of the thing binding, and Scripture does not use gentle language about it.**

*That thou shouldest enter into covenant with the Lord thy God, and into his oath* (Deuteronomy 29:12).

*They clave to their brethren, their nobles, and entered into a curse, and into an oath, to walk in God's law, which was given by Moses the servant of God, and to observe and do all the commandments of the Lord our Lord, and his judgments and his statutes* (Nehemiah 10:29).

**Into a curse, and into an oath.**

**Which means the parties accepted a sanction in advance: a stated consequence for the breach, agreed before the breach, and named in the instrument.**

**And the severity of it in the Judean covenant of Asa's reign is stated without qualification:**

*And they entered into a covenant to seek the Lord God of their fathers with all their heart and with all their soul; that whosoever would not seek the Lord God of Israel should be put to death, whether small or great, whether man or woman* (2 Chronicles 15:12–13).

**And the record of the event is that the nation rejoiced in it:**

*And they sware unto the Lord with a loud voice, and with shouting, and with trumpets, and with cornets. And all Judah rejoiced at the oath: for they had sworn with all their heart, and sought him with their whole desire; and he was found of them: and the Lord gave them rest round about* (2 Chronicles 15:14–15).

**Which means the oath was a relief and not a burden, and that a generation can celebrate being bound.**

**And the practical lesson is the one that modern houses of God find hardest.** A covenant with no sanction is a preference. **And the sanction does not have to be excommunication; the ordinary sanctions of Scripture are the withdrawal of the house's commendation, the loss of the office, and the inability to be sent (1 Timothy 5:22; Titus 3:10; Matthew 18:17).** But something must be named, in writing, before it is needed, **because a house that decides what to do about a breach in the week of the breach will decide it by whoever is angriest.**

### Five: the signatures, and the names of the men who bound themselves

**And the fifth element is the most concrete and the one most houses never produce, and it is a list of ninety-one names.**

**Nehemiah's document opens with a formal subscription:** *now those that sealed were, Nehemiah, the Tirshatha, the son of Hachaliah, and Zidkijah* (Nehemiah 10:1), **and the chapter continues for twenty-six verses listing the priests, the Levites and the chiefs of the people who put their seals to it** — a signed instrument, with named parties, held in a house that any subsequent generation could consult.

**And the same practice appears in the census and the genealogical registers, where a man's standing in the house of God depended on his name being found in the record:**

*These sought their register among those that were reckoned by genealogy, but they were not found: therefore were they, as polluted, put from the priesthood* (Ezra 2:62).

**Which means that the house of God has always been a house of written records, and that a man's name in the record was a matter of standing and not of sentiment.**

**And the instruction for a modern house is direct.** A generational covenant should be signed by both generations, by name, with the date, **and the signature of a young man who is eighteen is worth more than the signature of a leader who will be gone in five years, because the young man will still be there to be held to it.**

### Six: the witnesses, and the objects that speak

**And the sixth element is that the covenant is made in the presence of witnesses, and Scripture is not embarrassed by the fact that the witnesses include inanimate objects.**

*And Joshua said unto all the people, Behold, this stone shall be a witness unto us; for it hath heard all the words of the Lord which he spake unto us: it shall be therefore a witness unto you, lest ye deny your God* (Joshua 24:27).

**Which means the covenant was attached to a visible object, in a named place, under an oak by the sanctuary, so that a generation that had never heard the speech would still be able to find the witness.**

**And the people were made to witness against themselves:** *and Joshua said unto the people, Ye are witnesses against yourselves that ye have chosen you the Lord, to serve him. And they said, We are witnesses* (24:22).

**And the last instance of this architecture in the Old Testament is a book of remembrance, and it is written for a small group of people who spoke to one another about God:**

*Then they that feared the Lord spake often one to another: and the Lord hearkened, and heard it, and a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared the Lord, and that thought upon his name* (Malachi 3:16).

**Which means that the enduring witnesses of a covenant are the ones in heaven and the ones in the archive.** *And a book of remembrance was written before him.*

**And the practical form of this for a house of God is the record that this book has already argued for in an earlier chapter, and it must be stated again here because it belongs to the architecture.** The register of the members, the minutes of the meetings, the written account of what God has done, the list of those who signed, **and the object in the building that a child can point to and ask about.**

### Seven: the renewal, which is the component that keeps the others alive

**And the seventh element is the one without which the first six decay, and it is the reason the covenants of Scripture are plural.**

**Israel did not make one covenant. It made a series of them, and the same covenant was entered again by every generation that mattered.**

**Joshua made it at Shechem (Joshua 24:25). The nation renewed it under Samuel at Mizpeh (1 Samuel 7:6). David made a league with the elders at Hebron *before the Lord* (2 Samuel 5:3). Asa renewed it in his fifteenth year with trumpets and shouting (2 Chronicles 15:12–15). Jehoiada made it in the temple in the year of the boy king's coronation (2 Chronicles 23:16). Hezekiah's reform preceded it in his own time, and Josiah made it in the eighteenth year of his reign, having just heard the book read for the first time (2 Kings 23:3). And Nehemiah's generation made it after the wall was finished, and signed it (Nehemiah 9:38; 10:1).**

**Which means that the mechanism of renewal is the ordinary defence of a house against decay, and that the space between renewals is where a generation is lost.**

**And the pattern of renewal is the same in every case, and it has four stages, and they are worth naming in order because the order matters.**

**The book is read aloud to the assembly.** Josiah *read in their ears all the words of the book of the covenant* (2 Kings 23:2); Ezra read *from the morning until midday* (Nehemiah 8:3).

**The people are told what has broken down, with the record produced.** *For great is the wrath of the Lord that is kindled against us, because our fathers have not hearkened unto the words of this book* (2 Kings 22:13).

**The covenant is made, in writing, and signed.** *And the king stood by a pillar, and made a covenant before the Lord ... and all the people stood to the covenant* (2 Kings 23:3); *and because of all this we make a sure covenant, and write it; and our princes, Levites, and priests, seal unto it* (Nehemiah 9:38).

**And the ordinary life of the house is reorganised to match.** *And the king commanded all the people, saying, Keep the passover unto the Lord your God, as it is written in the book of this covenant* (2 Kings 23:21).

**Which means renewal is not an emotional service. It is a reading, an audit, a document, and a change to the calendar.**

**And the interval is a matter for the house's own decision, but the practice of Scripture runs to a generation, not to a decade.** Moses appointed the whole law to be read *at the end of every seven years* (Deuteronomy 31:10), and the history of Israel records that this was neglected and that the neglect is exactly what produced the generation that *knew not the Lord* (Judges 2:10). **And a house which has not tested its covenant in writing within the memory of its oldest member has no evidence that it still holds one.**

### Eight: the clause that carries it forward

**And the eighth component is the one that makes the instrument a generational covenant rather than an agreement between living men, and it is two sentences in the same chapter of Deuteronomy.**

**The first is the clause at the end of the parties:**

*But with him that standeth here with us this day before the Lord our God, and also with him that is not here with us this day* (Deuteronomy 29:15).

**And the second is the sentence about revelation that follows the whole instrument, and it states the principle of limitation:**

*The secret things belong unto the Lord our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law* (Deuteronomy 29:29).

**Unto us and to our children for ever.**

**Which means the revealed word is the property of two parties and not one, and that the purpose clause is *that we may do all the words of this law* — which is to say, the inheritance exists so that obedience may be possible for a generation that was not present.**

**And the same principle is stated in the promise to Abraham, and it is stated in a vocabulary of duration that exceeds every human institution:**

*Know therefore that the Lord thy God, he is God, the faithful God, which keepeth covenant and mercy with them that love him and keep his commandments to a thousand generations* (Deuteronomy 7:9).

**To a thousand generations.**

**And it is stated again in the psalm that the returned exiles sang:**

*He hath remembered his covenant for ever, the word which he commanded to a thousand generations* (Psalm 105:8).

**And the psalm of David's last words contains the phrase that should be carved over every house of God that intends to transmit:**

*Although my house be not so with God; yet he hath made with me an everlasting covenant, ordered in all things, and sure: for this is all my salvation, and all my desire, although he make it not to grow* (2 Samuel 23:5).

**Ordered in all things, and sure.**

**Which is the phrase that describes an architecture.** Not vague, not improvised, not depending on the character of the men who happen to be present: **ordered in all things, and sure.**

**And the reason a covenant can be ordered and sure is not the quality of the men who sign it, and David said so in the same sentence: *although my house be not so with God.***

**Which means the security of the covenant is not in the house. It is in the God who made it, and the whole of the New Testament is the announcement that the covenant is now administered by a mediator who does not die:**

*But now hath he obtained a more excellent ministry, by how much also he is the mediator of a better covenant, which was established upon better promises* (Hebrews 8:6).

**A better covenant, established upon better promises.**

**And the reason it is better, and the reason a house of God can hold an inheritance across generations at all, is stated in the sentence that closes the covenant promise of Jeremiah, and it is a change in the location of the document:**

*But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the Lord, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people ... for they shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest of them, saith the Lord: for I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more* (Jeremiah 31:33–34).

**Which means that the architecture of the new covenant is a writing on hearts rather than on stones, and that the promise is *they shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest of them.***

**From the least to the greatest. Which is the promise that abolishes the distinction between the two generations, and it is the answer to every chapter in this book.**

### And what a house must actually do

**And the whole architecture reduces to eight acts, and a house of God can begin them in a year, and none of them requires money.**

**Write the recital.** An account of what God has done in this house, with dates, names, provisions and failures, **read aloud to the whole congregation once a year.**

**Write the terms, and make them specific.** What will be taught, by whom, to whom, out of what text, at what frequency, and at whose cost. **A list of practices with numbers attaches to it, and a list of aspirations does not.**

**Name the sanctions, before they are needed.** What happens if a man teaching the young departs from the deposit; what happens if a signatory leaves; what the house will do about a breach. **And write it while nobody is angry.**

**Sign it, both generations, by name, with the date.** The young men's signatures are the ones that will be producing evidence in thirty years.

**Set the witnesses, and keep them.** A record in writing, a register of members, the minutes, the names of those who signed, **and a thing in the building that a child can point at and ask about, as the stones at Gilgal were.**

**Renew it on a fixed cycle, and renew it in the four stages of Scripture.** The book read aloud, the audit of what has broken down, the covenant made and signed again, and the calendar rearranged to match. **Do not renew it as a service of sentiment; renew it as an audit with the document on the table.**

**Append the clause, in the words of Deuteronomy 29:15, and mean it.** The document governs a generation that is not in the room and was not consulted; **and the house must therefore write its covenant in the expectation that the men who will read it do not yet exist.**

**And put the thing in the hearts, because the writing on the stones is a witness and not a substitute.** *I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts.*

**Which is the last word of this Part and the foundation of the next.** Every structure in this book — the household, the school, the assembly, the institution, the table and the covenant — is a means to an end, and the end is stated in a sentence of Jeremiah's that would be quoted by the Lord's apostles and by the author of Hebrews as the summary of the age in which we live.

**They shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest of them.**

**And a father who has built the architecture and not secured that is a man who has built a house and forgotten the fire.**

### Principles

**First: a covenant is the only instrument in Scripture that binds parties who were not present when it was made.** *Neither with you only do I make this covenant and this oath; but with him that standeth here with us this day before the Lord our God, and also with him that is not here with us this day.*

**Second: the parties are assembled in their orders, and the young are present and counted.** *Their wives, their sons, and their daughters, every one having knowledge, and having understanding.*

**And third: the recital comes before the terms, and it is long.** *And because of all this we make a sure covenant, and write it.* A generation that signs what it cannot explain has initialled a policy.

**And fourth: the terms are written, specific, and auditable to the last measure.** The third part of a shekel yearly; the wood offering; the firstfruits year by year; the firstborn of sons and cattle. *And to bring the firstfruits of our ground ... year by year, unto the house of the Lord.*

**And fifth: the covenant is made with an oath and a curse, and the parties accept a sanction in advance.** *They clave to their brethren, their nobles, and entered into a curse, and into an oath.* And *they sware unto the Lord with a loud voice ... and all Judah rejoiced at the oath.*

**Sixth: the instrument is signed by name, and the names are preserved.** *Now those that sealed were, Nehemiah, the Tirshatha, the son of Hachaliah, and Zidkijah* — and twenty-six further verses of names.

**And seventh: the record is a matter of standing and not of sentiment.** *These sought their register among those that were reckoned by genealogy, but they were not found: therefore were they, as polluted, put from the priesthood.*

**Eighth: the covenant is attached to visible witnesses, including objects and a written book.** *Behold, this stone shall be a witness unto us*; and *a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared the Lord.*

**And ninth: the covenant decays within the span of one generation and must be renewed, and the renewal has four stages: the reading, the audit, the writing and signing, and the reorganisation of the calendar.** *And the king read in their ears all the words of the book of the covenant ... And all the people stood to the covenant.*

**And tenth: the revealed word belongs to two parties and not one, and it is given so that obedience may be possible for a generation that was not present.** *But those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law.*

**And eleventh: the duration of the covenant exceeds every human institution, and the measure of it is a thousand generations.** *The faithful God, which keepeth covenant and mercy with them that love him and keep his commandments to a thousand generations.*

**And twelfth: the security of the covenant is not in the house that signs it.** *Although my house be not so with God; yet he hath made with me an everlasting covenant, ordered in all things, and sure.*

**And thirteenth: the new covenant's architecture is a writing on hearts, and its promise abolishes the difference between the generations.** *I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts ... and they shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest of them.*

### For the fathers

- **Write the recital before you write the terms.** A covenant that does not begin with what God has done in the house will be read as an administrative requirement.
- **Make the terms auditable.** Numbers, frequencies, names and costs, in the manner of Nehemiah's third part of a shekel and the wood offering and the firstfruits year by year.
- **Name the sanctions while nobody is angry.** A covenant with no consequence attached is a preference, and preferences are dropped by the second generation without anybody noticing.
- **Put the young men's signatures on it, by name, with the date.** And keep the document where a man who has not been born can find it.
- **Set a witness in the building that a child can point at, and keep the written record beside it.** *Behold, this stone shall be a witness unto us ... lest ye deny your God.*
- **And renew the covenant on a fixed cycle, in the four stages: the reading, the audit, the writing and signing, and the reorganisation of the calendar.**

### For the young

- **Ask to see the document before you sign it, and ask what it will cost you according to a number.** A covenant that cannot be costed is a speech.
- **Add your name to the record and let it be dated.** *Now those that sealed were, Nehemiah ... and Zidkijah.* A house that keeps a register will still be able to identify you in fifty years.
- **Be one of the men who speak often one to another.** *Then they that feared the Lord spake often one to another ... and a book of remembrance was written before him.* That sentence describes a small group of people and not a congregation.
- **And know which part of the inheritance you are holding as a written document and which part you are holding as a habit.** The first can be handed on by a stranger to his successor; the second dies with the man who has it.
- **And make your own covenant with the man who is coming after you, as Jonathan did with David.** *The Lord be between me and thee, and between my seed and thy seed for ever.* Which means the covenant is not with your own generation only, and that the clause you write will bind children who are not yet born.
- **And remember where the writing finally goes.** *I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts.* The stones are a witness, and the register is a record, and the covenant is kept by the Spirit of God in the heart of a man — **and a house that has the document and not the heart has preserved the architecture of a ruin.**

### Questions for reflection

1. Does your house have a written covenant at all, and if it does, when was it last read aloud to the assembly?
2. What does your covenant say that could be audited — a number, a frequency, a cost, a name?
3. Who signed it, and are the signatures dated?
4. What is the witness in your building that a child can point at and ask about?
5. What would have to be written into your house's covenant so that it would still oblige your grandchildren, who are not here with us this day?
