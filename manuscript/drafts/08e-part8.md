## The Sons and Daughters Who Prophesy

**And now the promise that governs the entire subject of this Part, and that was given through a prophet and claimed by an apostle on the day the Spirit came.**

**The question this chapter answers is not whether the young receive revelation. That has been settled in every previous chapter. The question is who, precisely, is named in the promise, and the answer is more inclusive than most generations have been willing to read aloud.**

*And it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh; and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, your young men shall see visions: and also upon the servants and upon the handmaids in those days will I pour out my spirit* (Joel 2:28–29).

**And on the day of Pentecost, three weeks after the ascension, Peter stood up with the eleven and told the assembled thousands of every nation under heaven that this was now fulfilled:**

*But this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel; and it shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams: and on my servants and on my handmaidens I will pour out in those days of my Spirit; and they shall prophesy* (Acts 2:16–18).

### Four categories and no distinction of rank

**The promise names four pairs, and the pairs are exhaustive of the divisions that had determined who could speak in the religious assemblies of the ancient world.**

**Sons and daughters.** Both sexes.

**Young men and old men.** Both ends of the age range, with a distinct mode of revelation assigned to each.

**Servants and handmaidens.** Both sexes of the slave class, over the specific word *handmaidens* — the female slaves, named by the same preposition and given the same verb: *they shall prophesy.*

**And *all flesh*** — the phrase that opens the promise and shuts every door against a restricted reading.

**Which means that the outpouring of the Spirit is the single most generational event in Scripture, because it distributes the gift of revelation across the very boundaries that the world uses to organise authority — age, sex, and rank — and it does so in a single sentence.**

**And the theological reason is stated by the Spirit through Peter in the phrase *the last days*.** The promise is the characteristic mark of the age between the ascension and the return. **Which means that a generation which restricts the speaking gift to one age or one sex or one class is not merely being conservative; it is refusing the defining gift of its own dispensation.**

**And note carefully that the old are not expelled by the promise.** *Your old men shall dream dreams.* The young receive visions and the old receive dreams, **and the two modes belong to the same Spirit and the same outpouring.** Which is the balance this whole book has argued for: **the promise is not that the young will replace the old but that both will speak.**

**And there is a second detail in Luke's account that is easy to miss and which is the practical proof of Peter's claim.** When the hundred and twenty gathered in the upper room after the ascension, Luke lists them: the eleven, **and *the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren* (Acts 1:14).** And the Spirit fell on that company.

**Which means the church that received the promised outpouring was a mixed-age, mixed-sex assembly, and the prophecy Peter quoted was fulfilled first in women and old men and the Lord's own brothers.**

### Five sisters who changed the law of inheritance

**And the Old Testament contains a case that settles the question of the daughters' inheritance in the most formal way possible — by statute, argued before the highest court in Israel, and decided by God Himself.**

**The setting is the census and the coming division of the land. And a man of the tribe of Manasseh had died in the wilderness, leaving five daughters and no sons.**

*Then came the daughters of Zelophehad, the son of Hepher, the son of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh, of the families of Manasseh the son of Joseph: and these are the names of his daughters; Mahlah, Noah, and Hoglah, and Milcah, and Tirzah. And they stood before Moses, and before Eleazar the priest, and before the princes and all the congregation, by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, saying, Our father died in the wilderness, and he was not in the company of them that gathered themselves together against the Lord in the company of Korah; but died in his own sin, and had no sons. Why should the name of our father be done away from among his family, because he hath no son? Give unto us therefore a possession among the brethren of our father* (Numbers 27:1–4).

**Take the elements of that petition in order, because they form a model of how a claim is made in the house of God.**

**They came to the right place.** *By the door of the tabernacle of the congregation* — the seat of judgement, where Moses and Eleazar and the princes sat.

**They came with a case and not a complaint.** Their argument is legal: their father was not among Korah's rebels; he died for his own sin, which is the common lot; **and the inheritance law as it stood would extinguish his name in his family.**

**And they came with the generational argument, which is the heart of the petition: *why should the name of our father be done away from among his family, because he hath no son?***

**They were not asking for property for its own sake. They were asking that their father's name should not disappear from Israel.** Which is a claim about continuity, and it is the reason this case appears in this chapter.

**And the procedure of the court is stated in a single verse: *And Moses brought their cause before the Lord* (27:5).**

**Moses did not rule. He did not tell the women that the law was clear and the matter settled. He took the case to God, and God gave a ruling that altered the law of inheritance for the whole nation:**

*And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying, The daughters of Zelophehad speak right: thou shalt surely give them a possession of an inheritance among their father's brethren; and thou shalt cause the inheritance of their father to pass unto them. And thou shalt speak unto the children of Israel, saying, If a man die, and have no son, then ye shall cause his inheritance to pass unto his daughter. And if he have no daughter, then ye shall give his inheritance unto his brethren* (Numbers 27:6–9).

**The daughters of Zelophehad speak right.**

**And the ruling is not a concession to five women. It is a statute of judgment for the whole nation, and it establishes a chain of descent that runs daughter, brother, father's brother, next kinsman (27:8–11).**

**Which means the inheritance of Israel was extended to daughters by the explicit ruling of God on the petition of five young women, and the extension became case law.**

**And the sequel makes the case even more instructive, because the ruling produced a second problem and a second ruling.**

**If a daughter who inherited married into another tribe, the land would move with her, and the tribal allocations would be disturbed within a generation. And the heads of the family of Gilead brought that objection to Moses (Numbers 36:1–4), and Moses ruled on the word of God:**

*This is the thing which the Lord doth command concerning the daughters of Zelophehad, saying, Let them marry to whom they think best; only to the family of the tribe of their father shall they marry ... And every daughter, that possesseth an inheritance in any tribe of the children of Israel, shall be wife unto one of the family of the tribe of her father, that the children of Israel may enjoy every man the inheritance of his fathers* (Numbers 36:6–9).

**So the freedom is real and it is bounded.** *Let them marry to whom they think best* — the choice is theirs — **and the inheritance must stay in the tribe, and the constraint is explained by the principle *that the children of Israel may enjoy every man the inheritance of his fathers.***

**Which is how a house of God should handle the tension between an individual's inheritance and the continuity of the whole.** The right of the daughters was upheld; the integrity of the tribal allocation was upheld; **and neither was sacrificed to the other, and the ruling came from God in both cases.**

**And the final proof that this was not theory is recorded forty years later, when the land was actually divided:**

*But Zelophehad, the son of Hepher, the son of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh, had no sons, but daughters: and these are the names of his daughters, Mahlah, and Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. And they came near before Eleazar the priest, and before Joshua the son of Nun, and before the princes, saying, The Lord commanded Moses to give us an inheritance among our brethren. Therefore according to the commandment of the Lord he gave them an inheritance among the brethren of their father* (Joshua 17:3–4).

**Two generations, and the promise was executed, and the record names the daughters and the officials who gave them the land.**

**Which means that a case decided in the wilderness was honoured on the other side of the Jordan by a different leader.** And the names of Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah are in the census book, the legal code, and the land record.

### The women who spoke in Israel

**And the women of the Old Testament who carried the word of God are not an exception noted with an apology. They are the ordinary functioning of the covenant, and the record names them without defence.**

**Miriam was a prophetess, and she led the women of Israel in the song at the sea:**

*And Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a timbrel in her hand; and all the women went out after her with timbrels and with dances. And Miriam answered them, Sing ye to the Lord, for he hath triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea* (Exodus 15:20–21).

**And four hundred years later God still named her as one of the three leaders of the exodus:**

*For I brought thee up out of the land of Egypt, and redeemed thee out of the house of servants; and I sent before thee Moses, Aaron, and Miriam* (Micah 6:4).

**A prophetess, a sister of the high priest, and one of the three sent before the people — and named by God in the same sentence as the lawgiver and the priest.**

**And Miriam's failure is recorded too, and it must be.** She and Aaron spoke against Moses' authority and claimed equal revelation: *Hath the Lord indeed spoken only by Moses? hath he not spoken also by us?* (Numbers 12:2). **And God answered by distinguishing the ordinary prophetic mode from the mode of Moses:**

*And he said, Hear now my words: If there be a prophet among you, I the Lord will make myself known unto him in a vision, and will speak unto him in a dream. My servant Moses is not so, who is faithful in all mine house. With him will I speak mouth to mouth, even apparently, and not in dark speeches; and the similitude of the Lord shall he behold: wherefore then were ye not afraid to speak against my servant Moses?* (Numbers 12:6–8).

**Which gives us a doctrine of enormous importance for this whole book, and it cuts across every generation.**

**Visions and dreams are the ordinary prophetic mode. Mouth-to-mouth is the mode of the mediator.** Which means a young man or a prophetess with a dream has not therefore been given the authority of the man who speaks face to face with God, **and the fact of a genuine revelation does not by itself confer the office.**

**Deborah judged Israel, and she was a prophetess, and the record states the facts in a single line without any editorial comment on the novelty of the arrangement:**

*And Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lapidoth, she judged Israel at that time. And she dwelt under the palm tree of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in mount Ephraim: and the children of Israel came up to her for judgment* (Judges 4:4–5).

**And when she summoned the general, he refused to go without her, and the honour of the victory was transferred by her own word:**

*And Barak said unto her, If thou wilt go with me, then I will go: but if thou wilt not go with me, then I will not go. And she said, I will surely go with thee: notwithstanding the journey that thou takest shall not be for thine honour; for the Lord shall sell Sisera into the hand of a woman* (Judges 4:8–9).

**And in her song she described her own function in the language of the household: *until that I Deborah arose, that I arose a mother in Israel* (Judges 5:7).**

**A mother in Israel.**

**Which is the most accurate description of the function in this whole book.** She was not a queen and not a commander. **She was a mother to a nation, and she held the word of God for a generation that had no father in the land.**

**And Huldah the prophetess was consulted by the high priest and the secretary of state, and answered them with the word of God about the fate of the nation; and her answer was canonical and was obeyed.**

**And Anna, in the temple, is the last prophet of the old dispensation, and she was a widow of about eighty-four, and she was in the temple when the child was brought in:**

*And there was one Anna, a prophetess, the daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of Aser: she was of a great age, and had lived with an husband seven years from her virginity; and she was a widow of about fourscore and four years, which departed not from the temple, but served God with fastings and prayers night and day. And she coming in that instant gave thanks likewise unto the Lord, and spake of him to all them that looked for redemption in Jerusalem* (Luke 2:36–38).

**Note the structure of her ministry: worship with fastings and prayers night and day, and speech about the child to everybody in Jerusalem who was waiting for redemption.**

**Which means the aged prophetess was the person in the temple whose business it was to tell the waiting people what God had done — and she did it to the generation that was already looking.**

**And note that the first person to speak publicly about the identity of Jesus in the temple was an eighty-four-year-old widow, and the second was an old man, Simeon, who asked to depart in peace (Luke 2:29).** The two witnesses of the Messiah's presentation in the house of God were the two oldest people in the building.

**And Mary's song must be placed here, because it is the song of a young woman and it is a song about generations:**

*And Mary said, My soul doth magnify the Lord, and my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour. For he hath regarded the low estate of his handmaiden: for, behold, from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed. For he that is mighty hath done to me great things; and holy is his name. And his mercy is on them that fear him from generation to generation* (Luke 1:46–50).

**A girl of perhaps fourteen or fifteen, singing that all generations would call her blessed, and that the mercy of God runs from generation to generation.**

**And the song she sang is built on the song of Hannah, the mother of Samuel, sung a thousand years earlier:**

*And Hannah prayed, and said, My heart rejoiceth in the Lord, mine horn is exalted in the Lord: my mouth is enlarged over mine enemies; because I rejoice in thy salvation. There is none holy as the Lord: for there is none beside thee: neither is there any rock like our God* (1 Samuel 2:1–2).

**Which means that the young woman's Magnificat is a quotation of a mother's prayer, and that the deposit of praise moved from a barren woman in Shiloh to a virgin in Nazareth across a thousand years.** And the son of the first woman anointed kings, and the son of the second was the King.

### The daughters who prophesied in the New Testament

**And in the New Testament the practice is stated in the same factual manner as in the Old, and the Church's earliest generations did not treat it as a problem.**

**Philip the evangelist, one of the seven men chosen to serve tables in Acts 6, had four unmarried daughters, and Luke records them in one clause:**

*And the same man had four daughters, virgins, which did prophesy* (Acts 21:9).

**The verse arrives in the middle of an itinerary.** Paul and his company are travelling to Jerusalem; they lodge with Philip at Caesarea; **and Luke notes as a matter of ordinary information that the household included four young women who exercised a prophetic gift.**

**And no controversy follows.** The next verses record a prophet named Agabus who took Paul's girdle and bound his own hands and foretold the imprisonment (21:10–11). **Which means a household with four prophesying daughters was a normal stop on an apostolic journey, and Luke records it in eight words.**

**And the apostle's own instructions assume the practice:**

*But every woman that prayeth or prophesieth with her head uncovered dishonoureth her head* (1 Corinthians 11:5).

**The sentence does not establish whether women may prophesy. It presupposes that they do, and regulates the manner.** Which is a distinction that has been lost in many generations of argument: **Paul was not legislating a permission but ordering an existing practice in the assembly at Corinth.**

**And the governing criterion for the whole assembly's speaking is stated three chapters later, and it is not the age or the sex of the speaker:**

*For ye may all prophesy one by one, that all may learn, and all may be comforted* (1 Corinthians 14:31).

**All may prophesy, one by one, and the purpose is that all may learn and all be comforted.** Which is the standard by which every speaking gift in the assembly is to be measured: **does it instruct and console the whole congregation, and is it offered in order and one at a time?**

**And the same principle is stated in the promise of the Spirit's distribution of gifts to *every man* as He wills (1 Corinthians 12:7–11), and in the flat declaration to the Galatians:**

*For ye are all the children of God by faith in Christ Jesus. For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus* (Galatians 3:26–28).

**Jew and Greek, bond and free, male and female.**

**Which is the exact list of the categories that had determined who belonged to the assembly of Israel — and the three that Joel's promise had specifically opened for the Spirit's outpouring.**

**Which means the promise and the epistle agree, and both announce the same three-way inclusion, and the church's task in every generation is to learn to live in it.**

### The women in the apostolic record of labour

**And the last chapter of Romans is the church's earliest staff record, and it is composed largely of names that most congregations have never read out.**

**Phoebe, described with a word used elsewhere of ministers:**

*I commend unto you Phebe our sister, which is a servant of the church which is at Cenchrea: that ye receive her in the Lord, as becometh saints, and that ye assist her in whatsoever business she hath need of you: for she hath been a succourer of many, and of myself also* (Romans 16:1–2).

**She is commended, she is to be received formally, and the congregation is instructed to assist her in her business.** Which is the language of a delegate carrying a letter, and probably the very letter to the Romans.

**Priscilla, who with Aquila took Apollos aside and expounded the way of God to him more perfectly (Acts 18:26).** Which means a woman, with her husband, instructed an eloquent preacher in the doctrine of Christ, **and Luke records it without a word of comment.**

**Mary, and Tryphena, and Tryphosa, and Persis:**

*Greet Mary, who bestowed much labour on us ... Salute Tryphena and Tryphosa, who labour in the Lord. Salute the beloved Persis, which laboured much in the Lord* (Romans 16:6, 12).

**And Junia, of note among the apostles:**

*Salute Andronicus and Junia, my kinsmen, and my fellowprisoners, who are of note among the apostles, who also were in Christ before me* (Romans 16:7).

**Note that the last clause names the criterion: *who also were in Christ before me.*** They were older in the faith than the apostle of the Gentiles, **and they are named as being of note among the apostles, and Paul's own seniority was by no means the oldest in the room.**

**Which is a reminder for every young leader who arrives at a work with a commission and a revelation.** In every congregation there are believers who were in Christ before you were, **and their names are in the record, and the record is not written by the leaders.**

### The aged women, and the household transmission

**And the New Testament assigns the preservation of the household deposit to older women by name and by task, and it attaches the highest possible stake to the assignment.**

*The aged women likewise, that they be in behaviour as becometh holiness, not false accusers, not given to much wine, teachers of good things; that they may teach the young women to be sober, to love their husbands, to love their children, to be discreet, chaste, keepers at home, good, obedient to their own husbands, that the word of God be not blasphemed* (Titus 2:3–5).

**The instruction is addressed to Titus, an apostolic delegate, about the internal organisation of the churches.** And the older women are given a formal teaching assignment with respect to the younger women, **and the purpose clause is that the word of God be not blasphemed.**

**Which means the reputation of the whole Christian message in the eyes of a watching society was made to depend on whether the older women of the churches were teaching the younger ones.**

**And the method of the teaching is specified by example rather than by lecture.** The list is dominated by conduct: sober, loving, discreet, chaste, domestic, good, subject to their own husbands. **Which means the older woman's curriculum is her own household, demonstrated, and reproduced.**

**And Paul's second letter to Timothy gives the case study and the chain:**

*When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in thee, which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice; and I am persuaded that in thee also* (2 Timothy 1:5).

*And that from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures* (2 Timothy 3:15).

**The unfeigned faith came first to the grandmother, then to the mother, and then to the man who would receive two apostolic letters and lead the church at Ephesus.**

**And note the household situation, because it matters. Timothy's father was a Greek (Acts 16:1), and the name is Greek — so the first carrier of the deposit in that home was Lois, and the second was Eunice, and the third was Timothy, and the three of them constituted a chain across three generations in which the believing transmission of Scripture ran through the women.**

**Which is not a demotion of fathers. It is the recognition of what God did in a house where the father was not the carrier, and it is the assurance to every household in the same position.**

**And the verse that closes the argument is the one that must be set over every conversation about the place of women in the generational work:**

*Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered* (1 Peter 3:7).

**Heirs together of the grace of life.**

**And the sanction is stated with unusual sharpness: *that your prayers be not hindered.***

**Which means a house in which the daughters' gift is suppressed, or the wife's inheritance is treated as secondary, or the aged women's teaching is dismissed as women's business, has a prayer problem and does not know it.**

**And that is the connection to the subject of this whole Part.** A generational work requires every vessel in the house to be functioning, **and a generation that halves the workforce and blocks the witnesses has no right to complain of a shortage of labourers.**

### The company of those that published it

**And there is a single verse in the sixty-eighth psalm that gathers the whole of this chapter, and it is one of the oldest processional verses in Scripture.**

*The Lord gave the word: great was the company of those that published it* (Psalm 68:11).

**The psalm is the song of the ascent of the ark, and verse 11 is its pivot: God gives the word, and those who publish it are a great host.**

**And the Hebrew participle for *those that published it* is feminine plural.**

**Which means the grammar of the verse is describing a great host of women proclaiming the word that God gave, and the psalm places them immediately after the mention of the Lord giving the word and before the scattering of kings.**

**Which is exactly the pattern of Exodus 15, where the word of the victory was published by Miriam and the women with their timbrels while Moses led the men; and it is the pattern of Psalm 68:25, where in the same psalm the singers go before and the players on instruments follow, *among them the damsels playing with timbrels.***

**The word of God is given, and the women publish it, and the psalm makes that a matter of liturgical fact.**

### And how a house of God handles it

**And the instruction of this chapter, reduced to practice, has five parts, and they are all plain.**

**First: the promise names the daughters, and a house that does not teach its daughters to hear the voice of God is not training half its children.** *Your sons and your daughters shall prophesy.* A daughter who is taught to be silent in the presence of God is being trained for something other than the covenant.

**Second: the young women who can prophesy must be recognised and given room, and the older women must be given the formal assignment of teaching them.** *That they may teach the young women.* Which is an appointment, and it must be made and honoured in the assembly and not only in the kitchen.

**Third: the elder men and the aged women of a congregation have work that nobody else can do.** Anna in the temple, speaking of the child to all who looked for redemption; Simeon, who was ready to depart because he had seen the salvation. **The old in a house of God are not to be politely accommodated; they are to be stationed where their sight of the end and their memory of the beginning can be heard.**

**Fourth: the manner of the speaking is the church's concern, and the fact of it is not.** Paul regulated the head covering and the order and the turn-taking; **he never treated the woman's prayer or prophecy as the problem.** A congregation that gets the manner right and the participation wrong has inverted the apostolic priority.

**And fifth: the honour given to the women of the house is the condition of the house's prayers.** *That your prayers be not hindered.* Which means the question of how a household treats its wife and its daughters is not a social question that has been imported into the church from the culture. **It is a condition of access to God, stated by an apostle, with the consequence attached.**

**And the closing observation is the one that gives this chapter its place in the Part.** The generation that will receive what the fathers could only see is not half a generation. **It is sons and daughters, young men and old men, servants and handmaidens — and the promise was pronounced with all six names in it before any of them had a gift to exercise.**

### Principles

**First: the outpouring of the Spirit names every category that the world uses to restrict authority.** *Your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your young men shall see visions, your old men shall dream dreams ... and upon the handmaids in those days will I pour out my spirit.*

**Second: the promise is the defining mark of the last days, so a generation that ignores it is refusing its own dispensation.** *But this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel ... in the last days, saith God.*

**Third: the old are not displaced by the promise.** *Your old men shall dream dreams.* The same Spirit is given to both ends of the age range, with different modes.

**Fourth: an inheritance for daughters was established by God's own ruling on the petition of five sisters, and it became case law.** *The daughters of Zelophehad speak right: thou shalt surely give them a possession of an inheritance.* And the ruling was executed forty years later by Joshua and Eleazar.

**And fifth: a right ruling can carry a legitimate constraint, and both came from God.** *Let them marry to whom they think best; only to the family of the tribe of their father shall they marry*, so that *every man* might enjoy *the inheritance of his fathers.*

**Sixth: the ordinary prophetic mode is vision and dream, and the mode of the mediator was mouth to mouth.** *If there be a prophet among you, I the Lord will make myself known unto him in a vision ... My servant Moses is not so.* A genuine revelation does not confer an office.

**And seventh: the women who held the word of God in Israel are named without apology.** Miriam the prophetess, one of the three sent before the people; Deborah, who judged Israel and was *a mother in Israel*; Huldah, whose word the high priest obeyed; Anna, the eighty-four-year-old widow who spoke of the child to all who looked for redemption.

**Eighth: Mary's song of the generations rests on Hannah's prayer of a thousand years earlier.** The deposit of praise crossed ten centuries from a mother to a virgin.

**Ninth: the practice in the apostolic church is stated as a fact and regulated in its manner.** *And the same man had four daughters, virgins, which did prophesy*; *every woman that prayeth or prophesieth*; *ye may all prophesy one by one, that all may learn.*

**And tenth: the transmission of the faith in the household is assigned to the aged women, and the honour given to a wife and a daughter is the condition of a household's prayers.** *That they may teach the young women* ... *heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.*

### For the fathers

- **Read the promise aloud with all six names in it.** Sons, daughters, young men, old men, servants, handmaidens. **A household that trains only its sons to hear God has halved its own inheritance.**
- **Give the aged women their assignment formally and honour it publicly.** *That they may teach the young women* is an appointment with an apostolic warrant, and the stake attached to it is that *the word of God be not blasphemed.*
- **Do not let your own record become the ceiling of the house.** Miriam was named by God beside Moses; Deborah judged while Barak commanded; Paul was younger in Christ than Andronicus and Junia.
- **Stay in the temple like Anna, praying and speaking to those who look for redemption.** The oldest believers belong where the waiting people gather.
- **And honour your wife as an heir together of the grace of life.** *That your prayers be not hindered* is a stated consequence, and it is attached to how you treat her.

### For the young

- **Daughters, the promise names you, and it was pronounced before you were born.** *Your daughters shall prophesy.* Ask God for the gift and learn to handle it in order.
- **Bring your case with an argument, not a complaint, and bring it to the right place.** *Why should the name of our father be done away from among his family?* And *Moses brought their cause before the Lord.*
- **Expect the ruling to be honoured, and be ready to wait decades for it.** Five sisters were given a statute in the wilderness and received the land under Joshua forty years later.
- **Do not confuse the gift with the office.** *If there be a prophet among you, I the Lord will make myself known unto him in a vision ... My servant Moses is not so.* A true dream is not a commission to lead.
- **Exercise the gift in order and for the instruction of the whole assembly.** *Ye may all prophesy one by one, that all may learn, and all may be comforted.* The purpose clause is the test of the gift.
- **And if you are a young woman in a house that gives you no room, know that God's record of the women who carried His word is long, and that your name is not the first He has written into it.**

### Questions for reflection

1. Does your congregation know the whole of Joel 2:28–29 and Acts 2:17–18, or only the half of it that concerns young men?
2. Where in your house is the aged woman's teaching assignment, and who is doing it?
3. Which daughters have been given an inheritance in your assembly — a name, a place, and a work — and which are still waiting at the door of the tabernacle?
4. Whose case has been brought to God lately, or have the rulings been made by the men in the room?
5. What would change in your prayers if you took the honour of the women in your house as a condition of them?
