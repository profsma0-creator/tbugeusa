## Young Prophets in an Aging Institution

**And now the case that recurs more often than any other in the history of the people of God, and that is the subject of this chapter: a young man with a living word inside an institution that has grown old.**

**The line of Scripture here is extremely long.** Samuel in the failing house of Eli. David in the court of a rejected king. The young man of God who confronted the altar at Bethel and was destroyed by an old prophet's lie. Amos, who came from the sycomore trees and was told to go home. Jeremiah, who said *I am a child* and preached for forty years to a nation that would not hear. Daniel, taken into Babylon as a boy and examined by a heathen university. And in the New Testament, Timothy, who was told not to let anyone despise his youth.

**And the condition of the institution in every one of those cases is described in a single verse from the book of Samuel, and it is the sentence this chapter is built on:**

*And the child Samuel ministered unto the Lord before Eli. And the word of the Lord was precious in those days; there was no open vision* (1 Samuel 3:1).

**Which is a description of a shortage.** The word of God was rare, and there was no vision being given out, **and in the middle of that shortage a boy was ministering at a lamp that had not yet gone out.**

### The institution at Shiloh

**And the condition of the institution at Shiloh is set out in the early chapters of 1 Samuel without any softening.**

**There was a high priest, Eli, who had held the office for decades.** There was a functioning sanctuary with an ark, a lamp, a priesthood, courses of worship, and an annual pilgrimage of the whole nation. **There was a man at Shiloh who had judged Israel for forty years (4:18).**

**And there were two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, priests by inheritance, and the record of their conduct is one of the ugliest in Scripture.** They took the flesh of the sacrifices by force before the fat was burnt (2:13–16); they lay with the women who assembled at the door of the tabernacle (2:22); **and the verdict on their father is the sentence that every leader of a family or a house of God should read with care:**

*For I have told him that I will judge his house for ever for the iniquity which he knoweth; because his sons made themselves vile, and he restrained them not* (1 Samuel 3:13).

**He restrained them not.**

**Not: he taught them nothing. Not: he did not believe in the office. The charge is that he knew and did not restrain.**

**And the judgement pronounced on the institution for that failure is profound:**

*Wherefore the Lord God of Israel saith, I said indeed that thy house, and the house of thy father, should walk before me for ever: but now the Lord saith, Be it far from me; for them that honour me I will honour, and they that despise me shall be lightly esteemed* (1 Samuel 2:30).

**A perpetual promise was cancelled.**

**Which means that an institution's inheritance is not guaranteed by its institutional continuity.** A house may hold the ark, the lamp, the altar and the priesthood, hold them for four hundred years, **and lose the whole commission in a single generation whose father would not restrain his sons.**

**And into that situation, and not into a healthy one, God brought a young prophet.**

**And the manner of the calling is one of the most tender passages in the Old Testament, and every detail of it is instructive.**

*Now Samuel did not yet know the Lord, neither was the word of the Lord yet revealed unto him. And the Lord called Samuel again the third time. And he arose and went to Eli, and said, Here am I; for thou didst call me. And Eli perceived that the Lord had called the child. Therefore Eli said unto Samuel, Go, lie down: and it shall be, if he call thee, that thou shalt say, Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth* (1 Samuel 3:7–9).

**Three observations.**

**The first is that the boy did not recognise the voice of God, and his mistake was to attribute it to the old man.** *He arose and went to Eli.* **Which is exactly what a young man in a house of God does with an unfamiliar call: he assumes it must belong to the established structure, because that is the only voice he has learned to hear.**

**And the second is that Eli, though under sentence, gave him the right instruction.** *Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth.* **The old man in decline taught the young man the words of response, and he taught them accurately.** There is no self-interest in that sentence, and no attempt to keep the access to himself.

**Which is the single most important thing an older generation can do for a younger one in a failing house.** It cannot pass on its own virtue. **It can still pass on the vocabulary of obedience, and Eli did.**

**And the third is that the revelation was given to the boy and not to the priest, and the content of it was the judgement of the priest's house.** *And the Lord said to Samuel, Behold, I will do a thing in Israel, at which both the ears of every one that heareth it shall tingle* (3:11).

**Which means the young man was given the message that the old man's institution was finished, and he had to carry it back to the man who had taught him how to answer.**

**And the record of that conversation is one of the great moments of integrity in Scripture:**

*Then Samuel told him every whit, and hid nothing from him. And he said, It is the Lord: let him do what seemeth him good* (3:18).

**Which is the pattern for the transmission of hard messages between generations.** The young man withheld nothing, and the old man submitted, and neither of them broke the relationship.

**And the result is stated in three verses that describe what a young prophet becomes when the old house declines around him:**

*And Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him, and did let none of his words fall to the ground. And all Israel from Dan even to Beer-sheba knew that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the Lord. And the Lord appeared again in Shiloh: for the Lord revealed himself to Samuel in Shiloh by the word of the Lord* (1 Samuel 3:19–21).

**Note where the appearing took place: in Shiloh, and by the word of the Lord.**

**Not in a new place. Not after a new institution had been founded.** The word of God came back to the same site, through a new vessel, **and *the Lord appeared again* is the sentence that a dying house needs to hear: the lamp can be relit in the same room.**

### The next crisis, and the aged prophet's rejection

**And the young prophet grew old, and the same pattern recurred within his own lifetime, and the account is worth reading closely because it is the hardest form of this problem.**

*Then all the elders of Israel gathered themselves together, and came to Samuel unto Ramah, and said unto him, Behold, thou art old, and thy sons walk not in thy ways: now make us a king to judge us like all the nations. But the thing displeased Samuel, when they said, Give us a king to judge us. And Samuel prayed unto the Lord* (1 Samuel 8:4–6).

**Note that the complaint was true.** Samuel was old, and his sons Joel and Abiah took bribes and perverted judgement (8:3). **The demand of the elders was factually founded, and it was still a rejection of God.**

**And God's word to the aging prophet is one of the most merciful sentences in the Old Testament:**

*And the Lord said unto Samuel, Hearken unto the voice of the people in all that they say unto thee: for they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them* (1 Samuel 8:7).

**They have not rejected thee.**

**Which is the word that every leader who is being displaced for the failures of the generation underneath him needs to hear.** Samuel had not failed. His sons had. **And the people's demand was addressed to him as the symbol of an older order, and God told him plainly that the rejection was not of him.**

**And it did not make the demand good.** Samuel was told to give them a king, and he was told to warn them what the king would take (8:11–18). **The house of God does not have to approve of what it must permit and announce.**

**And Samuel's own conduct after the anointing of Saul is the model of a displaced older leader.** He did not withdraw. He anointed the king, taught the nation the manner of the kingdom, wrote it in a book, prayed for the people, and said:

*Moreover as for me, God forbid that I should sin against the Lord in ceasing to pray for you: but I will teach you the good and the right way* (1 Samuel 12:23).

**He kept praying and kept teaching after his office had been superseded.** Which means that the older generation's usefulness does not end when its authority does.

### "Thou art but a youth"

**And the next young man in the line was anointed in his father's house and then placed inside the institution of his predecessor, and the arrangement produced both his preparation and his persecution.**

**The first correspondence between them is a servant's recommendation, and it is a small catalogue:**

*Behold, I have seen a son of Jesse the Beth-lehemite, that is cunning in playing, and a mighty valiant man, and a man of war, and prudent in matters, and a comely person, and the Lord is with him* (1 Samuel 16:18).

**Five qualities, and the last of them is the one that mattered.** And David was brought into the court of a king who was already under a sentence of rejection, **and he served him, and played for him, and became his armourbearer (16:21).**

**Which means that a young man called to replace an aging leader is often first set to serve him.**

**And the crisis came with the Philistine, and the words are the words every young generation hears:**

*And Saul said to David, Thou art not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him: for thou art but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth* (1 Samuel 17:33).

**Thou art but a youth.**

**And note that the king's assessment was reasonable, and had been true of every previous engagement.** Goliath had been presenting himself for forty days (17:16), and no man in Israel had gone out. **Saul's objection was not malice; it was experience.**

**And David's answer is the sentence that any young man facing that objection should carry with him:**

*David said moreover, The Lord that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine* (1 Samuel 17:37).

**He did not argue about his age and he did not claim to be stronger than he looked.** He cited his own private history with God — **the lion and the bear, the two events nobody had witnessed, the experience that had been given to him where nobody could see it.**

**Which is the answer to the objection. The qualification of a young man is not the strength he brings into the room. It is the record of what God has done for him in obscurity.**

**And then the detail that matters most in the whole episode, and it is a detail about equipment.**

*And Saul armed David with his armour, and he put an helmet of brass upon his head; also he armed him with a coat of mail ... And David said unto Saul, I cannot go with these; for I have not proved them. And David put them off him* (1 Samuel 17:38–39).

**The older generation's armour does not fit the younger one, and it is offered sincerely.**

**Every inheritance of a work comes with a set of methods, structures, forms, and habits that its previous owner used successfully.** And the armour was proved armour — it had kept Saul alive in real battles. **And it had not been proved on David, which is a different thing, and David knew the difference.**

**And he went out with a sling and five stones, and he said:**

*Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the Lord of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied ... And all this assembly shall know that the Lord saveth not with sword and spear: for the battle is the Lord's, and he will give you into our hands* (1 Samuel 17:45, 47).

**Which is the definition of a young generation that God can use inside an aging institution.** He respected the king, accepted the blessing, refused the armour, and named the battle as the Lord's.

**And the same scene would repeat with an older man's armour not fitting a younger man in another generation.** And it would be necessary then too.

### The young prophet, the old prophet, and the lie

**And now the passage that any reader of this chapter must be made to sit with, because it is the darkest case in the Old Testament of a young prophet destroyed by an older one.**

**A man of God came out of Judah by the word of the Lord to Bethel, while Jeroboam stood by the altar to burn incense** — and he cried against the altar and named by name a king who would not be born for three hundred years: *Behold, a child shall be born unto the house of David, Josiah by name* (1 Kings 13:2).

**And when the king offered him hospitality, he refused:**

*If thou wilt give me half thine house, I will not go in with thee, neither will I eat bread nor drink water in this place: for so was it charged me by the word of the Lord, saying, Eat no bread, nor drink water, nor turn again by the same way that thou camest* (1 Kings 13:8–9).

**And he went home another way, which means he was obedient in the matter of the return.**

**And then the old prophet of Bethel came after him.**

*Now there dwelt an old prophet in Bethel ... Then he said unto him, Come home with me, and eat bread. And he said, I may not return with thee, nor go in with thee: neither will I eat bread nor drink water with thee in this place: for it was said to me by the word of the Lord, Thou shalt eat no bread nor drink water there, nor turn again to go by the way that thou camest. He said unto him, I am a prophet also as thou art; and an angel spake unto me by the word of the Lord, saying, Bring him back with thee into thine house, that he may eat bread and drink water. But he lied unto him. So he went back with him, and did eat bread in his house, and drank water* (1 Kings 13:11, 15–19).

**And the word of the Lord came to the old prophet — the deceiver — and spoke the true sentence:**

*Thus saith the Lord, Forasmuch as thou hast disobeyed the mouth of the Lord, and hast not kept the commandment which the Lord thy God commanded thee, but camest back, and hast eaten bread and drunk water in the place, of the which the Lord did say to thee, Eat no bread, and drink no water; thy carcase shall not come unto the sepulchre of thy fathers* (1 Kings 13:21–22).

**And a lion met him on the road, and killed him, and stood by the carcass, and did not eat the body and did not attack the ass (13:24, 28).**

**The sentence against him was that he would not be buried with his fathers, and the corpse was left on the road.**

**And the record leaves no doubt about where the guilt lay for the disobedience.** The narrator says of the old prophet *he lied unto him* — and the young man's own defence, had he made one, would have been that he had been deceived by a man who claimed seniority and a fresh angelic revelation.

**Which means the exercise of the young prophet's judgement was required, and it failed.**

**And this is the doctrine of the passage, stated as carefully as it can be.**

**The young man had a specific word from God, given to him directly, about bread and water and the road.** He had obeyed it under pressure from a king. **And a senior prophet's claim of an angelic message, which contradicted the word he himself had received, was accepted without examination.**

**The lie was not: the Lord has spoken to me and told you to stay. It was: an angel spake unto me by the word of the Lord.** Which is untestable by the young man, and which is precisely why it must never be treated as authority over what a man has himself received from God.

**And note that the appeal of the false report was twofold, and both elements recur constantly in the interaction of generations.**

**The first element is seniority: *I am a prophet also as thou art.***

**And the second is a supplementary revelation: *an angel spake unto me.***

**Which means the young prophet was caught by the oldest mechanism in the history of the people of God: a claim to a later word from a more established vessel, used to alter an earlier word that had already been obeyed.**

**And the remedy is not suspicion of older men.** Samuel was taught by Eli. Elisha followed Elijah. Timothy was trained by Paul. **The remedy is that no man's testimony and no man's seniority can add to or subtract from what God has said, and every private report must be tested by what has been established.**

**And the old prophet's own end is the strangest part of the story.**

*And it came to pass, after he had buried him, that he spake to his sons, saying, When I am dead, then bury me in the sepulchre wherein the man of God is buried; lay my bones beside his bones* (1 Kings 13:31).

**The old man knew whose word had stood.** He had lied to the young prophet, and then he asked to be buried in the young prophet's grave, **which means the aged deceiver died as a man who knew exactly who had been sent from God.**

**Which is a warning to every generation of older believers.** It is possible to know that a young man is sent from God, to recognise the word he carries, and to undermine him anyway, **and to want to be associated with him at the end. And the association will not repair what was done.**

### The institution's own test

**And the two boys in Babylon tell us what an institution does to a young man it intends to use, and what a young man must have already settled before it begins.**

**The scale of the intake is stated precisely:**

*Children in whom was no blemish, but well favoured, and skilful in all wisdom, and cunning in knowledge, and understanding science, and such as had ability in them to stand in the king's palace, and whom they might teach the learning and the tongue of the Chaldeans. And the king appointed them a daily provision of the king's meat, and of the wine which he drank: so nourishing them three years, that at the end thereof they might stand before the king. Now among these were of the children of Judah, Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah* (Daniel 1:4–6).

**Three years of training, a royal diet, a foreign language and literature, and an examination at the end.**

**Which is the ordinary shape of the education any institution provides for the young men it intends to use:** a process, a provision, a curriculum, a standard, and a place to stand at the end of it.

**And the decisive act happened in the first year, before any of the education.**

*But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank: therefore he requested of the prince of the eunuchs that he might not defile himself* (Daniel 1:8).

**He purposed in his heart. Before the examination, before the promotion, before the interpretation of any dream.**

**And note the manner of his refusal: he requested.** He did not denounce the king's provision, organise the other Jewish boys, or make his objection a matter of public protest. **He made a request of the official who was responsible for him, and he proposed a test.** *Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days* (1:12).

**Which is the pattern for a young man whose conscience will not permit him to conform.** State the position privately to the responsible authority; propose a verifiable test; accept the outcome.

**And the result is recorded in terms of examination marks:**

*And the king communed with them; and among them all was found none like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah: therefore stood they before the king. And in all matters of wisdom and understanding, that the king enquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers that were in all his realm* (Daniel 1:19–20).

**Ten times better, in the institution's own subjects, by the institution's own examiners.**

**Which is the answer to the young man who imagines that fidelity to God requires intellectual mediocrity.** Daniel and his friends were the best students in Babylon, **and God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom, and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams (1:17).**

**And the manner of Daniel's first public ministry is worth noting, because he was about twenty years old and standing in front of an emperor who had ordered the execution of the entire professional class:**

*Then Daniel answered in the presence of the king, and said, The secret which the king hath demanded cannot the wise men, the astrologers, the magicians, the soothsayers, shew unto the king; but there is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets, and maketh known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days* (Daniel 2:27–28).

**He began by agreeing with the king's assessment of the other experts.**

**And the God of heaven had the credit, and Daniel had the standing, and he did not take the credit — which is what made him safe to promote.**

**And the same character is on display seventy years later, when the empire has changed hands and the boy is now an old man in a foreign court, and a queen mother describes him to a terrified king:**

*There is a man in thy kingdom, in whom is the spirit of the holy gods; and in the days of thy father light and understanding and wisdom, like the wisdom of the gods, was found in him ... now let Daniel be called, and he will shew the interpretation* (Daniel 5:11–12).

**The institution's memory of a young man outlived four kings.**

**Which is the deepest point about the training of the young inside a structure that does not share their faith.** The structure forgets nothing. It remembers the man who interpreted a dream, refused a diet, and named the God of heaven. **And when the crisis comes, the court's own record produces him.**

**And Joseph is the same figure in the same position, taken from a dungeon at thirty years old:**

*And Joseph answered Pharaoh, saying, It is not in me: God shall give Pharaoh an answer of peace ... And Pharaoh said unto his servants, Can we find such a one as this is, a man in whom the Spirit of God is?* (Genesis 41:16, 38).

**It is not in me.** Which is the sentence a young man says when he is finally given the opportunity he has waited for, and it is the sentence that secures it.

### The prescriptions for a young man in an old house

**And the apostolic instructions to the young men in the New Testament are short, specific, and free of sentiment. They are worth setting out as a list, because they are the operating rules for this whole chapter.**

**Let nobody despise your youth, and give them nothing to despise.** *Let no man despise thy youth; but be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity* (1 Timothy 4:12).

**The command is not to demand respect. It is to make the contempt impossible by the quality of the example, in six categories.**

**Be occupied with the reading.** *Till I come, give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine* (1 Timothy 4:13). **Which means the young man's remedy for inexperience is measured in hours spent in the text.**

**Do not rebuke an elder; address him as a father.** *Rebuke not an elder, but intreat him as a father; and the younger men as brethren* (1 Timothy 5:1). Which is a rule of address, and it governs the tone of every correction a young leader has to make. **The content may be firm and the form must be filial.**

**Know who taught you what.** *But continue thou in the things which thou hast learned and hast been assured of, knowing of whom thou hast learned them; and that from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures* (2 Timothy 3:14–15).

**Two sources, named in one sentence: a chain of teachers, and a mother and grandmother's Bible lessons from childhood.** *Knowing of whom thou hast learned them* is a test of the deposit itself. **A young man who cannot name his teachers has not been formed by anybody.**

**Speak with authority.** *These things speak, and exhort, and rebuke with all authority. Let no man despise thee* (Titus 2:15). **Which means the young man's teaching is not to be apologetic, and it is to be the same substance that the apostle would have taught.**

**And be protected by the men who sent you.** *Now if Timotheus come, see that he may be with you without fear: for he worketh the work of the Lord, as I also do. Let no man therefore despise him* (1 Corinthians 16:10–11). **Which means an older leader's duty to a young one is a public assignment of weight, given in advance, to a congregation that has not yet learned to give it.**

**And remember the Creator early, because the capacity for it does not last.** *Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth, while the evil days come not, nor the years draw nigh, when thou shalt say, I have no pleasure in them* (Ecclesiastes 12:1).

**Which is not a threat but a fact of biology and opportunity. The young man's advantages are available only while he is young.**

### And the two-way word of the seventy-first psalm

**And there is one passage in the Psalms that holds the entire chapter, and it is spoken by an old man and addressed to both generations in a single verse.**

*O God, thou hast taught me from my youth: and hitherto have I declared thy wondrous works. Now also when I am old and grayheaded, O God, forsake me not; until I have shewed thy strength unto this generation, and thy power to every one that is to come* (Psalm 71:17–18).

**The first line is the young man's testimony: taught by God from his youth.**

**And the second is the old man's request: do not take me yet, because I have not finished showing this generation what God's strength is.**

**Which is the shape of a healthy succession, in two clauses. The young man learns, and the old man transmits, and neither of them treats the other as an interruption.**

**And there is one more verse in the minor prophets that must be placed beside it, because it addresses the young man directly:**

*And said unto him, Run, speak to this young man, saying, Jerusalem shall be inhabited as towns without walls for the multitude of men and cattle therein* (Zechariah 2:4).

**An angel instructs a prophet to run and speak to a young man, and the message is about the future size of the city.**

**Which means that some words in Scripture are addressed to the youngest person in the room, and they concern what the place will become.**

### Principles

**First: the shortage of the word of God is the condition in which young prophets are called.** *And the word of the Lord was precious in those days; there was no open vision.* And the lamp had not yet gone out.

**Second: a failing institution can still teach the vocabulary of obedience, and Eli taught Samuel the words.** *Speak, Lord; for thy servant heareth.* The old man under judgement did not withhold the access.

**Third: the most damaging failure of an older leader is not ignorance but non-restraint.** *His sons made themselves vile, and he restrained them not.* And for that, a perpetuity was cancelled.

**Fourth: the young man must tell the old man the whole truth, and the old man must accept it.** *Samuel told him every whit, and hid nothing from him. And he said, It is the Lord.* Neither of them ended the relationship.

**Fifth: a rejection of the older leader may not be a rejection of him.** *They have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me.* And the older man still prayed and taught after his office was superseded.

**Sixth: the objection *thou art but a youth* is answered by private history with God and not by argument.** *The Lord that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear.*

**Seventh: the older generation's armour fits only the man who proved it.** *I cannot go with these; for I have not proved them.* The inheritance is real, and it includes the methods of the previous generation, and they must be proved before they are worn.

**Eighth: a claim of superior revelation from a senior man must be tested against what God has already said, and deference is not a defence.** *I am a prophet also as thou art; and an angel spake unto me ... But he lied unto him.*

**And ninth: an institution remembers the young man who honoured God in it.** *Ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers*; and seventy years later the court's own record said: *now let Daniel be called.*

**And tenth: the young man's duty is to be an example, and the old man's duty is to display the strength of God to the next generation.** *Be thou an example of the believers*; *until I have shewed thy strength unto this generation.*

> Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth, while the evil days come not, nor the years draw nigh, when thou shalt say, I have no pleasure in them.
> — Ecclesiastes 12:1

### For the fathers

- **Restrain your sons.** The charge against Eli was not that he taught nothing but that he restrained nothing, and a perpetual promise was cancelled because of it.
- **Teach the young man the words of response even if your own house is under judgement.** Eli's instruction to Samuel cost him nothing and preserved the access to God for the next generation.
- **Accept the whole truth from a young man, and submit to God in it.** *It is the Lord: let him do what seemeth him good.* And do not end the relationship over the message.
- **Offer the armour and do not insist on it.** Saul's armour was genuinely good. David was right to refuse what he had not proved, **and Saul blessed him and let him go (17:37).**
- **Never present a private revelation as a supplement to what God has already said, and never use seniority as its proof.** *I am a prophet also as thou art; and an angel spake unto me.* The cost of that sentence was a young man's life.
- **And keep praying and teaching after the office moves on.** *God forbid that I should sin against the Lord in ceasing to pray for you: but I will teach you the good and the right way.*

### For the young

- **Your qualification is what God has done for you where nobody was watching.** The lion and the bear came before Goliath.
- **Expect to serve the man you will succeed, and serve him well.** David played for Saul, carried his armour, and did not raise his hand against him.
- **Use the institution's own examinations to honour God.** *Ten times better* in Babylon's subjects, graded by Babylon's examiners, with the credit given to the God of heaven.
- **Make your refusals privately and propose a test.** Daniel requested; he did not protest; and he offered ten days.
- **Name your teachers.** *Knowing of whom thou hast learned them.* A deposit you cannot trace is a deposit you cannot defend.
- **And do not let a claim of seniority or of a newer revelation move you off what God has already told you.** Test everything by what is written, and remember the old prophet who wanted to be buried in the grave of the young man he had destroyed.

### Questions for reflection

1. Which part of your house is the house of Eli — the part where nobody restrains anybody, and everybody cites its long history?
2. Who is the young man in your congregation who has a word and no place to give it, and have you taught him the words of response?
3. What armour are you insisting that your successor wear, and has he proved it?
4. What private revelation, and whose seniority, is being used to move you off a word you know?
5. What has God done for you in obscurity that no room has yet heard about?
