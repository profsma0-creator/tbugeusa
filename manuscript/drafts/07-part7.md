## Jesus and the Mystery of Sonship

**The generational principle that this book has traced through Israel's histories is not a piece of ancient social policy that Christian ministry has borrowed. It is a description of something that was already true before the world was made.**

**The pattern is in the Godhead. There is a Father, and there is a Son, and the Son receives.**

**And everything this book has said about successors, mantles, deposits and transfers is a participation in that pattern by creatures who were made for it.**

**Which means that the question of how a generation hands over to the next is not finally a question about institutions, or culture, or the practicalities of leadership transition. It is a question about what God is like, and it is answered by looking at the relationship between the Father and the Son.**

### "The Son can do nothing of himself"

**And the doctrine begins with a set of statements that the Lord Jesus made about Himself, and every one of them is a statement of dependence.**

*Then answered Jesus and said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do: for what things soever he doeth, these also doeth the Son likewise* (John 5:19).

*I can of mine own self do nothing: as I hear, I judge: and my judgment is just; because I seek not mine own will, but the will of the Father which hath sent me* (John 5:30).

*Then said Jesus unto them, When ye have lifted up the Son of man, then shall ye know that I am he, and that I do nothing of myself; but as my Father hath taught me, I speak these things* (John 8:28).

*For I have not spoken of myself; but the Father which sent me, he gave me a commandment, what I should say, and what I should speak ... whatsoever I speak therefore, even as the Father said unto me, so I speak* (John 12:49–50).

**Read those four passages together and note the verbs.**

**The Son *can do nothing of himself*.** He does *what he seeth the Father do*. He *hears* and then judges. He does *nothing of himself*, and He speaks *as my Father hath taught me*. And the Father gives Him *a commandment, what I should say, and what I should speak*.

**Doing, judging, speaking — the whole of the Son's activity is described as received.**

**And the claim is made twice with the emphatic *verily, verily*, and three times with the phrase *of myself* — *ap' emautou*, from myself.**

**Which tells us what the pattern is, and it is not a pattern of delegation in the ordinary sense.** A delegation is a transfer of authority with an expectation of independent execution. **What the Son describes is not an assignment carried out at a distance, but a continuous reception: He sees, He hears, He is taught, He speaks what He is given.**

**And the same Lord said *I and my Father are one* (John 10:30).**

**So the doctrine is precisely this: the highest conceivable equality is expressed in the form of uninterrupted dependence.**

**And the application to our subject is direct and uncomfortable.** If the eternal Son, being equal with God, described His own ministry as receiving from the Father and doing only what He saw, **then a young man who has inherited a ministry and now intends to do what he sees fit has not merely departed from a convention. He has departed from the pattern of the Son.**

### "The Father loveth the Son, and sheweth him all things"

**And the next verse states the mechanism by which the Son receives, and the mechanism is not organisational and not procedural.**

*For the Father loveth the Son, and sheweth him all things that himself doeth: and he will shew him greater works than these, that ye may marvel* (John 5:20).

**The Father shows the Son *because* the Father loves the Son.**

**The transmission is an act of love, and the receiving is an act of love, and this is the sentence that must govern everything this book has said about the deposit.** A father who transmits out of duty, and a son who receives out of ambition, can reproduce the machinery of succession and never once participate in what the machinery is for.

**And the second clause is the promise that a true succession always produces an enlargement: *he will shew him greater works than these.***

**The Father does not show the Son a fixed body of work and then cease. He goes on showing, and what He shows next is greater than what He showed before.** Which is why the Lord's own ministry is progressive — water into wine, and then a healing, and then a resurrection, all the way to His own resurrection — **and why a son who has received the deposit faithfully should expect to be shown things his father did not do.**

**And the purpose of the greater works is stated: *that ye may marvel.***

**The enlargement is not for the son's reputation and not for the institution's statistics.** It is so that the observers will be astonished at God. **Which is a test for every young man who believes he has been given more than his father had: the greater works must produce marvel at God, and if they produce marvel at the man, they are not the greater works.**

### The self-restriction: the mind that made room

**And the passage most quoted about humility is also the passage that most clearly describes the pattern of a predecessor who makes room.**

*Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus: who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: but made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: and being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross* (Philippians 2:5–8).

**The Greek of verse 7 is *heauton ekenosen* — He emptied Himself.** And the sentence that follows describes what He emptied Himself into: *the form of a servant*, *the likeness of men*, *obedient unto death.*

**And verse 6 supplies the crucial fact: *he thought it not robbery to be equal with God.***

**Which means He did not give up the equality. He gave up the exercise of it, voluntarily, for a purpose, for a season.**

**And the applications to the whole subject of this book follow immediately and are numerous.**

**The first is that a predecessor's authority is not diminished by making room.** The Father is not less God because the Son is glorified. **And an older man who hands over real weight has not lost standing; he has exercised it in the manner of the One who emptied Himself while remaining equal.**

**The second is that the self-restriction was temporary and purposeful: *wherefore God also hath highly exalted him* (Philippians 2:9).**

**The emptying is not permanent and it is not an end in itself. It is the road to the exaltation.** And a succession that is done properly produces an older man who is more honoured and not less — **because the honour that comes from having raised up a successor is not transferable in the other direction, and nothing else in a ministry can produce it.**

**And the third application is the most searching. The Son did not merely tolerate the Father's glory; He came *for* it.**

*And now, O Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was* (John 17:5).

**Which is a prayer for the restoration of a glory that He had willingly suspended.**

**And note the shape of the whole arrangement: the Son's entire earthly ministry was an investment in another's glory, made in the confidence that the investment would be repaid.**

**Which is exactly what a father is invited to do with his remaining years.** He invests his strength in a successor's future, and the exaltation of the successor is the repayment. **And the man who cannot make that investment has decided, in the small sphere of a human ministry, that he will not do something the eternal Son did voluntarily in the larger one.**

### "Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience"

**And there is a doctrine in the letter to the Hebrews that tells us the Son was a learner, and that says why.**

*Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered* (Hebrews 5:8).

**The connective is *though*.** The statement is that the fact of sonship did not exempt Him from the learning process. **It required it.**

**And the method of the learning is stated: *by the things which he suffered.***

**He did not learn obedience from a syllabus.** He learned it by experience, in the same manner as the men we have studied in this book, and the word *learned* implies a real process with a real cost. **Which is precisely the doctrine of Hebrews 5:14 that appeared in the discussion of the successor's formation: the senses are *exercised* by use.**

**And the record of His growth as a young man is given in two verses that no other life so brief and so significant would trouble to record.**

*And he went down with them, and came to Nazareth, and was subject unto them ... And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man* (Luke 2:51–52).

**Four dimensions of growth: wisdom, stature, favour with God, favour with man.** And a period of submission: *he was subject unto them.*

**And immediately before that, at the age of twelve, He had said the sentence that every young man with a calling will be tempted to say in the wrong way:**

*How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?* (Luke 2:49).

**He knew His identity and He stated it — and He went home and was subject to His parents for another eighteen years.**

**Which is the pattern for the young man whose sense of calling has arrived before his appointment has.** He knows, and he says so, **and then he goes and does the mundane work under authority for as many years as it takes.**

**And the third text is the prophet's description of the learning, and it is the most beautiful statement of the process in the Old Testament:**

*The Lord God hath given me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary: he wakeneth morning by morning, he wakeneth mine ear to hear as the learned* (Isaiah 50:4).

**The tongue of the learned is given so that the servant can speak *a word in season* — in the right moment, to the right person, in the right condition.** And the acquisition of it is daily, and it is an ear being awakened *morning by morning*.

**Which means the capacity to speak well to people comes from a lifetime of listening first thing in the morning.** The order is stated: the ear is wakened *as the learned*, and then the tongue is the tongue *of the learned*.

**And the Son's own statement of arrival at the end is the fulfilment of that prophecy and of the whole learning process:**

*Then said I, Lo, I come (in the volume of the book it is written of me,) to do thy will, O God* (Hebrews 10:7).

**Lo, I come. Not *lo, I have arrived.* And the body which He came in was *prepared* for the purpose of doing the will of another.**

### The exegete of the Father

**And the purpose for which the Son received everything is stated in one verb in the prologue of John, and the verb is worth pausing over.**

*No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him* (John 1:18).

**The Greek for *he hath declared* is *exegesato*, from which we take the word *exegesis*.** It means to lead out, to set forth, to relate in full, to narrate.

**The Son is the exegete of the Father.**

**And note where He came from to do it: *which is in the bosom of the Father.***

**The revealing was done by the One who had the access.** He emerged from the innermost place and set forth what He had seen and heard there. **Which is a description of revelation as an act of transmission, and it means the whole of our knowledge of God depends on a messenger who came out from God and told us what God is like.**

**And the chain does not stop with the Son, and the next link is stated in the same Gospel.**

*He shall glorify me: for he shall receive of mine, and shall shew it unto you* (John 16:14).

**The Spirit receives from the Son and shows it to the disciples.** So the pattern runs: the Father gives to the Son; the Son gives to the Spirit; the Spirit gives to the church. **And in every link, the giver is glorified by the giving and not diminished.**

**And that is the pattern of all faithful ministry, and it is why the doctrine of this chapter underlies everything else in this book.** A man who has received from God and passes it on is participating in a transmission that began in the Godhead; **and a man who receives and keeps is not merely hoarding, he is breaking the pattern at a point where God has never broken it.**

### What was given to the Son

**And the inventory of what the Father gave to the Son is worth setting out, because it establishes what a succession consists of.**

*The Father loveth the Son, and hath given all things into his hand* (John 3:35).

*Jesus knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he was come from God, and went to God* (John 13:3).

*All things are delivered unto me of my Father: and no man knoweth the Son, but the Father; neither knoweth any man the Father, save the Son, and he to whomsoever the Son will reveal him* (Matthew 11:27).

*For as the Father hath life in himself; so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself* (John 5:26).

**All things. Every authority, every creature, and — most remarkably — *life in himself*.**

**And the passage that describes the largest transfer is the high-priestly prayer, and in it the categories become explicit.**

*As thou hast given him power over all flesh, that he should give eternal life to as many as thou hast given him ... I have manifested thy name unto the men which thou gavest me out of the world: thine they were, and thou gavest them me; and they have kept thy word ... For I have given unto them the words which thou gavest me; and they have received them* (John 17:2, 6, 8).

**Three categories of gift are in those verses.**

**The first is power over all flesh, given for the purpose of giving life to others.** The authority was not given to be held; it was given to be exercised in conferring something on a further set of recipients.

**The second category is the men themselves.** *Thine they were, and thou gavest them me.* **The twelve were the Father's gift to the Son, and the Lord's ministry to them was an administration of someone else's property.** Which is what every pastor must say about his congregation: they are not his, and they were given to him.

**And the third category is *the words*.** *I have given unto them the words which thou gavest me.* **The deposit of revelation passed through two hands before it reached the men who carried it to the world.**

**Which means that the Lord's relationship to His disciples is itself an image of what a father is supposed to do with a successor.** He received them as a trust; He gave them everything He had received; and He sent them with the same commission He had been given.

**And note the end of verse 6: *and they have kept thy word.***

**The evidence that the transmission succeeded is that the receiver kept what he was given.**

### "As thou hast sent me, even so have I sent them"

**And the sending of the disciples is stated in those exact terms, three times, and the formula is the whole of apostolic authority.**

*As thou hast sent me into the world, even so have I also sent them into the world* (John 17:18).

*Then said Jesus to them again, Peace be unto you: as my Father hath sent me, even so send I you* (John 20:21).

**And the Lord supplied the rule that governs both the sending and the receiving:**

*Verily, verily, I say unto you, The servant is not greater than his lord; neither he that is sent greater than he that sent him* (John 13:16).

**And He stated that the disciples were entering work they had not done:**

*I sent you to reap that whereon ye bestowed no labour: other men laboured, and ye are entered into their labours* (John 4:38).

**That last sentence is the verse of this entire book, and it was spoken by the Lord to a group of young men at the beginning of their ministry, about the harvest of a nation.**

**They were sent to reap what they had not planted.** And the reason they could be sent was that other men had laboured — **the prophets, the Baptist, the woman at the well, and above all the Lord Himself, who had said four verses earlier, *lift up your eyes, and look on the fields; for they are white already to harvest* (John 4:35).**

**Which means that the work of every generation after the first is, by the Lord's own description, inherited work.** And the young man who has inherited it should know both halves of the truth: that he did not earn it, **and that he is not a parasite for entering into it. He was sent.**

**And Romans states the logic of the sending as the permanent rule of the church's growth:**

*How then shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? and how shall they believe in him of whom they have not heard? and how shall they hear without a preacher? And how shall they preach, except they be sent?* (Romans 10:14–15).

**The chain has four links and it ends in the sending.** Which is why the New Testament's central noun for a ministry leader is a word that means *sent one* — *apostolos*, from *apostello*, to send forth with a commission. **And it is why Paul begins most of his letters by insisting on the source of his commission: *not of men, neither by man, but by Jesus Christ* (Galatians 1:1).**

**And the Lord's own title includes the office:** *consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession, Christ Jesus* (Hebrews 3:1). **The one who sends was Himself sent** — *as thou hast sent me, even so have I sent them* — which means that being sent by men is not the mechanism. **Being sent by the One who was sent is.**

### The commission that carries a presence

**And the sending of the disciples in Matthew's Gospel adds the thing that makes it survivable, and it is the last sentence of that Gospel.**

*And Jesus came and spake unto them, saying, All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen* (Matthew 28:18–20).

**Take the four parts in order.**

**The authority is stated first, and it is received authority: *all power is given unto me.*** The basis of the commission is a transfer that has already occurred.

**The command follows: *go ye therefore.*** The *therefore* matters. The mission is a consequence of the authority that has been given to the Sender, **not of the qualifications of the sent.**

**The content is *teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you*.**

**Note what is to be taught: not the memory of the Lord, and not the best extracts from His teaching, but *all things whatsoever I have commanded you.*** And the objective is observance — *teaching them to observe* — **which means the goal of the transmission is obedience and not information.**

**And the fourth part is the presence: *lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.***

**Which is the clause that makes the whole arrangement possible for people who are aware of their own inadequacy.** The commission exceeds the capacity of the commissioned, **and the remedy is not more capacity. It is the presence of the One who sent them, promised for the whole duration.**

**And the duration — *even unto the end of the world* — tells us that the sending is not a single event in the first century.** The command runs to the end of the age, and it is executed by a chain of men and women who received nothing directly from the Lord's lips, **and who are nonetheless sent by the same authority and given the same presence.**

**Which makes the church's transmission of the gospel across two thousand years a single continuous act of apostolic sending, performed by successive generations under one commission.**

### The end of the sending

**And there is a final element in the pattern, and it is the most humbling and the most necessary for anyone who has ever built anything.**

**The Son, having received all things, delivers the whole back.**

*Then cometh the end, when he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God, even the Father; when he shall have put down all rule and all authority and power. For he must reign, till he hath put all enemies under his feet. The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death ... And when all things shall be subdued unto him, then shall the Son also himself be subject unto him that put all things under him, that God may be all in all* (1 Corinthians 15:24–28).

**The Greek for *delivered up* is *paradidoi*, to hand over, to give into another's hands — the same verb family that describes the deposit (*paratheke*) that Timothy was to guard.**

**The Son of God, at the end of the age, hands the kingdom over.**

**Which means that the ultimate act of the Son's ministry is not to retain the throne but to return it.** And the purpose of the whole arrangement is stated in the last clause: *that God may be all in all.*

**And this is the last word of the Christ pattern and the first word of every correct theology of succession.**

**The Son received everything, administered it according to what He saw the Father doing, spoke only what He was given to speak, and then handed the finished work back to its source.**

**And a ministry that is modelled on that pattern will be characterised by the same three movements: reception, administration, and delivery.** The reception is the whole of the man's formation; the administration is his life's work; **and the delivery is the act that proves whether the first two were real.**

**And the man who cannot deliver has misunderstood what he was given.** The kingdom was never the Son's private possession, and the congregation was never the pastor's, and the deposit was never the institution's.

**That God may be all in all.** Which requires that every intermediate thing be surrendered, including the ministry of the men who built it.

### Two errors in the church's use of the pattern

**And the chapter closes by naming the two ways the pattern is misused, because it has been misused in both directions and the damage in each case is considerable.**

**The first error is to read the Son's dependence as a mere hierarchy and to reconstruct ministry as a chain of command.** On this reading, the transmission is about authority, and the test of faithfulness is compliance.

**But John 5:20 gives the mechanism of the transmission as love — *the Father loveth the Son, and sheweth him all things* — and Philippians 2:6 gives the disposition of the Son as self-emptying rather than self-assertion.** And the Lord's own summary of His relationship to the disciples is not a rank but a service: *I am among you as he that serveth* (Luke 22:27).

**A house ordered by the pattern of the Godhead is ordered by love that serves and by the free showing of everything the Father has.** A house ordered by command will produce compliance for exactly as long as the commander is present, **which is the Joash condition of an earlier chapter, and it fails at the graveside.**

**And the second error is the opposite: to read the Son's dependence as a mere metaphor for humility and to conclude that no one is ever to be over anyone.** But the New Testament appoints elders, commands obedience to them, and warns that the church must be *taught* and *charged* and *ruled* — and the Lord Himself said *as my Father hath sent me, even so send I you* (John 20:21).

**The pattern is therefore neither hierarchy nor the absence of authority. It is authority exercised in the manner of the Son: received from above, exercised for the good of those below, and surrendered at the end.**

**And that describes a father in the faith exactly.** He has no authority he did not receive; he uses it for the formation of the men under him and not for his own standing; **and he intends from the beginning to hand back what he built and to be found among those who are subject.**

### Principles

**First: the generational principle is derived from the Godhead and not from sociology.** There is a Father who gives and a Son who receives, and every human succession is a participation in that pattern.

**Second: the pattern of sonship is voluntary reception.** *The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do ... I have not spoken of myself; but the Father which sent me.* Doing, judging and speaking are all described as received.

**Third: the mechanism of the transmission is love.** *For the Father loveth the Son, and sheweth him all things that himself doeth.* Procedure can reproduce a succession and cannot reproduce this.

**Fourth: a faithful succession produces enlargement, and the enlargement is for God's glory.** *He will shew him greater works than these, that ye may marvel.* If the greater works produce marvel at the man, they are not the greater works.

**Fifth: the Son emptied Himself without ceasing to be equal, and was exalted for it.** *He thought it not robbery to be equal with God: but made himself of no reputation.* A predecessor does not lose standing by making room.

**Sixth: sonship does not exempt a man from learning.** *Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered.* And the growth was measured: *increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man.*

**Seventh: the whole of the Son's ministry was exegesis.** *The only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.* He came out from the innermost place and set forth what He had seen there.

**Eighth: the inventory of what was given is total.** All things into His hand, power over all flesh, the men themselves, the words, and life in Himself. And the men were given for a purpose: *that he should give eternal life to as many as thou hast given him.*

**And ninth: the sending is a repetition of the Son's own sending, and it carries a presence.** *As thou hast sent me, even so have I sent them ... lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.*

**And tenth: the end of the ministry is to hand it back.** *He shall have delivered up the kingdom to God, even the Father ... that God may be all in all.* The delivery is the proof that the reception and the administration were real.

### For the fathers

- **Show everything you have, and show it because you love him.** *The Father loveth the Son, and sheweth him all things that himself doeth.* Withholding information is not prudence; it is a failure of love.
- **Make room without imagining that you are diminished.** The Son emptied Himself while remaining equal with God, and was exalted for it. **The father who raises a successor gains honour that no other act of ministry can produce.**
- **Expect him to do greater works, and expect the marvel to go to God.** *He will shew him greater works than these, that ye may marvel.* If the greater works are being attributed to the man, something has gone wrong at the point of transmission.
- **Do not hand over a work you intend to keep.** The pattern ends with delivery: *he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God, even the Father.* Build for the handover from the beginning.
- **And do not confuse hierarchy with the pattern.** The bond between the Father and the Son is love, and the disposition of the Son is service. A house held together by rank will hold only as long as the rank is present.

### For the young

- **You are a receiver, and that is not a temporary stage.** *The Son can do nothing of himself.* The eternal Son, being equal with God, describes His ministry this way; it is not a probation you graduate from.
- **Know what you have been given, and name it.** All things in His hand; power over all flesh; the men; the words; life in Himself. Counting the deposit is how a man stops despising it.
- **Expect to learn obedience by the things you suffer.** *Though he were a Son.* The learning is not a sign that your sonship is unconfirmed; it is the method by which sons are made.
- **Be subject for as long as it takes.** He said *I must be about my Father's business* at twelve, and went home and was subject for eighteen years. **Knowing your calling is not the same as having your commission.**
- **You were sent, and you will reap what you did not plant.** *Other men laboured, and ye are entered into their labours.* Do not be ashamed of the inheritance, and do not pretend you built it.
- **And remember that the presence travels with the commission.** *Lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.* The commission will exceed your capacity for the whole of your life, and that is by design.
- **And prepare to hand it back.** The Son delivers the kingdom to the Father. What you build is not finally yours, and the delivery is the proof that you understood what you were given.

### Questions for reflection

1. What have you received that you have never counted, and what have you withheld from the man who comes after you?
2. Is the transmission in your house an act of love or an act of administration?
3. What has God shown you that is greater than what He showed your father in the faith — and whose glory is being served by it?
4. Where are you still asserting a standing that the Son of God was willing to empty Himself of?
5. What will you deliver up, and to whom, and when?
