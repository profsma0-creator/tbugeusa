## Paul, the Apostles and the Multiplication of Witness

**And now the method by which the message crossed the empire in thirty years, and it was not by the evangelisation of crowds.**

**It was by the multiplication of witnesses, and the multiplication was done deliberately, one man at a time, by an apostle who spent the last third of his life building a network of people rather than an organisation.**

**And the record of the method is embedded in the text of his own letters, in lists of names that most readers skim, and the lists are the achievement.**

**The book of Romans ends with twenty-six greetings to named individuals in a church Paul had never visited. Colossians ends with a roll of eight men and two house churches. And those chapters are the archives of the multiplication, and they show exactly how the greatest expansion of the faith in the first century was accomplished.**

### The team at Antioch

**And it began with a team, and the composition of the team is itself a doctrine.**

*Now there were in the church that was at Antioch certain prophets and teachers; as Barnabas, and Simeon that was called Niger, and Lucius of Cyrene, and Manaen, which had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul. As they ministered to the Lord, and fasted, the Holy Ghost said, Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them. And when they had fasted and prayed, and laid their hands on them, they sent them away* (Acts 13:1–3).

**Look at the five men who were leading that church, because they represent the whole of the Mediterranean world and several incompatible social classes.**

**Barnabas** — a Levite from Cyprus, a landowner who had sold his property, and a Jew of the diaspora.

**Simeon who was called Niger** — the Latin nickname for a dark-skinned man, almost certainly an African.

**Lucius of Cyrene** — another North African, from the city in modern Libya, and one of the men who had planted the church in the first place (Acts 11:20).

**Manaen, which had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch** — a courtier, a man raised in the household of the dynasty that had executed John the Baptist and mocked Jesus. **The Greek is *syntrophos*, a foster-brother, which means he was a member of Herod's own household.**

**And Saul of Tarsus** — a Pharisee trained under Gamaliel, and formerly the church's chief persecutor.

**So the leadership of the church that sent the first missionaries consisted of a Levite landowner, an African, another African, a man raised in a royal court, and a former inquisitor.**

**Neither the social distance nor the ethnic distance between those five men had been possible before.** And the verse records that they were together *ministering to the Lord and fasting* when the Spirit spoke.

**Which gives the first principle of mission, and it is not about strategy. The Spirit's sending was given to a team, not to an individual, and it was given to a team whose composition was itself evidence that the gospel had done something no other message had done.**

**And the sending was a church act.** *When they had fasted and prayed, and laid their hands on them, they sent them away.* The missionaries were not volunteers; **they were identified by the Spirit in a prayer meeting and commissioned by a congregation with fastings and the laying on of hands.**

### "He is a chosen vessel"

**And the man who was sent had been given his commission years earlier, in a manner that ought to encourage everyone who wonders whether an unlikely young man can be entrusted with anything.**

**Ananias was sent to the blinded persecutor, and he objected, and the answer he received is the statement of Paul's calling:**

*Go thy way: for he is a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel: for I will shew him how great things he must suffer for my name's sake* (Acts 9:15–16).

**Four things are in that sentence.**

**He is a vessel.** The Greek is *skeuos ekloges*, a vessel of choice, an instrument deliberately selected. **The man is a container, not an originator.**

**He is chosen by God, and not by the church.** Ananias was an ordinary believer in Damascus, and he did not want to go. **And the commission came with the impression that the church would have to receive a man it had every reason to fear.**

**He is sent to three audiences: Gentiles, kings, and the children of Israel.** Which is the whole of the known world — the nations, the rulers, and his own people. **A particular calling with a defined address.**

**And he is told in advance that he will suffer.** *I will shew him how great things he must suffer for my name's sake.* **Which means that the announcement of the calling included the cost, and the man accepted it, and the man then spent thirty years paying it.**

**And the practical lesson from the passage is for the older community rather than for the young man. Ananias had to be persuaded to go to the man God had chosen.** The church's natural instinct with a dangerous convert is caution, and the instinct must sometimes be overruled.

### He never travelled alone

**And the method of the apostle is visible in the company he kept, and one verse of Acts lists the men who were with him on a single journey:**

*And there accompanied him into Asia Sopater of Berea; and of the Thessalonians, Aristarchus and Secundus; and Gaius of Derbe, and Timotheus; and of Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus* (Acts 20:4).

**Seven men from four or five different churches, travelling together with him.**

**Which is the operational form of the whole doctrine of this book.** Paul did not conduct a solo mission with an occasional assistant. **He executed a strategy of continuous companionship in which every journey had a group of men on it, and the men were being trained while the work was being done.**

**And the composition of the group changes over the years, which shows the deliberate pattern.** Barnabas and John Mark on the first journey. Silas and Timothy on the second. Timothy, Luke, Titus, Erastus, Gaius, Aristarchus on later ones. **And the men he took with him came back and became leaders of churches, and the churches planted further churches.**

**The Greek word for the relationship is *synergoi*, fellow-workers, and Paul uses it constantly.** *Timotheus my workfellow* (Romans 16:21). *Priscilla and Aquila my helpers in Christ Jesus* (Romans 16:3). *Marcus, Aristarchus, Demas, Lucas, my fellowlabourers* (Philemon 24).

**And the reason it matters is that a man who works alone can only ever be as useful as his own strength, whereas a man who works in company multiplies everything by the number of men whose formation he is attending to.**

### He ordained elders in every church

**And the single most decisive act of Paul's strategy was the refusal to leave a church dependent on himself.**

*And when they had preached the gospel to that city, and had taught many, they returned again to Lystra, and to Iconium, and Antioch, confirming the souls of the disciples, and exhorting them to continue in the faith, and that we must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God. And when they had ordained them elders in every church, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed* (Acts 14:21–23).

**Consider the sequence of that journey.**

**They returned through the cities where they had been persecuted, to strengthen the disciples.** Which is the first act of a father: he goes back.

**They exhorted them to continue, and told them plainly that tribulation was the road into the kingdom.** Which is the second: he tells the truth about the cost.

**And then they ordained elders in every church.**

**The Greek for *ordained* is *cheirotonesantes*, from *cheir*, hand, and *teino*, to stretch — appointing by the raising of hands, and therefore with the consent of the congregation.** And the phrase *in every church* is emphatic.

**Which means that the apostle's departure was always accompanied by the installation of local leadership, and the installation was the point.** Paul could have retained oversight of the churches he planted; the churches would have been glad of it; **and instead he appointed elders in each city and then *commended them to the Lord*, which is to say he let go.**

**And this is the mechanism by which a movement becomes multigenerational rather than dependent on a founder.** Every church had a plurality of men in it who could lead, teach and settle disputes in the apostle's absence — and they had been in the church long enough to be known.

**And the second journey was, by Paul's own description, an audit of the arrangement:**

*Let us go again and visit our brethren in every city where we have preached the word of the Lord, and see how they do* (Acts 15:36).

**He went back to find out how the elders were doing.** Which means his interest in the churches continued after the authority had been handed over, **and it was exercised by visiting and inspecting rather than by remote control.**

### "Well reported of by the brethren"

**And the way he selected his closest companion for the rest of his life shows the standard he applied to the younger generation.**

*Then came he to Derbe and Lystra: and, behold, a certain disciple was there, named Timotheus, the son of a certain woman, which was a Jewess, and believed; but his father was a Greek: which was well reported of by the brethren that were at Lystra and Iconium* (Acts 16:1–2).

**The qualification is in the last clause: he was *well reported of by the brethren*.**

**The Greek is *emartyreito*, he was witnessed to, he had testimony borne about him — by the churches of two cities.** And the man was a young convert from the church Paul had planted on his first journey, whose character had been observed by the local believers for several years.

**Which means Paul did not recruit a gifted stranger on the strength of an impression. He took a man whose reputation in his own church was established.**

**And that is the pattern for the selection of successors in every generation. The man to look at is not the man who impresses you on the first meeting; it is the man about whom others have testified over time — and the testimony is local, from the people who have lived with him.**

**And the further implication is that a local church is the proper nursery for the next generation of leadership, and a young man who is unknown in his own congregation is not yet ready to be known anywhere else.**

### Two years in a lecture hall

**And the most efficient piece of missionary strategy in the New Testament is described in two verses, and it does not mention crowds, and it produced the evangelisation of an entire province.**

*But when divers were hardened, and believed not, but spake evil of that way before the multitude, he departed from them, and separated the disciples, disputing daily in the school of one Tyrannus. And this continued by the space of two years; so that all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks* (Acts 19:9–10).

**Note what he did and what he did not do.**

**He left the synagogue and *separated the disciples* — he gathered the believers apart from the hostile institution.** And he took a room, presumably hired, in the premises of a man named Tyrannus, **and he taught there *daily* for two years.**

**Which means that for seven hundred days or more, Paul conducted a daily class.** And the effect stated by Luke is that *all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus* — **not all who attended, and not all who heard Paul. All they which dwelt in Asia.**

**The province was evangelised by the training of a group of men in a room, over two years, who then went and did the work in the cities.**

**And the practical arithmetic is worth stating.** If he taught thirty or forty men daily for two years, and each of them returned to his city and taught what he had learned, **the province is covered, and the apostle never visits most of it.**

**Which is the great principle of multiplication, and it is the reason Paul could say at the end of his life that he had *fully preached the gospel of Christ* from Jerusalem round about unto Illyricum (Romans 15:19) while having personally visited only a fraction of the cities in question. He had preached it in the men he had taught.**

**And the contrast with the method of a contemporary celebrity ministry is total.** A gifted man can gather thousands in a stadium. **Paul hired a room and taught thirty men every day for two years, and got a province.**

### The catalogue of names

**And the proof of the method is in the letters, because Paul's correspondence is full of names, and most of the names belong to people we know nothing else about.**

**The letter to the Romans, written to a church he had never visited, closes with twenty-six personal greetings:**

*I commend unto you Phebe our sister, which is a servant of the church which is at Cenchrea ... Greet Priscilla and Aquila my helpers in Christ Jesus: who have for my life laid down their own necks: unto whom not only I give thanks, but also all the churches of the Gentiles. Likewise greet the church that is in their house. Salute my wellbeloved Epenetus, who is the firstfruits of Achaia unto Christ ... Salute Tryphena and Tryphosa, who labour in the Lord. Salute the beloved Persis, which laboured much in the Lord. Salute Rufus chosen in the Lord, and his mother and mine* (Romans 16:1–5, 12–13).

**Read what is in those greetings, because every clause is a record of a relationship.**

**Phoebe is a *diakonos* of the church at Cenchrea and a *succourer* — *prostatis* — of many, and of Paul himself.** A woman carrying the letter to Rome, and credited with having protected the apostle.

**Priscilla and Aquila laid down their own necks for Paul's life, and the churches of the Gentiles owed them thanks, and there was a church meeting in their house.** A married couple who had risked their lives and whose home was a congregation.

**Epenetus was the first convert in Achaia.** Paul remembered the name of his firstfruits in a province.

**Tryphena and Tryphosa laboured in the Lord; Persis laboured much.** Women whose labour is recorded by name.

**And Rufus' mother had also mothered Paul.** *Kai ten metera autou kai emou* — and his mother and mine. **Which means Paul had been taken into a household and had been cared for as a son by a woman who was not his mother.**

**And that, and not a statistic, is what the multiplication of witness looked like.** The churches were networks of households, and the networks were held together by named relationships between people who had lived together and suffered together.

**And Colossians provides the same catalogue, with the addition of the most ordinary domestic instructions:**

*All my state shall Tychicus declare unto you, who is a beloved brother, and a faithful minister and fellowservant in the Lord ... With Onesimus, a faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you ... Epaphras, who is one of you, a servant of Christ, saluteth you, always labouring fervently for you in prayers ... Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas, greet you. Salute the brethren which are in Laodicea, and Nymphas, and the church which is in his house ... And say to Archippus, Take heed to the ministry which thou hast received in the Lord, that thou fulfil it* (Colossians 4:7, 9, 12, 14–15, 17).

**And the last verse of that list is the one to sit with.**

**Paul, writing a letter to a church he had not planted, adds a personal instruction to a man named Archippus.** *Say to Archippus, Take heed to the ministry which thou hast received in the Lord, that thou fulfil it.*

**The apostle was in a Roman prison, he had churches across three continents to think about, and he took the trouble to send a message to one local worker to tell him to fulfil his ministry.**

**Which is what the multiplication of witness actually consists of. Not a strategy document, but a man who knows every labourer's name, remembers what each of them owes, and reminds him of it by letter from a prison cell.**

### And the older apostle he had to correct

**And the flow of correction was not all one way, and that matters for the shape of this book, because it shows that the generational relationship in the New Testament was not a chain of command from old to young.**

*But when Peter was come to Antioch, I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed. For before that certain came from James, he did eat with the Gentiles: but when they were come, he withdrew and separated himself, fearing them which were of the circumcision. And the other Jews dissembled likewise with him; insomuch that Barnabas also was carried away with their dissimulation. But when I saw that they walked not uprightly according to the truth of the gospel, I said unto Peter before them all, If thou, being a Jew, livest after the manner of the Gentiles, and not as do the Jews, why compellest thou the Gentiles to live as do the Jews?* (Galatians 2:11–14).

**Paul rebuked Peter — his senior, an apostle of the original twelve, the man who had walked with Jesus — and he did it publicly, *before them all*, and he recorded it in a letter that the whole church has read ever since.**

**And he did it publicly because the offence was public.** Peter's withdrawal from the Gentile table had taught the whole church in Antioch a falsehood about the gospel, and Barnabas had been carried away with it, **and a private conversation would have left the congregation with the impression that the two most senior men in the world disagreed about what everybody had just seen.**

**Which is the doctrine of this book applied in the other direction.** A young man may correct an older one, and sometimes must — **and the conditions are that the matter must be an actual departure from the truth of the gospel, that the correction must be proportionate to the publicity of the error, and that the motive must be that the men *walked not uprightly according to the truth of the gospel* and not that the older man is finished.**

**And note Paul's own account of why he had authority to do it.** He had already stated that he received his gospel by revelation (Galatians 1:12) and that the pillars at Jerusalem had given him the right hand of fellowship (2:9). **He was not a young man resenting his elders. He was a steward of a deposit, protecting it in an older man's conduct.**

### The mixed motives he refused to police

**And there is a final element of the method, and it is a generosity that most movements cannot manage.**

*Some indeed preach Christ even of envy and strife; and some also of good will: the one preach Christ of contention, not sincerely, supposing to add affliction to my bonds: but the other of love, knowing that I am set for the defence of the gospel. What then? notwithstanding, every way, whether in pretence, or in truth, Christ is preached; and I therein do rejoice, yea, and will rejoice* (Philippians 1:15–18).

**Paul was in prison, and men were preaching Christ in order to make his imprisonment harder.**

**And his verdict is *I therein do rejoice.***

**Which means that the multiplication of witness was not policed for the purity of the motives of the witnesses.** He was not indifferent to motive — he names the envy and the contention plainly — **and he refused to make the purity of other men's motives the condition of his own joy. The message was sound, and the message was being preached, and the man rejoiced.**

**And the companion principle is Paul's ambition about territory:**

*Yea, so have I strived to preach the gospel, not where Christ was named, lest I should build upon another man's foundation* (Romans 15:20).

**Which is the disposition of a man who has no interest in protecting a franchise.** He would not build on another man's foundation, and he would not object to another man building on his. **He wanted places he had not reached rather than places he had.**

**And the result in Thessalonica was a church that did the same thing within months of its founding:**

*And ye became followers of us, and of the Lord, having received the word in much affliction, with joy of the Holy Ghost: so that ye were ensamples to all that believe in Macedonia and Achaia. For from you sounded out the word of the Lord not only in Macedonia and Achaia, but also in every place your faith to God-ward is spread abroad; so that we need not to speak any thing* (1 Thessalonians 1:6–8).

**A church at most a year old had become the pattern for two provinces and had broadcast the word everywhere.**

**Which is the multiplication of witness in one sentence.** A young church, formed under persecution by men who had lived among them and given them their own souls, **was reproducing within a year, and its reproduction was doing the work the apostle could not do himself.**

### The final inventory

**And the last letter of the man tells us what he considered his life's work at the end of it, and it is not what an institution-builder would list.**

*For I am now ready to be offered, and the time of my departure is at hand. I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith ... Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry ... The cloke that I left at Troas with Carpus, when thou comest, bring with thee, and the books, but especially the parchments* (2 Timothy 4:6–7, 11, 13).

**Read the four items of his last inventory.**

***I have kept the faith* — the deposit, intact.**

**Three men by name: Luke with him, Mark to be fetched, Timothy addressed throughout.**

**A coat left at Troas, and the books, and especially the parchments.**

**And no mention of a building, a budget, an institution, or a title.**

**Which is the summary of the whole method, and the reason the multiplication of witness outlasted him.** The faith had been kept, the men had been made, and the documents were being collected. **And within thirty years of his execution, a church existed in Rome, in Ephesus, in Corinth, in Philippi, in Antioch, in Alexandria, and in cities whose names are in the letters — and the men leading them were the men who had travelled with him.**

**And his own summary of the method is the sentence from 2 Timothy 2:2 that has already appeared in this book, and it belongs here again as the conclusion of his practice:** *And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.*

**He said it, and the lists of names in the letters prove that he did it.**

### Principles

**First: the sending of missionaries was a church act performed by a multi-ethnic team.** *Barnabas, and Simeon that was called Niger, and Lucius of Cyrene, and Manaen, which had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul.* Africa, Cyprus, the Herodian court and Tarsus, in one leadership, fasting together.

**Second: the church was required to receive a man it had reason to fear.** *He is a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel.* Ananias had to be persuaded, and the commission included the cost: *I will shew him how great things he must suffer.*

**Third: Paul never travelled alone, and the composition of his company changed as men were formed.** Sopater, Aristarchus, Secundus, Gaius, Timothy, Tychicus, Trophimus — seven men from four churches on a single journey.

**Fourth: he ordained elders in every church and then let go.** *When they had ordained them elders in every church, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord.* And the next journey audited the arrangement: *let us go again ... and see how they do.*

**Fifth: his closest successor was chosen on local testimony.** *Timotheus ... which was well reported of by the brethren that were at Lystra and Iconium.* Not a gifted stranger, but a man his own congregation vouched for over years.

**Sixth: the province was won by two years of daily teaching in a hired room.** *This continued by the space of two years; so that all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks.*

**Seventh: the achievement is recorded as a list of names.** Phoebe the succourer, Priscilla and Aquila who laid down their necks, Epenetus the firstfruits, Tryphena and Tryphosa who laboured, and Rufus' mother who had mothered Paul. **And *say to Archippus, Take heed to the ministry which thou hast received in the Lord, that thou fulfil it.***

**Eighth: correction travelled in both directions.** *When Peter was come to Antioch, I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed* — publicly, because the error was public, and on a matter of the truth of the gospel.

**And ninth: he refused to police the motives of other preachers, and would not build on another man's foundation.** *Every way, whether in pretence, or in truth, Christ is preached; and I therein do rejoice.*

**And tenth: his final inventory was a kept faith, three named men, and some books and parchments.** Which is what a multiplication of witness looks like at the end.

### For the fathers

- **Work in company, and change the company deliberately as men are formed.** Seven men from four churches on one journey. A man who works alone is limited to his own strength.
- **Ordain others and then let go.** *They commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed.* A church that still needs you in year ten has not been planted; it has been rented.
- **Go back and audit.** *Let us go again ... and see how they do.* Interest after the handover is exercised by visiting, not by remote control.
- **Choose successors on local testimony, not on your own impression.** *Well reported of by the brethren that were at Lystra and Iconium.* Ask his own congregation what they know of him.
- **Give your strength to a small room if that is what multiplies.** Two years of daily teaching in a hired hall produced the evangelisation of a province.
- **Keep the names.** Paul's letters are full of ordinary people remembered by name, and he sent a message from a prison to one local worker to fulfil his ministry. **The multiplication of witness is a network of named relationships, and it survives the death of every individual in it.**

### For the young

- **Be well reported of where you already are, before you want to be known anywhere else.** Timothy's qualification was the testimony of two ordinary congregations.
- **Accept correction from any direction, and give it only on the ground of the truth of the gospel.** Paul withstood Peter publicly because the error was public, and he did it about the gospel and not about his own standing.
- **Do not despise the daily class and the small room.** *Disputing daily in the school of one Tyrannus*, for two years, and the province heard the word.
- **Rejoice when others preach, even when their motives toward you are bad.** *Every way, whether in pretence, or in truth, Christ is preached; and I therein do rejoice.* A man who can say that is free of the fear that destroys older leaders.
- **And do not be surprised to find your name in somebody's list.** Paul remembered Epenetus as the firstfruits of a province and a woman's mothering of him. **Your labour is recorded by the God who sees, and frequently by the men you least expect to have noticed.**
- **And be a link in a chain, not a destination.** *Commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.* The measure of your usefulness is the fourth generation, and you will not meet most of them.

### Questions for reflection

1. Who is travelling with you at present, and what are they being formed into by the journey?
2. Which of the churches or people in your care still depend on your presence to function, and how would you begin to change that this year?
3. Whose local testimony have you verified before giving a man responsibility?
4. What small and unglamorous room could you teach in daily for two years?
5. If your life's work were inventoried at the end, would it be a kept faith, some men by name, and some books — or a building and a title?
