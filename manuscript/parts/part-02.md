Revelation has a vocabulary, and Scripture is not embarrassed by it. There are prophets and seers, dreamers and interpreters, men and women who perceived what others could not and who were sometimes believed and sometimes thrown into pits. This part examines that vocabulary and the people who carried it, and in the process asks an uncomfortable question: why does God so often place a revelation in the hands of someone who cannot yet use it, and whose possession of it is bound to be resented by the generation that raised him? Joseph's dream provoked his brothers for thirteen years before it fed them. Samuel heard a word that condemned the priest who had taught him to answer. David was anointed in secret and hunted in public. In each case the youthful carrier is not merely young; he is *premature*, and the prematurity is part of the design.
