Beneath the narrative of Scripture there is a mechanism, and this part attempts to describe it. Revelation has a timeline: God can speak a thing centuries before the generation appointed to fulfil it appears. Purposes have soils and seasons, and a harvest cannot be forced. A word moves outward — from one person, to a household, to a people, to a generation, to the nations — and it changes form as it travels. Some things can be transmitted and some cannot: a mantle can be handed on, an anointing cannot. Disciples become carriers at definable moments. And the two sides of the generational covenant have opposite failure modes — the young can become fatherless, and the old can become childless in vision — and both failures are fatal to the same work.
