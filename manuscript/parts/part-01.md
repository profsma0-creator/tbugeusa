A word that God speaks to one person and that no one else ever hears is a word that dies in the room where it was spoken. Everything in this opening part is an exploration of that fact and of its consequence: that revelation, in Scripture, is never merely given — it is given *into custody*. Five chapters establish the pattern. They argue that God's normal method is a chain of responsible hands; that the men who receive the largest promises are frequently the men who die before seeing them; that seeing a thing and building it are separate callings; that God has chosen to be known by a name with three generations inside it; and that what a father can hand to a son is real, but it is not the same thing as what God must give him.
