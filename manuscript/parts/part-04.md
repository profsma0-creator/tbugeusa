This part is addressed to fathers, elders, mentors and the old, and it is the hardest section of the book to read from that position. Its argument is that the chief cause of generational loss in Scripture is not youthful rebellion but elderly silence. A generation can hold the whole counsel of God and forfeit it for the next generation by declining to speak — through neglect, through shame, through a mistaken sense of stewardship, or through the comfortable assumption that proximity is the same thing as transmission. Judges 2 does not say that the generation after Joshua rebelled. It says that they did not know. Somebody knew. The knowledge did not travel. Seven chapters examine what Scripture says about the responsibility of the older generation to speak before it is too late.
