### A Framework for Generational Discernment

Seven distinctions, each of which is regularly collapsed in practice. The right-hand column in each case is not the opposite of the left; it is the thing most easily mistaken for it.

#### 1. Inheritance from assignment

**Inheritance** is what can be handed over: a name, a body of instruction, material provision, a position, an introduction to God, a place among the people of God. It is transferable by ordinary human acts — teaching, blessing, commissioning, provision.
**Assignment** is what only God can give: a personal call, a personal encounter, a personal commission, and a personal accountability for the outcome. It is not transferable, and the attempt to transfer it produces men who occupy offices they have no interior authority to fill.
**The test:** Can the person articulate a call that is theirs, in their own words, without reference to their predecessor?

#### 2. Tradition from revelation

**Tradition** is the accumulated practice by which a community carries what it has received: forms of worship, patterns of order, habits of speech, inherited customs. It is necessary, and Scripture does not despise it — Paul commends the Corinthians for keeping the traditions as he delivered them (1 Corinthians 11:2).
**Revelation** is what God has spoken, and it is the standard by which every tradition must be judged. The distinction is not between old and new but between the servant and the master.
**The test:** Does this practice carry the word of God, or does it compete with it? Mark 7:13 describes traditions that make the word of God "of none effect."

#### 3. Experience from wisdom

**Experience** is what has happened to a person. It accumulates automatically and requires no virtue.
**Wisdom** is the skill of living rightly before God, and it may be present in the young and absent in the old. Solomon, asking for "an understanding heart," asked for wisdom, not for experience (1 Kings 3:9). Job's friends were older and wrong.
**The test:** Has this person's experience produced humility, or has it produced confidence in their own experience?

#### 4. Youthful vision from youthful arrogance

**Youthful vision** is a genuine perception of what God intends, held with the humility that recognizes how much is not yet known. It speaks, but it listens; it acts, but it submits.
**Youthful arrogance** is the same perception held as a possession, used as a warrant to dismiss instruction. Its characteristic marks are contempt for the past and impatience with process.
**The test:** How does the person respond to correction from someone with nothing to gain?

#### 5. Elderly wisdom from institutional resistance

**Elderly wisdom** is the accumulated discernment of a life lived before God, offered for the good of the next generation, and willing to be superseded.
**Institutional resistance** is the defence of a position using the language of fidelity. Its mark is that the objection is always to the *person* rather than to the *principle*.
**The test:** Would this objection have been raised if the proposal came from someone already inside the circle of influence?

#### 6. Prophecy from speculation

**Prophecy** is a word from God, and it has defining characteristics: it honours Christ, it accords with Scripture, it edifies the church, and it is subject to the judgment of others (1 Corinthians 14:29).
**Speculation** is a strong impression, often vivid, frequently sincere, and accountable to no one. Its characteristic is that it cannot be examined without offence being taken.
**The test:** Can this word be weighed, by more than one person, against Scripture, without the speaker treating the examination as hostility?

#### 7. Spiritual succession from personality imitation

**Spiritual succession** is when the substance of a ministry — its doctrine, its discipline, its love for the people — passes to another, who then operates as himself.
**Personality imitation** is when the surface is reproduced: the mannerisms, the vocabulary, the style, the preferences. It produces a photocopy rather than an heir.
**The test:** Would this successor be recognisable as the same *kind* of minister by a stranger who had never met the predecessor, and yet unmistakably a distinct person?
