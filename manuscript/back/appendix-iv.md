### Biblical Profiles of Fathers and Mentors

A companion to Appendix III, with the same method applied to the older generation.

#### Abraham

The first recipient of the covenant, and the first man of whom it is said that he would "command his children and his household after him" (Genesis 18:19). His method was conversation: he spoke to his servant about the angel (Genesis 24:7) and to Isaac about the oath. His principal legacy to his sons is a promise he did not see fulfilled.

#### Moses

The most complete example of deliberate succession in the Old Testament. He numbered his days by asking God for a successor (Numbers 27:16), identified him publicly, laid hands on him, gave him a charge, and encouraged him twice in the same speech (Deuteronomy 31:7–8). His failure was not in succession but at Meribah, and it cost him the land.

#### Eli

A priest for forty years, and the clearest negative example in Scripture of a father who did not speak. His sons "made themselves vile, and he restrained them not" (1 Samuel 3:13). The word of God came in his own house, in the same building, and he had to ask a child what it said.

#### David

Gathered the materials for a house he could not build, received the pattern by the Spirit, organised the worship of a temple that did not yet exist, charged the officers publicly, and prayed for his son in front of the whole assembly (1 Chronicles 29:18–19). His private failures were grave, and his public stewardship of the future was exemplary. Both are recorded.

#### Elijah

A prophet of enormous power and a man of sudden collapse. After Carmel he fled, asked to die, and was met by God not with rest but with a successor — a man in a field with twelve yoke of oxen, to whom he said nothing except by casting his mantle. He trained Elisha by proximity, over years, with almost no recorded instruction.

#### Paul

Wrote thirteen letters, most of them to churches he could not visit and individuals he could not supervise in person. His method of transmitting the deposit is visible in the pastoral epistles: doctrine as a "form of sound words" (2 Timothy 1:13) to be kept and re-committed, and personal affection as the mechanism by which doctrine survives.

#### Barnabas

The most underrated mentor in the New Testament. He vouched for Paul when the apostles were afraid of him (Acts 9:27), brought him into ministry at Antioch (Acts 11:25–26), and then took John Mark when Paul refused to (Acts 15:39). Both of his protégés, one of whom cost him a partnership, ended well.
