### The Generational Transitions of Scripture

Ten transitions, each stated in the same four lines: what was handed over, what the receiver had to do, what went wrong, and what Scripture says about the outcome.

#### 1. Abraham → Isaac

**Handed over:** the promise of land, seed and blessing; the covenant sign of circumcision.
**Required of the receiver:** to remain in the land during famine rather than flee to Egypt (Genesis 26:2).
**What went wrong:** Isaac repeated his father's lie about his wife (Genesis 26:7).
**Outcome:** the oath renewed explicitly "because that Abraham obeyed my voice" (Genesis 26:5); the wells redug and renamed (26:18).

#### 2. Isaac → Jacob

**Handed over:** the blessing, obtained by deception.
**Required of the receiver:** to leave, and to meet God for himself at Bethel.
**What went wrong:** twenty years of deception under Laban; a divided household.
**Outcome:** the name changed at Peniel; the promise renewed, though Jacob dies in Egypt.

#### 3. Jacob → Joseph

**Handed over:** a favoured position, and a coat.
**Required of the receiver:** endurance through slavery and prison.
**What went wrong:** the brothers' hatred sold the heir into Egypt.
**Outcome:** preservation of the line, and an oath about bones that holds for four hundred years.

#### 4. Moses → Joshua

**Handed over:** leadership, the pattern of the tabernacle, and a public charge.
**Required of the receiver:** courage, and a personal meditation on the book of the law (Joshua 1:8).
**What went wrong:** nothing in the transfer itself; the failure at Ai was Joshua's own.
**Outcome:** "all came to pass" (Joshua 21:45), and yet a partial possession (Judges 1).

#### 5. Eli → Samuel

**Handed over:** not an office but a function — prophetic hearing.
**Required of the receiver:** to answer a voice he did not yet recognise.
**What went wrong:** Eli's sons, and Eli's refusal to restrain them.
**Outcome:** "the Lord appeared again in Shiloh" (1 Samuel 3:21) — within a failing institution.

#### 6. Saul → David

**Handed over:** nothing; the throne was taken, not given.
**Required of the receiver:** fifteen years of waiting while anointed and hunted.
**What went wrong:** the old king used the whole apparatus of the state against his successor.
**Outcome:** a kingdom, and a permanent warning about refusing to release.

#### 7. David → Solomon

**Handed over:** the pattern, the materials, the workforce, the charge, and a public prayer.
**Required of the receiver:** to ask for an understanding heart rather than a new vision.
**What went wrong:** Solomon's later idolatry, and a divided kingdom under his son.
**Outcome:** the house built, and the warning that building is not finishing.

#### 8. Elijah → Elisha

**Handed over:** the mantle, and a place in the prophetic company.
**Required of the receiver:** to leave twelve yoke of oxen, and to refuse to leave his master's side.
**What went wrong:** nothing recorded; a clean succession.
**Outcome:** "the spirit of Elijah doth rest on Elisha" (2 Kings 2:15).

#### 9. Paul → Timothy

**Handed over:** the deposit of doctrine, the model of ministry, and personal affection.
**Required of the receiver:** to "hold fast the form of sound words," and to entrust it further.
**What went wrong:** Timothy's youth, his timidity, and a stomach ailment.
**Outcome:** two letters that have taught the church for two thousand years how to hand on what it has.

#### 10. Christ → the Apostles

**Handed over:** authority, the Spirit, the mission, and the interpretation of the Scriptures.
**Required of the receiver:** to wait, to be witnesses, and to make disciples.
**What went wrong:** denial, desertion, and a dispute about greatness on the night of the betrayal.
**Outcome:** Pentecost, and the church.
