Each profile below follows the same shape: the age or stage at which the person appears, the revelation they carried, the opposition they met, and what became of the word in their hands.

#### Joseph

Called by dreams at seventeen (Genesis 37:2). Sold at seventeen, enslaved, imprisoned, and elevated at thirty (Genesis 41:46). The revelation was given at the beginning of the thirteen-year gap, not at the end. Opposition came from his own household first. The word proved true in a foreign court, and its effect was to preserve the family that had rejected him.

#### Samuel

Called as a child — old enough to serve in the tabernacle, young enough not to know the voice of God (1 Samuel 3:7). The revelation was a judgment on the household of the priest who had raised him, and he had to deliver it in the morning. Opposition: none recorded, but the content of the word made him, from that day, a public figure (1 Samuel 3:20).

#### David

Anointed as a youth, probably no older than his early teens, and omitted by his own father when the family gathered before the prophet (1 Samuel 16:11). Killed Goliath, served in Saul's court, fled, and waited roughly fifteen years for the promise. The psalms written during that period are the church's permanent record of what it sounds like to carry an unfulfilled promise.

#### Josiah

King at eight, seeking God at sixteen, purging the land at twenty, and confronted by the recovered book at twenty-six (2 Kings 22:1; 2 Chronicles 34:3, 8). The revelation was not new — it was old, neglected, and found in the house of the Lord. His response, the rending of his clothes, is the model of a generation that receives its inheritance as judgment rather than as property.

#### Jeremiah

Called in the thirteenth year of Josiah, and objecting on the grounds of youth (Jeremiah 1:6). Told that he had been appointed before his birth (1:5) and forbidden to plead his age (1:7). Served for forty years without visible success, and died, according to tradition, in Egypt.

#### Daniel

Carried into captivity as a young man, probably in his mid-teens, and refusing the king's meat within the first years (Daniel 1:8). Received revelation in the night at the point where the empire's learning failed (Daniel 2:19). Served under at least four kings across seventy years, and outlived Babylon itself.

#### Mary

A young woman in an obscure town, told by an angel that she would bear the Christ, and answering with the only words that make the incarnation possible in a human life: "Behold the handmaid of the Lord" (Luke 1:38). Her song is a statement of the divine reversal, and it is the first theological utterance of the new covenant.

#### Timothy

The son of a Jewish mother and a Greek father, instructed in the Scriptures from childhood (2 Timothy 3:15), and taken by Paul at the age of perhaps twenty. Twice in Paul's letters he is told not to let anyone despise his youth (1 Timothy 4:12) and that he must not be ashamed (2 Timothy 1:8).

#### Titus

A Greek, uncircumcised, taken to Jerusalem as a test case (Galatians 2:3), and later left in Crete to "set in order the things that are wanting" (Titus 1:5). His task was institutional: to appoint elders in every city.

#### John Mark

A young man from a house that was a meeting place for the church (Acts 12:12), who left the first missionary journey at Perga (Acts 13:13), and whose departure caused a quarrel between Paul and Barnabas (Acts 15:39). Named by Paul as "profitable to me for the ministry" some twenty years later (2 Timothy 4:11), and credited by tradition with the second Gospel.
