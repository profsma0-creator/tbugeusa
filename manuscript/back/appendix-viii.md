These are addressed to the reader who is in the receiving position, and they assume that the reader takes both halves of this book seriously: the genuine responsibility of the young to carry what they have been given, and the genuine danger of youthful presumption.

#### On receiving

1. Name your elders. Not the people who have taught you most, but the people whose correction you would actually receive. If the list is empty, who is responsible?
2. Have you ever thanked a mentor specifically, in writing, for something they taught you? Do it.
3. Which of your convictions did you arrive at yourself? Which were handed to you? Can you tell the difference, and does it matter?
4. Is there a question you want to ask an older believer but have been afraid to ask? What are you protecting?

#### On your own calling

5. What has God called you to? State it in your own words, without quoting anyone else. If you cannot yet, what are you waiting for?
6. Has your calling been confirmed by anyone other than yourself? If not, what does that suggest?
7. Which part of your calling do you think God gave you directly, and which part did you receive from someone else?
8. Are you more willing to be given a vision than to execute a vision already given?

#### On impatience

9. What do you want to be doing in five years that you are not doing now? What is the honest reason you are not doing it?
10. When you disagree with a decision made by your elders, what do you do with the disagreement?
11. Is there an institution or tradition you find absurd? Have you read its history or asked why it exists?
12. What would you say to a younger version of yourself about how you have treated your elders?

#### On ambition

13. Describe the outcome you would consider success in your ministry. Then describe the outcome God would consider success. Compare them.
14. Who would have to fail for your ambitions to be fulfilled?
15. Do you want to be known for a doctrine, a gift, or a life? Be honest.
16. If you were never publicly recognised for your work, would you keep doing it?

#### On character

17. What is the temptation that most often defeats you? Who knows about it?
18. Is there a person to whom you are accountable for how you spend your time, your money and your attention?
19. What is the last thing you did when no one was watching that you would be ashamed to describe?
20. Name one area of character — not gift — that you are actively working on. What is the practice?

#### On the long obedience

21. Do you believe that God will still be doing His work if you never see the fruit of your labour?
22. What would you want your children to know about how you lived? Are you living that way?
23. If your generation is remembered for one thing, what will it be?
24. What will you hand to the generation after you that you did not receive?
25. Whom are you preparing? Name them.
