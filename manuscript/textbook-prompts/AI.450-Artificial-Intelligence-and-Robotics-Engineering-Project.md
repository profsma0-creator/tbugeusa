# AI.450 — PROJECT

**Course code:** AI.450  
**Course title:** Artificial Intelligence and Robotics Engineering Project  
**Year / semester:** Year 4 — Semester 2 (final year)  
**Proposed textbook title:** Artificial Intelligence and Robotics Engineering Project  
**Prompt type:** Textbook-generation prompt (final-year project handbook)  
**Status:** Verbatim transcription from curriculum document, with the TEXTBOOK PRODUCTION STANDARD appended.

> Write a comprehensive undergraduate textbook and project handbook titled **“Artificial Intelligence and Robotics Engineering Project”** for final-year Bachelor students, course code AI.450.
>
> The book must guide students through the complete lifecycle of a professional AI/robotics engineering project, from identifying a problem to research, system design, implementation, testing, documentation and final defence.
>
> Cover project-topic selection; problem identification; background study; literature review; research questions; objectives; requirements engineering; feasibility studies; system architecture; hardware selection; software selection; AI-model selection; robotic-platform selection; methodology; experimental design; dataset preparation; algorithm development; simulation; prototype development; integration; testing; validation; performance metrics; risk assessment; ethics; safety; budgeting; project scheduling; version control; technical documentation; academic writing; citation; plagiarism avoidance; results presentation; discussion; limitations; recommendations and future work.
>
> Include complete examples of AI/robotics projects such as:
>
> 1. Autonomous obstacle-avoiding robot.
> 2. Vision-based object-sorting robot.
> 3. AI-powered agricultural robot.
> 4. Autonomous mobile robot.
> 5. Voice-controlled robot.
> 6. Facial-recognition robot.
> 7. Smart surveillance robot.
> 8. Robot navigation using SLAM.
> 9. AI-based robotic arm.
> 10. Human-robot interaction system.
> 11. IoT-enabled autonomous robot.
> 12. Reinforcement-learning robot.
>
> For each example, provide the problem statement, objectives, literature-review direction, system architecture, hardware requirements, software requirements, methodology, algorithm, pseudocode, implementation approach, testing procedure, evaluation metrics, expected results, limitations, budget and possible future improvements.
>
> Include a complete template for writing the final-year project report from Chapter One through the final chapter, together with guidance for proposal writing, project presentation, viva voce defence and demonstration.
>
> Include assessment rubrics, supervisor checklists, project milestones, risk-management templates, testing tables and final-project evaluation criteria.

## TEXTBOOK PRODUCTION STANDARD (appended to this prompt)

> **TEXTBOOK PRODUCTION STANDARD:** Structure the textbook into approximately 10–15 substantial chapters, depending on the breadth of the course. Each chapter must contain:
>
> 1. Chapter title
> 2. Introduction
> 3. Learning objectives
> 4. Prerequisite knowledge
> 5. Core concepts explained progressively
> 6. Definitions of technical terminology
> 7. Worked examples
> 8. Mathematical derivations where applicable
> 9. Algorithms and pseudocode
> 10. Engineering/AI/robotics applications
> 11. Practical laboratory activity
> 12. Programming exercise where appropriate
> 13. Case study
> 14. Common mistakes and troubleshooting
> 15. Chapter summary
> 16. Key terms
> 17. Review questions
> 18. Multiple-choice questions
> 19. Short-answer questions
> 20. Numerical/problem-solving questions
> 21. Programming/design exercises
> 22. Mini-project
> 23. Further reading and references.
>
> Make the textbook **self-contained**. Do not assume that students already understand advanced concepts. When a concept depends on mathematics, programming, electronics, mechanics or another discipline, briefly teach the prerequisite before applying it.
>
> Use realistic engineering scenarios rather than artificial examples wherever possible. Every major theoretical concept should be connected to an actual AI, robotics, automation, embedded-system or autonomous-system application.
>
> Where programming is required, provide complete runnable examples, explain the code line by line where pedagogically useful, explain expected output, identify common errors and provide extension exercises.
>
> Where hardware is involved, explain the components, connections, operating principles, safety considerations and troubleshooting procedures.
>
> Where algorithms are involved, provide the intuition, formal description, pseudocode, complexity analysis, worked example and practical implementation.
>
> Where mathematical equations are involved, define every symbol and explain the physical or computational meaning of the equation.
>
> Maintain a clear distinction between **theory, worked examples, practical laboratory work, case studies and independent exercises**.
>
> Do not merely mention technologies such as Python, C++, ROS, OpenCV, MATLAB, Arduino, Raspberry Pi, TensorFlow or PyTorch. Teach their relevant use within the context of the course.
>
> End the complete textbook with a comprehensive glossary, bibliography, subject index, practical-laboratory index and list of recommended software/hardware tools.
>
> The final manuscript must read like a professionally authored university textbook rather than AI-generated lecture notes. Avoid repetitive chapter structures, repetitive explanations and unnecessary filler. Use varied explanations, authentic engineering examples and technically precise language throughout.
