## From Mentorship to Fatherhood

**And this Part begins with a distinction that the apostle Paul drew in one verse, and the verse has been quoted in conferences for two hundred years without producing the thing it describes.**

*For though ye have ten thousand instructors in Christ, yet have ye not many fathers: for in Christ Jesus I have begotten you through the gospel* (1 Corinthians 4:15).

**Ten thousand instructors, and not many fathers.**

**Which means that Paul was not describing a shortage of teaching. He was describing a surplus of it.**

**The word he uses for *instructors* is *paidagōgous*, and it names a specific function in the first-century household.** A *paidagōgos* was a trusted slave whose duty was to escort a boy to his lessons, supervise his conduct, and hand him over to his teachers. **He was competent, necessary, and paid for. And he was not the boy's father, and nothing he did for the boy could make him so.**

**Which is the distinction this chapter exists to establish, and it is not a criticism of mentoring. It is a statement about a category difference.**

**A mentor is a man who meets another man's need. A father is a man who gives another man his life.** And the two are separated by a boundary that no amount of scheduling, curriculum design or goodwill can cross, **because what is on the other side of the boundary is ownership and sacrifice and grief and a name.**

**And Paul's own account of which of the two he was is given in a sentence that no instructor would ever write:**

*So being affectionately desirous of you, we were willing to have imparted unto you, not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls, because ye were dear unto us* (1 Thessalonians 2:8).

**Not the gospel only, but also our own souls.**

**Which is the definition of the thing this whole Part is about. The deposit can be transmitted by an instructor. The life cannot.**

### What mentoring is, and what it is not

**And the case for mentoring is not being dismantled here, because every father in Scripture was also a mentor, and the relation between Elijah and Elisha was conducted in the ordinary way — through time, travel, work, meals and errands.**

**But the limits of the arrangement must be stated before the thing itself is described, and there are four of them.**

**The first limit is scope.** A mentor is engaged for a purpose and within a category. **He teaches the skills of preaching, or the handling of finances, or the reading of Greek.** And when the skill is transferred the arrangement has achieved its objective, **and it may properly end, or become something else, without any failure on either side.**

**The second limit is schedule.** A mentor gives time and keeps the rest. **And a father's whole day is available, including the hours in which he would rather be alone.**

**And the third limit is exposure.** A mentor's failures are not the pupil's inheritance, and a pupil who discovers that his mentor was wrong about something can quietly take what was useful and leave. **A son has no such option, and he will either be damaged by his father's faults or carry them to his own grave.**

**And the fourth limit is death.** A mentor's departure ends an arrangement. **A father's departure is a thing that a son has to survive, and the son who watched Elijah rise in a whirlwind tore his own clothes in two at the sight of it (2 Kings 2:12).**

**Which means that the change from mentor to father is not a matter of a longer programme or a deeper friendship. It is a change in what the older man is willing to lose.**

**And the sentence that states it exactly is the one Paul wrote to the Thessalonians, and it is addressed to a congregation he had been with for a matter of weeks:**

*But we were gentle among you, even as a nurse cherisheth her children* (1 Thessalonians 2:7).

**A nurse cherishing her children. Which is the imagery of a woman with an infant at her breast, applied by an apostle to his own ministry, in a letter.**

**And then the next verse, in the same breath: *so being affectionately desirous of you, we were willing to have imparted unto you, not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls.***

**Which means the affection preceded the labour and the cost was measured in himself.**

### Moses and Joshua, or the man who was a servant before he was an heir

**And the oldest case in Scripture is the clearest on the mechanics, because the process is described in the vocabulary of service before it is described in the vocabulary of succession.**

**Joshua appears in the narrative for the first time in the middle of a battle plan, and he is given a troop and an objective:**

*And Moses said unto Joshua, Choose us out men, and go out, fight with Amalek: to morrow I will stand on the top of the hill with the rod of God in mine hand* (Exodus 17:9).

**Which is a delegation of authority with a visible demonstration that the authority is not the man's own.** Moses stood on the hill and Joshua fought in the valley, **and the outcome of the battle was determined by the hands of the man who was not fighting.**

**And the detail of verse 12 belongs in every discussion of fatherhood, because it records what the older man needed.**

*But Moses' hands were heavy; and they took a stone, and put it under him, and he sat thereon; and Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands, the one on the one side, and the other on the other side; and his hands were steady until the going down of the sun.*

**His hands were heavy. And they put a stone under him.**

**Which means that the leader who was sustaining the whole battle had to be physically supported by two men, and that the leadership of Israel at that moment was a bench, a stone and four hands.**

**And the bond that was formed between the young soldier and the old man whose hands were held up was not professional.**

**And the label attached to Joshua from then on is the one that defines the intermediate stage: *And Moses rose up, and his minister Joshua: and Moses went up into the mount of God* (Exodus 24:13).**

**His minister. In the Hebrew, *meshāreth*, the one who serves at table.**

**Which means the future leader of the nation was a servant in the household of the prophet, and he went up the mountain because he carried the things.**

**And the next appearance of Joshua is the one that tells us what was happening in him, and it is jealousy for his father's honour:**

*And Joshua the son of Nun, the servant of Moses, one of his young men, answered and said, My lord Moses, forbid them. And Moses said unto him, Enviest thou for my sake? would God that all the Lord's people were prophets, and that the Lord would put his spirit upon them!* (Numbers 11:28–29).

**Which is a young man reporting to his master that other men were prophesying without permission, and the master's rebuke, which is also the first correction of the young man's instinct in the whole book.**

**Enviest thou for my sake?**

**And the two sentences in that exchange must be placed against each other, because they show what the two men were made of.** Joshua's instinct was that the honour of the house belonged to the house. **Moses' instinct was that the Spirit of God should fall on everybody, including men outside the arrangement.**

**Which means that the older man had work to do before the younger man was fit to lead, and the work was not the transfer of information.** He had to be cured of the instinct to defend a franchise.

**And he was. Forty years later, when Moses was dead and Joshua was in command, Joshua's account of his commission to the two and a half tribes is the language of a man who knew whose gift he was administering (Joshua 22:1–6), and by the end of his life he could say *as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord* (24:15) — which is the sentence of a father and not of a manager.**

### Elijah and Elisha, or the mantle that was thrown and the water that was poured

**And the second case is the fullest account of a fatherly succession in the Old Testament, and it is told with a detail that many sermons pass over, because the detail is the boring part.**

**The beginning was a covering, and it was given without a conversation:**

*So he departed thence, and found Elisha the son of Shaphat, who was plowing with twelve yoke of oxen before him, and he with the twelfth: and Elijah passed by him, and cast his mantle upon him* (1 Kings 19:19).

**And what Elisha did with it is recorded in two verses, and it is the most complete break with a previous life in the whole of Scripture:**

*And he left the oxen, and ran after Elijah, and said, Let me, I pray thee, kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow thee. And he said unto him, Go back again: for what have I done to thee? And he returned back from him, and took a yoke of oxen, and slew them, and boiled their flesh with the instruments of the oxen, and gave unto the people, and they did eat. Then he arose, and went after Elijah, and ministered unto him* (1 Kings 19:20–21).

**He slaughtered the yoke. He burned the harness for fuel. He fed the neighbourhood with the meat of his own livelihood.**

**Which means the mantle was cast over a man who was in the middle of a working day, and his answer was to liquidate the business within the hour, and then *he arose, and went after Elijah, and ministered unto him.***

**And the last three words are the curriculum. He ministered. For how long, we are not told, but the range is somewhere between eight and twenty-five years, and the work was whatever the old man needed.**

**And the proof of what the work consisted of is in the mouth of a servant of the king of Israel, twenty years later, when a king asked whether there was a prophet of the Lord available:**

*Here is Elisha the son of Shaphat, which poured water on the hands of Elijah* (2 Kings 3:11).

**Which poured water on the hands of Elijah.**

**That was the description on record.** In the national memory of the northern kingdom, the successor to Elijah was the man who brought the basin and poured the water when the prophet washed before eating. **And the king's response was *the word of the Lord is with him.***

**Which means that the qualification that mattered was not his education and not his eloquence. It was that he had washed the hands of the man whose spirit he now carried.**

**And the final journey is the last examination, and it was conducted as three refusals:**

*And Elijah said unto Elisha, Tarry here, I pray thee; for the Lord hath sent me to Beth-el. And Elisha said unto him, As the Lord liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee* (2 Kings 2:2).

**Three times, at Gilgal, at Bethel, and at Jericho, the old man told the young man to stay behind.** And three times the answer was the same oath: *as the Lord liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee.*

**Which is a test and not a rejection.** Elijah was not trying to get rid of him; he was establishing whether the young man would go as far as the work went. **And note that the sons of the prophets at both towns knew what was happening and told Elisha so, and his answer was *yea, I know it; hold ye your peace* (2:3, 5).** He knew the old man was leaving, and he stayed attached to him anyway.

**And the question at the crossing is the only recorded instance in Scripture of a successor naming his own request:**

*And it came to pass, when they were gone over, that Elijah said unto Elisha, Ask what I shall do for thee, before I be taken away from thee. And Elisha said, I pray thee, let a double portion of thy spirit be upon me* (2 Kings 2:9).

**A double portion.**

**And the Hebrew is the technical term of the inheritance law, *pi shnayim*, the portion that the firstborn son received — twice what the other sons received (Deuteronomy 21:17).**

**Which means the request was not for twice as much of Elijah's power as Elijah had. It was the request of a firstborn son for his father's estate, in the language of the law, made by a man who had been pouring water on his father's hands for years.**

**And Elijah's answer is the sentence that makes the whole arrangement a matter of God and not of the older man:**

*And he said, Thou hast asked a hard thing: nevertheless, if thou see me when I am taken from thee, it shall be so unto thee; but if not, it shall not be so* (2 Kings 2:10).

**Which means the older man had no power to grant the request, and said so, and gave the condition instead.**

**And the end of the scene is where the word *father* is finally used, and it is not used by Elijah. It is used by Elisha, shouting at the sky:**

*And Elisha saw it, and he cried, My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof. And he saw him no more: and he took hold of his own clothes, and rent them in two pieces* (2 Kings 2:12).

**My father. And then he tore his own clothes, which is the act of a mourner for a parent.**

**Which means that whatever had been arranged in the official vocabulary of the schools of the prophets, the actual relation between the two men was father and son, and the son named it at the moment he lost it.**

**And the immediate proof that the mantle had been transferred is the return across the same river:**

*And he took the mantle of Elijah that fell from him, and smote the waters, and said, Where is the Lord God of Elijah? and when he also had smitten the waters, they parted hither and thither: and Elisha went over* (2 Kings 2:14).

**Where is the Lord God of Elijah?**

**Which is not a rhetorical question and not a cry of doubt. It is a son standing at a river with his father's garment in his hand, asking whether his father's God is his own God, and discovering that He is.**

**And the sons of the prophets at Jericho gave the verdict: *the spirit of Elijah doth rest on Elisha* (2:15).**

**Which means the confirmation of a fatherly succession is not the announcement of the older man. It is what the watching community says when they see the son do the work.**

### And what a father leaves to a family, not to a man

**And the account of Elisha must include the detail that most readings of the succession omit, and it is the difference between a father and a mentor.**

**A mentor takes one man. A father has a household, and the household outlives the arrangement.**

**And the record of Elisha's ministry contains three scenes in which the people who came to him were addressed as a family, and the family had needs.**

**The first is a widow of one of the sons of the prophets, and her crisis was that the community's provisions had not survived her husband's death:**

*Now there cried a certain woman of the wives of the sons of the prophets unto Elisha, saying, Thy servant my husband is dead; and thou knowest that thy servant did fear the Lord: and the creditor is come to take unto him my two sons to be bondmen* (2 Kings 4:1).

**And note what Elisha's answer required of her and of her children:**

*Then he said, Go, borrow thee vessels abroad of all thy neighbours, even empty vessels; borrow not a few. And when thou art come in, thou shalt shut the door upon thee and upon thy sons, and shalt pour out into all those vessels, and thou shalt set aside that which is full* (2 Kings 4:3–4).

**The miracle was performed in a locked house with a widow and her two sons, and it stopped when there were no more vessels.**

**Which means that a fatherly ministry leaves a house with an income and not merely with a memory.** And the last instruction is the provision: *go, sell the oil, and pay thy debt, and live thou and thy children of the rest* (4:7).

**The second scene is the building project, and it is the least supernatural story in the whole account:**

*And the sons of the prophets said unto Elisha, Behold now, the place where we dwell with thee is too strait for us. Let us go, we pray thee, unto Jordan, and take thence every man a beam, and let us make us a place there, where we may dwell. And he answered, Go ye* (2 Kings 6:1–2).

**The place where we dwell with thee is too strait for us.**

**Which means the company had outgrown the premises, and the answer was a building programme in which every man cut a beam.** And the old man went with them when asked (6:3), **and when the borrowed axe head fell into the water, the crisis was announced as *alas, master! for it was borrowed* (6:5), which is the anxiety of a young man with an obligation he cannot meet.**

**And the iron swam, and the word of God was applied to a man's debt.**

**Which means that a father's ministry extends to his sons' borrowed tools.** There is no subject too small for the household to bring to him.

**And the third scene is the founding of the schools themselves, and the phrase is the one this whole chapter is about.** *The sons of the prophets.* Which is what the community of Elisha's disciples was called, in Scripture, as a matter of course, **and the name was not sentimental. It described what they were.**

### Paul, and the vocabulary of fatherhood

**And the New Testament does not leave the matter to imagery. Every one of the relationships Paul had with the men he raised was described by him in family terms, and he used the whole range of them.**

**To Timothy, four times over:**

*Unto Timothy, my own son in the faith* (1 Timothy 1:2).

*To Timothy, my dearly beloved son* (2 Timothy 1:2).

*This charge I commit unto thee, son Timothy, according to the prophecies which went before on thee* (1 Timothy 1:18).

*Thou therefore, my son, be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus* (2 Timothy 2:1).

**To Titus: *mine own son after the common faith* (Titus 1:4).**

**To Onesimus, a runaway slave whom he had met in a prison: *I beseech thee for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds* (Philemon 10).**

**To the Corinthians, in the verse that defines the chapter: *I write not these things to shame you, but as my beloved sons I warn you ... for in Christ Jesus I have begotten you through the gospel* (1 Corinthians 4:14–15).**

**And to the Galatians, in the language of a mother, because the imagery of the household is not restricted to the male:**

*My little children, of whom I travail in birth again until Christ be formed in you* (Galatians 4:19).

**And the substance of the relation is set out in three sentences that no mentor has ever said about a pupil.**

**The first is about what the older man wanted.** *For I seek not yours, but you: for the children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children* (2 Corinthians 12:14).

**Not yours, but you.**

**Which is the sentence that separates the two categories. An instructor wants the pupil's attendance, fees, effort and achievement. A father wants the man.**

**And the second is about the cost, and it is stated with a willingness that is almost frightening:** *And I will very gladly spend and be spent for you; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved* (2 Corinthians 12:15).

**And the third is about what a father does when the son fails, and it is the language of discipline:**

*Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby ... for what son is he whom the father chasteneth not?* (Hebrews 12:11, 7).

**Which means the correction is the evidence of the relation and not the proof of its absence, and that a man who has never been corrected by another man in the house of God should ask what kind of relation he is in.**

**And Paul's own use of that instrument is on record, and he used it deliberately:**

*But I will come to you shortly, if the Lord will, and will know, not the speech of them which are puffed up, but the power ... What will ye? shall I come unto you with a rod, or in love, and in the spirit of meekness?* (1 Corinthians 4:19, 21).

**Shall I come with a rod?**

**Which means the father was prepared to wound, and said so in advance, and offered the congregation the choice.** And the same man wrote *and I will very gladly spend and be spent for you* in another letter, **and the two sentences are the same disposition applied to two different conditions of the son.**

### "As a son with the father, he hath served with me"

**And the working relation between Paul and Timothy is the fullest case in the New Testament, and its details are worth assembling because they answer the practical question of what a father and a son actually do together.**

**They served together in the gospel.** *But ye know the proof of him, that, as a son with the father, he hath served with me in the gospel* (Philippians 2:22).

**The criterion Paul uses for Timothy is not that he was taught. It is that he had been proved.** And the proof is stated in terms of service in the same work.

**And the older man sent the younger into difficulty.** *So he sent into Macedonia two of them that ministered unto him, Timotheus and Erastus; but he himself stayed in Asia for a season* (Acts 19:22). **Which means Timothy was deployed, not merely accompanied.**

**And the older man recommended him to churches that did not know him.** *Now if Timotheus come, see that he may be with you without fear: for he worketh the work of the Lord, as I also do. Let no man therefore despise him* (1 Corinthians 16:10–11).

**Which is an older leader doing what an older leader can do and a young man cannot: assigning weight to a man in advance, in writing, to strangers.**

**And the older man sent the younger to represent him.** *For this cause have I sent unto you Timotheus, who is my beloved son, and faithful in the Lord, who shall bring you into remembrance of my ways which be in Christ* (1 Corinthians 4:17). **Which means the son was the vehicle by which the father's ways were carried to a place the father could not go.**

**And the older man trusted the younger with difficult work, and named the reason.** *But I trust in the Lord Jesus to send Timotheus shortly unto you, that I also may be of good comfort, when I know your state. For I have no man likeminded, who will naturally care for your state. For all seek their own, not the things which are Jesus Christ's* (Philippians 2:19–21).

**I have no man likeminded.**

**Which is an older man saying in writing, about all his other colleagues, that only one of them carried the same concern.** And the word for *likeminded* is *isopsychos*, of equal soul. **Which is what a son is to a father and a colleague is not.**

**And the older man gave the younger his own instruction and his own doctrine.** *And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also* (2 Timothy 2:2). **Which is the fourth link in a chain — Paul, Timothy, faithful men, others also — and it is stated in a single verse as the normal arrangement.**

**And in the letters, the father hands over his whole charge, and does it with a formula of inheritance:** *This charge I commit unto thee, son Timothy ... that thou by them mightest war a good warfare* (1 Timothy 1:18).

**And the last thing the father says about the son is the proof that the arrangement worked.** In the final letter, written from a prison, expecting execution, with a list of men who had left and a list of men who had stayed:

*Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry* (2 Timothy 4:11).

**Take Mark, and bring him with thee.**

**Which is a sentence about a man who had once failed and been abandoned, and whose restoration is the next case in this chapter.**

### Barnabas, and the father of other men's rejections

**And the last case is the one that should be read by every man who has ever been given a young person nobody else wanted, and it belongs to Barnabas, whose name in Scripture is explained as *the son of consolation* (Acts 4:36).**

**The first thing he did was to take Saul of Tarsus when the whole church in Jerusalem was afraid of him:**

*And when Saul was come to Jerusalem, he assayed to join himself to the disciples: but they were all afraid of him, and believed not that he was a disciple. But Barnabas took him, and brought him to the apostles, and declared unto them how he had seen the Lord in the way, and that he had spoken to him, and how he had preached boldly at Damascus in the name of Jesus* (Acts 9:26–27).

**They were all afraid of him, and Barnabas took him.**

**Which means the man who became the apostle of the Gentiles was admitted into the church by another man's personal guarantee, and without that guarantee he would have remained outside.**

**And the second thing he did was to go and find him when he was no longer anywhere:**

*Then departed Barnabas to Tarsus, for to seek Saul: and when he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch. And it came to pass, that a whole year they assembled themselves with the church, and taught much people. And the disciples were called Christians first in Antioch* (Acts 11:25–26).

**He went to seek him. Which means Saul had been absent for years, and was not pursuing an opportunity, and was found by a man who had decided to go and get him.** And the year in Antioch, the partnership in teaching, and the name *Christian* first applied to the disciples, are all recorded in the same two verses.

**Which means the single most consequential ministry in the history of the church began with a man fetching a forgotten brother out of Tarsus.**

**And the third thing he did was to take the young man nobody else would take, and to lose his own ministry over him.**

*And some days after Paul said unto Barnabas, Let us go again and visit our brethren in every city where we have preached the word of the Lord, and see how they do. And Barnabas determined to take with them John, whose surname was Mark. But Paul thought not good to take him with them, who departed from them from Pamphylia, and went not with them to the work. And the contention was so sharp between them, that they departed asunder one from the other: and so Barnabas took Mark, and sailed unto Cyprus* (Acts 15:36–39).

**So Barnabas took Mark, and sailed unto Cyprus.**

**Which means that the older man chose the son over the partnership, and the separation was permanent, and the Book of Acts follows Paul from that point and says almost nothing more about Barnabas.**

**And the judgment of history appears to have been against Barnabas for a long time, because Paul's argument was cogent.** Mark had abandoned the work at Pamphylia; the mission needed reliability; and Paul was the greater man.

**And the last mention of Mark in Scripture is in Paul's final letter, and it is Paul asking for him:**

*Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry* (2 Timothy 4:11).

**Which means that the son Barnabas rescued became profitable to the man who had refused him, and that Paul wrote the sentence himself, and that the man who wrote it was about to die.**

**And the assessment of Barnabas's decision is now beyond dispute, because the same Mark is named by Peter as *Marcus my son* (1 Peter 5:13), and the church that has read the Gospel of Mark for two thousand years has been reading the work of the abandoned son.**

**Which means that the father who loses a partnership to keep a son has made an investment that a calculation of the ministry's efficiency cannot evaluate.**

### The five things a father gives that a mentor cannot

**And the difference between the two relations can be set down as an inventory, because Scripture demonstrates each item.**

**The first is the name.** A mentor has pupils; a father has sons, and he says so in public. *Unto Timothy, my own son in the faith*; *mine own son after the common faith*; *my son Onesimus.* **And a name in the mouth of an older man is a standing in the house, and it is the cheapest and most powerful thing he owns.**

**The second is access — and access to the older man's failures and not only his strengths.** *And Elisha ... poured water on the hands of Elijah.* Which means he was in the room when the old man ate, and travelled, and was tired, and was angry, and was wrong. **A son learns from a father's defeats, and an apprentice is shown only the workshop.**

**And the third is a third thing: the older man's own soul.** *We were willing to have imparted unto you, not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls, because ye were dear unto us.* **Which is what makes the relation durable and what makes it dangerous, because a father is vulnerable in proportion to his investment and will be wounded in proportion to both.**

**The fourth is correction that costs the father something.** *What will ye? shall I come unto you with a rod, or in love, and in the spirit of meekness?* And *the rod and reproof give wisdom: but a child left to himself bringeth his mother to shame* (Proverbs 29:15). **Which means that a man who never corrects the young men around him does not love them, whatever he feels, because *he that spareth his rod hateth his son* (Proverbs 13:24).**

**And the fifth is a place in the family that survives the father's death.** *And the sons of the prophets* — a community that outlived Elijah and continued after Elisha, with dwellings and widows and debts and provisions, because a household had been founded and not merely an apprenticeship conducted.

**And the last thing a father gives is the thing that cannot be given by an instructor at all, and it is stated in the promise of Malachi and claimed by the Lord for John the Baptist:**

*And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse* (Malachi 4:6).

*And he shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children* (Luke 1:17).

**The heart.**

**Which means that the whole work of this Part is a work of the affections, and that a house of God can run ten thousand programmes and produce no children, because the missing element is not the curriculum. It is a heart turned.**

### And the young man's part in this

**And the chapter must end with what is required on the other side, because the relation cannot be manufactured by the older man alone.**

**Elisha answered the mantle by burning his harness.** The call came to him in the middle of an ordinary working day and he responded before he had any idea what the work would be. **And what he did for the next years is described in one word: he ministered.**

**And Jonathan's case is the one for a man whose father is in the wrong, and the record of what he did is worth setting out, because it is a model for a whole category of readers.**

**His father was the king, and he had ordered the death of David, and Jonathan *delighted much in David* (1 Samuel 19:1–2).** And what he did was threefold.

**He warned him.** *Now therefore, I pray thee, take heed to thyself until the morning, and abide in a secret place, and hide thyself.*

**He spoke to his father on David's behalf, and spoke well of him.** *And Jonathan spake good of David unto Saul his father* (19:4). **Which is the hardest duty of a son who sees his father's fault, and it is the opposite of the son who takes the grievance to the gate.**

**And he kept the covenant even after he understood that the cost was his own throne.** *And he said unto him, Fear not: for the hand of Saul my father shall not find thee; and thou shalt be king over Israel, and I shall be next unto thee; and that also Saul my father knoweth. And they two made a covenant before the Lord* (1 Samuel 23:17–18).

**And the sentence that describes what a young man in that position is for is a single line: *and he went to David into the wood, and strengthened his hand in God* (23:16).**

**He strengthened his hand in God.**

**Which is what a son of a failing house can still do for the generation that is coming, and it is not rebellion and it is not silence. It is finding the man in the wood, and strengthening him in God, and making a covenant with him before the Lord, and going home.**

### The practical construction of the relation

**And this Part exists to construct rather than to describe, and so the chapter ends with the arrangements, and there are six of them.**

**First: define the relation and name it out loud.** A man who has been called a trainee for five years has not been fathered, however much he has learned. **The naming of a son is the act that begins the relation, and it costs the older man his independence.**

**Second: put the son in the room where the work is difficult, and not only where it is presented.** Elisha poured water on hands; Timothy was sent into Macedonia while the father stayed in Asia. **A son who has only ever watched is a spectator with good notes.**

**Third: let him see your household.** The qualification for office in Timothy's letters is the household, and the reason is that the household is the only place where a man cannot project an image. **A young man who has never been in your house has not seen you and cannot inherit you.**

**Fourth: correct him, and do it before the other men in the house.** The rod and reproof give wisdom; and the correction proves the relation. **And a father who praises his son publicly and never corrects him privately is producing a man who will be destroyed by the first honest assessment he receives.**

**Fifth: give him your work, and not merely your lessons.** *The things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men.* **And the committing is an act, with a date, after which the son is responsible for the thing.**

**And sixth: be prepared to lose for him what Barnabas lost.** A partnership, a reputation, a preferred plan, and in the end the years. **And the last word on the calculation is the sentence Paul wrote about a man who had once been rejected, which he wrote at the end of his own life, from a cell, with almost nobody left: *take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.***

**Which means the son who was rescued by the man who lost everything for him became, in the end, the one who was worth bringing.**

### Principles

**First: the New Testament distinguishes instructors from fathers, and it was written to a congregation with ten thousand of the first and not many of the second.** *For though ye have ten thousand instructors in Christ, yet have ye not many fathers: for in Christ Jesus I have begotten you through the gospel.*

**Second: a father gives his own soul and not only the message.** *So being affectionately desirous of you, we were willing to have imparted unto you, not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls, because ye were dear unto us.*

**And third: the older man's care is described in the imagery of nursing, and his authority in the imagery of a father charging his children.** *Even as a nurse cherisheth her children*; and *how we exhorted and comforted and charged every one of you, as a father doth his children.*

**Fourth: the intermediate stage between the call and the inheritance is service, and it lasts for years.** Joshua was *his minister* and Elisha *poured water on the hands of Elijah*, and that was the line on the national record decades later.

**And fifth: a young man's instinct to defend his father's franchise must be corrected by the father himself.** *Enviest thou for my sake? would God that all the Lord's people were prophets.*

**Sixth: the son asks for the inheritance in the language of the law, and the father has no power to grant it.** *Let a double portion of thy spirit be upon me* — *thou hast asked a hard thing.*

**Seventh: the relation is not crowned until the son does the work of the father.** *And he took the mantle of Elijah that fell from him, and smote the waters* — *and when he also had smitten the waters, they parted.*

**And eighth: the confirmation of the succession comes from the watching community and not from the departing man.** *The spirit of Elijah doth rest on Elisha.*

**Ninth: a father leaves a household with provisions and not only a memory.** *Go, sell the oil, and pay thy debt, and live thou and thy children of the rest*; and *the place where we dwell with thee is too strait for us*; and *alas, master! for it was borrowed.*

**And tenth: the relation is named in public and in writing, and the naming creates the standing.** *Unto Timothy, my own son in the faith*; *mine own son after the common faith*; *my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds.*

**Eleventh: a father seeks the man and not the man's usefulness.** *For I seek not yours, but you: for the children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children.*

**And twelfth: a father corrects, and the correction is the evidence of the relation.** *For what son is he whom the father chasteneth not?* And *he that spareth his rod hateth his son.*

**And thirteenth: a father keeps the son even at the cost of a partnership, and the outcome is beyond the reach of his own calculation.** *So Barnabas took Mark, and sailed unto Cyprus* — and thirty years later, *take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.*

### For the fathers

- **Ask which men you have taught and which men you have fathered, and count them separately.** Ten thousand instructors is a large and respectable number.
- **Give a man your soul and not only your subject.** *We were willing to have imparted unto you, not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls.*
- **Name him in public and in writing, and give him the standing that the name carries.** *Unto Timothy, my own son in the faith.* A father's public naming is the cheapest inheritance he owns and the one most often withheld.
- **Take him into your house, and let him see you eat.** Elisha's qualification was that he poured water on the hands of Elijah, and the hands were washed at a table.
- **Correct him, and do it before he fails publicly.** *What will ye? shall I come unto you with a rod, or in love, and in the spirit of meekness?*
- **Correct also the instinct to defend your position rather than the truth.** *Enviest thou for my sake? would God that all the Lord's people were prophets.*
- **And be prepared to lose a partnership for a son.** Barnabas took Mark and sailed to Cyprus, and the last word about Mark in Scripture is Paul asking for him by name.
- **And leave a household, not a memory.** Provisions for widows, a place to dwell, and a debt paid for a borrowed axe head.

### For the young

- **Answer the call in the middle of an ordinary day.** Elisha was ploughing with the twelfth yoke when the mantle came, and he burned the harness before he knew what the work was.
- **Count on serving before you inherit.** *Elisha the son of Shaphat, which poured water on the hands of Elijah* — that was the description, and it was not an insult.
- **Stay with him to the end, and refuse the three offers to stay behind.** *As the Lord liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee.* And do not be surprised that the man you are following is dying; *I know it; hold ye your peace.*
- **Ask for the inheritance and not for the office.** *Let a double portion of thy spirit be upon me.* And accept that the older man cannot give it to you and that God must.
- **Do not wait for a partnership to work.** Timothy was sent into Macedonia while the father stayed in Asia; the proof of him was that *as a son with the father, he hath served with me in the gospel.*
- **Recognise your father where God has given him and not where you would prefer him.** Timothy's own father was a Greek, and the faith came to him through his grandmother and his mother, and the father God gave him was the man who wrote *my dearly beloved son.*
- **And if your natural father is in the wrong, do what Jonathan did.** Warn, speak well of him to his face, make your covenant before the Lord with the man who is coming, strengthen his hand in God, and go home.
- **And be findable.** Barnabas had to go to Tarsus and search for Saul, and the whole history of the church turned on that search.

### Questions for reflection

1. Whose son in the faith are you, and would he say so if he were asked?
2. Who would call you father, and can they name the day you said it?
3. What have you poured water on his hands for — or is your mentorship conducted entirely in a classroom?
4. Which of your sons has never seen you fail, and what has that cost him?
5. Whose rejection are you prepared to carry the way Barnabas carried Mark's?
