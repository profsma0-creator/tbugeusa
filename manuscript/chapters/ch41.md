## "Greater Works": The Theology of Continuation

**And now the promise itself, stated twice by the Lord Jesus, and stated in terms that would be presumptuous if He had not said them.**

*Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that believeth on me, the works that I do shall he do also; and greater works than these shall he do; because I go unto my Father* (John 14:12).

**And the reason attached to the promise is the part that is usually ignored, and it is the whole of the doctrine of continuation.**

**The greater works are conditional on His departure. *Because I go unto my Father.***

**Which means the enlargement of the work was not going to happen while the master was present, and it was not going to happen despite his absence. It was going to happen *because* of it.**

### The departure as the mechanism

**And the Lord said this more than once, and in the plainest possible terms.**

*Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you* (John 16:7).

**The Greek for *expedient* is *sumpherei*, it is profitable, it is to your advantage, it is better for you.** And what was better for them was His absence.

**Which is a sentence that every older man in Christian work should read while imagining his own retirement.** The Lord of glory told His disciples that their advantage lay in His removal, **and He said it to men who had just been told that they would all be scattered and that one of them would deny Him.**

**And the explanation is given in the same Gospel, in a parenthesis that the Evangelist supplies so that nobody would misunderstand the timing:**

*But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive: for the Holy Ghost was not yet given; because that Jesus was not yet glorified* (John 7:39).

**The Spirit could not be given while Jesus was unglorified, and Jesus could not be glorified without departing.**

**So the architecture of the whole plan is this: the departure, the glorification and the outpouring are one movement, and the greater works are the fruit of it.** The Lord's presence in a body in Galilee was a limitation on the number of people He could reach and the number of places He could be. **And the coming of the Spirit in His place removed the limitation.**

**Which gives the first application of this chapter, and it is the hardest one for an older man to accept.**

**A ministry that is tied to one man's presence is, by the Lord's own comparison, a lesser ministry than one that is not.** And the reason is not that the man is deficient but that presence in a body is spatially and temporally bounded, **whereas a ministry carried by the Spirit in a body of believers is not.**

**A father who has never made his ministry independent of himself has, without intending it, capped it at the size of his own body.** And the reason it feels like humility to say *I must be here for this to work* is that it is in fact the most flattering sentence a leader can utter.

### Luke's word: "began"

**And the New Testament's own structure demonstrates the doctrine, and it does so with a single word in the first verse of the book of Acts.**

*The former treatise have I made, O Theophilus, of all that Jesus began both to do and teach, until the day in which he was taken up* (Acts 1:1–2).

**The Gospel of Luke is a record of what Jesus *began* to do and teach.**

**Which means that the book of Acts is the continuation of the same work, performed by other hands.** Luke wrote two volumes of one history, and in the second volume the principal actor is the Holy Spirit working through men who were not the Lord Jesus — **and Luke describes the second volume as the continuation of what the first volume began.**

**And the same language appears in Hebrews, in a passage that describes the transmission of the gospel as a chain of custody:**

*How shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation; which at the first began to be spoken by the Lord, and was confirmed unto us by them that heard him; God also bearing them witness, both with signs and wonders, and with divers miracles, and gifts of the Holy Ghost, according to his own will?* (Hebrews 2:3–4).

**Three stages: spoken by the Lord, confirmed by those who heard Him, and attested by God with signs.** And the writer of Hebrews is a fourth stage, reporting what he received from the men who heard.

**Which means the New Testament itself is a document of generational transmission.** Every book in it was written by somebody who received what he wrote from somebody else, or from the Lord directly, **and the chain is stated openly and treated as a credential and not as an embarrassment.**

**And there is no book in the New Testament written by a man who claimed to have invented anything.**

### The day the promise was delivered

**And the delivery of the promise is described as an event with the marks of an inauguration.**

*And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place. And suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind, and it filled all the house where they were sitting. And there appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance* (Acts 2:1–4).

**Note four details, and each of them is deliberate.**

**They were all together, and of one accord.** *Homothumadon* — with one passion, one impulse. **The same word marks every significant advance in Acts, and it describes a unity of purpose rather than a uniformity of opinion.**

**The sound came from heaven and filled the house.** The Greek is *pnoes biaias*, a violent breath, a blast. **The Spirit arrives as a wind that occupies space.**

**The fire sat on each of them — *eph' hena hekaston auton*, upon each one individually.** Not on the group as a body, and not on a leader who would then distribute it. **The endowment was personal and universal in that room.**

**And they spoke as the Spirit gave them utterance.** *Kathos to pneuma edidou apophtheggesthai autois* — the Spirit was giving them the utterance, continuously. **The speaking was not a skill they possessed; it was an operation performed through them.**

**And then Peter explains the event by quoting a prophecy whose first content is generational:**

*And it shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams: and on my servants and on my handmaidens I will pour out in those days of my Spirit; and they shall prophesy* (Acts 2:17–18).

**Read the categories: sons and daughters, young men and old men, servants and handmaidens.**

**The first thing the Spirit-filled church announced about itself was that revelation had been distributed across the generations and across the social orders.** The young would see visions and the old would dream dreams, **and both would be speaking, and both would be the Spirit's instruments, on the same day, in the same room.**

**And the explanation of the whole event is stated by Peter in one sentence about the ascended Lord:**

*Therefore being by the right hand of God exalted, and having received of the Father the promise of the Holy Ghost, he hath shed forth this, which ye now see and hear* (Acts 2:33).

**The Greek for *hath shed forth* is *execheen*, he poured out.** And the subject of the verb is the exalted Christ.

**So the Spirit was poured out by the departed Lord, from the Father's right hand.** Which means the absence of the master was not a vacancy. **It was a relocation, and the work that followed was the same work, executed from a higher position and through a greater number of people.**

### The numbers, and what they prove

**And the promise of greater works is recorded in quantity as well as in quality, and the arithmetic of the book of Acts is part of the evidence.**

**A hundred and twenty in the upper room** (Acts 1:15). **Three thousand added in a day** (Acts 2:41). **Five thousand men** shortly after (Acts 4:4), and multitudes added (Acts 5:14). **And by the time Paul returns to Jerusalem, the estimate given to him by the elders is already in the tens of thousands:**

*Thou seest, brother, how many thousands of Jews there are which believe; and they are all zealous of the law* (Acts 21:20).

**The Greek is *myriades* — myriads, tens of thousands.**

**Compare the numbers.** The Lord's public ministry was conducted in a small country, and its largest recorded crowd was perhaps five thousand men plus women and children, gathered once and dispersed. **Here are myriads of believers in one city, within thirty years, without the Lord being physically present at any of it.**

**And the greater works are also visible in kind.**

**During the three years of the Lord's ministry there is no record of any of the Twelve healing a person who then rose from the dead.** After Pentecost there are two such accounts, and both are incidental to the narrative rather than staged:

*But Peter put them all forth, and kneeled down, and prayed; and turning him to the body said, Tabitha, arise. And she opened her eyes: and when she saw Peter, she sat up* (Acts 9:40).

*And Paul went down, and fell on him, and embracing him said, Trouble not yourselves; for his life is in him ... And they brought the young man alive, and were not a little comforted* (Acts 20:10, 12).

**A widow in Joppa, restored to her sewing and her almsdeeds; and a young man in Troas who fell out of a third-storey window during a long sermon, restored to his friends.**

**Which is what the promise looked like in practice.** Not a larger version of the same spectacular ministry, but the same power distributed among more people, in more places, over more decades, **until a man in Rome could write letters to churches in four provinces that had been planted by people he had never met.**

**And the glory of the arrangement is that the Lord's own words were vindicated.** He said the works would be greater. **And they were.**

### The body that grows by itself

**And the mechanism of the continuation is described in the letter to the Ephesians in one of the most remarkable sentences in the New Testament.**

*And he gave some, apostles; and some, prophets; and some, evangelists; and some, pastors and teachers; for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ ... From whom the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth, according to the effectual working in the measure of every part, maketh increase of the body unto the edifying of itself in love* (Ephesians 4:11–12, 16).

**Take the sentence apart, because it is a description of a self-replicating organism.**

**The gifted men are given *for the perfecting of the saints*** — the Greek is *pros ton katartismon ton hagion*, toward the equipping, the mending, the completing of the ordinary believers. **The purpose of the leadership gifts is to make the members capable, not to do the work on their behalf.**

**And the object of the equipping is *the work of the ministry*** — which by the grammar belongs to the saints and not to the officers. **The ministry is done by the body, and the officers exist to prepare the body to do it.**

**And then the growth is described: *the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth.***

**The Greek for *every joint* is *pases haphes*, every contact, every point of connection — and the verb is *epichoregias*, supplying, funding, furnishing.** The body grows by what passes through its own joints. **There is no external pump, and no leader under whom the whole thing hangs.**

**And the measure is individual: *according to the effectual working in the measure of every part.***

**Each part has its own measure and operates in its own measure, and the body's increase is the sum of those operations.** Which means a body in which only the leader is operating in his measure is a body that is not growing, **whatever the attendance figures say.**

**And the result is stated in the last four words: *the edifying of itself in love.***

**The body builds itself.** *Eis oikodomen heautou en agape.* **A ministry that requires the founder's presence for the edification to happen has not yet become a body.**

**And Paul's other image is the same doctrine in a different figure.** *For the body is not one member, but many* (1 Corinthians 12:14); *now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular* (1 Corinthians 12:27). **And in that body the weaker members are necessary (12:22), the less honourable receive the greater honour (12:23), and the members have the same care one for another (12:25).** Which is the arrangement that makes a continuation possible, **because a body in which every member depends on one member dies with that member.**

### The four generations, stated as a command

**And the most direct statement of the mechanism in the whole New Testament is a single sentence with four generations in it.**

*And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also* (2 Timothy 2:2).

**Count them.**

**Paul, who has heard and taught.** **Timothy, who heard from Paul.** **Faithful men, who receive from Timothy.** **Others also, who are taught by the faithful men.**

**Four generations in one sentence, in the last letter of an apostle who knew he was about to die, written as a command to a young man who was still working out how to do his job.**

**And note the three qualifications attached to the deposit and the men.**

**The deposit is *the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses*.** Not a private revelation, not a personal interpretation: **the apostolic teaching, delivered publicly and verifiable by those who were present.**

**The men are *faithful*** — the Greek is *pistois*, trustworthy, reliable — and the word is not *gifted*, or *eloquent*, or *effective*. **The criterion for receiving the deposit is reliability.**

**And they must be *able to teach others also*** — *hikanoi kai heterous didaxai*, competent also to teach others.

**Which means the test of a candidate for the deposit is whether he can pass it on.** Not whether he understands it, not whether he appreciates it, and not whether he will keep it safe: **whether he can teach it, which means whether he will produce a fourth generation.**

### The pattern in creation: seed in itself

**And the arrangement is not a New Testament innovation. It belongs to the order of creation.**

*And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so* (Genesis 1:11).

**The phrase that identifies the principle is *whose seed is in itself*.**

**God did not create a world of isolated objects. He created a world of organisms each of which contains its own means of reproduction.** And the clause is repeated in verse 12 as a description of what actually happened. **Which means that reproduction is not an occasional activity of certain things; it is the definition of a living kind.**

**And the Lord Jesus applied the same logic to His own death, and to the fruitfulness of His disciples:**

*Verily, verily, I say unto you, Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit* (John 12:24).

**And note the alternative offered: *it abideth alone.***

**A seed that does not die remains a single seed, and it is not condemned for it — it simply has no fruit.** And the same sentence applies to a ministry that will not release anything: **it abideth alone, and its aloneness is not a mark of purity. It is the consequence of a refusal.**

**And the reward of the dying seed is stated by Isaiah of the Servant, in words that describe the fruit of the cross:**

*Yet it pleased the Lord to bruise him; he hath put him to grief: when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin, he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the Lord shall prosper in his hand. He shall see of the travail of his soul, and shall be satisfied* (Isaiah 53:10–11).

**He shall see his seed.** And **he shall be satisfied** — which is the promise given to every father in the faith who is wondering whether the cost was worth it.

**And the letter to the Hebrews applies that whole passage to Christ and to His people, and it quotes Isaiah 8 to do it:**

*For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren, saying, I will declare thy name unto my brethren, in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto thee ... And again, Behold I and the children which God hath given me* (Hebrews 2:11–13).

**Behold I and the children which God hath given me.**

**That is Isaiah 8:18, which appeared in an earlier chapter of this book as the statement of a prophet about the disciples he had gathered to himself.** And the writer of Hebrews applies it to Christ and the church. **Which tells us that the relationship between a teacher and the men he has formed is an image of the relationship between Christ and believers, and the image is one of a father and children.**

**And the Psalms record the same arithmetic as a divine accounting:**

*A seed shall serve him; it shall be accounted to the Lord for a generation. They shall come, and shall declare his righteousness unto a people that shall be born, that he hath done this* (Psalm 22:30–31).

**Note the clause *it shall be accounted to the Lord for a generation.***

**The Hebrew idea is that a lineage is credited to the Lord as a generation — the seed standing in the record as a generation of worshippers.** And the declaration runs forward: *a people that shall be born.*

**Which is the promise of continuation stated in the language of book-keeping.** A man's seed is counted as a generation, and the generation declares to a people not yet born.

### The two ways the promise is evaded

**And the great promise of greater works is evaded in two directions, and both evasions are common in the same house.**

**The first evasion is the house that defers the promise indefinitely.** It honours the founders, it teaches the doctrine of the founders, it celebrates the history of the founders, **and it has never expected anything greater than what the founders did, and it discourages the young men who suggest that something might be.**

**And the tell of such a house is not that it lacks the language. It is that the young people in it can describe the past in detail and the future only in the subjunctive.** *Perhaps one day ... if the Lord tarries ... when we have the resources.*

**And the second evasion is the young man who claims the greater works as his own achievement.**

**Corinth had this problem in its earliest years, because the congregation had begun to align itself with personalities and to take credit for the ministries it preferred.** And Paul's answer is one of the most important paragraphs in the New Testament:

*Who then is Paul, and who is Apollos, but ministers by whom ye believed, even as the Lord gave to every man? I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase. So then neither is he that planteth any thing, neither he that watereth; but God that giveth the increase. Now he that planteth and he that watereth are one: and every man shall receive his own reward according to his own labour* (1 Corinthians 3:5–8).

**Note the three elements.**

**They are *one*.** The planter and the waterer are not rivals; they are two operations of one work. **Which is the answer to the whole question of whether the young successor eclipses the founder.**

**And the increase is *God's*.** *Neither is he that planteth any thing* — the Greek is *ti*, what? What is Paul? What is Apollos? **Ministers by whom you believed, and nothing more, and the measure of what they were given was distributed by God.**

**And every man is rewarded *according to his own labour*** — which means that the planter is not diminished by the waterer's fruit, and the waterer is not credited with the planter's work. **Each has his own reward, and the reward is not the size of the harvest.**

**Which is the only way for two generations to work the same field without either despising the other.** The predecessor will be rewarded for planting, the successor for watering, and God will be glorified for the increase — **and the increase will be greater than either of them could have produced alone, which was the point of the promise all along.**

### The measure of a continuation

**And the chapter closes with the test, because a promise of greater works can be claimed by anybody and the claim must be examined.**

**The test is not the size of the crowd at the peak, and it is not the budget, and it is not the reputation of the man.**

**The test is whether the work is still being done after he is gone, and by men he did not have to supervise.**

*Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain* (John 15:16).

**Fruit that remains.**

**And the way to know whether fruit is going to remain is to look for a fourth generation.** Not the men who admire you, and not the men who attend your meetings, **but the people those men have taught and cannot stop talking about.**

**And if you cannot find a third or fourth generation, the answer is not despair. It is the recognition of where the chain stopped, and the determination to build it in the time that remains.** A man who has been in Christian work for thirty years and has no spiritual grandchildren should treat that fact as the most urgent information he has ever received about his ministry.

**And there is a further test, and it is the one the Lord's own method supplies.** *The works that I do shall he do also.* **Which means the continuation is recognisable.** The disciples did the kinds of things their master did — healing, casting out, preaching, feeding, washing feet — **because they had been with Him, and what they received was not merely information about Him but His manner of working.**

**So the question for a father is not only *will the work continue* but *will the work that continues be this work.*** And the only way to guarantee the second is to have given the men not only what you know but what you are.

**Which is the whole of it.** The greater works are the works of the Lord, distributed among more people than He had while He walked, **and the distribution happens through fathers who give their lives to men the way Paul gave his — the gospel, and also their own souls (1 Thessalonians 2:8).**

### Principles

**First: the greater works were conditioned on the Lord's departure.** *Greater works than these shall he do; because I go unto my Father.* And *it is expedient for you that I go away.*

**Second: the Spirit could not be given until the Son was glorified.** *The Holy Ghost was not yet given; because that Jesus was not yet glorified.* The departure, the glorification and the outpouring are one movement.

**Third: the New Testament presents itself as a continuation.** *Of all that Jesus began both to do and teach* — Luke's second volume is the same work done by other hands. And Hebrews 2:3 describes the gospel as spoken by the Lord and confirmed by those who heard Him.

**Fourth: the first thing the Spirit-filled church announced about itself was a multi-generational outpouring.** *Your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams.* Both generations, on the same day, in the same room.

**Fifth: the promise was fulfilled in quantity as well as in kind.** One hundred and twenty, then three thousand, then five thousand, then multitudes, then *myriades* of believing Jews in Jerusalem — and men raised from the dead by disciples who could not do it while the Lord was present.

**Sixth: the body grows by its own joints, not by an external pump.** *Fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth ... maketh increase of the body unto the edifying of itself in love.* Leadership exists *for the perfecting of the saints*.

**Seventh: the command of continuation is a sentence containing four generations.** *The things that thou hast heard of me ... commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.* And the qualification is not gifting but reliability plus the capacity to teach.

**Eighth: reproduction belongs to the created order.** *Whose seed is in itself.* And the seed that will not die *abideth alone* — not condemned, and fruitless.

**And ninth: the continuity is recognisable, and the measure of it is fruit that remains.** *The works that I do shall he do also*; and the test is whether somebody taught by somebody you taught is still doing this work.

### For the fathers

- **Build the thing so that it works when you are not in the room.** *It is expedient for you that I go away.* A ministry that requires your presence is capped at the size of your body.
- **Equip the saints rather than performing for them.** *For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry.* The officers exist to make the members capable, and the body increases through every joint.
- **Give the deposit to reliable men who can teach, and measure them by the fourth generation.** *Faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.* If a man cannot teach, he is not yet a candidate however gifted he is.
- **Do not defer the greater works indefinitely.** A house where the future can only be described in the subjunctive has stopped expecting the promise.
- **Accept that your reward and your successor's reward are different and both are real.** *Every man shall receive his own reward according to his own labour.* The planter is not diminished by the waterer.
- **And be willing for the seed to fall into the ground.** *Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone.* Aloneness is not purity; it is the consequence of a refusal.

### For the young

- **You have been given a foundation you did not lay, and the promise was made for your generation.** *Greater works than these.* Do not spend your life merely conserving the work of the men who came before you.
- **But do not claim the increase.** *Neither is he that planteth any thing, neither he that watereth; but God that giveth the increase.* And the planter and the waterer are one.
- **Learn your predecessor's manner of working and not only his message.** *The works that I do shall he do also.* What you received is meant to be recognisable, and that requires more than his notes.
- **Be the man who can teach others also.** That is the whole qualification in 2 Timothy 2:2, and it means that your usefulness will be measured by people who are not in the room yet.
- **And make sure there is somebody behind you.** If you are the last link in the chain, the chain stops with you regardless of how well you have done your own work.
- **And remember whose seed you are.** *He shall see his seed ... and shall be satisfied.* The travail of the ministry is not wasted, and it is not measured in a single lifetime.

### Questions for reflection

1. What happens in your work during the weeks when you are absent?
2. Which of your young men can teach what he has received, and who is he teaching?
3. Where have you described the future only in the subjunctive, and what would it take to say it in the indicative?
4. What did your father in the faith give you that was not his message — and have you given it to anybody?
5. If the greater works are to be greater through your successors, what are you doing this year to make them possible?
