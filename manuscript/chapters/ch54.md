## The Danger of Untested Revelation

**And this chapter begins with two commands that stand next to each other in the same letter, and the second is the consequence of the first.**

*Despise not prophesyings. Prove all things; hold fast that which is good* (1 Thessalonians 5:20–21).

**Despise not. Prove. Hold fast.**

**Which means that the discipline of testing is not the enemy of the prophetic gift. It is the only arrangement under which the gift can be honoured for very long.**

**And the reasoning is plain once it is stated. A house that despises prophecy never tests it, because there is nothing to test. And a house that believes every claimed word will eventually despise prophecy, because the false words accumulate until the whole gift is discredited by association.**

**Which leaves exactly one possible position, and it is the apostolic one. Expect the word of God to be spoken among you; examine everything that claims to be it; keep what survives.**

**And the New Testament commands the testing as explicitly as it commands the speaking:**

*Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world* (1 John 4:1).

**Believe not every spirit.**

**Which is written by the apostle of love, in the letter about love, to a congregation he loved.**

### The five tests that Scripture supplies

**And the tests are not left to the intuition of the hearer. They are given, and there are five of them, and a house that uses all five will rarely be deceived.**

**The first is the scriptural test, and it is the master test, because it does not depend on anything in the man or the moment.**

*To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them* (Isaiah 8:20).

**Which is the standard to which every claimed revelation is brought. And the model of its use is a congregation in a Macedonian synagogue:**

*These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so* (Acts 17:11).

**Note the two halves of that commendation, because they must be held together. They received the word with all readiness of mind — the Bereans were eager for it, and not suspicious. And they searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.**

**Which means that the noblest congregation in the book of Acts was simultaneously the most receptive and the most examining, and the two qualities are described as one virtue.**

**And the scriptural test outranks the credentials, the signs, and the name of the speaker. Paul extended it as far as it can be extended:**

*But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed* (Galatians 1:8).

**Even an angel.**

**The second test is the Christological one, and it is the test of the person and work of the Lord:**

*Hereby know ye the Spirit of God: Every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God: and every spirit that confesseth not that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is not of God: and this is that spirit of antichrist* (1 John 4:2–3).

**Which is a test of substance and not of vocabulary.** The question is not whether the name of Jesus is used — the false prophets use it; the question is whether the Christ who is confessed is the Christ whom the apostles preached, **incarnate, crucified, risen, and Lord.**

**And the third test is the moral test, and it is the most startling of the five, because it establishes that a sign can be genuine and the message still be false:**

*If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder, and the sign or the wonder come to pass, whereof he spake unto thee, saying, Let us go after other gods, which thou hast not known, and let us serve them; thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet ... for the Lord your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul* (Deuteronomy 13:1–3).

**The sign comes to pass, and the message is false, and God is proving His people.**

**Which means that the power of a word is not the test of its source.** A man may perform what he said he would perform and be leading the congregation away from God, **and the fulfilment is not evidence — it is the examination.**

**And the same test is given by the Lord in the Sermon on the Mount, applied to teachers:**

*Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Ye shall know them by their fruits ... Wherefore by their fruits ye shall know them* (Matthew 7:15–16, 20).

**By their fruits, which in the context of a teacher means what his ministry produces in the lives of the people who follow it.** And the Lord's own standard of fruit is obedience: *not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven* (7:21).

**The fourth test is the fulfilment test, and it is the one Deuteronomy gives to the ordinary Israelite who has no means of judging otherwise:**

*And if thou say in thine heart, How shall we know the word which the Lord hath not spoken? When a prophet speaketh in the name of the Lord, if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which the Lord hath not spoken, but the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously: thou shalt not be afraid of him* (Deuteronomy 18:21–22).

**Thou shalt not be afraid of him.**

**Which is the point of the test. The purpose of testing is to remove fear as well as error.** A congregation that knows how to test a word does not have to live in dread of the next one.

**And the Lord gave the same test about His own words: *heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away* (Matthew 24:35).** Which means that the standard of prophetic accuracy in Scripture is not a high percentage. It is exactness.

**The fifth test is the corporate one, and it is the one that protects a congregation from a single voice:**

*Let the prophets speak two or three, and let the other judge. If any thing be revealed to another that sitteth by, let the first hold his peace. For ye may all prophesy one by one, that all may learn, and all may be comforted. And the spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets. For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints* (1 Corinthians 14:29–33).

**Four elements, and every one of them is a rule.**

**Two or three, and the others judge.** No revelation in the assembly is self-authenticating; it is spoken in the presence of men who are charged to weigh it.

**If something is revealed to another that sits by, the first holds his peace.** The word does not become a competition, and a man who is speaking is expected to stop when another is given something.

**The spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets.** Which means that a man in the grip of a genuine revelation remains in control of his own conduct and his own timing. **Any claimed word that takes a man over and makes him disorderly is failing the apostolic test in the verse itself.**

**And the reason is stated: God is not the author of confusion, but of peace.**

**Which means that disorder in an assembly is not evidence of the Spirit's presence. It is evidence to the contrary.**

### The four hundred and the one

**And the clearest case in the Old Testament of the necessity of testing is a consultation between two kings, and it is a case in which the majority were unanimous and wrong.**

*And Jehoshaphat said unto the king of Israel, Enquire, I pray thee, at the word of the Lord to day. Then the king of Israel gathered the prophets together, about four hundred men, and said unto them, Shall I go against Ramoth-gilead to battle, or shall I forbear? And they said, Go up; for the Lord shall deliver it into the hand of the king* (1 Kings 22:5–6).

**Four hundred prophets, one answer, and the answer was favourable.**

**And Jehoshaphat, a king who had his faults and who was nevertheless a man who feared God, asked the one question that saved the consultation:**

*And Jehoshaphat said, Is there not here a prophet of the Lord besides, that we might enquire of him?* (1 Kings 22:7).

**Is there not here a prophet of the Lord besides?**

**Which is the question every older believer should be willing to ask when a large meeting has given a unanimous word.** Not because unanimity is wrong, but because unanimity is not a test.

**And Ahab's answer is one of the most revealing sentences in Scripture:**

*And the king of Israel said unto Jehoshaphat, There is yet one man, Micaiah the son of Imlah, by whom we may enquire of the Lord: but I hate him; for he doth not prophesy good concerning me, but evil. And Jehoshaphat said, Let not the king say so* (1 Kings 22:8).

**He hated him, in his own words, because the man did not prophesy good concerning him.**

**And with that sentence the whole mechanism is exposed. The supply of prophecy in Ahab's court was determined by the appetite of the king.** The four hundred said *go up* because *go up* is what the king had come to hear, **and the one man who said otherwise was in disfavour for that reason alone.**

**And the messenger's advice to Micaiah on the way to the court is the sentence that every young prophet in an older institution will hear in some form:**

*Behold now, the words of the prophets declare good unto the king with one mouth: let thy word, I pray thee, be like the word of one of them, and speak that which is good* (1 Kings 22:13).

**Let thy word be like the word of one of them.**

**Which is the offer of respectability, and it is made by a servant who means well and who is described as hoping to save the prophet trouble.** And the answer is the only answer available to a man who has actually heard from God:

*And Micaiah said, As the Lord liveth, what the Lord saith unto me, that will I speak* (1 Kings 22:14).

**And then the vision of the throne, and the spirit who offered to be a lying spirit in the mouth of all the prophets, and God's permission, and the conclusion:**

*Now therefore, behold, the Lord hath put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these thy prophets, and the Lord hath spoken evil concerning thee* (1 Kings 22:23).

**Which is the most sobering sentence in the chapter, and it is stated twice — once by an unnamed prophet earlier in Ahab's reign (21:20–22) and once here — that God may give a man over to the deception he has chosen.**

**And the response of the court to the true word was twofold, and both elements are instructive.**

**The slap: *But Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah went near, and smote Micaiah on the cheek, and said, Which way went the Spirit of the Lord from me to speak unto thee?* (22:24).**

**Which is the question of a man who has been prophesying by his own spirit and who cannot tell the difference.** He assumed the Spirit had left him for Micaiah in the way that an office changes hands.

**And the imprisonment: *Put this fellow in the prison, and feed him with bread of affliction and with water of affliction, until I come in peace* (22:27).**

**Which is what a house does with the one voice it does not want to hear.** And the king did not come in peace; he died in that battle, and the dogs licked his blood as the word of the Lord had said (22:35–38).

**And the last thing Micaiah said is the finest thing in the passage, and it is his own offer of a test:**

*And Micaiah said, If thou return at all in peace, the Lord hath not spoken by me. And he said, Hearken, O people, every one of you* (1 Kings 22:28).

**He submitted his word to verification, publicly, and invited the whole nation to judge the outcome.**

**Which is the mark of a man who knows where his word came from.** A false prophet cannot afford to be tested; a true one can afford nothing else.

### Hananiah, and the prophecy of peace

**And the second case is the one that gives the church its bias, and it occurred within the lifetime of Jeremiah and in the temple, in front of the priests and all the people.**

**Jeremiah had been walking about with a wooden yoke on his neck, announcing that Judah would serve Babylon. And Hananiah the prophet spoke:**

*Thus speaketh the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, saying, I have broken the yoke of the king of Babylon. Within two full years will I bring again into this place all the vessels of the Lord's house, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon took away ... And I will bring again to this place Jeconiah the son of Jehoiakim king of Judah, with all the captives of Judah ... for I will break the yoke of the king of Babylon* (Jeremiah 28:2–4).

**And Jeremiah's first response was not a denunciation:**

*Even the prophet Jeremiah said, Amen: the Lord do so: the Lord perform thy words which thou hast prophesied* (Jeremiah 28:6).

**Amen.**

**Which means that the true prophet's instinct was to desire the pleasant word to be the true one.** He was not enjoying his message; he wanted the captivity to end.

**And then he gave the test, and it is the sentence on which the church's judgement about comfortable prophecies should rest to this day:**

*The prophets that have been before me and before thee of old prophesied both against many countries, and against great kingdoms, of war, and of evil, and of pestilence. The prophet which prophesieth of peace, when the word of the prophet shall come to pass, then shall the prophet be known, that the Lord hath truly sent him* (Jeremiah 28:8–9).

**The prophets before me and before thee prophesied war, and evil, and pestilence.**

**Which is a statement about the historical pattern of the prophetic office.** The word of God to a nation in sin is rarely a word of comfort, and the men who carried it were almost always men the nation would rather not have heard.

**And the prophet of peace must wait for vindication.** Not for the approval of the congregation, and not for the agreement of the other prophets, **but for the event.**

**And Hananiah did what false prophets do when they are contradicted.** He took the yoke from Jeremiah's neck and broke it, in front of everyone, and restated the prophecy with a date attached (28:10–11). **And Jeremiah went his way** — which is the only possible response when a man is determined.

**And the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah afterwards, and it replaced wood with iron, and it is one of the most terrible messages in the book:**

*Thus saith the Lord; Thou hast broken the yokes of wood; but thou shalt make for them yokes of iron* (Jeremiah 28:13).

**And the conclusion:**

*Then said the prophet Jeremiah unto Hananiah the prophet, Hear now, Hananiah; The Lord hath not sent thee; but thou makest this people to trust in a lie. Therefore thus saith the Lord; Behold, I will cast thee from off the face of the earth: this year thou shalt die, because thou hast taught rebellion against the Lord. So Hananiah the prophet died the same year in the seventh month* (Jeremiah 28:15–17).

**Two months.**

**Which means the date Hananiah gave — two full years — was answered by a death within two months.**

**And the charge against him is stated exactly: *thou makest this people to trust in a lie*, and *thou hast taught rebellion against the Lord.***

**Which is the seriousness of the offence. A false prophet is not a man who is mistaken. He is a man who builds a structure of false confidence under a people, and its collapse takes them with it.**

### "I have dreamed, I have dreamed"

**And the third case is the longest passage in Scripture about false prophecy, and it is Jeremiah's, and it is directed at the mechanism rather than the men.**

*Thus saith the Lord of hosts, Hearken not unto the words of the prophets that prophesy unto you: they make you vain: they speak a vision of their own heart, and not out of the mouth of the Lord. They say still unto them that despise me, The Lord hath said, Ye shall have peace; and they say unto every one that walketh after the imagination of his own heart, No evil shall come upon you* (Jeremiah 23:16–17).

**They speak a vision of their own heart.**

**Which is the most precise description of the phenomenon in Scripture.** Not a lie invented for gain, and not a demonic oracle, **but a vision that came out of the man's own heart and was delivered in the name of the Lord.**

**And the content of those visions is given, and it is always the same message:**

*They say still unto them that despise me, The Lord hath said, Ye shall have peace; and they say unto every one that walketh after the imagination of his own heart, No evil shall come upon you.*

**Peace to those who despise God, and no evil to those who follow their own imagination.**

**And the question that follows is the test of the office:**

*For who hath stood in the counsel of the Lord, and hath perceived and heard his word? who hath marked his word, and heard it?* (Jeremiah 23:18).

**Who has stood in the counsel of the Lord?**

**And then the complaint itself, and the phrase that captures the whole problem:**

*I have heard what the prophets said, that prophesy lies in my name, saying, I have dreamed, I have dreamed. How long shall this be in the heart of the prophets that prophesy lies? yea, they are prophets of the deceit of their own heart; which think to cause my people to forget my name by their dreams which they tell every man to his neighbour, as their fathers have forgotten my name for Baal* (Jeremiah 23:25–27).

**Which think to cause my people to forget my name by their dreams.**

**Which is the effect of untested revelation, and it is stated with terrible accuracy. The dreams were not denying God. They were being told in His name, every man to his neighbour, and their cumulative effect was to fill the memory of the congregation with the dreams until there was no room left for the name.**

**Which means that the danger of a flood of unexamined revelation is not that the people will believe something false about God. It is that they will gradually stop knowing anything true about Him, having had their capacity for attention occupied by a stream of words that were pleasant, and spiritual, and their own.**

**And the Lord's verdict on the two is a question of grain:**

*The prophet that hath a dream, let him tell a dream; and he that hath my word, let him speak my word faithfully. What is the chaff to the wheat? saith the Lord. Is not my word like as a fire? saith the Lord; and like a hammer that breaketh the rock in pieces?* (Jeremiah 23:28–29).

**What is the chaff to the wheat?**

**Which is the gentlest and most useful of all the tests in this chapter, because it does not require the hearer to identify the source of a word. It requires him to tell the difference between chaff and wheat.**

**And every farmer knows the difference without a laboratory.** Chaff is the husk that comes off the grain and blows away; it is light, it is plentiful, and it has no food in it. **And wheat is heavy, and it feeds people, and it has to be ground before it can be eaten.**

**Which means that the final test of a word about God is what it does when a man takes it into his life.**

**And the words that break rocks are the ones that survive.** *Is not my word like as a fire? and like a hammer that breaketh the rock in pieces?*

**And the last sentence of that section gives the effect of the counterfeits, and it is the sentence this chapter exists to prevent:**

*Behold, I am against them that prophesy false dreams, saith the Lord, and do tell them, and cause my people to err by their lies, and by their lightness; yet I sent them not, nor commanded them: therefore they shall not profit this people at all, saith the Lord* (Jeremiah 23:32).

**By their lightness.**

**Which is the whole method. A word that has not been through the fire of a man's own obedience, that costs the speaker nothing, that fits the mood of the audience, and that could be said by anyone — that is lightness, and God says it will not profit this people at all.**

### The man whose eyes were open and whose heart was hired

**And the fourth case is the most troubling in the Old Testament, because it establishes that a man may be given an accurate revelation and be destroyed by what he wanted.**

**Balaam was a prophet of genuine power, and the record of what he received is extraordinary:**

*And he took up his parable, and said, Balaam the son of Beor hath said, and the man whose eyes are open hath said: he hath said, which heard the words of God, which saw the vision of the Almighty, falling into a trance, but having his eyes open* (Numbers 24:3–4).

**Falling into a trance, but having his eyes open.**

**And the man's own declarations were impeccable:**

*If Balak would give me his house full of silver and gold, I cannot go beyond the commandment of the Lord, to do either good or bad of mine own mind; but what the Lord saith, that will I speak* (Numbers 24:13).

**And he prophesied the star out of Jacob and the sceptre out of Israel, four hundred years before David, and it was fulfilled.**

**And the judgement of Scripture on him is stated three times in the New Testament, and every time it is about what he loved:**

*Which have forsaken the right way, and are gone astray, following the way of Balaam the son of Bosor, who loved the wages of unrighteousness* (2 Peter 2:15).

*Woe unto them! for they have gone in the way of Cain, and ran greedily after the error of Balaam for reward* (Jude 11).

*But I have a few things against thee, because thou hast them that hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel* (Revelation 2:14).

**He loved the wages. He ran greedily for reward. And when he could not curse the people, he taught Balak how to make them curse themselves.**

**Which means that the revelation was real, the man was accurate, and the man was destroyed.** And the lesson is precisely the one the whole church needs in an age of abundant spiritual gifts: **the genuineness of the gift is not the test of the man. It never was.**

**And that lesson is the subject of the next chapter.**

### A true statement from a false spirit

**And the fifth case is the one that most directly instructs a congregation, because it concerns a testimony that was entirely accurate and was nevertheless refused.**

**Paul was going to prayer in Philippi, and a certain damsel possessed with a spirit of divination followed him and his company for days, crying:**

*These men are the servants of the most high God, which shew unto us the way of salvation* (Acts 16:17).

**Every word of that was true.** Paul and Silas were the servants of the most high God, and they were showing the way of salvation, **and the announcement was a free advertisement that any ministry-minded man would have accepted with pleasure.**

**And Paul cast the spirit out:**

*And this did she many days. But Paul, being grieved, turned and said to the spirit, I command thee in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her. And he came out the same hour* (Acts 16:18).

**Being grieved.**

**Which means the apostle's reaction to accurate publicity from a wrong source was grief, and his response was to silence it at the cost of a riot and a night in prison (16:19–24).**

**Which is the practical rule that follows from this case.** The truth of the words does not sanctify the source. **A testimony is not acceptable to God merely because its content is correct; the vessel matters, and the endorsement of the wrong spirit is a liability and not an asset.**

**And the application for a house of God is uncomfortable but clear.** A ministry that is promoted by a source it cannot sanctify will be entangled by that source, **and the moment of refusing the endorsement is frequently the moment when the trouble begins.**

### The Bereans, and the commendation of testing

**And against the five cases, Scripture sets the congregations and the apostles who tested, and it commends them.**

**The Bereans are called *more noble* than the Thessalonians for examining an apostle daily by the Scriptures (Acts 17:11).**

**The church at Ephesus is commended by the Lord Himself for the same discipline:**

*And thou hast tried them which say they are apostles, and are not, and hast found them liars* (Revelation 2:2).

**Which means that the church that tested and found liars was praised for it in the second chapter of the Revelation, in the letter dictated by Christ.**

**And Paul submitted his own revelation to examination, and the passage is one of the most remarkable in his letters:**

*Then fourteen years after I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, and took Titus with me also. And I went up by revelation, and communicated unto them that gospel which I preach among the Gentiles, but privately to them which were of reputation, lest by any means I should run, or had run, in vain* (Galatians 2:1–2).

**I went up by revelation, and communicated unto them that gospel.**

**Which means that the man who had received his gospel by direct revelation from Jesus Christ still laid it before the other apostles for examination, and gave a reason: *lest by any means I should run, or had run, in vain.***

**And the conclusion for every generation is unavoidable.** If the chiefest of the apostles submitted his revelation for review, no younger man, however vivid his experience and however clear his call, can claim exemption from the same process. **Testing is not an insult to the prophet. It is the ordinary condition of the gift.**

### The counterfeits of testing

**And the chapter must end with a warning on the other side, because the discipline of testing has its own corruptions and they are as damaging as the absence of the discipline.**

**The first counterfeit is cynicism, which is a house that has stopped expecting revelation and calls its unbelief discernment.** And the corrective for it is the command that precedes the command to test: *despise not prophesyings.*

**The second counterfeit is the manner test, which is the habit of judging a word by the accent, the age, the grammar, or the style of the man who delivers it.** Which is the same error as those who dismissed Peter and John as *unlearned and ignorant men* (Acts 4:13), **and it results in a house that has excellent taste and no food.**

**And the third counterfeit is testing that has already reached its verdict, which is a court that examines a man in order to record his failure.** And that is the error of the council that had *agreed already, that if any man did confess that he was Christ, he should be put out of the synagogue* (John 9:22).

**And the possibility of all three counterfeits does not remove the duty.** It means the duty must be done by men who have also obeyed the first command.

**And the practical arrangement for a house of God is therefore straightforward, and it consists of six parts.**

**Revelation is received publicly, in the assembly, and judged there** — *let the prophets speak two or three, and let the other judge.*

**The record is kept.** *The prophet which prophesieth of peace, when the word of the prophet shall come to pass, then shall the prophet be known.* The fulfilment test cannot be applied by a congregation that keeps no record of what was said. **And a written register of prophecies, with the outcomes marked, is one of the most sobering documents a church can own.**

**The five tests are applied in order: does it agree with Scripture; does it confess the Christ of the apostles; what fruit has the speaker's ministry produced; has the word come to pass; and do two or three judgements agree about it?**  The first two can be applied immediately, and the last two require time. **Which means a house must be able to hold a word in suspense without either enthroning or discarding it.**

**The judgement is of the word and never of the man's soul.** *Let the other judge* is an instruction to weigh a message and not to pronounce on a man's standing before God.

**And the young are given the right to be tested rather than the right to be believed.** Which is the greatest protection they can be given, because it means their true words will be established by process and their false ones will be removed without their destruction.

**And the supply is not adjusted to the appetite.** *For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears; and they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned unto fables* (2 Timothy 4:3–4).

**Which is the mechanism of Ahab's four hundred prophets stated for the church.** Where the appetite of the congregation governs the content of the preaching, the fables will arrive to fill the demand, **and the last state of that house will be a great deal of revelation and no word of God.**

**And the summary of the whole chapter is the two verses with which it began, and every house of God should be able to recite them:**

*Despise not prophesyings. Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.*

### Principles

**First: the command to test stands immediately after the command not to despise, and the second is the consequence of the first.** *Despise not prophesyings. Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.*

**Second: the New Testament commands the testing of spirits as explicitly as it promises the outpouring of the Spirit.** *Believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world.*

**Third: the master test is Scripture, and the noblest congregation in the book of Acts was the one that examined an apostle daily.** *To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them*; and *they searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.*

**And fourth: the test outranks the credentials and the messenger, and even an angel is not exempt.** *But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you ... let him be accursed.*

**Fifth: the Christological test concerns the Christ who is confessed and not the name that is used.** *Every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God.*

**Sixth: a sign may come to pass and the message still be false, and the fulfilment may be a proving rather than an endorsement.** *Thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet ... for the Lord your God proveth you.*

**Seventh: unfavourable words must be tested by their fruit, and comfortable words must wait longest for their vindication.** *By their fruits ye shall know them*; and *the prophet which prophesieth of peace, when the word of the prophet shall come to pass, then shall the prophet be known.*

**And eighth: the appetite of the audience determines the supply of prophecy.** Ahab hated Micaiah *because he doth not prophesy good concerning me, but evil*; and the four hundred said what the king had come to hear.

**Ninth: a true prophet submits his word to verification and invites the whole assembly to judge the outcome.** *If thou return at all in peace, the Lord hath not spoken by me. And he said, Hearken, O people, every one of you.*

**And tenth: the counterfeit of revelation does not usually deny God; it fills the memory of the congregation with dreams until the name is forgotten.** *Which think to cause my people to forget my name by their dreams which they tell every man to his neighbour.*

**And eleventh: the final test is whether a word is chaff or wheat, and that is discovered by taking it into a life.** *What is the chaff to the wheat? saith the Lord. Is not my word like as a fire ... and like a hammer that breaketh the rock in pieces?*

**And twelfth: a true revelation delivered by a man who loves the reward still ruins him.** *Balaam ... who loved the wages of unrighteousness*; and *ran greedily after the error of Balaam for reward.*

**And thirteenth: an accurate testimony from a wrong spirit must be refused, and the refusal will cost.** *And Paul, being grieved, turned and said to the spirit, I command thee in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her.*

**And fourteenth: the apostles themselves submitted their revelations for examination.** *I went up by revelation, and communicated unto them that gospel which I preach among the Gentiles ... lest by any means I should run, or had run, in vain.*

### For the fathers

- **Test, and never despise.** *Despise not prophesyings. Prove all things.* A house that stops expecting the word of God has nothing left to examine and no food to give.
- **Ask Jehoshaphat's question when the room is unanimous.** *Is there not here a prophet of the Lord besides?* Unanimity is a comfort and not a test.
- **Keep the record of what has been said among you, with the outcomes marked.** The criterion of Jeremiah 28:9 cannot be applied by a congregation that remembers nothing.
- **Set the appetite of the house to the text and not to the mood.** *They shall heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears.* Where the demand sets the content, the fables arrive.
- **And submit your own words to the same process you require of the young.** Paul went up by revelation and laid his gospel before other men, *lest by any means I should run, or had run, in vain.*

### For the young

- **Invite the test as Micaiah did.** *If thou return at all in peace, the Lord hath not spoken by me.* The man who offers verification is the man whose word can be established.
- **Recognise the sound of the messenger's advice.** *Let thy word be like the word of one of them.* It will be offered kindly, and it is the beginning of the end of your usefulness.
- **Read the record of the comfortable prophecies.** Hananiah gave a date, broke a yoke, and was dead in two months, and the people who believed him were left trusting a lie.
- **Distinguish your gift from your motive, and examine the second harder than the first.** Balaam saw the vision of the Almighty with his eyes open and loved the wages of unrighteousness.
- **Let your word be wheat and not chaff, and expect the difference to be discovered when it is eaten.** What survives the fire and breaks the rock is the word that came from God.
- **And accept the verdict on the message without hearing it as a verdict on your soul.** Every true prophet in Scripture was tested; the process is the ordinary condition of the gift and not an insult to the man.

### Questions for reflection

1. Which of the five tests is your house actually using, and which one has it never applied?
2. When did your congregation last hear a word it did not want, and what did it do with the man who brought it?
3. Is there a written record of the prophecies given among you, and of what became of them?
4. Whose appetite determines what is preached in your house?
5. What endorsement have you accepted because the words were true, without asking where the words came from?
