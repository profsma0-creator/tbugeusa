## The Twelve: Jesus' Investment in an Unlikely Generation

**If the Lord Jesus had applied any of the ordinary criteria by which leadership is selected, He would not have chosen the men He chose, and the record is deliberately written so that we can see them as they were.**

**He chose twelve men from a subject province of a minor empire, most of them from a single fishing region, none of them from the priesthood, the academy or the aristocracy, and He gave them the world.**

**And for three years He invested everything He had in them, and they misunderstood Him, disputed among themselves about status, attempted to destroy a Samaritan village, wanted to restrict the work to their own group, and all of them ran away on the night He was arrested.**

**And He knew all of that in advance, and He chose them anyway.**

**Which means that the New Testament's doctrine of generational investment does not begin with a set of promising young men. It begins with a deliberate, prayerful selection of unlikely ones — and it is that decision, and not their qualifications, that made them the foundation of the church.**

### The night before He chose them

**And the selection was not a matter of recruitment. It was the outcome of a night of prayer.**

*And it came to pass in those days, that he went out into a mountain to pray, and continued all night in prayer to God. And when it was day, he called unto him his disciples: and of them he chose twelve, whom also he named apostles* (Luke 6:12–13).

**Note the sequence, because it is the pattern for every selection of men that any of us will ever make.**

**He prayed all night, and then He chose in the morning.**

**The Greek for *continued all night in prayer* is *en nykteuon en te proseuche tou theou* — spending the night in the prayer of God.** And the Evangelist, who had access to the account from the people involved, thought the detail worth preserving. **The Lord Jesus did not choose twelve men by assessing suitability. He chose them by asking His Father who they were.**

**And the doctrine behind the choice is stated in the prayer He prayed on the last night, where He names the men as a gift:**

*I have manifested thy name unto the men which thou gavest me out of the world: thine they were, and thou gavest them me; and they have kept thy word* (John 17:6).

**The men given to the Son were *thine* before they were His.**

**Which means the identification of a successor is an act of revelation before it is an act of judgement, and a house that selects its leaders by the assessment of their current performance has replaced prayer with a personnel process.**

**And it explains why the selections in Scripture so frequently look wrong.** A tax collector, a zealot, several fishermen, and one man who kept the money and eventually stole it. **The Father gave them, and the Son received them, and the criteria were not the criteria anyone would have used.**

**And the command attached to the choice is stated in Mark with the same emphasis:**

*Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain* (John 15:16).

**Chosen, ordained, sent to fruitfulness, and promised that the fruit will remain.** Every element of a proper succession is in that verse, **and it begins with a reminder that none of the men in the room had initiated anything.**

### Who they actually were

**And the most useful thing to do with the list of the Twelve is to stop treating it as a roll of honour and to read it as a description of an unlikely and incompatible group of men.**

*And Simon he surnamed Peter; and James the son of Zebedee, and John the brother of James; and he surnamed them Boanerges, which is, The sons of thunder: and Andrew, and Philip, and Bartholomew, and Matthew, and Thomas, and James the son of Alpheus, and Thaddeus, and Simon the Canaanite, and Judas Iscariot, which also betrayed him* (Mark 3:16–19).

**Set out what is actually in that list.**

**Four of them were commercial fishermen from the same shoreline** — Peter, Andrew, James and John — and the Gospels show them leaving boats and nets and a hired servant (Mark 1:20) to follow Him. **They were working men with a business, and none of them had any formal training in the law.**

**One was a tax collector.** Matthew, sitting at the receipt of custom (Matthew 9:9), a man who collected the Roman levy from his own people and was therefore excluded from decent Jewish society. **He would have been refused a place in the synagogue, and he was selected as an apostle.**

**One was a zealot.** *Simon the Canaanite* is Simon the Kananaios, which is the Aramaic for zealot — a member of the party whose programme was the violent expulsion of the Romans and the collaborators who served them.

**And put those two facts together, because they are the most striking thing in the list.**

**A tax collector and a zealot were in the same team of twelve.**

**The zealot's political conviction was that the tax collector and his kind should be eliminated, and the tax collector's livelihood was the collection of the revenue the zealot wanted to overthrow.** These two men had, on the ordinary reading of their politics, a reason to kill each other.

**And the Lord put them in the same band and made them brothers, and by the day of Pentecost they were in the same upper room, praying together, and afterwards they were co-founders of a community that held everything in common.**

**Which tells us what the church is and what it is not.** The church is not a gathering of like-minded people who happen to share a culture; it is a new creation in which the enmities between men are abolished. And an apostolic team containing both a publican and a zealot is the standing proof of it.

**And the assessment of their intellectual qualifications is recorded by their opponents:**

*Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus* (Acts 4:13).

**The Greek for *unlearned* is *agrammatoi* — illiterate in the technical sense, without the rabbinical education.** And *idiotai* — the word for private persons, laymen, men without professional standing.

**So the men who preached the first Christian sermon and stood before the Sanhedrin were described by the Sanhedrin as illiterate laymen.**

**And that is what the Lord chose, and it is not incidental.** He did not choose men who could reason in Greek or argue in Hebrew; **and He did not choose them because they were unsuitable, but because the whole of the New Testament's doctrine of calling requires that the excellence of the ministry be attributed to the power of God and not to the abilities of the men.**

*But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty ... that no flesh should glory in his presence* (1 Corinthians 1:27, 29).

**And note that the Lord said exactly what He thought of their intellectual capacity when the occasion arose:**

*Then he said unto them, O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken* (Luke 24:25).

**Which He said to two of them on the road to Emmaus, after three years of personal instruction, on the day of His resurrection.**

### The three purposes of the appointment

**And the purpose of the appointment is stated in a single verse with three clauses, and the order of the clauses is the whole of the doctrine of formation.**

*And he ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach, and to have power to heal sicknesses, and to cast out devils* (Mark 3:14–15).

**The first purpose is that they should be *with him*.**

**The Greek is *hina osin met' autou* — that they might be with Him.** Not enrolled in a school, not employed in a mission, not assigned to a district: **with Him, in the same house, on the same roads, eating the same meals, for three years.**

**And this is the requirement that most modern formation omits and cannot substitute for.** A man may be trained thoroughly in a seminary and never once have lived alongside the man who is forming him. **And the difference is not one of intensity; it is one of kind. Information is transferred in classrooms, and character is transferred in houses.**

**The second purpose is that He might send them *to preach*.**

**Note the Greek: *apostelle* — that He might send them as apostles.** The being-with preceded the sending, and it did not end when the sending began. **The disciples were sent out as apprentices during the ministry (Mark 6:7), and they returned, and they went with Him again.**

**Which is the pattern of a proper apprenticeship: residence, then assignment, then return, then residence again, until the sending becomes permanent.**

**And the third purpose is power: *to have power to heal sicknesses, and to cast out devils.***

**The Greek for *power* is *exousia*, the right and the ability, and it is given.** It was not the natural gift of the men; it was an endowment transmitted by the appointment.

**And the order of the three clauses is therefore a test of every ministry that claims this pattern.** Whoever they are with, and whatever they are being sent to do, the power belongs to the appointment and not to the man. **And a young man who has skipped the first clause and gone straight to the third will eventually discover that the power he is operating in was never his.**

### The names given before the characters

**And there is a practice of the Lord's that ought to be studied by every father who ever looked at a young man and wondered whether he could become anything.**

**He gave them names that described what they were going to be, long before they were anything of the kind.**

*And he brought him to Jesus. And when Jesus beheld him, he said, Thou art Simon the son of Jona: thou shalt be called Cephas, which is by interpretation, A stone* (John 1:42).

**The Aramaic *Kepha*, the Greek *Petros*, the English *Peter*: a rock.**

**And the man standing in front of Him at that moment was anything but a rock.** He was a fisherman from Bethsaida, he had been introduced by his brother, and within three years he would boast that he would die with his master, deny him three times to a servant girl, and go back to his boat.

**And the Lord named him a stone on the day they met.**

**And He named the brothers Zebedee *Boanerges*, the sons of thunder** — Mark 3:17 — a nickname given to two men who, on the only occasion where the name is explained by their conduct, **wanted to call fire down from heaven and burn a Samaritan village because it had not received them** (Luke 9:54).

**Thunder is the right word for men like that, and the name was given in the knowledge that the thunder would one day be used to announce the resurrection.**

**What the Lord was doing in both cases is what a father does when he speaks a future over a young man who has not yet become it.** He does not flatter him. **He names the thing that is possible because the naming is part of the mechanism by which it comes to pass.**

**And note the fourth instance, which is the most important for our subject because it concerns a man who was given everything and lost it.**

**He was named an apostle, given the same power to heal and to cast out devils, sent out with the same commission as the others, and entrusted with the money.** And the Lord said of him, in advance, and to his face as it were:

*Have not I chosen you twelve, and one of you is a devil?* (John 6:70).

**So the naming of a future and the eventual outcome are not the same thing, and Scripture does not pretend otherwise.** A father can name a man, invest in him, and be right — and still lose him. **Which is not an argument against investing. It is an argument against investing for the purpose of self-validation.**

### The failures that the investment tolerated

**And the record of what those three years contained is, if it is read without the varnish of two thousand years of Christian familiarity, close to a scandal.**

**They failed in the storm.** A great wind came down on the lake while they were crossing, and the boat was filling, and the Lord was asleep on a pillow, and they woke Him with the accusation *Master, carest thou not that we perish?* (Mark 4:38). **Twelve men who had seen Him heal every disease in Capernaum concluded that He was indifferent to their drowning.**

**They failed on the mountaintop.** Nine of them were left at the bottom while three went up, and a man brought his demonised son, and *they could not* cast it out (Mark 9:18). **And the Lord's response was the complaint of a teacher at the end of his patience: *O faithless generation, how long shall I be with you? how long shall I suffer you?***

**They failed in the garden.** He asked three of them to watch with Him for an hour, and they fell asleep twice, and the second time He asked them the question that ends the record of their strength: *Sleep on now, and take your rest* (Mark 14:41).

**They failed at the arrest.** It is one verse, and it is total: *And they all forsook him, and fled* (Mark 14:50).

**And they failed afterwards, corporately, by going back to work.** *Simon Peter saith unto them, I go a fishing. They say unto him, We also go with thee. They went forth, and entered into a ship immediately; and that night they caught nothing* (John 21:3).

**Seven of them, on the Sea of Tiberias, after the resurrection.** They had seen the empty tomb, and the folded linen, and some of them had seen Him alive. **And they went back to the nets.**

**And what the Lord did with all of that must be stated plainly, because it is the most encouraging fact in this chapter.**

**He kept them. He did not re-select, He did not downgrade them, and He did not take the ministry away from them.** He let them be wrong in the boat and taught them; He corrected the nine at the bottom of the mountain and did not send them home; **He let them sleep and then went to the cross anyway; He let them run and then came and stood among them with the words *peace be unto you*; and on the beach at daybreak He made them breakfast before He asked Peter anything.**

**And the mechanism of His patience is explained in the high-priestly prayer:** *While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy name: those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition; that the scripture might be fulfilled* (John 17:12).

**He kept them.** The Greek is *eteroun*, I guarded, I preserved, and it is the same verb used of a garrison holding a city. **The formation of the Twelve was sustained by the Lord's own watchfulness, and not by the quality of the raw material.**

### The ambition He had to dismantle

**And the hardest part of the three years was not the teaching of doctrine. It was the dismantling of twelve men's ambition, which kept regenerating in the middle of the instruction.**

**The clearest instance is the argument on the road.**

*And he came to Capernaum: and being in the house he asked them, What was it that ye disputed among yourselves by the way? But they held their peace: for by the way they had disputed among themselves, who should be the greatest. And he sat down, and called the twelve, and saith unto them, If any man desire to be first, the same shall be last of all, and servant of all* (Mark 9:33–35).

**They were arguing about rank on the way to Capernaum while He was on His way to Jerusalem to be crucified.** And in case that was not enough, the same argument is recorded again at the last supper (Luke 22:24), **after He had taken bread and a cup and told them He was about to give His body for them.**

**And the most brazen instance is the approach of the mother of James and John.**

*Then came to him the mother of Zebedee's children with her sons, worshipping him, and desiring a certain thing of him. And he said unto her, What wilt thou? She saith unto him, Grant that these my two sons may sit, the one on thy right hand, and the other on the left, in thy kingdom* (Matthew 20:20–21).

**The request is for the two highest offices in the kingdom, made by a mother on behalf of her sons, by means of a religious approach — *worshipping him, and desiring a certain thing*.**

**And the answer contains the whole doctrine of Christian leadership, and the Lord gave it after asking them whether they could drink His cup.**

*But Jesus called them unto him, and said, Ye know that the princes of the Gentiles exercise dominion over them, and they that are great exercise authority upon them. But it shall not be so among you: but whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister; and whosoever will be chief among you, let him be your servant: even as the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many* (Matthew 20:25–28).

**Three things in that answer must be extracted.**

**The first is that the ambition is not rejected; it is redirected.** *Whosoever will be great among you* — the desire for greatness is assumed and permitted, and the method is changed. **The Lord does not tell them to stop wanting to be first; He tells them what being first will cost.**

**The second is the total inversion: *let him be your minister ... let him be your servant.*** The Greek words are *diakonos* and *doulos*, and they describe a table-waiter and a bondslave. **Which means that the office a man should want is the office of serving, and the men who want it for the status have misunderstood what they are applying for.**

**And the third is that the pattern is Himself.** *Even as the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister.* **The authority in the house runs downward as service, or it does not run at all.**

**And a cohort assembled by a young man — the phenomenon of an earlier chapter — has never learned this, because it has never watched a master serve.** The men who grew up with Rehoboam advised severity. **The men who lived with Jesus for three years watched Him wash their feet (John 13:5) and were told that they should do the same.**

### The intolerances He corrected

**And there are two further tendencies in the Twelve that the Lord corrected directly, and both of them are the characteristic errors of young zeal in every generation.**

**The first is the desire to restrict the work to their own group.**

*And John answered him, saying, Master, we saw one casting out devils in thy name, and he followeth not us: and we forbad him, because he followeth not us. But Jesus said, Forbid him not: for there is no man which shall do a miracle in my name, that can lightly speak evil of me. For he that is not against us is on our part* (Mark 9:38–40).

**The stranger was operating in the Lord's name and getting results, and the disciples' objection was that he was not part of their company.** And the Lord's ruling is the standard for every later attempt to police the boundaries of the work: **the question is not whether a man is one of us. It is whether he is against Him.**

**And the second is the desire to destroy those who reject the message.**

*And when his disciples James and John saw this, they said, Lord, wilt thou that we command fire to come down from heaven, and consume them, even as Elias did? But he turned, and rebuked them, and said, Ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of. For the Son of man is not come to destroy men's lives, but to save them* (Luke 9:54–56).

**They cited Elijah as their precedent, which is the ordinary method by which a young man justifies severity: he finds a text and a hero and does the opposite of what the Lord is doing.**

**And the Lord's diagnosis is not that the proposal was tactically unwise. It is that the men did not know what spirit was operating in them.** *Ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of.*

**Which means that a young man's zeal can feel exactly like the Holy Spirit and be something else entirely, and the discernment of the difference is not a matter of sincerity.** It requires precisely what the Twelve had and did not use: three years of proximity to a master who was gentle, **and the willingness to be told that the spirit in you is not the one you thought it was.**

### The evidence that the investment worked

**And the account of the return on the Lord's investment is written in the book of Acts, and the transformation is one of the most remarkable records in world history.**

**The men who fled in Gethsemane stood in front of the Sanhedrin within weeks, and the Sanhedrin's own minutes record the reason:**

*Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus* (Acts 4:13).

**They had been with Jesus.** The Greek is *hoti syn to Iesou esan* — the whole three years is compressed into one subordinate clause, **and the Sanhedrin, which had legal training and official standing and every advantage the disciples lacked, could see the difference and could not explain it.**

**Which is the exact fulfilment of the first clause of Mark 3:14.** He ordained twelve *that they should be with him.* **And the effect of the first clause was the qualification that their enemies were forced to acknowledge.**

**And the second piece of evidence is the change in what produced their joy.**

*And they departed from the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for his name* (Acts 5:41).

**The same men who had once boasted about approaching thrones and argued about rank came out of a beating rejoicing that they had been permitted to be humiliated for His name.**

**Which is the completion of the dismantling of ambition.** The Lord had spent three years telling them that greatness is service and that the first must be last; **and here they are, beaten with rods in front of the nation, regarding the experience as an honour — and it is the exact inversion of the argument at Capernaum.**

**And the third piece of evidence is the appointment of a successor by the method already established in this book.**

*Wherefore of these men which have companied with us all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us, beginning from the baptism of John, unto that same day that he was taken up from us, must one be ordained to be a witness with us of his resurrection* (Acts 1:21–22).

**The qualification for the twelfth office was that the man had been present throughout the whole of the Lord's ministry.**

**Not the ablest preacher, and not the most senior, and not the one with the best understanding — the one who had been in the room for the entire duration.** And two men qualified, and the church prayed, and the lot fell on Matthias, **and the principle is permanent: the men who inherit an apostolic deposit are the men who were present while it was being formed.**

**Which is the reason a father must keep his young men physically near him, and the reason a young man should refuse a promotion that removes him from the room before the formation is complete.**

### And the one who was lost

**And the account would be dishonest if it did not include the man who took the money and left, and Scripture includes him without any attempt to soften the record.**

*While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy name: those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition* (John 17:12).

*And they gave forth their lots; and the lot fell upon Matthias; and he was numbered with the eleven apostles* — while of Judas the church said, *that he might take part of this ministry and apostleship, from which Judas by transgression fell, that he might go to his own place* (Acts 1:25–26).

**One of twelve. A man who heard every sermon, watched every miracle, and was trusted with the common purse.**

**And there are three lessons in that fact that must be given to every father in the faith, because they will all be needed.**

**The first is that the Lord knew, and chose him, and used him.** *Have not I chosen you twelve, and one of you is a devil?* The betrayal was not a failure of the Lord's discernment. **It was the fulfilment of Scripture, the mechanism by which the cross became possible, and the occasion of the Lord's own greatest act. And the wickedness of the act remained the wickedness of the man who did it.**

**The second is that the presence of one bad outcome does not invalidate the investment.** If a father measures the authenticity of his work by whether every young man turns out well, **he will be undone by his first Judas and will become suspicious of every Timothy.** The Lord's own band had a one-in-twelve failure rate, and the Lord's method did not change.

**And the third is the most sobering of all: proximity does not guarantee transformation.**

**Three years of living with the Son of God, and a man went to his own place.** Which means no father can give what only the Spirit can produce, **and no amount of exposure will convert a heart that is not being converted.**

**And the remedy is not despair and it is not suspicion. It is what the Lord did: choose in prayer, invest everything, keep them as far as it is given you to keep them, name their future, correct their ambition, refuse their intolerances, and wash their feet.** And then, having done all of that, **commend the outcome to the Father, who is the only one who can make anything of these unpromising men.**

### Principles

**First: the selection of the twelve was the outcome of a night of prayer, not an assessment of suitability.** *He continued all night in prayer to God. And when it was day, he called unto him his disciples: and of them he chose twelve.* And the men were the Father's gift: *thine they were, and thou gavest them me.*

**Second: the group was deliberately incompatible.** A publican and a zealot in the same band of twelve. **The church is not a gathering of the like-minded; it is the abolition of enmity between men.**

**Third: they were unlearned and ignorant men, and the world was given to them.** *Agrammatoi* and *idiotai*, according to their enemies — *that no flesh should glory in his presence.*

**Fourth: the appointment had three purposes in a fixed order.** *That they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach, and to have power.* Presence, then sending, then power. The order cannot be reversed.

**Fifth: the names were given before the characters.** *Thou shalt be called Cephas ... Boanerges, the sons of thunder.* A father speaks a future over a young man who cannot yet become it, and the naming is part of the mechanism.

**Sixth: the investment tolerated an enormous quantity of failure.** They feared for their lives, failed to cast out a devil, slept in the garden, and all fled at the arrest — and *while I was with them, I kept them.*

**Seventh: the hard part of the formation was dismantling their ambition.** Three recorded arguments about rank, and a mother's request for two thrones, and the answer: *whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister.*

**Eighth: the two intolerances were both corrected directly.** *Forbid him not: for he that is not against us is on our part*, and *ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of.* Young zeal wants to restrict the work and to destroy the rejecters, and both instincts came from the Twelve.

**And ninth: the transformation was public and unarguable.** *They took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.* And the qualification of the successor was that he had *companied with us all the time.* The men who inherit the deposit are the men who were in the room while it was formed.

**And tenth: one of the twelve was lost, and the method did not change.** *One of you is a devil.* Proximity is not transformation, and the presence of a Judas does not invalidate the investment.

### For the fathers

- **Choose in prayer, and expect the answer to look unpromising.** *He continued all night in prayer to God ... and of them he chose twelve.* And the men were the Father's gift to the Son.
- **Put incompatible men in the same room and refuse to let them be separate.** A publican and a zealot in one band of twelve. If your house only works with people like you, it is not yet a church.
- **Require presence before you require performance.** *That they should be with him, and that he might send them forth.* The order of Mark 3:14 is the whole of formation, and it is the clause most modern training has abandoned.
- **Name their future out loud.** *Thou shalt be called Cephas ... Boanerges, the sons of thunder.* Say the thing over the young man that he cannot yet be, and keep saying it.
- **Do not measure your work by whether every man turns out.** *One of you is a devil.* If your first Judas makes you suspicious of every Timothy, you will lose the whole generation to protect yourself from one man.
- **And dismantle ambition deliberately, because it does not dismantle itself.** *Whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister.* Say it early, say it often, and prove it by washing feet.

### For the young

- **Ask whether you are actually in the room.** The first purpose of Mark 3:14 was presence, and the successor in Acts 1 had to have been there *all the time.* If your assignment keeps you away from the man who is forming you, you are being traded for a job title.
- **Expect your ambition to be dismantled, and cooperate with the process.** The Twelve argued about rank three times, and the argument was still going on at the last supper. Say what you want, and let it be answered.
- **Do not police the boundaries of the work or destroy the people who refuse you.** *Forbid him not*, and *ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of.* Both rebukes were given to men who were absolutely sincere.
- **Let your failures be known by what happens after them, and get back in the boat.** Seven of them went fishing on the night He rose; He met them on the shore with bread and fish.
- **Count what you were given, and thank God for a foundation you did not lay.** *The Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister.* You were made by somebody's three years of patience.
- **And be prepared to be called a stone for a long time before you are one.** The name was given at the first meeting. The man took twenty-five years and a denial and a restoration to grow into it.

### Questions for reflection

1. Who in your care is unpromising, and would you have chosen them?
2. Which young man is physically present in your life, in the ordinary sense of sharing time and meals — and which ones are only receiving your teaching?
3. Whose future have you spoken out loud this year, and did you speak it in prayer first?
4. What is currently in the room where your young men live — and is it a spirit you would recognise?
5. Who is the Judas you are afraid of, and what has that fear cost the Timothys?
