## When the Student Becomes the Carrier

There is a moment in every formation when the relationship changes, and the moment is rarely marked. No ceremony attends it, no certificate records it, and the two people involved frequently do not notice it has happened. And it is the point on which the whole purpose of the previous chapter depends.

**A student receives. A carrier transmits.** And the transition from one to the other is a distinct event with identifiable signs.

This chapter is about that transition: what produces it, what obstructs it, and how the two parties can tell that it has happened.

### The withheld portion

And we begin with a fact about the Lord's own method that has no counterpart in any educational system on earth.

*I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now. Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth: for he shall not speak of himself; but whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak: and he will shew you things to come* (John 16:12–13).

**Read the first sentence, because it is extraordinary.** The Lord Jesus, on the last night of His life, with hours remaining and everything at stake, says that **He has more to say and is deliberately not saying it.**

**And the reason given is not that the disciples are unworthy, or that the material is secret. It is that they are *not able to bear it yet*.** The Greek is *ou dynasthe bastazein arti* — you are not able to carry it now. The verb *bastazo* means to bear a weight, to carry a load.

**The teaching is described as a weight, and the disciples' capacity to carry it is a function of their stage.**

**And the Lord's solution is not to simplify the material, or to give it in a form they can handle now, or to write it down for later. It is to leave it to another teacher:**

*Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth.*

**The completion of their education is assigned to the Holy Spirit.** The Greek is *hodegesei hymas eis pasan ten aletheian* — he will lead you on the road into all the truth. The verb is *hodegeo*, to be a guide on a journey, and the construction with *eis* indicates movement into a place.

**So the sequence in the Lord's own arrangement is: partial instruction now, an inability to carry the rest, and a promised Guide who will complete the formation after the teacher has gone.**

**And note the last clause of verse 13, which explains why the Lord can leave the job unfinished: *for he shall not speak of himself; but whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak.***

**The Spirit does not originate. He transmits.** And the reason that matters here is that it makes the continuation continuous. **The teaching that the Lord did not give to the disciples directly is not lost; it is routed through the Spirit, who hears and speaks.**

**Which means there is a category of revelation that is only available to a man after his teacher has gone.** Not additional doctrine, but the understanding of it, and the application of it to circumstances the teacher never saw.

**And that is the first mark of the transition: the student begins to be taught by the Spirit directly.**

### The question at the Jordan

The second mark appears in the first public act of a successor's independent ministry, and it is a question rather than a demonstration.

*And he took the mantle of Elijah that fell from him, and smote the waters, and said, Where is the Lord God of Elijah? and when he also had smitten the waters, they parted hither and thither: and Elisha went over* (2 Kings 2:14).

**This is Elisha's first recorded action on his own. He is standing at the Jordan, on the far bank from where he needs to be, with a garment that used to belong to someone else.**

**And he asks a question, and the question is the whole of the transition: *Where is the Lord God of Elijah?***

**Note what the question implies.** Elisha is not asking where Elijah is; he watched him ascend. **He is asking where the God of Elijah is — and whether that God will act for him.**

**That is the question every successor has to ask, and it cannot be answered by any amount of inherited information.** The God of the father must become *my* God, and the only way to find out whether He has is to act in his absence.

**And the answer is given in the same verse: *the waters parted hither and thither, and Elisha went over.***

**The transition happened at the riverbank, in the act of smiting the water with someone else's cloak, asking someone else's God a question about Himself.**

**And it could not have happened while Elijah was standing next to him.** That is the point of the whole arrangement, and it is why the preparation of a successor must include **assignments in which the predecessor is not present**.

### The test of independence

**And Paul has the same structure in his own history, and he states it in the most confronting terms, in a passage where he is defending his apostolic standing against people who claimed he was derivative.**

*But I certify you, brethren, that the gospel which was preached of me is not after man. For I neither received it of man, neither was I taught it, but by the revelation of Jesus Christ* (Galatians 1:11–12).

**And then the sequence of his independence:**

*But when it pleased God, who separated me from my mother's womb, and called me by his grace, to reveal his Son in me, that I might preach him among the heathen; immediately I conferred not with flesh and blood: neither went I up to Jerusalem to them which were apostles before me; but I went into Arabia, and returned again unto Damascus. Then after three years I went up to Jerusalem to see Peter, and abode with him fifteen days* (Galatians 1:15–18).

**Set out the timetable.**

**Paul was converted around AD 34. He went to Arabia. He returned to Damascus. And he did not go to Jerusalem to see Peter for three years.**

**Three years in Arabia, with no apostolic supervision, no correction by the original twelve, and no instruction from the men who had walked with Jesus.**

**And when he finally went to Jerusalem, he spent fifteen days with Peter and saw no other apostle except James.**

**Paul's point in the passage is that his gospel did not come from the Jerusalem church.** And that claim has an implication for succession which is frequently missed.

**The most influential teacher in the history of the church was formed substantially outside the established line of transmission.**

**Which does not mean that the established line was unnecessary — Paul went to Jerusalem, he spent fifteen days with Peter, he submitted his gospel to the leaders in Acts 15, and he says so. But it does mean that the formation of a carrier is not reducible to enrolment in a school of the previous generation.**

**And the mechanism of the difference is stated in verse 16: *it pleased God ... to reveal his Son in me.***

**The Greek is *apokalypsai ton huion autou en emoi* — to reveal His Son in me.** Not *to me*, but *in me*. **The revelation was internal, and it was the ground of everything Paul subsequently taught.**

**That is the transition, and it is not a promotion.** It is the point at which the deposit already received becomes personally appropriated, and the Spirit who was promised as the Guide does the work.

### The signs

And now the practical question. **How does a person know they have moved from receiving to carrying?**

Scripture gives five signs, and they are all characteristics of function rather than states of feeling.

**The first sign is that you can be taught by the Spirit without a human intermediary.** John 16:13, and Galatians 1:16. **Not that you no longer need teachers — Paul had them, and Timothy had Paul — but that the primary channel is no longer a person.**

**The second sign is that you can function without the master in the room.** Elisha at the Jordan. **The test is deliberately constructed by the arrangement of the assignments: the predecessor sends the successor alone precisely to find out whether this has happened.**

**The third sign, and it is the one that most clearly distinguishes a carrier from a reciter, is that you can think *with* the deposit rather than quote it.**

The evidence of this is the emergence of material the predecessor did not produce. **Elisha's ministry is not a continuation of Elijah's; it is different in kind.** Elijah confronts kings, calls down fire, and lives in caves. **Elisha heals a foreign general, feeds a school of prophets, makes an axe head float, and raises a dead child in a borrowed room.** The mantle was inherited and the shape of the ministry was not.

**The fourth sign is that you begin to have students of your own.** 2 Kings 2 is full of *the sons of the prophets* watching the transfer, and by 2 Kings 4 Elisha is the one they come to. **A ministry that has never produced a person has not yet reproduced, and reproduction is the definition of a carrier.**

**And the fifth sign is a capacity to bear the weight that the teacher withheld.** *I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now.* **A man who has not grown in capacity will find that the material he needs remains inaccessible to him.** The New Testament states this as a fault:

*For when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God; and are become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat* (Hebrews 5:12).

**The failure named there is not ignorance. It is duration without growth.** The Greek is *dia ton chronon* — because of the time. **They had been at it long enough to be teachers, and they still needed milk.**

**And the remedy described is practice: *strong meat belongeth to them that are of full age, even those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil* (Hebrews 5:14).**

**The Greek for *use* is *hexis*, a settled habit acquired by repeated exercise.** And for *exercised* it is *gegymnasmena*, from which we take *gymnasium* — trained, worked out.

**The capacity to carry the weight is produced by use, which is to say by exercise, which is to say by years of doing the thing.**

### "Greater works than these"

And there is a further element of the transition that must be stated, because it is the most encouraging thing in the chapter and the most frequently disbelieved.

**The Lord expected His successors to exceed Him.**

*Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that believeth on me, the works that I do shall he do also; and greater works than these shall he do; because I go unto my Father* (John 14:12).

**And the New Testament records that they did.**

Consider the modes of power in the two apostolic ministries, because the difference is instructive and the difference is exactly what this chapter predicts.

**Peter's shadow healed the sick.** *Insomuch that they brought forth the sick into the streets, and laid them on beds and couches, that at the least the shadow of Peter passing by might overshadow some of them* (Acts 5:15).

**And Paul's handkerchiefs did the same.** *And God wrought special miracles by the hands of Paul: so that from his body were brought unto the sick handkerchiefs or aprons, and the diseases departed from them, and the evil spirits went out of them* (Acts 19:11–12).

**Two different mechanisms, in two different men, in two different cities, both effective.** And Luke calls Paul's *special miracles* — the Greek is *dynameis ou tas tychousas*, powers not of the ordinary kind.

**The successor did not reproduce the predecessor's method.** Peter was in Jerusalem and people carried their sick into the street; Paul was in Ephesus and cloths were carried from him. **The power was the same and the expression was not, and nobody in the New Testament treats the difference as a problem.**

**That is the shape of a healthy succession, and it is what most institutions cannot survive.** A founder who requires the successor to reproduce his methods has mistaken the mantle for the ministry. **And a successor who refuses to find his own expression has refused the transition and remained a student.**

### The perpetual student

And now the failure, because the New Testament warns about it in terms that make it a category of its own.

**The perpetual student is the man who has been in formation long enough to have completed it, and has not.** Hebrews 5:12 again: *for when for the time ye ought to be teachers.*

**And the characteristics of the type are recognisable.**

**He is always enrolled in something.** A new course, a new mentor, a new conference, another degree. **And the enrolment functions as a postponement.**

**He is fluent in the vocabulary and has not done the work.** He can discuss the deposit at length and has never administered it.

**He is attached to a man rather than to the calling.** His identity is *the disciple of X*, and the attachment is preferable to the responsibility.

**He believes that more information will produce readiness.** This is the specific error of the intellectually able perpetual student: he thinks the transition is a matter of knowing enough, and it is a matter of having done enough.

**And he becomes, over time, a rival to his own teacher.** Because a man who will not become a carrier must do something with his accumulated knowledge, and the only available use for it is to evaluate other people's ministries, including the one that formed him.

**And the scriptural verdict on the condition is not mild.** *For when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God.*

**The judgement is *for the time*.** The problem is not the need for milk. It is the need for milk after the weaning date.

### Two failures of the transition

And there are two ways the transition fails, and one belongs to each party.

**The failure of the predecessor is to prevent it.** He appoints the successor and retains the decision. He sends him and calls him back. He gives the mantle and keeps the authority that the mantle signified. **And the mechanism is usually fear — of irrelevance, of the work being done badly, of having nothing left to be.**

**And the failure of the successor is to refuse it.** He waits to be told. He does not act until permission is given, and permission is never given, and therefore he never acts. **And the mechanism is also fear — of failure, of the cost of being wrong in public, of discovering that the ability was not there.**

**And both failures have the same effect: the ministry continues to be carried by one man past the point at which he can carry it, and then it stops.**

**And the antidote to both is the one thing that Scripture keeps returning to in this area: the deliberate sending of the successor alone.** The predecessor must send, and the successor must go, and neither can be certain of the outcome until the waters have been smitten and the question has been asked.

### "Thou hast asked a hard thing"

And the request that Elisha made, and the condition attached to it, contain the mechanics of the transition more precisely than anything else in Scripture.

*And it came to pass, when they were gone over, that Elijah said unto Elisha, Ask what I shall do for thee, before I be taken away from thee. And Elisha said, I pray thee, let a double portion of thy spirit be upon me. And he said, Thou hast asked a hard thing: nevertheless, if thou see me when I am taken from thee, it shall be so unto thee; but if not, it shall not be so* (2 Kings 2:9–10).

**The phrase *double portion* is not a request for twice as much as Elijah had.** It is a legal term. *But he shall acknowledge the son of the hated for the firstborn, by giving him a double portion of all that he hath: for he is the beginning of his strength; the right of the firstborn is his* (Deuteronomy 21:17).

**Elisha is asking to be recognised as the firstborn heir.** He is not asking for more power; he is asking for the position, and the position in Israel's law carried the double share and the headship of the house.

**And Elijah's answer is not a refusal and not a promise. It is a condition: *if thou see me when I am taken from thee.***

**The transfer is made contingent on the successor's presence at the moment of the predecessor's removal.**

**Which is a strange criterion, and a demanding one.** It means that the one thing required of Elisha is that he stay close enough, long enough, and attentive enough to witness the end. **He could not be away on an assignment, could not be managing the succession paperwork, could not be preparing his acceptance speech. He had to be watching.**

**And the test is repeated four times in the chapter before it is answered.** The sons of the prophets at Bethel, at Jericho, and the fifty men all tell Elisha he is about to lose his master, and three times he answers *Yea, I know it; hold ye your peace* (2 Kings 2:5). **The information was public. The position was not. Everyone knew that Elijah was leaving; only one man was willing to walk the whole distance to watch him go.**

**And the distance matters, because Elijah keeps trying to leave him behind.** *Tarry here, I pray thee* — at Gilgal, at Bethel, at Jericho (2 Kings 2:2, 4, 6). **Three times the master offers the successor an honourable exit, and three times the successor refuses it.**

**Any predecessor who reads this passage honestly will recognise what he is doing when he gives the successor a way out.** And any successor will recognise the moment at which the double portion is won or lost: it is won by refusing to be sent home.

**And when it was over, Elisha did two things.**

*And Elisha saw it, and he cried, My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof. And he saw him no more: and he took hold of his own clothes, and rent them in two pieces* (2 Kings 2:12).

**He mourned, and he put on the mantle.** Note the order: the old identity is torn before the new one is worn. **The rent clothes are the garment of the student, and they are destroyed in the same hour the mantle is taken up.**

**And the recognition came from outside him.** *And when the sons of the prophets which were to view at Jericho saw him, they said, The spirit of Elijah doth rest on Elisha* (2 Kings 2:15).

**The observers saw it before he announced it, and that is the ordinary pattern.** A man does not become a carrier by declaring himself one. **The evidence is public, and it is read off the life.**

### Sent out while the Master is still alive

And the Lord's practice of the transition is more deliberate than most institutions, and it is not the pattern of handing over at a funeral.

*And he called unto him the twelve, and began to send them forth by two and two; and gave them power over unclean spirits* (Mark 6:7).

*After these things the Lord appointed other seventy also, and sent them two and two before his face into every city and place, whither he himself would come* (Luke 10:1).

**Note the two things in the second sentence.** They were sent *before his face* — ahead of Him, as His advance party. And they were sent **into the places He intended to visit**, which means their work was to prepare the way for a visit that had not yet happened.

**And they came back with results.** *And the seventy returned again with joy, saying, Lord, even the devils are subject unto us through thy name* (Luke 10:17).

**Three years before Pentecost, and the seventy are already casting out demons under His name, on assignment, without Him.** The Lord's method was to distribute the work before He left rather than bequeath it after.

**And His response to their joy is a correction that has direct bearing on the carrier's inner life.**

*Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven* (Luke 10:20).

**He relocates the ground of joy from results to standing.** Because results fluctuate, and a man whose joy is attached to his results will be undone by the first assignment that fails.

**And the first assignment that fails is recorded, and it failed precisely while the Master was not available.**

*One of the multitude answered and said, Master, I have brought unto thee my son, which hath a dumb spirit ... and I spake to thy disciples that they should cast him out; and they could not* (Mark 9:17–18).

**Where were the disciples when this happened?** At the foot of the mount of transfiguration, with Jesus on the mountain, out of reach.

**And Mark preserves the diagnosis, and it is the most important sentence in the passage for our subject.**

*And when he was come into the house, his disciples asked him privately, Why could not we cast him out? And he said unto them, This kind can come forth by nothing, but by prayer and fasting* (Mark 9:28–29).

**They had cast out unclean spirits before. The power had been given; the assignment had succeeded in other places.** And now the same men failed, and the cause given is not a defect in the gift. **It is a defect in the life that sustained the gift.**

**Which is the hazard of the carrier, and it belongs to the carrier alone.** *Receiving* is a moment; *carrying* is a life. **The gift that was given at the sending can be held while the discipline that made it powerful is quietly abandoned, and no one notices until the day of the assignment.**

**And this is why the transition cannot be managed by an institution.** Nobody can certify that another man prays and fasts. **The apparatus of appointment can confirm a gift; it cannot sustain the life, and it cannot detect the day the life stopped.**

### The sentence that travelled two generations

And there is a small detail in the narrative that demonstrates what a carried deposit actually looks like across time, and it is easy to miss because it appears thirty chapters later.

**Elisha's cry in 2 Kings 2:12:** *My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof.*

**And Elisha's own death, in 2 Kings 13:14:** *Now Elisha was fallen sick of his sickness whereof he died. And Joash the king of Israel came down unto him, and wept over his face, and said, O my father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof.*

**Joash says the sentence Elisha said at Elijah's departure, and he says it to Elisha at his departure.**

**So the exact words spoken at one transition were spoken, fifty years later, at the next.** The phrase travelled through the man. **Elisha bequeathed more than his gift and his mantle; he bequeathed a form of speech, a way of seeing his own role, and a vocabulary that could only have been acquired by standing close enough to hear it.**

**And that is what a deposit looks like when it has been received in the way this chapter describes.** A man who merely took notes on Elisha's teaching would not have said that sentence. **The sentence is not doctrine; it is perception, and perception is transferred by proximity.**

**And here the account is sober, because Joash was not a good king.** *And he did that which was evil in the sight of the Lord; he departed not from all the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel sin* (2 Kings 13:11).

**The deposit travelled to a man who did not keep the covenant.**

**Which tells us two things, and both are uncomfortable.** The first is that transmission is not a guarantee of character; a man can carry a sentence he does not obey. **The second is that the generation inherits the language and the perception even when it refuses the obedience — and then fails to be able to say why it started weeping.**

**Which is precisely why the earlier chapters of this book spent so long on obedience, on silence, and on the destruction of a man's own house.** Inheritance travels whether or not it is deserved. **The only question is whether the inheritor knows what he is holding.**

### "No man, having put his hand to the plough"

And the transition requires one further thing from the successor, and the Lord names it with a hard sentence.

*And another also said, Lord, I will follow thee; but let me first go bid them farewell, which are at home at my house. And Jesus said unto him, No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God* (Luke 9:61–62).

**The request is not wicked. It is a farewell to his family.** And it is refused, and the reason is agricultural.

**A ploughman sets one hand on the handle and keeps his eye on a fixed point ahead. The work is done by a straight furrow, and a straight furrow requires an uninterrupted line of sight.** A man who turns his head to look back will deviate within a few paces and will continue deviating for the length of the field.

**The problem is not the affection. It is the geometry.** The looking back is not a single act; it is a change in the line of sight, and its effect is measure in an ever-widening drift.

**And note that Elisha was permitted exactly what this man was denied.** *Let me, I pray thee, kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow thee. And he said unto him, Go back again: for what have I done to thee?* (1 Kings 19:20).

**Elijah allowed the farewell. The Lord did not.** And the difference is visible in what Elisha had already done before he asked.

*And he returned back from him, and took a yoke of oxen, and slew them, and boiled their flesh with the instruments of the oxen, and gave unto the people, and they did eat. Then he arose, and went after Elijah, and ministered unto him* (1 Kings 19:21).

**Elisha destroyed his own means of return.** He had twelve yoke of oxen — an operating farm, and a livelihood, and a family business. **He slew the oxen with the plough, burned the yoke for fuel, cooked a meal, and then left.**

**Which is the point of no return executed with his own assets.** He did not merely leave the plough; he converted it into a furnace and a feast. **And having done that, a farewell was harmless.**

**The would-be disciple in Luke 9 had done nothing of the kind.** He wanted to negotiate the terms on the way to the field. **And the Lord's answer means what it says: a man who is still keeping his options is not a successor, because a successor is the man who has nothing to go back to.**

**And this is the last requirement in the chapter, and it cannot be supplied by any predecessor.** Elisha's farewell was permitted because the decision had already been executed. **The predecessor can hand over the mantle, the deposit, the honour and the command. He cannot make the successor burn the plough.**

**And no succession in Scripture is complete until someone has.** Whether it is a fishing boat abandoned on a shore at Capernaum, a farm burned at Abel-meholah, or three years in Arabia with the institutional door left closed — **the carrier is always a man who has made it impossible for himself to go home.**

### Principles

**First: the teacher withholds on purpose.** *I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now.* The unfinished curriculum is not a failure of the teacher; it is the arrangement by which the Spirit becomes the teacher.

**Second: there is revelation available only after the teacher has gone.** The promised Guide leads into truth that the disciples could not have received while the Lord was present.

**Third: the transition is tested in the predecessor's absence.** Elisha at the Jordan, asking *Where is the Lord God of Elijah?* — the question could not have been asked while Elijah stood beside him. **Send the successor alone, and do it early enough that a failure is recoverable.**

**Fourth: the carrier can think with the deposit rather than merely recite it.** Elisha's ministry is not Elijah's; the power is the same and the shape is not.

**Fifth: the Lord expected His successors to exceed Him.** *Greater works than these shall he do.* A predecessor who does not want this has not understood his own position.

**Sixth: the capacity to carry the withheld material is produced by use.** *Those who by reason of use have their senses exercised.* Not by more study, and not by more time.

**Seventh: the perpetual student is a scriptural category and a warning.** *When for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again.*

**And eighth: the transition requires an act of faith from both parties and cannot be arranged by any institutional mechanism.** The predecessor must send without guaranteeing the outcome. The successor must go without a guarantee of success. **And the question at the riverbank is answered only after it has been asked.**

### For the fathers

- **Withhold deliberately, and say so.** *Ye cannot bear them now.* Tell your successor that there is material you are not giving him yet, and why, and that the Spirit is the one who will teach it.
- **Send him alone, and early.** Elisha's transition happened at the Jordan with nobody watching. Construct assignments in which the successor has the whole responsibility and no access to you.
- **Expect his ministry to look different from yours.** Peter's shadow and Paul's handkerchiefs. If you require the reproduction of your methods, you have mistaken the mantle for the ministry.
- **Want him to exceed you, and say it.** *Greater works than these shall he do.* Say the sentence out loud, to him and to the people who will judge him.
- **Do not allow anyone to remain a student indefinitely.** *When for the time ye ought to be teachers.* If a man has been in your formation for long enough to teach, and is not teaching, something has gone wrong and it may be your arrangement.

### For the young

- **The transition is yours to make, and it will not be scheduled for you.** Nobody will hold a ceremony. There is a riverbank, and there is a cloak, and there is a question to ask.
- **Ask the question.** *Where is the Lord God of Elijah?* Ask it of God directly, and expect the answer to be given in the act of doing something.
- **Do not repeat your teacher's ministry; carry his God.** The deposit is to be guarded; the expression is yours to find, and the difference is a sign of health rather than of rebellion.
- **Exercise, and the capacity will come.** *By reason of use.* The material you cannot yet carry will become carryable by handling what you can.
- **Watch for the perpetual student in yourself.** If you have been in formation long enough to be teaching and are not teaching, the problem is not more information.
- **And expect to be sent.** The assignment where nobody is watching and there is no one to consult is not abandonment; it is the mechanism.

### Questions for reflection

1. What material has your teacher withheld from you, and does he have a reason?
2. When did you last do something in your calling with the person who formed you out of reach?
3. Whose ministry does yours most resemble, and is that because the deposit is the same or because you have not found your own expression?
4. Who is being formed by you? If nobody, what does that say about whether you are carrying or receiving?
5. What question have you not yet had to ask because you have never been alone at the riverbank?
