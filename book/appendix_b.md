# APPENDIX B|Structure for an Integrated Psychological Report|Appendix B: Structure for an Integrated Psychological Report

The structure below is adaptable to setting and purpose. Its logic is that the report should state the question, present the evidence, show the reasoning and answer the question, in that order.

## B.1|Identifying information

Client name, date of birth, reference number, date or dates of assessment, assessor and supervising clinician, source of referral, and the intended recipients of the report.

## B.2|Reason for referral

The question as the referrer stated it, followed by the question or questions the assessment addressed. Where the two differ, explain why.

## B.3|Sources of information

Interview with the client; interviews with informants, named by relationship; records reviewed, with dates; instruments administered, with forms and norm groups; observation, including setting and duration. State what was unavailable.

## B.4|Relevant background

Presenting problem and its development; developmental, family, educational, occupational and medical history; substance use; previous assessments and interventions; current circumstances. Distinguish reported from documented information.

## B.5|Behavioural observations and mental status

Appearance and behaviour, speech, mood and affect, thought form and content, perception, cognition, insight and judgement. Record observed behaviour separately from reported experience. Note cooperation, effort, fatigue and any factors affecting validity.

## B.6|Assessment methods

For each instrument: what it measures, the form used, the norm group, the reliability and validity relevant to this use, and any departure from standard administration. State the rationale for the selection.

## B.7|Results by clinically meaningful domain

Present results by domain — for example, mood, anxiety, cognition, personality, functioning — rather than by instrument. For each score report the obtained value, its confidence interval, its percentile or band, and a plain-language description. Integrate each result with the corresponding interview and informant evidence.

## B.8|Integrated interpretation and differential considerations

The hypotheses considered, the evidence for and against each, the reasoning that led to the preferred explanation, and the alternatives that remain open. Where sources conflict, state the conflict and its resolution or its persistence.

## B.9|Diagnostic conclusions

Diagnoses where the evidence supports them, stated with the classification system used and with provisional status where appropriate. Where a diagnosis is not made, state why.

## B.10|Formulation

Predisposing, precipitating, maintaining and protective factors; the explanatory account; the implications for intervention; and the predictions the formulation makes.

## B.11|Strengths and protective factors

The client's capacities, resources, relationships, values and achievements, and the factors that support recovery. This section is substantive, not ceremonial.

## B.12|Recommendations

Specific, feasible, sequenced and linked to identified needs, each naming an action, an agent, a setting and a review point. Distinguish what is required from what is desirable.

## B.13|Limitations and conditions affecting interpretation

What was assessed and what was not; the limits of norms, language, validity and time; and the conditions under which the conclusions would need revision.

## B.14|Summary for the reader

A short summary, written for the referrer, stating the question, the answer and the recommended next steps. Where the report will be read by the client, include a version written for them.
