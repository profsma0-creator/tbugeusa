# APPENDIX A|Assessment Planning and Quality Checklist|Appendix A: Assessment Planning and Quality Checklist

The checklist is intended for use before, during and after an assessment. It is not a substitute for clinical judgement; it is a device for making the reasoning explicit and for catching the omissions that recur in practice.

## A.1|Before the assessment

- Restate the referral question in terms of the decision it informs.
- Identify the decision-maker, the timescale and the standard of evidence required.
- Identify immediate safety, medical or safeguarding needs and plan for them.
- Establish what information already exists: records, previous assessments, school or employer reports.
- List the competing hypotheses and the evidence that would discriminate among them.
- Select methods that address the hypotheses directly, and record the rationale for each.
- Check the qualifications required for each instrument and confirm that they are held.
- Confirm the availability of norms appropriate to the client's age, language, culture and ability.
- Plan for language, literacy, sensory, motor and cognitive access, and record any accommodation.
- Set the time budget for the session or sessions and identify what will be deferred if time runs short.
- Prepare the environment: room, seating, materials, freedom from interruption.
- Confirm consent procedures, confidentiality limits and arrangements for feedback.

## A.2|During the assessment

- Explain the purpose, process, duration and limits of confidentiality before beginning.
- Observe and record behaviour, not only responses.
- Record any departure from standard administration at the time, with its reason.
- Record verbatim responses where the instrument requires it.
- Monitor effort, fatigue, distress and engagement, and record them.
- Follow up discrepancies between sources as they arise.
- Ask directly about risk and record what was asked and what was disclosed.
- Note any new hypothesis that emerges and record it.

## A.3|Scoring and analysis

- Score from the record rather than from memory.
- Apply the manual's rules exactly, including reversal, basal and ceiling rules.
- Enter demographic data accurately and verify that the correct norms were applied.
- Recompute totals independently.
- Compute confidence intervals for the scores that will be interpreted.
- Compute the standard error of the difference before interpreting any difference.
- Check the base rate of any pattern that will be interpreted.
- Evaluate performance validity and response style before interpreting clinical scales.

## A.4|Before finalising the report

- Confirm that the report answers the referral question explicitly and early.
- Confirm that each conclusion is traceable to stated evidence.
- Confirm that the evidence has been weighed rather than averaged.
- Confirm that alternative explanations have been considered and recorded.
- Confirm that uncertainty is stated specifically, with what would resolve it.
- Confirm that limitations are stated: language, norms, missing information, validity concerns.
- Confirm that recommendations name an action, an agent, a setting and a review point.
- Confirm that the language is free of pejorative terms and is intelligible to its readers.
- Confirm that consent, confidentiality and disclosure decisions are recorded.
- Arrange peer consultation for any high-stakes or ambiguous conclusion.

## A.5|After the assessment

- Provide feedback, invite correction and record the client's response.
- Revise the formulation where the client's response requires it.
- Record the review point and the arrangements for follow-up.
- Follow up a sample of recommendations to establish whether they were implemented and why.
- Record any assessment in which the outcome differed from the prediction, for later calibration.
