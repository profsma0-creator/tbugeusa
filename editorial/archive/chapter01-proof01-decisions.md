Repetition is unusually extensive: a sentence-based reading aid reduces it to approximately 8,210 distinct-sentence words, but this reading aid is not itself an edited manuscript and segmentation can split author initials. The work is based on the extracted chapter text, its cases, tables, objectives, exercises, and bibliography. A reading aid covering all source pages retained distinct sentence strings to expose repeated content; the original page-based source remains available. This is not a claim that all 446,000 words of the book have received close editorial reading. No work presented here establishes that the book has passed the original whole-book review requirement or the final independent proofreading requirement.

## Citation treatment

Eighty occurrences of the formula beginning “This approach is consistent with the broader methodological caution represented by” occur in the source chapter. Their source-to-claim relationships are not established by the surrounding prose. Those formulaic clauses have been removed from the proposed reading text **provisionally**, with every clause retained verbatim in `citation-clauses-for-review.csv`. This is a reviewable developmental intervention, not an assertion that all eighty citations are wrong.

The twenty original bibliographic entries remain in a clearly labelled source bibliography. No new reference, DOI, author name, publication year, or quotation has been created. Because source support is unresolved, the proof is deliberately not presented as having a complete in-text citation apparatus. Publication requires verification of support, restoration or replacement of appropriate citation anchors, and reconciliation of every retained reference against the final text.

The original spelling of reference titles and institutional names has been retained rather than silently regularised to British English. For example, whether a source's authentic title uses “behavior” or “behaviour”, and the correct official spelling of the World Health Organization's name, require record verification before the bibliography is finalised.

## Author Information Required — Chapter 1

### H01 — History of stress science

- **Source:** Section 1.3, original pp. 74–79; related historical overview in opening material.
- **Exact heading:** “1.3 History Of Stress Science”.
- **Missing information:** A source-supported historical account: named contributions, relevant milestones, and the limits of the associated evidence. No literal placeholder marks the missing content.
- **Why required:** The present general methodological advice does not fulfil the heading's promise.
- **Suggested replacement:** Connected historical prose, with verified citations for each substantive milestone; avoid an unsupported list of names and dates.

### H02 — Early lesion and behavioural research

- **Source:** Section 1.4, original pp. 79–84.
- **Exact heading:** “1.4 Early Lesion And Behavioural Studies”.
- **Missing information:** Identified studies, their actual observations, and a supported explanation of their relevance and interpretative limitations.
- **Why required:** No specific early study can be responsibly inferred from the heading alone.
- **Suggested replacement:** Study attribution and date; question/design; finding; limitation; justified relevance, with full references.

### H03 — Psychophysiology

- **Source:** Section 1.5, original pp. 84–89.
- **Exact heading:** “1.5 Rise Of Psychophysiology”.
- **Missing information:** A documented developmental account and relevant named research rather than another general methods paragraph.
- **Why required:** The edited proof can preserve existing measurement cautions but cannot manufacture the historical narrative.
- **Suggested replacement:** Source-supported progression connecting measurement developments to the questions they enabled.

### H04 — Neuroendocrinology

- **Source:** Section 1.6, original pp. 89–93.
- **Exact heading:** “1.6 Development Of Neuroendocrinology”.
- **Missing information:** Verified historical and scientific development specific to neuroendocrinology and traumatic stress.
- **Why required:** Assay cautions do not substitute for an account of the field's development.
- **Suggested replacement:** Narrative with relevant discoveries, their interpretation, and supporting primary or authoritative historical sources.

### H05 — Brain imaging

- **Source:** Section 1.7, original pp. 93–98.
- **Exact heading:** “1.7 Growth Of Brain Imaging”.
- **Missing information:** Supported historical milestones and named imaging investigations, including what they did and did not establish.
- **Why required:** General caution about imaging interpretation is not a historical account.
- **Suggested replacement:** Evidence-based narrative; study/design, finding, limitation, and citation.

### D01 — Terms without substantive definitions

- **Source:** Original definition passages, pp. 57–60; the book-wide glossary register also identifies generic entries.
- **Exact terms:** “homeostasis”, “allostasis”, “adaptation”, “biomarker”, “translation”, and related foundational terminology.
- **Missing information:** Precise, supported definitions and distinctions appropriate to the course.
- **Why required:** The original repeatedly calls these “precise scientific” terms without defining them. The proof flags the gap instead of reproducing false definitions.
- **Suggested replacement:** Term; concise definition; boundaries; relevant measurement or example already supported by the book; verified source.

### C01 — Evidence and bibliographic verification

- **Source:** Eighty citation clauses and twenty source bibliography entries, original pp. 64–113 and 132–134.
- **Required:** Support mapping for retained claims; complete bibliographic metadata verified against original records; decision on quarantine and replacement of source citations.
- **Why required:** Stylistic editing cannot validate a source's relevance or substitute for an evidence audit.
- **Suggested replacement:** Claim/section; supporting source; exact supporting location; proposed final citation and full reference.

## Typesetting and editorial checks

- Book proof uses a 7 × 10 inch page, mirrored inner/outer margins, 11 pt Source Serif 4 on 14.2 pt leading, and embedded regular/bold fonts. Font licence is stored with the source assets.
- PDF contents are generated from actual headings and pagination; navigation bookmarks are also generated. The DOCX contains an updateable contents field and may reflow on another system; refresh it in Word after editing.
- Primary sections and subordinate sections are numbered consistently; review/assignment features remain unnumbered.
- Tables have repeated header rows if split and retain whole rows; both tables are conceptual, with no numerical data to invent or alter.
- Compact figures use vector text and shapes in the PDF. The Word manuscript embeds images of the same figures. Diagrams state their non-causal, non-empirical scope.
- Running furniture and folios consistently identify this as a developmental proof, not a released book.
- Chapter text is secular. No devotional passage was identified in the reviewed chapter material. This does not certify that all other chapters or the original front matter have been secularised.
- A separate layout/checking pass found and corrected font-name collisions that initially prevented distinct bold-face embedding, and adjusted table widths to improve word wrapping.
- Machine checks and representative page inspection are recorded separately in `quality-checks.json`. This is not an independent full-book proofread.
