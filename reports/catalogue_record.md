## 5. Scope note

*Drawn from the book's preface:*

> This book is written for doctoral students and practising psychologists who are learning to conduct psychological assessment at a level that can withstand scrutiny — by a supervisor, a multidisciplinary team, a tribunal, or a client who reads the report and asks, reasonably, how the conclusion was reached.

The book is organised in five parts across sixteen chapters. Part I establishes the foundations: the purposes of assessment, the design of interviews, and the psychometric basis of score interpretation. Part II addresses the assessment of dispositions and abilities — personality, cognitive function, and achievement, aptitude and career. Part III takes up the major clinical presentations: mood, anxiety, obsessive-compulsive, trauma-related, bipolar, psychotic, substance-related and enduring personality difficulties. Part IV considers children and adolescents, couples and families, and the cultural, linguistic and disability considerations that condition every interpretation. Part V addresses risk and safeguarding, and closes with the integration of evidence into formulation, reports and feedback. Each chapter carries learning objectives, key terms, an Advanced Case Conference on a composite case, a summary, review and discussion questions, and a reading list.
